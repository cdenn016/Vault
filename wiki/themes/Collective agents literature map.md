# Collective agents literature map

This page is the reading map for the 256 references that the manuscript on communication, coherence and collective agents cites and that the vault did not yet hold. They were ingested on November 10, 2026; 109 have a local PDF and the others are recorded from their abstracts, from the reading records kept with the manuscript bibliography, or from their bibliographic records alone. Each source note states its reading basis. The map groups the references by the question they answer. It describes what each group of sources covers and what the manuscript takes from it, and it does not restate the results of the manuscript.

The manuscript also cites sources that were already in the vault, among them the notes gathered in [[Active inference communication and collective boundaries]] and the concept pages [[Markov Blanket]], [[Collective active inference]], [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], [[Coarse Graining]], [[Renormalization group flow]], [[Opinion dynamics]] and [[Rational inattention]]. Those are not repeated here.

> [!note] Editorial: scope of the notes
> The source notes were written from the first pages of the local PDFs, from abstracts and from the reading records of the manuscript bibliography. They are not page-by-page readings, and a note built on an abstract or a record says so. Claims in the wiki that need more than a note supports should return to the source.

## Communication as action, signs and shared worlds

These sources treat an utterance, a sign or a trace left in the environment as an act with consequences for other agents. They cover speech acts, the grounding of symbols, stigmergy, strategic signaling, shared intentionality and the cultural scaffolding of expectations, together with active-inference models of belief sharing and cumulative culture. The manuscript draws on them for its treatment of messages, performative acts and records as actions that pass through the external states.

- [[lindley-1956-measure-information-provided]] (Lindley 1956): The paper introduces a measure of the information an experiment provides, derived from Shannon's work and defined relative to a prior distribution over the parameter space, and applies it to compare experiments and to design them.
- [[grasse-1959-reconstruction-nid-coordinations]] (Grassé 1959): A 1959 article in Insectes Sociaux on nest reconstruction and coordination among individuals in two termite taxa, whose title announces a theory of stigmergy as an interpretation of the behavior of building termites.
- [[austin-1962-how-do-things]] (Austin 1962): A book first published in 1962 by Clarendon Press, Oxford; the manuscript's record is a library catalog entry and the text itself was not read.
- [[jeffrey-1965-logic-decision]] (Jeffrey 1965): A 1965 book by Richard C. Jeffrey published by McGraw-Hill, with a second edition from University of Chicago Press in 1983.
- [[crawford-1982-strategic-information-transmission]] (Crawford and Sobel 1982): The paper models strategic communication between a better-informed sender, who sends a possibly noisy signal, and a receiver, who then acts in a way that determines the welfare of both.
- [[harnad-1990-symbol-grounding-problem]] (Harnad 1990): A 1990 paper in Physica D on the symbol grounding problem, a problem of how symbols acquire meaning.
- [[theraulaz-1999-brief-history-stigmergy]] (Theraulaz and Bonabeau 1999): A historical account of stigmergy, a class of mechanisms mediating interactions among animals, in the context of social insects, with a discussion of two kinds: quantitative and qualitative stigmergy.
- [[tomasello-2005-understanding-sharing-intentions]] (Tomasello et al. 2005): A target article proposing that the difference between human cognition and that of other species is shared intentionality, the ability to take part in collaborative activities with shared goals and intentions.
- [[griffiths-2007-language-evolution-iterated]] (Griffiths and Kalish 2007): The paper analyzes iterated learning, in which each learner acquires a language from the previous one, for Bayesian learners that combine a prior with linguistic data.
- [[pearl-2009-causality-models-reasoning]] (Pearl 2009): A monograph, in its second edition of 2009, presenting the probabilistic, manipulative, counterfactual and structural approaches to causation as a mathematical theory with tools relating causal connections to statistical associations, according to the...
- [[heylighen-2016-stigmergy-universal-coordination]] (Heylighen 2016): An article in Cognitive Systems Research, volume 38, on stigmergy as a coordination mechanism, with the title naming definition and components as its subject.
- [[ramstead-2016-cultural-affordances-scaffolding]] (Ramstead et al. 2016): A conceptual framework for how human agents engage with cultural affordances, extending the notion of affordance to sociocultural forms of life and giving a multilevel account of how affordances are learned and transmitted.
- [[sasaki-2017-cumulative-culture-can]] (Sasaki and Biro 2017): An experiment with homing pigeons shows that groups whose members are repeatedly replaced improve their homing performance over generations, which the authors read as cumulative culture arising from collective intelligence.
- [[constant-2019-regimes-expectations-active]] (Constant et al. 2019): The paper introduces "deontic value", a construct built on active inference and Markov decision processes, to model how people acquire shared expectations about how to behave in normalized social settings, and so to formalize social conformity.

- [[catal-2025-belief-sharing-blessing]] (Çatal et al. 2025): The paper models communication between active-inference agents as belief sharing, in which one agent's beliefs become an observation modality for another.

## Active inference, expected free energy and Markov blankets

This group holds the active-inference sources that the manuscript uses or argues with: expected free energy and its variants, deep and hierarchical generative models, the particular partition and the debate on whether sparse coupling yields a blanket, multiscale and morphogenetic applications, and renormalizing generative models. It also holds the sources on conditional independence and on the decay of conditional mutual information that the definition of an approximate blanket relies on.

## Double counting, fusion and naive social learning

Estimation theory and economics reached the same problem from two sides: information that travels along several paths is counted more than once unless the receiver models the paths. The sources cover track-to-track correlation and decentralized data fusion, Gaussian belief propagation, consensus filters, persuasion bias, correlation neglect, naive herding and learning, echo chambers, and the value of public information. The manuscript places this ladder, from literal reading to exact fusion, inside each receiver's generative model.

- [[kalman-1960-approach-linear-filtering]] (Kalman 1960): The paper re-examines linear filtering and prediction using the Bode-Shannon representation of random processes and the state-transition method for dynamic systems.
- [[bar-shalom-1981-track-track-correlation]] (Bar-Shalom 1981): A two-page note on testing whether two tracks represent the same target, which presents a test that accounts for correlation between the two tracks' state estimates.
- [[lai-1982-least-squares-estimates]] (Lai and Wei 1982): The paper establishes strong consistency and asymptotic normality of least squares estimates when the regressors are stochastic, under weak assumptions on regressors and errors, and applies the results to interval estimation and to recursive identification...
- [[bar-shalom-1986-effect-common-process]] (Bar-Shalom and Campo 1986): A short note on how common process noise affects the fusion of state estimates of one target from two sensors that each run their own tracking subsystem.
- [[grime-1994-data-fusion-decentralized]] (Grime and Durrant-Whyte 1994): A 1994 article in Control Engineering Practice on data fusion in decentralized sensor networks, as its title and venue establish.
- [[weiss-2001-correctness-belief-propagation]] (Weiss and Freeman 2001): An analysis of loopy belief propagation when the graph's nodes are jointly Gaussian, with an analytical relation between true and loopy posteriors, sufficient conditions for convergence, and the result that converged propagation gives correct posterior...
- [[morris-2002-social-value-public]] (Morris and Shin 2002): A study of the welfare effect of public information when agents want their actions to match an underlying state and also to coordinate with one another through a strategic complementarity.
- [[demarzo-2003-persuasion-bias-social]] (DeMarzo et al. 2003): A boundedly rational model of opinion formation on a social network in which agents fail to adjust for repetition in the information they receive (persuasion bias).

- [[eyster-2010-naive-herding-rich]] (Eyster and Rabin 2010): A social-learning model in which people naively believe that each earlier action reflects only that person's private information, so that early signals are overweighted.
- [[galeotti-2010-law-few]] (Galeotti and Goyal 2010): The paper starts from the empirical finding that most individuals get most of their information from a small group of influencers who differ little in observable traits, and builds a model in which that pattern arises in equilibrium.

- [[hendrickx-2010-distributed-anonymous-discrete]] (Hendrickx et al. 2010): The paper defines a model of deterministic computation by a network of identical, anonymous nodes with bounded memory that know only their own neighbors, and characterizes which functions of the nodes' initial values such a network can compute.

- [[mossel-2016-efficient-bayesian-learning]] (Mossel et al. 2016): A group of Bayesian agents on a connected graph each holds a Gaussian private signal of a common real-valued state, repeatedly announces its current estimate to its neighbors, and updates by Bayes' rule.

## Spectral partitions, eigenvalue bounds and analytic tools

The definition of a collective by contrast compares an internal relaxation rate with an outward one, so the manuscript needs spectral clustering and its guarantees, bounds on eigenvalues and on the rotation of invariant subspaces under perturbation, and the decay of the entries of inverses of banded or localized matrices. A few general analytic references that the proofs cite are filed here as well.

- [[sheppard-1899-application-error-cases]] (Sheppard 1899): A memoir applying the theory of error to statistical data whose frequency curves are approximately normal, including the joint frequencies of correlated attributes.
- [[temple-1928-rayleigh-principle-applied]] (Temple 1928): Temple examines and extends Rayleigh's method of approximating the frequency of the gravest vibration mode of a continuous system from a trial function, and devises an upper bound on the error of the approximation.
- [[kato-1949-upper-lower-bounds]] (Kato 1949): A 1949 article by Tosio Kato in the Journal of the Physical Society of Japan on upper and lower bounds of eigenvalues.
- [[loomis-1949-inequality-related-isoperimetric]] (Loomis and Whitney 1949): A two-page 1949 note in the Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society on an inequality related to the isoperimetric inequality, as its title states.
- [[aronszajn-1950-reproducing-kernels]] (Aronszajn 1950): A 1950 article in the Transactions of the American Mathematical Society on the theory of reproducing kernels, as the title and venue establish.
- [[davis-1970-rotation-eigenvectors-perturbation]] (Davis and Kahan 1970): A study of how far the invariant subspaces of a Hermitian operator can move under a small perturbation, characterized through angles between subspaces and bounded using an eigenvalue gap.
- [[combes-1973-asymptotic-behaviour-eigenfunctions]] (Combes and Thomas 1973): A 1973 paper in Communications in Mathematical Physics on the asymptotic behavior of eigenfunctions of multiparticle Schrödinger operators, as its title states.
- [[stewart-1973-error-perturbation-bounds]] (Stewart 1973): The paper describes a technique for error bounds on characteristic subspaces in the eigenvalue problem, the generalized eigenvalue problem and the singular value decomposition, based on an iterative process for generating the subspaces.
- [[demko-1984-decay-rates-inverses]] (Demko et al. 1984): A proof, via spectral theory and classical approximation theory, that entries of the inverse of a band matrix decay exponentially, with a rate given in terms of the spectrum.
- [[jaffard-1990-proprietes-matrices-bien]] (Jaffard 1990): The paper studies two classes of infinite matrices whose entries decay exponentially or polynomially away from the diagonal, and shows that invertibility on $\ell^2$ implies the inverse has the same type of decay.
- [[lieb-2001-analysis]] (Lieb and Loss 2001): A graduate textbook in analysis in the American Mathematical Society series Graduate Studies in Mathematics, second edition.
- [[kannan-2004-clusterings-good-bad]] (Kannan et al. 2004): The paper develops a bicriteria measure of clustering quality and analyzes a popular spectral clustering algorithm, with worst-case guarantees for one variant.
- [[newman-2006-modularity-community-structure]] (Newman 2006): The paper reformulates the modularity quality function for network divisions in terms of the eigenvectors of a modularity matrix, which yields a spectral algorithm for community detection.
- [[carlsson-2010-characterization-stability-convergence]] (Carlsson and Mémoli 2010): A paper on hierarchical clustering that treats dendrograms as ultrametrics and states an existence and uniqueness theorem, according to the abstract as read when the manuscript was written.

## Potential games and stochastic approximation

The sources give the theory of potential and near-potential games, logit-response and statistical-mechanics treatments of strategic interaction, the stochastic-approximation theorems that connect a recursion with noise to its mean-field map, and the design of payoff functions for members of a collective.

## Synchronization, balance and frustration

Consistency of pairwise translations around cycles is a synchronization problem. The sources are the spectral and semidefinite approaches to angular and group synchronization, structural balance of signed graphs and its generalization to gain graphs, and the frustration of a plaquette in spin glasses.

- [[harary-1953-notion-balance-signed]] (Harary 1953): A 1953 paper in the Michigan Mathematical Journal on the notion of balance of a signed graph, as its title and venue establish.
- [[cartwright-1956-structural-balance-generalization]] (Cartwright and Harary 1956): A paper in Psychological Review whose title presents it as a generalization of Heider's theory of structural balance.
- [[toulouse-1977-frustration-effect-spin]] (Toulouse 1977): A 1977 paper on the frustration effect in spin glasses, as its title and venue establish.
- [[zaslavsky-1989-biased-graphs-bias]] (Zaslavsky 1989): A 1989 paper on biased graphs, treating bias, balance and gains, as its title and venue establish.
- [[singer-2011-angular-synchronization-eigenvectors]] (Singer 2011): The paper treats recovery of unknown angles from noisy measurements of their pairwise offsets, mod $2\pi$, and gives an eigenvector method and a semidefinite relaxation that tolerate many outlier measurements.
- [[abbe-2018-group-synchronization-grids]] (Abbe et al. 2018): The paper studies when unknown elements of a compact group, attached to the vertices of a graph, can be recovered from noisy observations of their differences along edges, with the graph taken to be a $d$-dimensional grid.

## Random fields, phase ordering and long-range order

Whether a population with quenched disorder or long-range coupling can order, and how domains coarsen when it does, is classical statistical mechanics. This group holds the Imry and Ma argument and its rigorous forms, correlation lengths of the random-field Ising model, phase-ordering kinetics, the exact solution of the planar Ising model, long-range one-dimensional models, and models of dialect and language competition that use the same mechanisms.

The energetic statements of the manuscript use stochastic thermodynamics: dissipation as a relative entropy between forward and reversed paths and its estimation from data, information flow in bipartite and causal networks, the minimal cost of measurement and erasure, and semantic information defined through viability.

- [[gomez-marin-2007-bounding-dissipation-stochastic]] (Gomez-Marin et al. 2007): The paper extends an exact expression for the average dissipated work in driven systems from Hamiltonian dynamics to stochastic dynamics, writes it as a relative entropy between forward and backward path probabilities, and derives lower bounds on...
- [[kawai-2007-dissipation-phase-space]] (Kawai et al. 2007): For a Hamiltonian system driven between two canonical equilibrium states, the average dissipated work equals $kT$ times the relative entropy between the phase-space densities of the forward and time-reversed protocols, taken at the same intermediate time.
- [[sagawa-2009-minimal-energy-cost]] (Sagawa and Ueda 2009): The Letter derives lower bounds on the work needed to measure and to erase information in a memory in contact with a heat bath, and calls the result a second law of information thermodynamics.
- [[roldan-2010-estimating-dissipation-single]] (Roldán and Parrondo 2010): The time-reversal asymmetry of a stationary time series carries information about the entropy production of the mechanism that generated it, even without knowledge of that mechanism.
- [[ito-2013-information-thermodynamics-causal]] (Ito and Sagawa 2013): The letter describes nonequilibrium dynamics of several interacting fluctuating systems with a causal network (Bayesian network) and derives generalizations of the second law and the fluctuation theorem that include an informational term set by the network...
- [[hartich-2014-stochastic-thermodynamics-bipartite]] (Hartich et al. 2014): The paper studies the stationary state of a Markov process on a bipartite system, in which only one of two variables changes in any jump, and relates work extraction, transfer entropy and a Maxwell's demon interpretation of one subsystem.
- [[horowitz-2014-thermodynamics-continuous-information]] (Horowitz and Esposito 2014): The paper gives a thermodynamic formalism for information transfer between two interacting systems, autonomous or driven, within stochastic thermodynamics, in which the entropy balance of one system acquires a term tied to the mutual information rate.
- [[kolchinsky-2018-semantic-information-autonomous]] (Kolchinsky and Wolpert 2018): The paper defines semantic information as the syntactic information a physical system has about its environment that is causally necessary for the system to maintain its own existence, and analyzes it with non-equilibrium statistical physics.

## Evolution and selection of collectives

Selection among collectives is treated with the Price equation and with models of evolutionary transitions in individuality.

- [[price-1970-selection-covariance]] (Price 1970): A two-page note in Nature expressing the change in a gene frequency as a covariance between offspring number and gene frequency, plus a transmission term.
- [[harman-2020-when-science-mirrors]] (Harman 2020): A historical article on the origins of the Price equation, arguing that George Price's turn to mathematical population genetics was connected to his personal life.
- [[bourrat-2022-tradeoff-breaking-model]] (Bourrat et al. 2022): A mathematical and conceptual analysis arguing that particle and collective fitness cannot decouple in evolutionary transitions in individuality, and proposing trait tradeoffs and their breaking as the description of such a transition.
