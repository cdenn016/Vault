# Bundle scale space

## Definition

A **bundle scale space** is the family of node-feature transformations obtained by running a
diffusion PDE on an *associated vector bundle* over a graph, generated by a generalized Laplacian.
Node features are modelled not as plain vectors but as **sections** $\varphi\in\Gamma(E)$ of the
associated bundle; a processing architecture is then a map of sections
$\mathcal{F}:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)$, and running the diffusion for time $t$ gives a scale-space
family indexed by $t$ ([[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]]).

The defining property, and the reason for the name, is the **fixed-point set**. An ordinary Gaussian
scale space washes everything out: its only fixed points are constants. A bundle scale space instead
has *non-trivial* fixed points, namely the [[Harmonic map|harmonic sections]] of the associated
bundle ([[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]]). Curvature in the connection obstructs the existence of
a globally constant section, so what survives infinite diffusion is a structured field rather than a
featureless one.

## Why it matters here

This is the **local-gauge half** of the Heidelberg program, and it is the closest published match to
the geometric skeleton of the [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. The architectures are
equivariant with respect to *local* actions of a general symmetry group $G$ — $G$ may act on the
feature $\varphi_v$ at each node $v$ by an **individual** $g_v\in G$ — and local symmetry is
preserved *by construction*, because features are sections of an associated bundle rather than
elements of a fixed vector space ([[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]]). That is the same design
argument the program makes for [[Agents as fibre-bundle sections|agents as sections]], reached
independently and one step earlier.

Two things this construction supplies that the program can import rather than re-derive: a worked
**discretization recipe** that keeps local symmetry intact (vector diffusion maps plus lattice gauge
theory), and a clean statement of what a gauge-covariant diffusion's equilibrium *is* (harmonic
sections, not constants). Two things it does not supply: the fibers are **vector spaces**, not
[[Statistical manifold|statistical manifolds]], and the objective is a scale-space/diffusion
semigroup, not a [[Variational free energy|variational free energy]] over a population of agents.

> [!note] Editorial: The pairing "bundle scale spaces supply the bundle-and-local-gauge half;
> [[Sigma flow|sigma flows]] supply the statistical-manifold-fiber half; neither combines them" is
> the vault's own synthesis. The three Heidelberg papers cite one another but none states this
> division, and none of them frames its object as an approximate-inference objective.

## Details

**Construction.** Start from a principal-bundle-style local symmetry: a general group $G$ acting
node-wise. Model features as sections of the associated vector bundle $E$. Build $\mathcal{F}$ from
a diffusion PDE generated by a **generalized Laplacian** on $E$ — the bundle analogue of the
Laplace–Beltrami operator, in which the plain derivative is replaced by a covariant derivative. Run
it: the resulting one-parameter family is the bundle scale space, its short-time behaviour is
smoothing, and its long-time behaviour is projection onto harmonic sections
([[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]]).

**Discretization.** Local symmetry survives discretization only if the discretization respects it.
The paper uses **vector diffusion maps** and **lattice gauge theory** for this purpose
([[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]]) — the same two ingredients the vault already tracks under
[[Graph synchronization and connection Laplacians]] and [[Lattice gauge theory]]. Parametrizations
suited to supervised learning are outlined, with a proof-of-concept experiment; the paper is a short
conference contribution (SSVM 2025) rather than a full theory.

**Where the theory is worked out.** The companion long paper
[[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]] develops the discrete apparatus in full: discrete vector bundles,
graph voltages as discretized gauge fields, gauged Laplacians and their heat kernels, an axiomatic
characterization of those kernels among all gauge-invariant transformations, and a **discrete
Yang–Mills energy** used to characterize the resulting operators. It also supplies an appendix
deriving the discrete objects *from* smooth ones — graph voltages from
[[Parallel transport|parallel transport]], discrete Laplacians from covariant derivatives — which is
the bridge a program working in the continuum but computing on a finite graph most needs.

> [!warning] Compact group, and the caveat carries over
> The concrete machinery in [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]] is developed for $\mathrm{SO}(d)$-valued
> graph voltages, and the vector-diffusion-map lineage underlying
> [[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]] is likewise orthogonal-group. The program's structure group is
> the **noncompact** $\mathrm{GL}(K)$. The existing caution on
> [[Graph synchronization and connection Laplacians]] therefore applies unchanged: adjointness,
> positivity, spectral-gap and rounding statements proved in the compact setting do not transfer
> without a declared fiber metric and fresh proofs.
