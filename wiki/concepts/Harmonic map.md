# Harmonic map

## Definition

A **harmonic map** is a critical point of the *harmonic energy* (equivalently the *Dirichlet
energy*) of a smooth map $f:(M,h)\to(N,g)$ between two Riemannian manifolds,

$$
E(f)\;=\;\tfrac12\int_M \lVert \mathrm{d}f\rVert^2_{h,g}\,\mathrm{dvol}_h ,
$$

where the norm of the differential is taken with respect to the *domain* metric $h$ on $M$ and the
*target* metric $g$ on $N$. The Euler–Lagrange equation is the vanishing of the tension field, a
nonlinear elliptic system; the associated $L^2$ gradient flow is a geometric diffusion-type PDE.

Two degenerate cases fix the intuition. When $N=\mathbb{R}$, the energy is the ordinary Dirichlet
integral and its critical points are harmonic functions. When $M=S^1$, the Euler–Lagrange equation is
the geodesic equation. Harmonic maps in general therefore behave, in the phrasing of
[[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]], as **generalized higher-dimensional geodesics**: the energy penalizes
how much the map has to stretch, measured intrinsically in both geometries at once.

Everything here is a statement about *two* metrics. Changing $h$ changes what "small gradient" means
on the domain; changing $g$ changes what "far apart" means in the target. This double dependence is
what makes the harmonic-energy template so reusable — and it is precisely the handle that
[[Sigma flow|sigma flows]] turn into a learnable component.

## Why it matters here

The harmonic energy is the mathematical object sitting underneath the **covariant Dirichlet term**
of the program's free energy. In the [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] the minimal-coupling
vertex that transports a belief across a base-lattice edge by the connection and scores the mismatch
is exactly a discrete covariant Dirichlet / Gaussian-Markov-random-field energy — see
[[Non-flat connection and the photon analogy]], where the frozen-connection attention-free variant
is identified as the closed-ELBO form. Reading that term as "a harmonic energy for a bundle-valued
field" connects the program to a large classical PDE literature and, more immediately, to the
Heidelberg group's program, which studies essentially that term *on its own*.

> [!note] Editorial: The identification "the program's covariant Dirichlet term is a discrete
> harmonic energy" is the vault's own reading. The Heidelberg sources establish the harmonic-energy
> apparatus; they do not discuss the program's free energy, and no source asserts the equivalence.

## Details

**Existence theory is more delicate than the definition suggests.** [[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]]
(§1.2.2) surveys the relevant results and is candid about which hypotheses they need. For compact
$M$ and $N$, lower semicontinuity of the energy with respect to $L^2$-convergence is obtained by
$\Gamma$-convergence, and existence is proved *assuming the target $N$ has nonpositive sectional
curvature*, which supplies the convexity the argument needs. For a compact connected domain with
nonempty boundary and a complete target, existence for the Dirichlet problem is available under a
positive upper bound on the target's sectional curvature. A third line, based on the
Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, treats the problem comprehensively but still assumes the
target manifold is closed, at the cost of substantial Banach-manifold machinery on Sobolev map
spaces.

These hypotheses matter because the constructions that interest this vault **violate** them. As
[[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]] states in §1.3, its own target manifold is open, has positive sectional
curvature, and carries a non-metric affine connection, so the authors explicitly leave existence and
global convergence of their gradient flow to future work and confine themselves to geometric
structure plus a Lyapunov argument. Anyone importing the harmonic-map template into an
information-geometric setting inherits exactly that gap.

**Harmonic sections.** The same variational idea applies to sections of a vector bundle rather than
maps into a manifold: replace the differential $\mathrm{d}f$ by a covariant derivative
$\nabla^\omega\varphi$ built from a connection, and the critical points become **harmonic sections**
— the covariantly-constant-as-possible fields compatible with the given connection. This is the
form [[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]] uses: a diffusion PDE on an associated vector bundle,
generated by a generalized Laplacian, whose non-trivial fixed points are harmonic sections of that
bundle (see [[Bundle scale space]]). The contrast the paper draws is instructive — ordinary Gaussian
scale space flattens everything to a constant, whereas a *bundle* scale space has a richer fixed-point
set precisely because the connection can have holonomy that forbids a global constant section.

**Relation to the connection Laplacian.** In the discrete setting the covariant Dirichlet energy of
a section over a graph is the quadratic form of a
[[Graph synchronization and connection Laplacians|gauged / connection Laplacian]], and its minimizers
are the discrete harmonic sections. [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]] makes the whole chain explicit,
deriving discrete Laplacians from covariant derivatives and graph voltages from parallel transport.
