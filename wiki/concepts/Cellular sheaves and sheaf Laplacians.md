## Definition

A cellular sheaf $\mathcal F$ on a regular cell complex $X$ assigns a vector space (stalk) $\mathcal F(\sigma)$ to each cell and a linear restriction map $\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}:\mathcal F(\sigma)\to\mathcal F(\tau)$ to each incident pair $\sigma\le\tau$, subject to $\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\sigma}=\mathrm{id}$ and the composition law $\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\rho}=\mathcal F_{\tau\trianglelefteq\rho}\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}$; equivalently a functor from the face poset to $\mathbf{Vect}$ ([[hansen-2018-toward-a-spectral-theory-of-cellular-sheaves]], Definition 2.4). On a graph the composition law is vacuous, and a sheaf is just a stalk per vertex and edge and one map per incidence. With an orientation $e=u\to v$, the coboundary is
$$(\delta x)_e=\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}x_v-\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}x_u,$$
its kernel is the space of global sections $H^0(G;\mathcal F)$, and the degree-0 sheaf Laplacian is $L_{\mathcal F}=\delta^{\ast}\delta$, with diagonal blocks $\sum_{e\ni v}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}^{\ast}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}$ and off-diagonal blocks $-\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}^{\ast}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}$ ([[hansen-ghrist-2021-discourse-sheaves]], Eqs. 2.3–2.7). On a higher complex the cochains $C^k=\bigoplus_{\dim\sigma=k}\mathcal F(\sigma)$ form a complex, and the Hodge Laplacians $\Delta_k=\delta_k^{\ast}\delta_k+\delta_{k-1}\delta_{k-1}^{\ast}$ satisfy $\ker\Delta_k\cong H^k(X;\mathcal F)$ with the orthogonal decomposition $C^k=\mathcal H^k\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta_{k-1}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta_k^{\ast}$ (Theorem 3.1 of the 2018 paper).

The adjoints require inner products on the stalks (a weighted sheaf), and Hansen–Ghrist stress that this choice is not innocent. The Laplacian does not determine the sheaf, since nonisomorphic sheaves can share one Laplacian (Fig. 3.1, "one cannot hear the shape of a sheaf"); normalization is a reweighting of stalk inner products rather than of the matrix (Definition 3.5, Lemma 3.6); and for invertible restriction maps the sheaf–cosheaf duality given by inverses agrees with the duality given by adjoints only when the maps are unitary (§3.5).

## Results in the spectral-theory paper

A sheaf with invertible restriction maps is a discrete vector bundle, the cellular form of a local system, and the paper states that its coboundary is a discretization of the connection "whose flatness is manifest" in $\delta^2=0$ (§3.5). A discrete $O(n)$ bundle is defined as one whose restriction maps are positive multiples of orthogonal maps, so that edge weights are separated from transport, and its degree-0 Laplacian is the graph connection Laplacian of Singer and Wu (§3.6; [[Graph synchronization and connection Laplacians]]).

The harmonic-extension result (Proposition 4.1) says that data prescribed on a subcomplex $B$ has a harmonic extension to $X\setminus B$, unique when the relative cohomology $H^k(X,B;\mathcal F)$ vanishes; for 0-cochains existence is unconditional. Kron reduction, the Schur-complement elimination of interior vertices that for graphs always yields a smaller graph Laplacian, fails for sheaves in general (§4.2). The counterexample is a star whose $\mathbb R^2$ center restricts to three $\mathbb R$ leaves by the two coordinate projections and their sum; its sections on the boundary form a two-dimensional space that no sheaf on the three boundary vertices can reproduce, because the interior vertex imposes a constraint among the boundary vertices that pairwise edges cannot express. Reduction does survive for vertex stalks of dimension at most one, through closure of factor-width-two matrices under Schur complements (Theorem 4.3).

The spectral section transfers standard graph facts. The nonzero spectrum of $\Delta_k$ is the disjoint union of those of the up and down Laplacians, and the up Laplacian in degree $k$ and the down Laplacian in degree $k+1$ share nonzero eigenvalues, both being squared singular values of $\delta_k$ (Propositions 5.1–5.2). Normalized up Laplacians on simplicial complexes have spectrum bounded by $k+2$ (Proposition 5.5). Deleting an upward-closed set of cells interlaces eigenvalues with a shift given by a codimension of cohomology (Theorem 5.7, Proposition 5.8). Pushforward along a locally injective cellular map is isospectral (Proposition 5.10), the spectrum of a sheaf is contained in that of its pullback to a covering space (Proposition 5.12), and product sheaves have Kronecker-sum Laplacians (Propositions 5.15–5.17). Effective resistance and spectral sparsification by resistance-weighted sampling generalize to cosheaves (Theorem 6.4). The Bandeira–Singer–Spielman Cheeger inequality for $O(n)$ bundles ([[bandeira-2013-connection-cheeger]]) is restated, and the authors show that its rounding argument already fails for partial isometries (§7.1). A structural Cheeger inequality, relating the spectrum to the distance to the nearest sheaf that has a nontrivial section, is posed but not proved (§7.2, §9.2).

The applications section is a set of sketches. Heat flow $\dot x=-\Delta_k x$ converges exponentially to the orthogonal projection onto harmonic cochains (Proposition 8.1). Flocking is a sheaf with $\mathbb R^3$ vertex stalks and $\mathbb R$ edge stalks given by bearing projections (§8.2). Communication compression replaces the constant sheaf by edge projections onto low-dimensional quotients chosen to preserve $H^0$, with a stated condition under which total communication falls (§8.5–8.6). Synchronization is restated as finding an approximate section of an observed sheaf, or alternatively the nearest sheaf that admits a section, which is the cycle-consistency denoising problem (§8.7). The closing questions list metrics and moduli of sheaves, random walks, and directedness, which the authors expect to need cones, semimodules or nonabelian cohomology (§9.5).

## Results in the discourse-sheaf paper

A discourse sheaf places a private opinion space on each agent and a discourse space on each edge, with restriction maps read as the linear expression of private opinions on the topics discussed. Two agents are in expressed agreement when $\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}x_u=\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}x_v$, which does not require equal private opinions. Varying stalks allow private topics, kernels of restriction maps allow selective silence, scalings model exaggeration, and negative scalings model lying, which may differ by listener (§3).

The sheaf heat equation $\dot x=-\alpha L_{\mathcal F}x$ converges to the orthogonal projection onto $H^0$ (Theorem 4.1), so the only stable opinion profiles are global sections, and an example with polite lies supports a stable polarized section (Example 4.2, Fig. 4). With stubborn agents on a set $U$, the dynamics converge to the harmonic extension of their opinions nearest the initial condition (Theorem 5.1), which minimizes $\lVert\delta x\rVert^2$ subject to the clamp. Driving the inputs on $U$ makes the system stabilizable when $H^0(G,U;\mathcal F)=0$, and observing a set $Y$ makes it detectable when $H^0(G,Y;\mathcal F)=0$ (Theorems 6.1–6.2). Finite reluctance is modeled by attaching to each agent a stubborn parent vertex with an identity-weighted edge, which reduces it to the stubborn case (§7, Fig. 5).

Expression can evolve instead of opinion. The flow $\dot{\mathcal F}_{v\trianglelefteq e}=-\beta(\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}x_v-\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}x_u)x_v^{\top}$ converges to the Frobenius-nearest sheaf for which $x$ is a global section (Theorem 8.1). In the two-agent example the restriction map of the more extreme agent changes sign, so that agent "learns to lie" (Example 8.2). Running both flows together is gradient descent on $\tfrac12x^{\top}\delta^{\top}\delta x$ in a rate-weighted metric (Lemma 9.1). Smooth equilibria are Lyapunov stable (Theorem 9.2), and the diagonal blocks of $\delta^{\top}\delta-xx^{\top}$ are conserved along the joint flow, so that if any such block is indefinite at the start the limit has $x\neq0$ (Theorem 9.3); the paper's proof uses equal rates. The norms $\lVert\delta\rVert_F$, $\lVert x\rVert$ and $\lVert\delta x\rVert$ and the Rayleigh quotient $\lVert\delta x\rVert^2/\lVert x\rVert^2$ do not increase (Theorem 9.4).

Inserting an edgewise map between $\delta$ and $\delta^{\top}$ gives nonlinear Laplacians $\delta^{\top}\nabla U\circ\delta$. When each edge potential is radially unbounded with a unique minimum at zero, trajectories still converge to the projection onto $H^0$ (Proposition 10.1). A potential that vanishes beyond a threshold $D_e$ gives bounded confidence on sheaves (§11). Its equilibria are exactly the global sections of the subsheaf on the edges whose discrepancy lies below threshold (Theorem 11.1), and equilibria strictly inside such a region attract their neighborhoods to the nearest section of that subsheaf (Theorem 11.2). Antagonistic edges with potential $-\lVert y\rVert^2$ give a signed Laplacian $\delta^{\top}S\delta$ that is indefinite, hence unstable, whenever the negative edges form a cut across which some local section does not extend by zero (Proposition 12.1). The authors name probabilistic elements and higher-order (simplicial) interactions as open extensions (§13).

## The agent network as a weighted sheaf

> [!note] Editorial: The correspondences in this section are derivations made for this page, not results of either paper.

For Gaussian beliefs $q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ and links $\Omega_{ij}$, the mean part of the alignment term $\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}\mathrm{KL}(q_i\Vert\Omega_{ij}q_j)$ is
$$\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}(\mu_i-\Omega_{ij}\mu_j)^{\top}P_{ij}^{-1}(\mu_i-\Omega_{ij}\mu_j)=\tfrac12\lVert\delta\mu\rVert^2,\qquad P_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}\Sigma_j\Omega_{ij}^{\top},$$
since the quadratic term of $\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p)$ is weighted by the precision of the second argument, here the transported sender. (An earlier version of this page weighted it by $\Sigma_i^{-1}$, which is the precision of the reverse divergence; corrected October 7, 2026.) This is the Dirichlet energy of a sheaf on the directed multigraph (one edge per ordered pair) with Euclidean edge stalks $\mathbb R^K$ and restriction maps $\mathcal F_{i\trianglelefteq e}=\sqrt{\beta_{ij}}P_{ij}^{-1/2}$ and $\mathcal F_{j\trianglelefteq e}=\sqrt{\beta_{ij}}P_{ij}^{-1/2}\Omega_{ij}$; the restriction maps depend on $\Omega_{ij}$ and $\Sigma_j$, and the energy is quadratic in the means only at fixed $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$. Asymmetry of the KL causes no difficulty for the energy, since each ordered pair is its own edge. At fixed $\beta$, $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$ the mean update is therefore sheaf diffusion, and the self-coupling to a prior is the stubborn-parent construction of §7 of [[hansen-ghrist-2021-discourse-sheaves]], with the prior precision as reluctance. The stationary means are a harmonic extension with the priors as boundary data. Row-normalized attention with receiver-only updates is not a gradient flow of the total energy, so the convergence theorems apply to joint descent and not to row-only schedules.

A gauge transformation $\mu_i\mapsto g_i\mu_i$, $\Sigma_i\mapsto g_i\Sigma_ig_i^{\top}$, $\Omega_{ij}\mapsto g_i\Omega_{ij}g_j^{-1}$ is a sheaf isomorphism. The vertex maps are $g_i$ and the edge maps are the orthogonal factors $R_e=(g_iP_{ij}g_i^{\top})^{-1/2}g_iP_{ij}^{1/2}$, using $P_{ij}\mapsto g_iP_{ij}g_i^{\top}$, which satisfy $R_eR_e^{\top}=I$. Consequently $\dim H^0$ and the energy are gauge invariant for every $g\in\mathrm{GL}(K)$, compact or not. The spectrum of the Laplacian is invariant only when the vertex inner products transform covariantly, for example the Fisher metric $\Sigma_i^{-1}$ on the means, for which the operator $\Sigma L$ that generates the natural-gradient mean flow transforms by similarity. With Euclidean vertex inner products the transformed Laplacian is congruent, not similar, to the original, so its kernel and inertia survive while its eigenvalues do not. This refines the compact-versus-noncompact warning on [[Graph synchronization and connection Laplacians]]: kernel dimensions are metric-free, and spectral gaps are statements about a chosen metric.

For invertible links on a connected graph, a global section satisfies $x_i=\Omega_{ij}x_j$ on every edge, so $H^0$ is isomorphic to the subspace of one fiber fixed by every cycle holonomy based there (the monodromy invariants of the local system). Exact consensus up to transport therefore lives only on the holonomy-invariant subspace, which for generic noncompact holonomy is zero. In that case the stationary configuration is the prior-anchored harmonic extension rather than a section. The soft-kernel caveat of [[Bounded confidence]] still applies: the attention kernel has nonzero tails, so the sharp equilibrium structure of Theorems 11.1–11.2 is at most a low-temperature or hard-support limit of the model, not a property of it.

The conservation law of Theorem 9.3 is a Noether identity. The vertex group $\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal F(v))$ acts by $x_v\mapsto gx_v$ and $\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}\mapsto\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}g^{-1}$ and leaves $\delta x$ unchanged. Under gradient flow with rates $\alpha$ for opinions and $\beta$ for maps, the moment map $\beta^{-1}\sum_{e\ni v}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}^{\top}\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}-\alpha^{-1}x_vx_v^{\top}$ is conserved, which is the balancedness invariant of deep linear networks ([[du-2018-balanced-layers]]) and an instance of the symmetry-induced conservation laws of [[kunin-2021-neural-mechanics]]. In the gauge-VFE setting the same argument says that joint flow of beliefs and links under a gauge-invariant energy conserves a moment map whose form depends on the metric of the flow, so the Euclidean invariant above is not conserved under natural-gradient updates.

Kron reduction is the sheaf form of integrating out an agent. For scaled-orthogonal bundles, eliminating vertex $v$ composes transports, giving a pairwise link $\Omega_{wv}\Omega_{vw'}$ on each pair of its neighbors (the star–mesh transform with transport), because a weighted variance of transported messages with isotropic weights decomposes into pairwise squared differences. With rank-deficient restrictions or anisotropic precisions the eliminated vertex leaves a constraint on its whole star that need not be pairwise, which is the Hansen–Ghrist obstruction. This bears on [[Coarse Graining]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], where integrating out constituents of a parent can generate couplings among more than two agents.

## Hodge Laplacians, plaquettes and holonomy

> [!note] Editorial: This section answers a user question of October 7, 2026, about whether the Hodge Laplacian is useful above one base point. It is analysis for this page and has not been checked against a primary source beyond the ones named.

On a 2-complex whose 2-cells are declared plaquettes, a sheaf built from invertible links exists only if every plaquette holonomy is trivial, because the composition law forces the two routes from a vertex into a face to agree. This is the content of "flatness is manifest in $\delta^2=0$". A curved link field is therefore a sheaf on the 1-skeleton only. If the lattice covariant exterior derivative is extended to plaquettes anyway, $D_1D_0$ is no longer zero but acts on vertex data by the plaquette curvature, the discrete form of $D^2=F$. The operator $D_0D_0^{\ast}+D_1^{\ast}D_1$ remains positive semidefinite and gauge covariant, but its kernel is no longer a cohomology, and the cross term $\langle D_0x,D_1^{\ast}y\rangle=\langle D_1D_0x,y\rangle$ measures the failure of the Hodge decomposition.

Hodge theory applies exactly in three places. The first is scalar, gauge-invariant edge data on the constant sheaf. The antisymmetric part of the log attention weights, $\log\beta_{ij}-\log\beta_{ji}$, splits on the clique complex into a gradient part (a per-agent potential, that is, a rank or status), a curl part (triangle-local cyclic preference), and a harmonic part (cyclic flow around unfilled cycles). Because the row normalizers enter only as $\log Z_j-\log Z_i$, a pure gradient, the curl and harmonic parts depend only on the asymmetry of the transported divergences. This is the HodgeRank construction (Jiang, Lim, Yao and Ye, 2011, Math. Program. 127:203–244; not in the vault), and it gives a cheap test of whether directed attention organizes into a hierarchy or into cycles. The second is the linearization of link fluctuations about a flat background $\rho$. There the adjoint-twisted complex is a genuine complex, $H^0(X;\mathrm{ad}\rho)$ is the Lie algebra of the unbroken gauge stabilizer, $H^1(X;\mathrm{ad}\rho)$ is the tangent space to flat links modulo gauge, and $H^2$ carries obstructions (the Weil–Goldman deformation complex; not in the vault). The plaquette term of the linearized link operator recorded on [[Lattice gauge theory]], $\sum_pc_p\mathbf C^{\top}\mathbf C$, is the up Laplacian $\delta_1^{\ast}\delta_1$ on adjoint-valued 1-cochains, and adding the gauge-fixing term $\delta_0\delta_0^{\ast}$ completes the Hodge 1-Laplacian. The decomposition of a link perturbation into $\operatorname{im}\delta_0$, which is pure gauge and invisible to every plaquette energy, $\operatorname{im}\delta_1^{\ast}$, which carries curvature, and harmonic modes, which are flat but carry holonomy around cycles bounding no declared plaquette, separates training motion along gauge orbits from motion that changes gauge-invariant content. The third is abelian or log-linearized curvature, where the plaquette field is $\delta_1$ of the link logarithms and the Bianchi identity is $\delta_2F=0$ on 3-cells; nonabelian curvature obeys only a covariant Bianchi identity.

The harmonic sector depends on which cycles are filled. On a complete graph whose triangles are all declared plaquettes the complex has trivial first cohomology, so every flat link field is pure gauge. On a ring or a sparse local window without plaquettes, the holonomy around the unfilled cycle is a gauge-invariant modulus that no plaquette energy penalizes. The choice of 2-cells is a modeling decision that must be stated, as [[Lattice gauge theory]] already requires. For $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ the adjoint action is not isometric for any positive-definite inner product on $\mathfrak{gl}(K)$, so cohomology dimensions are well defined while harmonic representatives and spectral gaps depend on the chosen fiber metric. Any small-graph computation of these objects grounds the construction and does not set the large-$N$ behavior.

## Sheaf coboundary and the base covariant derivative

> [!note] Editorial: Answer to the companion user question of October 7, 2026.

For an invertible-link sheaf with the edge stalk identified with the receiver's fiber, $(\delta x)_{i\leftarrow j}=\Omega_{ij}x_j-x_i$ is the lattice covariant difference $U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)-\psi(x)$, so the coboundary is a covariant derivative along the agent graph, valued in edge stalks rather than paired with a tangent vector. A general sheaf is weaker: it compares two agents in a third space through possibly singular maps, which is the comparison-cospan structure of [[GL(K) gauge-equivariant attention]].

For invertible links the sheaf formalism adds no structure beyond the bundle formalism. An invertible-restriction sheaf on a graph is a discrete vector bundle with connection, that is, a local system ([[hansen-2018-toward-a-spectral-theory-of-cellular-sheaves]], §3.5); its coboundary is the lattice covariant difference the links already define, its global sections are the covariantly constant configurations, and its relative-cohomology criteria are twisted cohomology of the local system. The holonomy principle behind the statement that $H^0$ is the holonomy-fixed subspace is already recorded as an exact analogy in the MultiAgentELBO companion (`manuscripts/MultiAgentELBO_Companion/m02_04_foundations.tex`, analogy following `prop:geo-three-agreements`). In the other direction the bundle formalism is the larger one, because it carries plaquette curvature that a sheaf on a 2-complex cannot. The sheaf language contributes new content only for singular restriction maps or edge stalks that differ from the vertex fibers: comparison cospans, low-rank heads and communication subspaces, where $H^0$ is no longer the holonomy-fixed subspace, agent elimination can produce constraints that are not pairwise, and expressed agreement separates from private agreement.

When $\dim\mathcal C>0$ this graph direction is separate from the base direction, and the two combine into one connection on the product of the base with the agent complex. The total derivative is $d_{\nabla}+\delta$, with $\nabla_a\mu_i=\partial_a\mu_i+A^{(i)}_a\mu_i$ along the base and $\delta$ along the edges. Its curvature has a base–base component $F^{(i)}_{ab}$, a graph–graph component (the plaquette holonomy), and a mixed component, the covariant derivative of the link as a bifundamental field,
$$\nabla_a\Omega_{ij}=\partial_a\Omega_{ij}+A^{(i)}_a\Omega_{ij}-\Omega_{ij}A^{(j)}_a,$$
which is the holonomy of an infinitesimal mixed plaquette (along the edge at $c$, then along the base, then back). Frame-derived data $\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$ with frame-derived base connections make all three components vanish, under matching sign conventions. Independently supplied links have kinetic energy $\lVert\nabla\Omega\rVert^2$ along the base, the structure of the condensed moose of [[arkani-hamed-2001-deconstructing-dimensions]], whose low-energy action (their Eq. 2.5) is $f_s^2\sum_j\operatorname{tr}\lvert D_\mu U_{j,j+1}\rvert^2$ with $D_\mu U_{j,j+1}=\partial_\mu U_{j,j+1}-iA^j_\mu U_{j,j+1}+iU_{j,j+1}A^{j+1}_\mu$, read there as a gauge theory latticized along the quiver direction only. Expanded about constant links, this term is a sheaf Laplacian of the agent graph acting on the base connections (the ring Laplacian of their Eq. 2.8 in the ring case), so it pulls the agents' base connections into agreement up to transport. The paper's continuum extra dimension requires locality of the quiver and large $N$, and its links are unitary condensates of a compact group; it does not require translation invariance and allows quivers without a manifold interpretation (§3, §7). A complete attention graph lacks the locality, so only the form of the kinetic term and the Laplacian mass matrix transfer to it. Above each $c$ the global sections $H^0(G;\mathcal F_c)$ form the consensus space; where their dimension is constant they form a vector bundle over $\mathcal C$, and jumps in dimension mark base points where the transported consensus changes.

## What does not transfer

Both papers work with vector stalks, Euclidean inner products, fixed linear restriction maps, undirected edges and deterministic states. The covariance part of the Gaussian KL, $\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\Omega\Sigma_j\Omega^{\top})^{-1}\Sigma_i\bigr)+\log\det(\Omega\Sigma_j\Omega^{\top})-\log\det\Sigma_i$, is not a function of a linear edge discrepancy, so it lies outside the nonlinear Laplacians of §10, which require the form $\delta^{\top}\nabla U(\delta x)$. A sheaf valued in the SPD manifold or in the Gaussian family is needed, and neither paper supplies one. The receiver-normalized softmax couples all edges of a receiver's star through $-\tau\log\sum_j e^{-E_{ij}/\tau}$ rather than edge by edge. "Learning to lie" by a sign flip passes through a singular restriction map, which an exponential chart onto $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ cannot reach. For even $K$ the map $-I$ nevertheless lies in $\mathrm{SO}(K)\subset\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$, so continuous rotation can produce inversion of the expressed opinion (editorial). Directedness is named by Hansen–Ghrist as open (§9.5).

## Status in the MultiAgentELBO theory

> [!note] Editorial: Assessment of October 7, 2026, made by reading the live theory folder `manuscripts/MultiAgentELBO_Theory/`. Label names are the theory's; the corollary below was added to the theory on the same day.

Most of the sheaf machinery is already in the theory under its own names. The connection Laplacian of a block, `def:cg-connection-laplacian` in `c10_network_state.tex`, is the degree-0 Laplacian of the sheaf with restriction maps $W_e^{1/2}$ and $W_e^{1/2}\Theta_e$, and `prop:cg-kernel-holonomy` identifies its kernel with $H^0$ and, for positive-definite weights, with the holonomy-fixed subspace at a root, citing the discrete Hodge theorem of [[hansen-2018-toward-a-spectral-theory-of-cellular-sheaves]]. The comparison cospan of `def:obs-selective-comparison` (`c06_attention.tex`), with a comparison space $H_e^{\mathrm{cmp}}$ and query and key maps $S^Q_e,S^K_e$, is a discourse sheaf whose edge stalk is the comparison space, and `c15_related_work.tex` already cites [[hansen-ghrist-2021-discourse-sheaves]] for it. The expression-learning flow $-(\delta x)x^{\top}$ of that paper is the link current of `cor:link-delta-rule`, a precision-weighted prediction error times the source state, here taken in the Lie algebra and balanced by a generator prior. Stubborn agents correspond to the prior and self terms of the form $\Lambda$ in `prop:cg-fixed-section-compression`, whose properness criterion $\operatorname{range}\iota\cap\ker\Lambda=\{0\}$ plays the role of the relative-$H^0$ controllability condition. The failure of Kron reduction is cited in `c15` as prior art for the obstructions classified in Supplement G. The theory extends the discourse model in the directions Hansen and Ghrist list as open: probabilistic states, directed (one-way) attention, and noncompact $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ links with covariance blocks. Whether later literature has already done this has not been surveyed. Signed or antagonistic edges have no counterpart in the theory, and the Noether conservation law of Theorem 9.3 is broken by the anchored priors, so at most a balance law with a prior torque survives; that has not been checked.

The Hodge 1-Laplacian enters through the link prior of `c08b_lie_links.tex`. Its loop action $\mathscr S=\sum_{p\in\mathbb L}\mathrm c_p\mathfrak Q_p$ runs over a declared family $\mathbb L$ of closed walks, which makes $\mathbb L$ the set of 2-cells of a declared complex, and the loop term $\sum_p\mathrm c_p\mathbf C_p^{\top}\mathbf C_p$ of the linearized operator $\mathbf K$ is the up-Laplacian $\delta_1^{\ast}\delta_1$ on $\mathfrak g$-valued 1-cochains. `cor:link-cycle-splitting` (Corollary 9.35 in the October 7 build) makes this exact. The kernel of the cycle map of the positively weighted loops splits orthogonally into exact generators $\omega_e=a_i-a_j$, of dimension $\dim\mathfrak g(|V|-\kappa)$, and harmonic generators, of dimension $\dim\mathfrak g(|E_+|-|V|+\kappa-r_{\mathbb L})\ge\dim\mathfrak g(|E_+|-|V|+\kappa-|\mathbb L_+|)$. Under the quadratic loop action the anchor M-step correction lies in $\mathring\Sigma\operatorname{im}\mathbf C_+^{\top}$, which is $\mathring\Sigma^{-1}$-orthogonal to both, so the anchor update corrects only the circulating part. On the kernel, $\mathbf K$ reduces to anchor, episode and matter terms. Because generators are measured in a reference frame, exact generators are physical link changes $\Theta_e\mapsto\Theta_e+a_i-a_j$ with the frames held (translation instance), and they are not gauge redundancy. At large $N$ this makes $\mathbb L$ a structural declaration comparable to the support of the source prior: a family of triangles on a sparse, low-clustering graph leaves most of the cycle space unpenalized. Beyond second order, nonabelian loop defects are not functions of $\mathbf C\omega$.

For the HodgeRank diagnostic above, one-way attention ($\pi_{ij}>0$, $\pi_{ji}=0$) makes $\log\beta_{ij}-\log\beta_{ji}$ undefined. The edge flow $\beta_{ij}-\beta_{ji}$, or restriction to reciprocal pairs, is needed.

Three spectral directions remain incompletely connected to current formation studies; the supplied Gaussian graded-retention comparison above now executes part of the first. The first is graded retention. The fixed rank $f_I=\dim\ker L_I$ is sharp, while the user's working picture of formation is soft; the spectral subspace of $L_I$ below a threshold would grade retained degrees, and the needed estimate is the structural Cheeger inequality that Hansen and Ghrist pose but do not prove (small $\lambda_1(L_I)$ if and only if the links are near a configuration with a fixed vector). The Bandeira–Singer–Spielman bound covers $O(K)$ only, and for $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ the statement has to be made in a covariant metric such as the Fisher metric, since only kernel and inertia are metric-free. The second is covering and pullback isospectrality (Propositions 5.10–5.12), which gives a holonomy-twisted Bloch reduction for translation-invariant links on periodic lattices, an exact spectral tool at 512 or more agents. The third is interlacing under cell deletion (Theorem 5.7), which bounds how much the retained spectrum moves when a hard attention mask removes edges.
