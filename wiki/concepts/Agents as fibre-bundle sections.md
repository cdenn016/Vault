# Agents as fiber-bundle sections

## Definition

In the [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] an *agent* is not a point or a Gaussian parameter
tuple but an ordered pair of local sections of associated law bundles over a base manifold
$\mathcal{C}$. The construction stacks three pieces:

1. a **base manifold** $\mathcal{C}$, a smooth $n$-dimensional "parameter / index space of contexts" — explicitly *not* spacetime, but a noumenal domain of inquiry (Dennis 2026, Def. Base Manifold);
2. a **principal $G$-bundle** $\pi : \mathcal{N} \to \mathcal{C}$ carrying the gauge freedom, with
   $G\leq\mathrm{GL}(K,\mathbb{R})$; the current theory starts with compact $G$, while full $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ is a later extension;
3. two **associated bundles** $\mathcal{E}_b$ and $\mathcal{E}_m$ whose fibers are declared spaces
   of normalized probability laws over belief and model sample spaces. Smooth statistical manifolds,
   Fisher geometry, exponential families, and Gaussians are successively stronger regularity or
   computational choices.

A hierarchy-specific presentation expands this pair into two primitive sections, two reference
sections, and a gauge-frame field:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &: \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{state}}|_{\mathcal{U}_i} && \text{(belief, primitive)}\\
s_i &: \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}|_{\mathcal{U}_i} && \text{(generative model, primitive)}\\
p_i &= \Omega_{i,I}\big[q_I^{(s+1)}\big] \in \Gamma\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{state}}|_{\mathcal{U}_i}\right) && \text{(prior, derived)}\\
r_i &= \tilde\Omega_{i,I}\big[s_I^{(s+1)}\big] \in \Gamma\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}|_{\mathcal{U}_i}\right) && \text{(hyper-prior, derived)}\\
\phi_i &: \mathcal{U}_i \to \mathfrak{g} && \text{(gauge frame field)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The current primitive state-bearing content is more compactly
$z_i=(q_i,s_i)\in\Gamma(\mathcal E_b\times_{\mathcal C}\mathcal E_m|_{\mathcal U_i})$.
Reference sections $p_i,r_i$ are derived by cross-scale [[Parallel transport]] only where the
hierarchy and its compatibility hypotheses close; otherwise they are declared boundary data. Gauge
frames, connections, interaction incidence, normalized record kernels, and the variational rule are
relational or nomological structure used to compare and couple the section-valued agents.

## Why it matters here

This is the foundational ontological commitment of the model: it is what makes the framework "multi-agent" and "gauge-theoretic" at once. Several distinctive features of the [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] follow directly from agents being *sections* rather than vectors:

- **Spatial extension and overlap.** Because $q_i$ is a field $c \mapsto q_i(c)$ over $\mathcal{U}_i$, multiple agents can hold *different* beliefs about the *same* region of $\mathcal{C}$ wherever their supports overlap ($\mathcal{U}_i \cap \mathcal{U}_j$). Intersubjective structure — the model's formal correlate of objectivity — is generated by this multiplicity; a single agent cannot produce it.
- **Comparison requires transport, not subtraction.** Two sections live in different gauge frames; comparing them is only meaningful after gauge-covariant transport $\Omega_{ij} = U_i U_j^{-1}$ — taking the frame to be $U_i = \exp(\phi_i)$, this is exactly the $\exp(\phi_i)\exp(-\phi_j)$ form (equivalently $g_i g_j^{-1}$) used in [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] and [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]] (see [[Gauge transformation]], [[Holonomy]]). This is why the consensus/alignment energy is the *transported* KL $\mathrm{KL}(q_i \,\|\, \Omega_{ij}[q_j])$ rather than a naive distance.
- **The variational hierarchy is geometric where its closure hypotheses hold.** The four statistical sections are organized as
$$
r_i \to s_i \to p_i \to q_i \to \text{observations},
$$
The prior and hyper-prior arrows are realized cross-scale only under the declared hierarchy and
compatibility hypotheses; otherwise those references are boundary data. This keeps hierarchical
[[Variational free energy]] geometric without attributing the derivation to every finite realization.
- **The zero-dimensional reduction.** When the base collapses to a point, each section becomes one distribution per agent. This alone does not recover arbitrary standard attention: strict shared-frame Regime I gives identity transport, and a general QK bilinear map is an additional structural choice. ## Details

**Law fibers and the Gaussian realization.** The ambient fibers are normalized-law spaces. On a
regular smooth tier they may carry a Fisher-Rao metric (see [[Fisher information metric]]). A useful
finite-dimensional realization chooses the Gaussian families

$$
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{state}} = \{\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma): \mu\in\mathbb{R}^K,\ \Sigma\succ 0\},
\qquad
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{model}} = \{\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma): \mu\in\mathbb{R}^M,\ \Sigma\succ 0\}.
$$

For Gaussian agents the sections specialize componentwise to $q_i(c) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_i(c),\Sigma_i(c))$, etc., with $\Sigma \in \mathbb{S}^K_{++}$, and the frame expands in a Lie-algebra basis $\phi_i(c) = \phi_i^a(c)\,G_a$. This is a tractable coordinate backend, not the general definition of an agent.

**Associated-bundle gluing.** Gauge structure is attached to probability by the associated-bundle equivalence $(n\cdot g,\, b) \sim (n,\, \rho(g)b)$, where $\rho: G \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{B})$ is a representation. For Gaussians the representation acts by linear transport of the mean and **congruence** of the covariance:

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{state}}(g)\cdot \mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma) = \mathcal{N}(g\mu,\ g\Sigma g^{\top}).
$$

This is exactly the transport law implemented as $\mu' = \Omega_{ij}\mu_j$, $\Sigma' = \Omega_{ij}\Sigma_j\Omega_{ij}^{\top}$. Changing the gauge frame thus transforms an agent's belief *consistently*, which is what makes "the same belief in a different frame" a well-defined notion (cf. [[Group equivariance]]).

**Local trivializations.** A general principal bundle has no global section; the framework therefore works in local trivializing patches $\{\mathcal{U}_i\}$, each $\phi_i$ a local section over agent $i$'s domain. On overlaps the transports $\Omega_{ij}$ are transition functions satisfying the Čech cocycle condition $\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{jk} = \Omega_{ik}$. Because each $U_i$ is defined on its whole patch, this Čech data is a coboundary, so the reconstructed bundle is canonically trivializable — the bundle language is organizational, and nontrivial bundle topology is out of scope in the present construction.

**Two roles of the frame.** The frame field $\phi_i$ does double duty. In its *transport role* (Role A) it is gauge-redundant: dynamics depend only on relative transports $\Omega_{ij}$, invariant under the global right-translation $U_i \mapsto U_i g$. In its *state role* (Role B) the per-agent frame is treated as an internal cognitive state contributing to ontology (distinct pulled-back geometries, distinct phenomenal character). Role-B quantities are invariant only under the constant-per-agent residual subgroup, not the full local gauge. The gauge-redundancy of Role A places agents-as-sections within [[Structural realism]] — what is real is the relational/transport structure, not the frame-dependent representatives — and the per-agent frames function as internal [[Quantum reference frames]] against which only relational quantities are physical.

> [!note] Editorial: The manuscript explicitly flags that the partition/single-tree hypotheses behind the derived sections $p_i,r_i$ are idealizations the reported emergence simulation does not satisfy; in the single-scale runs $p_i,r_i$ are primitive boundary conditions and the slow subsystem $(s_i,r_i)$ is held fixed ($\gamma_{ij}=0$).

### Vertical law change, horizontal transport, and histories of sections

At a fixed context $c\in\mathcal C$, a law is one point of the fiber $(\mathcal E_x)_c$. A change
from $A$ to $B$ is a curve $\Gamma:J\to(\mathcal E_x)_c$, not merely the ordered pair of endpoints.
It is intrinsically vertical because $T\varpi_x(\dot\Gamma)=0$; no connection is needed for that
statement. A connection is needed only when a total-space curve projects to a nonconstant base curve
and one wants the split

$$
\dot\Gamma=H^\omega_\Gamma\dot\gamma+\operatorname{ver}^{\omega}\dot\Gamma.
$$

A whole-agent update is instead a curve $r\mapsto s_r$ in the space of sections. For
$\Sigma(r,c)=s_r(c)$, the identity $\varpi_x\Sigma(r,c)=c$ makes
$\partial_r\Sigma(r,c)$ vertical at every fixed $c$. Thus the contextual base can remain fixed and
timeless while belief and model sections change. The curve parameter is disposable bookkeeping;
orientation and an agent-relative information duration require the additional VFE/Fisher construction
recorded in [[gauge-vfe-rg-pullback-geometry-2026-08-01]].

Given a connection, the precise base-facing derivative of a section is its covariant vertical first
jet $D^\omega s=\operatorname{ver}^\omega\circ Ts$. Pulling the vertical [[Fisher information metric]]
back through this jet gives the connection-relative perceived tensor
$h_s^\omega=(D^\omega s)^*g^F$. It is passive-gauge invariant under the stated equivariance
hypotheses but generally only positive semidefinite, with
$\operatorname{rad}h_s^\omega=\ker D^\omega s$. Passive gauge invariance therefore does not imply
independence from the selected connection.

### The nearest external construction (2026-08-12)

The features-as-sections move is not unique to this program, and it is worth knowing exactly how far
the external version goes. [[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]] models node features on a graph as
sections of an **associated vector bundle** precisely so that architectures are equivariant under
*local* actions of a symmetry group $G$ — $G$ acting on the feature at each node by an individual
$g_v$ — and argues, as this page does, that sections preserve local symmetry *by construction*
rather than by penalty. [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]] then carries that to a fully finite
formalism: a section assigns to each node a class $\phi_i=[\xi_i,x_i]$ pairing a frame
$\xi_i\in\mathrm{SO}(d)$ with a feature $x_i$, which is the same equivalence-class device used above
for $(n\cdot g, b)\sim(n,\rho(g)b)$.

The differences are structural rather than cosmetic. Their fiber is a **vector space** carrying a
linear representation; here the fiber is a [[Statistical manifold|statistical manifold]] of Gaussian
laws, with congruence acting on covariance. Their objective is a diffusion semigroup or a discrete
Yang–Mills energy; here it is a [[Multi-agent variational free energy|population variational free
energy]]. And nothing on their side corresponds to overlapping agent supports, derived priors from a
meta-agent one scale up, or the frame's Role-B reading as internal state.
