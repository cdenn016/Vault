# Belief perseverance and confirmation bias

**Belief perseverance** is persistence after the original evidence has been withdrawn or discredited. **Confirmation bias** is a family of biases in search, attention, evaluation, memory, and interpretation ([[nickerson-1998-confirmation-bias]]).

## Mechanisms in the literature

The causal-explanation mechanism documented by [[anderson1980-belief-perseverance]] explains perseverance after evidence discrediting: a state that stores the explanation survives the removal of the original evidence. Active selection of evidence is a separate route to confirmation bias. In [[albarracin-2022-epistemic-communities]] each agent chooses which neighbor to observe, and active confirmation-biased sampling lets epistemic communities segregate.

[[nevin-mandell-atak-1983-behavioral-momentum]] provides an empirical resistance-to-disruption construct in conditioned behavior. It motivates perturbation designs but is not evidence that opinions obey Hamiltonian mechanics.

Persistence and oscillation also arise in second-order opinion models: [[martins-2015-opinion-particles]] provides inertial opinion particles, [[baumann-sokolov-tyloo-2020-second-order-consensus]] provides network resonance, and [[sampson-porter-restrepo-2025-oscillatory-opinion]] provides oscillatory group-feedback dynamics.
