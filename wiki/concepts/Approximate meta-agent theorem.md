# Approximate meta-agent theorem

## Definition

A meta-agent is a set of agents that is itself an agent: it has coarse internal, sensory, active and external states, its external states obey a law of their own given its act, and its internal states encode a belief about them (see [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]). In the linear-Gaussian framework of the collective-agents manuscript this holds exactly under hypotheses that include exact closure of the coarse exterior and of the coarse internal state and exact blanket conditions. The approximate meta-agent theorem is the family of statements in [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]] and [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-blanket-conditions]] that replace exact closure, and in the second note also the exact blanket conditions, by declared coarse models with measured defects and bound the resulting errors.

The declared model asserts that a retained variable $y=Cx$ of a fine process $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t+\mathfrak n_t$ obeys $y_{t+1}=\bar Ay_t+\bar Bu_t+\hat w_t$. Its closure defect is $D=CA-\bar AC$ and its input defect is $E=CB-\bar B$. When the meta-agent reads a summary $s=\mathsf Tz$ of a finer record $z_{t+1}=H_{\mathrm x}x_t+v_t$ through a declared observation model $s_{t+1}=\bar Hy_t+\hat v_t$, that model has an observation defect $\mathsf G=\mathsf TH_{\mathrm x}-\bar HC$ as well. The quantity bounded for the belief is the excess relative entropy, the expected relative entropy from an exact predictive law of $y_t$, the reference, to the belief of the stationary [[Kalman filter]] of the declared model.

## Why it matters here

[[Coarse Graining]] generically produces memory, so a summary of many agents is closed only in special cases, and a theory in which collectives are agents needs to say what a collective that models a non-closed summary with a Markov law loses. Before these results the manuscript had separate bounds for the blanket, for the memory of the exterior and for agency, and an error term for the belief that held only under a smallness condition. The first set of results gives an error term without that condition, assembles it with the exact parts into one theorem, and treats the case in which the internal summary, not the external one, fails to close.

A second set removes restrictions of the first. It lets the declared observation model be wrong and the record be a summary of what the members receive, drops the exact blanket conditions from the assembled theorem, gives literal members a statement about their belief in addition to the bound on the memory of their average, and bounds the departure of a retained coordinate from a first-order autoregression without reversibility.

## Details

### The filter bound

The difference between the exact and the declared predictive covariances obeys an exact quadratic matrix equation, and the declared filter contracts in the metric of its own predictive covariance. These two facts bound the upper limit of the excess relative entropy by a covariance term and a mean term, the latter proportional to the square of the forgetting time of the declared filter and to the energy of the part of the defect that the record resolves. The bound is finite for every declared model with a detectable filter and is of second order in the defect and the noise mismatch. In the upper limit it holds from every Gaussian start and for a fine process that is detectable and not necessarily stable; at a finite round only a bound that depends on the start is available ([[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]]).

### The assembled theorem

For a stable linear-Gaussian collective whose members run a consensus filter on information contributions ([[olfati-saber-2005-distributed-kalman-filter]] is the classical antecedent), with exact blanket conditions, the five properties of the exact theorem hold in the following form. The coarse [[Markov Blanket]] and the closure of the coarse internal state remain exact. The path law of the coarse exterior, the belief encoded by the members' average and the law of the joint act carry error terms in the defects of the declared model and in the members' computation noise, with no smallness condition. The exterior need not be stable on its own: stability of the joint process and the blanket hypothesis together make the exterior detectable through the coarse sensory state.

### A record that the declared model misreads

In the filter bound the record reads the retained variable alone and the declared observation model is exactly right. [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-blanket-conditions]] treats a finer record that reads the whole fine state, acts that may depend on that finer record, and a meta-agent that reads a linear summary of it through a declared observation model. The reference stays the exact predictive law of the retained variable given everything that is recorded. The contraction of the declared filter is unchanged, since it is a property of the declared matrices alone. The error of the mean is driven by an innovation term, whose stationary energy the covariance equation gives exactly, and by the parts of the closure defect and of the observation defect that the finer record resolves; the unresolved part of the observation defect enters as noise of the summary that is correlated with the prediction error. The bound on the upper limit of the excess has the form of the filter bound, a covariance part plus half the square of the forgetting time times the defect drive plus the root of the innovation energy. It is attained when the defect drive vanishes, and, on families on which a stated list of norms stays bounded and the largest of the following quantities is at most one, it is of second order jointly in six quantities: the energies of the two defects, two noise mismatches, the covariance of the unresolved observation defect with the prediction error, and the gain residual of the summary.

The gain residual measures the insufficiency of the summary (see [[Sufficient statistics]]), the part of the innovation covariance that no gain on the summary can remove. The excess splits exactly into the excess relative to the law given a coarse record plus the conditional mutual information between the retained variable and the finer record given the coarse record, and every belief computed from the coarse record pays at least that information. When the initial mean of the fine state is deterministic, the initial mean of the belief is a function of the randomization and the acts read the summary alone, the information term equals $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T,t})$, with $P_{\mathrm e,t}$ and $P_{\mathsf T,t}$ the predictive covariances of the exact filters of the finer record and of the summary, and no declared model changes it, so no bound that vanishes with the model defects can hold for a summarized record. When the acts read the finer record they carry part of what the summary withholds, and for a deterministic initial mean and a coarse record that contains the summary and the acts the term is at most that value, and the law given the coarse record is a mixture that need not be Gaussian. A companion proposition covers a recursion with any fixed gain, any claimed covariance in whose metric the closed-loop matrix contracts, and an additive perturbation of arbitrary dependence; this is the form in which the argument reaches averages that are not the stationary filter of any model.

### Approximate blanket conditions

The assembled theorem takes the coarse sensory state as the record, which is legitimate only when that state is the information-form statistic of the members' sensors and the exterior reads the members through the coarse act. The second theorem of [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-blanket-conditions]] drops that hypothesis. It keeps the canonical partition, in which no internal and no active variable reads the exterior and the exterior reads no internal variable, per-channel noise, a stable joint process, the consensus protocol and the Gaussian start. Five kinds of violation are then allowed at any size: sensors that read exterior coordinates outside the coarse exterior, a coarse sensory state formed with other weights, sensors with memory, sensors that read members' acts or internal states, and an exterior that reads the members one by one or reads their sensory channels. The device is a change of record. The members' sensory channels, net of what they read inside the collective, are a finer record in the sense of the preceding subsection, the coarse sensory state is a summary of it plus a term that the collective knows, and the exterior is driven by a fine act that the collective knows.

The five properties then read as follows. The exact coarse [[Markov Blanket]] is lost: the pair of coarse sensory state and coarse act need not be a coarse path blanket, and no bound on its leak is asserted. In its place stands a filtering statement, that the law of the exterior given the paths of all collective variables is the Gaussian law computed by an exterior filter from the members' channels, with a deterministic covariance that no member's computation or act changes. The closure of the coarse internal state stays exact, and deliberation still follows exactly the group posterior of the declared model. The path law of the coarse exterior, the belief and the joint act carry error terms in the closure defect, which now contains what the exterior reads of the members beyond the coarse act, in the observation defect, which contains hidden exterior coordinates and the memory and reafference of the sensors, and in the insufficiency of the coarse sensory state. The error terms for the path law and the belief are of second order in the violations. The limit of the excess of the belief exists, does not depend on the start, and is fixed by the stationary covariance of the joint process and the exact predictive covariance.

The reference law changes with the record. The law of the coarse exterior given the members' channels is what the collective as a whole could know; the law given a coarse record is what an observer of the coarse states could know. For a coarse record that determines the mean of the belief, the two excesses differ by an information term that is bounded uniformly in time, that is at most the leak of the coarse blanket on the window when the record contains the coarse blanket path, and that vanishes when that leak vanishes. When only the weights are wrong and every model is declared truthfully, the excess relative to the members' channels tends to an insufficiency term $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}=\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e}^{-1}P^-_{\mathrm b})$. That term is a floor over declared input matrices when the collective reads its sensors through the coarse sensory state alone. It is not a floor when the active channels read more of the members' channels and the covariance of the coarse act with the error of the belief is not annihilated by the closed-loop matrix of the declared filter: declared input matrices arbitrarily close to the true one then give a smaller limit, because the common update reads the act as a further observation. Sensors that sense only a part of the coarse exterior, which the assembled theorem excludes, are covered through a reduced statistic when the declared drift is detectable through the singular information matrix.

### Members that fuse literally

When members fuse messages literally with fixed gains, their average is a fixed linear filter and its recursion is not closed. Its departure from closure is controlled by three structural quantities of the update of the members' means, the dispersions of its row sums and of its column sums and a mixing constant on the members' deviations, together with two second moments of the process. The memory of the average on its own past needs both dispersions; the memory through the inputs needs only the column dispersion. The closure defect in its information form has a floor that mixing does not lower, which separates it from the energy of the defect as a measure of memory ([[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]]).

> [!note] Editorial: Reading of the floor
> The floor arises because the deviations injected by one round of internal noise reach the average in the next round before any mixing, at a size comparable with the fresh noise of the average. It is a property of the one-step information measure, in line with the observation of the manuscript that information-form defects misstate the memory of aggregates. This reading is the wiki's summary of the proof and is not a separate claim of the source.

### The belief of members that fuse literally

For scalar literal members with exact code models and channel noise specific to the receiver, [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-blanket-conditions]] gives the counterpart of the belief property. The exterior of such members is the world state together with the agents outside the collective, each of which hears individual members, and the record is what the members receive from outside. Given that record and the members' own randomization, the exterior has an exact Gaussian conditional law computed by a filter whose covariance converges from every start, also for a world state that is not stable, provided the update of the network is. The excess relative entropy of the belief $\mathcal N(\bar m(t),P_{\mathrm c})$ carried by the average, for any claimed variance $P_{\mathrm c}$, has an exact value: half the logarithm of the inefficiency of the average as an estimator, the ratio of its mean square error to the exact predictive variance, plus the price of a claimed variance that differs from that mean square error. The error of the mean obeys a recursion with four drivers: the mismatch between the members' pooled fixed gains and the exact gain, the outside senders' errors that the coarse sensory state reads as if they were the world state, the memory defect of the average, and the noise of the messages inside the collective. When the mean row sum $\bar\lambda$ of the update satisfies $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert<1$, this yields a bound on the limit of the excess that is of second order jointly in the drivers and in $P_{\mathrm e}/P_{\mathrm c}-1$, on families on which $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert$ stays away from one and $P_{\mathrm c}$ from zero, and that is attained when no agent is outside the collective and the column sums are equal. Under $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert<1$, mixing of the deviations and a small-gain condition on the product of the two dispersions a second bound holds which, when no agent is outside the collective, is a function of the parameters of the collective alone. Of the two claimed variances that the network itself defines, the predictive variance of the closed recursion read as a filter and the members' own claims under literal fusion, neither is calibrated in general.

### The Markov error of one coordinate

The Markov error of a coordinate $y=\phi^\top x$ of a stable recursion $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+\mathfrak n_t$ is the largest distance over lags between its autocorrelation and the geometric sequence $\mu_1^u$ of the slowest mode. [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-blanket-conditions]] bounds it without reversibility, under a power bound $\Vert A_{\perp\perp}^l\Vert\le M_\perp\alpha_\perp^l$ on the block of the deviations and a gap $\mathsf{gap}_\perp$ between the retained coefficient $\bar\lambda=\phi^\top A\phi$ and $\alpha_\perp$ with $4M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}<\mathsf{gap}_\perp^2$ for the dispersions defined below, and a slow eigenvalue $\mu_1<1$. In suitable coordinates the recursion is a cascade: the deviations from the slow mode evolve on their own and drive a first-order autoregression of the coordinate through the column dispersion $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$. Three factors multiply in the bound: the column dispersion; the covariance of the deviations with the coordinate, which for isotropic noise has a part from the tilt of the slow mode, of size $\mathsf s_{\mathrm{row}}=M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}/(\mu_1-\alpha_\perp)$ for the row dispersion $\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}$, and a part from the persistence of the deviations; and a ratio of the two timescales. For isotropic noise and small dispersions the a priori form is $\frac43M_\perp^2\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{row}})/\mathsf{gap}_\perp^2$ times $\mu_1(1-\mu_1^2)/(1-\mu_1\hat\alpha_\perp)$, with $\hat\alpha_\perp$ a bound on the other eigenvalues, and the isotropic bound is attained to leading order on a family of two coordinates. For equal column sums the coordinate is an exact first-order autoregression for every noise.

Two propositions fix what cannot be improved. For anisotropic noise the error is of first order in the column dispersion on an explicit family, because the noise of the deviations is then correlated with the noise of the coordinate and the column dispersion feeds it back one round later. The powers of $M_\perp$, the first in the terms of first order in $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$ and the second in the term of second order, are attained on a family whose deviation block is a multiple of an oblique projection; the amplification is transient growth of the deviations and not slow relaxation, a difference from the continuous-time statement of the manuscript, whose hypothesis makes the semigroup of the deviations contract. For literal members with the exterior held fixed, a corollary bounds the Markov error of the error of their average; since the local noise variance of such a member depends on its gains, that error is in general of first order in the column dispersion.

### Relation to closure notions

Exact closure of a coarse variable of a linear system is invariance of the kernel of the coarse map, the linear-Gaussian case of lumpability ([[kemeny-snell-1960-finite-markov-chains]]) and of aggregation of nearly decomposable systems ([[simon-1961-aggregation-variables-dynamic-systems]]). Its approximate forms are ordered measures of memory, of which the one-step closure defect is the largest. The projection identities behind the memory terms are those of [[zwanzig-1961-memory-effects]] and [[mori-1965-transport-collective-motion]], and the dynamical-independence and informational-closure notions of [[barnett-2023-dynamical-independence-discovering]] and [[rosas-2024-software-natural-world]] coincide with exact closure in the stationary linear-Gaussian case without input.

## Exact closure and sufficient coarse records from symmetry, November 14, 2026

[[rosas-2026-symmetry-hierarchical-emergence]] bears on two points of this page from the exact side (version read: the arXiv preprint arXiv:2512.00984v1). On closure, it shows for discrete-state Markov chains that equivariance of the transition kernel under a finite group makes the orbit variable informationally closed in the sense of [[bertschinger-2006-information-closure-systems]] and [[rosas-2024-software-natural-world]], with one closed level per subgroup, and it names lumpability as the necessary condition. This adds a structural sufficient condition to the notions listed under "Relation to closure notions"; the paper says nothing about approximate closure or about defects. On summarized records, its Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 give a case in which a coarse record supports an exact filter: when the latent dynamics, the measurement channel and the initial law are equivariant, the orbit variables $(Z_t,V_t)$ of state and measurement form a hidden Markov model, and the belief $p(z_t\mid v_{0:t})$ is updated from the coarse record alone with no model defect. Its Corollary 4 states when nothing is lost relative to the fine record: if $I(Z_t;Y_{0:t})-I(Z_t;V_{0:t})=0$, then $p(z_t\mid v_{0:t})$ equals the marginal over orbits of the fine belief $p(x_t\mid y_{0:t})$. The paper states that this condition fails in its Ehrenfest example, where the coarse belief is exact for the coarse record and still less informative than the marginalized fine belief.

> [!note] Editorial: the condition of Corollary 4 and the information term of this page
> Because $V_{0:t}$ is a function of $Y_{0:t}$, the chain rule gives $I(Z_t;Y_{0:t})-I(Z_t;V_{0:t})=I(Z_t;Y_{0:t}\mid V_{0:t})=\mathbb E[\mathrm{KL}(p(z_t\mid Y_{0:t})\Vert p(z_t\mid V_{0:t}))]$. The quantity in Corollary 4 is therefore the conditional mutual information between the retained variable and the finer record given the coarse record, which is the kind of term that the subsection on a misread record shows every belief computed from the coarse record must pay. The identity is exact and elementary. The paper's Eq. (30) gives the expected log-ratio form, but it does not write the condition as a conditional mutual information. The correspondence of settings is interpretive: the paper has discrete states, no acts, exactly declared models and a filtering posterior, whereas the statements here are linear-Gaussian, include acts and declared models with defects, and take a predictive law as reference. Within those limits the Ehrenfest example is a discrete analogue of the situation behind the insufficiency term $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}$, in which every model is exact and the coarse record is still not sufficient. The paper gives no bound on the term when its condition fails.

## What is not established

The second set of results settles, under its hypotheses, items that the first version of this page listed as open. Approximate blanket conditions are combined with the theorem, for the canonical partition, per-channel noise, a stable joint process and members that run the consensus filter, with the members' channels as the record. Collectives of literal members have an exact value and bounds for the error of their belief, for scalar members with exact code models and channel noise specific to the receiver. The Markov error of an average under a non-reversible update is bounded with the exterior held fixed, under a power bound and a gap condition. Sensors that sense a part of the coarse exterior are covered when the declared drift is detectable through the information matrix.

The following remains open. Under violated blanket conditions the leak of the coarse blanket is not bounded through the defects, and the information term that separates the two reference laws has no bound that is uniform in the window and vanishes with the defects; it is capped by the insufficiency of the coarse sensory state only when the first two kinds of violation act alone, from a start with a deterministic conditional mean and for a record that contains the coarse blanket path. A bound on the excess relative to a coarse record that omits the insufficiency term is not given, and no proof decides whether that excess tends to zero when the acts carry part of what the members' channels hold. The least excess over the declared models is not determined. All closed-form bounds on beliefs concern the limit; the bounds that hold at every round depend on the start, and none is uniform in the round. A singular noise covariance of the summary or of the declared observation model, noise correlated between the sensory channels and the exterior, a declared observation model with memory, starts in which the exterior is not Gaussian given the initial collective variables, and members that do not run the consensus filter under violated blanket conditions are outside the theorems. For literal members the bounds on the belief take second moments of the outside senders' errors as data when members hear outside senders, the sharp constant for the colored drivers is unknown, and channel noise shared between a member and an outside listener, inexact code models, vector-valued states and performative acts are not treated; the lower bound on the closure defect is proved only for channel noise specific to the receiver. No bound on the Markov error is given when the exterior responds, none in terms of the precision-based contrast of the manuscript, none jointly for several blocks, and none through a measure of non-normality finer than $M_\perp$. The constants of the filter bound are not sharp. Nonlinear and non-Gaussian dynamics are outside all of these statements, and the open problem of the manuscript on an approximate meta-agent theorem is narrowed and not closed.

## In this work

The results belong to the multi-agent line of the program ([[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]). They concern a linear-Gaussian model of communicating agents and are not a statement about the gauge-theoretic free-energy functional or its implementation.
