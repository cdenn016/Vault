# Common knowledge and Bayesian agreement

## Definition

An event is common knowledge among agents when everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on through all finite levels. In the information-partition model this corresponds to the cell of the common-knowledge partition generated by the agents' information partitions.

[[aumann-1976-agreeing-disagree]] proves that if Bayesian agents share a common prior and their posterior probabilities for an event are common knowledge, those posterior probabilities are equal. The theorem is often summarized as "agents cannot agree to disagree," but every clause matters.

## Assumptions

- The agents' beliefs are conditional probabilities derived from one common prior on the same state space.
- Their private information is represented by partitions or equivalent sigma-algebras.
- The values of the posterior probabilities at issue are common knowledge, not merely announced once or mutually known at one level.
- The event and conditioning operations are commonly specified.

Remove the common prior or common-knowledge condition and persistent disagreement can be rational. The theorem also does not say that a realistic dialogue reaches common knowledge in finite time.

## What agreement does not imply

Posterior equality does not imply that the agreed probability is calibrated, that the underlying model contains the truth, or that all agents possess the same information. It does not specify a distributed computation, message complexity, or robustness to strategic reporting. Nor does it justify multiplying posteriors that contain overlapping evidence.

## Why it matters here

MultiAgentELBO's shared generative model may resemble a common-prior assumption, but the present theory does not automatically construct epistemic partitions or common knowledge of posterior reports. A gauge transformation aligns representations under a modeled symmetry; it does not create higher-order mutual knowledge. Consequently, gauge agreement, parameter consensus, common knowledge, and truth learning need separate definitions and tests.

## In this work

Use Aumann as an assumption audit, not a convergence citation. A genuine epistemic extension should define states, priors, information sigma-algebras, announcements, and the common-knowledge operator. If studying a finite dialogue, verify separately whether the protocol reaches equal posteriors and under what observability conditions.
