# Soft formation and the activation clock

A meta-agent, or parent, in the [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] is a coarse description that keeps some coordinates of its members and discards the rest (see [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Coarse Graining]]). The question "when did the collective form?" then becomes the question of when the discarded coordinates stop carrying information the coarse description needs. The one case worked out to the end is the seeded early prefix of the scalar shared-link four-cycle recorded in [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]], and there the answer is a crossover in rescaled time rather than an instant. This page collects that result with the slow–fast, probability, statistical-physics and social-science literature that bears on it. Every mapping between the model and an external source is interpretive unless stated otherwise.

> [!note] Editorial:
> The four-cycle is a grounding and validation case. Under the program's standing direction (adopted 2026-09-28), small networks ground and check mechanisms but do not set the theory's direction; the target is networks of 512 or more agents with gauge groups $GL(K,\mathbb R)$, $GL(K,\mathbb C)$ and $SU(N)$. What the four-cycle establishes below is a mechanism, an imprint-to-noise cutoff in the discarded coordinates. It is not a verdict on whether formation in large networks is a crossover or a transition.

## A threshold in rescaled time

In that model the temperature is $T=\epsilon^8$. The seed of the early prefix affects the discarded (hidden) coordinates only through an imprint of norm at most $\rho_\circ=1/100$, which the held Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow contracts at hidden rates of at least $4$. At a deterministic activation time $\theta$, the discarded optimizing conditional relative entropy is bounded by $[\eta(\theta)+S_2(p_\theta)]/(1-p_\theta)$ with $\eta(\theta)=9\rho_\circ^2e^{-8\theta}/(2T)+O(e^{-16\theta})$, and the bound vanishes when $\theta-\log(1/\epsilon)\to\infty$ (with $\epsilon^4\theta\to0$). A matching lower bound shows that the discarded relative entropy diverges when $\log(1/\epsilon)-\theta\to\infty$; at $\theta=\tfrac12\log(1/\epsilon)+1$ it is of order $T^{-1/2}$. The deterministic activation threshold is therefore $\log(1/\epsilon)+O(1)=\tfrac18\log(1/T)+O(1)$, and the window $\theta=\log(1/\epsilon)+O(1)$ itself is undecided [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]].

In the rescaled variable $s=\theta/\log(1/\epsilon)$ this is a sharp threshold at $s=1$ as $\epsilon\to0$, while in $\theta$ itself it is an $O(1)$ crossover. Activating the parent at the first qualifying completion time of the first-block mark, which on the seeded prefix satisfies $t_c\le\tfrac12\log(1/\epsilon)+1$, falls on the wrong side: the discarded relative entropy is at least $7.2\times10^{-8}c_mT^{-5/8}(1+o(1))$, and the derivation record shows that delaying activation to $t_c+h$ works once $h-\tfrac58\log(1/\epsilon)\to\infty$ [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]]. The binding restriction is the activation clock, not the learning mobility.

### The threshold as a small-noise cutoff

The leading term $\eta(\theta)$ is of order one exactly when the imprint amplitude $\rho_\circ e^{-4\theta}$ is of order the thermal scale $\sqrt T=\epsilon^4$, which happens at $\theta\approx\log(1/\epsilon)$. A threshold located by an imprint-to-noise ratio, rather than by a relaxation rate alone, is the cutoff phenomenon. [[aldous-1986-shuffling-cards-stopping-times]] named it for card shuffling, with a critical time at which the distance to equilibrium drops from near 1 to near 0, and remarked that spectral asymptotics miss it; [[diaconis-1996-cutoff-phenomenon-markov-chains]] gave the formal $(a_n,b_n)$ definition, with a window $b_n=o(a_n)$. For small-noise diffusions, [[barrera-2016-abrupt-convergence-small-perturbations]] prove profile cutoff in one dimension, and [[barrera-2020-thermalisation-small-random-perturbations]] prove it for $dx=-F(x)\,dt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,dB$ near a hyperbolic attractor. The cutoff time is $\frac{1}{2\lambda}\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ plus lower-order terms, the window is of order $1/\lambda$, and the profile is a shifted-Gaussian distance, where $\lambda$ is the rate of the slowest component actually present in the initial condition. In the four-cycle's held flow the discarded coordinates have noise variance $\varepsilon=2T=2\epsilon^8$ and slowest rate $\lambda=4+5T$ (manuscript section `sec:phil-early-parent`, archived with [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]]), so the Barrera–Jara time is $\frac{1}{8+10T}\ln\frac{1}{2T}=\log(1/\epsilon)+O(1)$ with a window of about $1/4$. That is the threshold of `prop:phil-early-seeded-parent`.

The match is in time scale and mechanism, not a direct application. Barrera and Jara's theorem concerns the total-variation distance to equilibrium of the full, unconditioned law from a deterministic start, under global coercivity and growth conditions that have not been established for the four-cycle. The model's quantity is the discarded conditional relative entropy of a law conditioned on the seed event and held. The two threshold locations agree because both distances are monotone in the Mahalanobis length of a Gaussian mean shift, an elementary linear computation that the theorem does not supply, and the $O(1)$ profile inside the window does not transfer.

Relative entropy itself is treated by [[boursier-2023-universal-cutoff-dyson-ornstein-uhlenbeck]]. Their exact Ornstein–Uhlenbeck identity (eq. 3.3) has, coordinate by coordinate, the same closed form as the Gaussian relative entropy in the proof of `prop:phil-early-seeded-parent`, $\frac12[-a-\log(1-a)+\lambda az^2/T]$ with $a=e^{-2\lambda r}$ [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]]. Their bias/variance split separates the founding-imprint (mean-shift) term, which carries the factor $1/T$ and so sets the $\log(1/\epsilon)$ threshold, from a covariance term that relaxes in $O(1)$ time at fixed dimension, and their explicit relative-entropy profiles have a window of size one. They also record that in fixed dimension with vanishing noise there is no cutoff from initial conditions of small norm. [[diaconis-1996-cutoff-phenomenon-markov-chains]] makes the same point with the Ehrenfest urn, which has a cutoff when started at $0$ but relaxes gradually, with no threshold, when started at $d/2$. Read for the model, a collective whose members begin near consensus has no sharp activation time, and the sharpness comes from a founding imprint that is large compared with $\sqrt T$. None of these results covers the relative entropy of a coarse-graining channel's discarded conditional law, conditioning on a rare seed event (the model's $1/(1-p)$ and $S_2$ terms have no counterpart), or the behavior inside the window, where the four-cycle result has neither a vanishing nor a positivity statement. The textbook background is [[levin-undated-markov-chains-and-mixing-times-retrieved-author-text]].

The slow–fast literature says the same thing in state space. Reduced (adiabatically eliminated) dynamics is valid only after an initial transient, and the founding condition leaves an "initial slip" in the slow variables ([[geigenmuller-1983-elimination-fast-variables]], [[haake-1983-adiabatic-drag-initial-slip]], [[vankampen-1985-elimination-fast-variables]]); [[cox-1995-initial-conditions-models]] guarantees only $O(e^{-\beta t})$ closeness in state space. Converting that closeness to relative entropy at temperature $T$ requires $e^{-\beta t}\ll\sqrt T$, which is what stretches an $O(1)$ fast-relaxation layer into $\theta\sim\log(1/\epsilon)$. None of these slow–fast sources performs the conversion; the cutoff results above supply it for unconditioned Ornstein–Uhlenbeck laws.

### Many agents: the number of coordinates as a second large parameter

In [[lachaud-2005-cutoff-hitting-times-ornstein-uhlenbeck]] and [[boursier-2023-universal-cutoff-dyson-ornstein-uhlenbeck]] the large parameter is the number of coordinates, not the noise. A sample of $n$ independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes has a total-variation cutoff at $\log(n)/(2\alpha)$ with an $O(1)$ window (Lachaud, abstract level), and the $n$-dimensional benchmark of Boursier, Chafaï and Labbé has its cutoff at $\log(n|z_0|)\vee\frac14\log n$ in total variation, Hellinger, relative entropy and $\chi^2$ (their Theorem 1.2). [[diaconis-1996-cutoff-phenomenon-markov-chains]] attributes cutoffs in general to high multiplicity of the second eigenvalue. The subject of Boursier, Chafaï and Labbé is the interacting Dyson process, whose cutoff their title calls universal; the locators verified for this wiki concern its non-interacting benchmark.

> [!note] Editorial:
> This bears directly on the program's large-network target. If a parent of many agents discards of order $n$ coordinates with a common rate $\lambda$, the model's per-coordinate formula summed over them gives a covariance term of about $na^2/4$, which falls below one only after $\frac{1}{4\lambda}\log n+O(1)$. A founding imprint spread over those coordinates turns the bias cutoff into $\frac{1}{2\lambda}\log(n/T)+O(1)$, as in the $\log(n|z_0|)$ of Boursier, Chafaï and Labbé. The activation clock of a parent formed in a 512-agent network could then depend on $\log n$ as well as on $\log(1/\epsilon)$. This is an extrapolation that assumes independent coordinates with a common rate; interacting gauge networks with spread rates have not been analyzed.

## What a collective discards and what it keeps

The parent discards the fast within-pair disagreements once the founding imprint in them has relaxed, and it keeps the slow shared modes, the source assignment (who attends to whom), and the loops. This split is the one [[simon-1961-aggregation-variables-dynamic-systems]] makes for nearly decomposable systems, one aggregate per block with the within-block configuration dropped, and the one [[haken-1982-slaving-principle-stochastic]] makes between order parameters and slaved modes. The separation in Simon and Ando comes from weak coupling between blocks; in the four-cycle it comes from strong restoring rates within pairs. [[diaconis-1996-cutoff-phenomenon-markov-chains]] remarks that one feature of a chain can mix at a different time from the whole chain, so "formed" has to be stated feature by feature: the discarded coordinates cross their threshold near $\log(1/\epsilon)$, while the retained source assignment can persist far longer.

The social-science record shows the same split. In Sherif's autokinetic experiments individual deviations are discarded by mutual convergence and the shared norm is kept, carried by every member, so it survives when the group is removed ([[sherif-1935-social-factors-perception]], [[sherif-1936-psychology-social-norms]]). Members who began with a private norm converge less completely than members who began together, and that residual shrinks over later sessions, the analogue of discarded coordinates that still carry the founding imprint early on. In [[centola-2015-spontaneous-emergence-conventions]] a population discards losing alternatives and keeps one winner or several local winners; network structure decides which, and in well-mixed populations a dominant convention emerges only "after the initial transient." [[campbell-1958-common-fate-social-entities]] (title-level only) and [[krakauer-2020-information-theory-individuality]] treat group-ness and individuality as graded rather than as events, and [[rosas-2024-software-natural-world]] supplies a stationary criterion (informational closure) that a parent can reach only asymptotically.

Opinion and network models show where founding order is kept. In the bounded-confidence model of [[deffuant2000-bounded-confidence]], which cluster an agent ends in is fixed by its initial opinion, with basins of attraction overlapping near cluster frontiers (Fig. 3, checked against the full text on 2026-09-28), so the founding configuration is kept in the assignment and discarded in the within-cluster detail. In the fitness model of [[bianconi-2001-bose-einstein-condensation-networks]] a finite fraction of links can condense onto one source, a transition in who attends to whom; in its first-mover phase older nodes keep more links, but the leader's share still decays as $t^{-1/2}$. Both concern the retained assignment rather than the discarded coordinates, and neither accounts in relative entropy.

## Two remedies

The first remedy is to wait out the initial layer: activate the parent only after the discarded coordinates have forgotten the founding imprint, or equivalently fold the slow-relevant part of the imprint into the kept coordinates once, by projecting the initial condition along the fast fibers ([[cox-1995-initial-conditions-models]]). Its exact form in the theory of Markov chains is a strong uniform time ([[aldous-1986-shuffling-cards-stopping-times]]), a stopping time at which the state is exactly at equilibrium and carries no information about the start, so a parent activated there would discard no information at all; for a continuous Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow the analogue is a coupling time. Its social counterpart is turnover. In [[jacobs-1961-arbitrary-tradition]] and [[macneil-1976-norm-change-arbitrariness]] an arbitrary founding tradition decays toward the natural norm as members are replaced one generation at a time, so generational replacement is the clock that erases the imprint. MacNeil and Sherif also find that more arbitrary norms lose a larger fraction of their displacement per generation. Because that fraction is normalized by amplitude, the finding contradicts a single linear rate constant and points to a nonlinear, latitude-bounded restoring force; the logarithmic dependence of the threshold on imprint size is a feature of linear relaxation that this social data does not share.

The second remedy is to keep the imprint as an explicit memory variable of the parent. [[zwanzig-1961-memory-effects]] shows that a projected equation carries an initial-value term unless the initial law already lies in the range of the projection, and suggests removing long-time memory by enlarging the set of relevant variables; [[cox-1995-initial-conditions-models]] (Sec. 3.1) shows that when fast modes do not relax, no memoryless projection works for all time. The imprinting literature constrains when this remedy is warranted. [[marquis-2013-imprinting-multilevel-theory]] require persistence as part of the definition of an imprint, and the three reasons [[stinchcombe-1965-social-structure-organizations]] gives for why founding structure persists (still efficient, self-reinforcing, not selected against) describe slow or conserved coordinates. On this reading a memory variable is justified for imprints carried by slow structure, such as the source assignment or the loop, and not for the decaying within-pair modes. Nonlinearity can promote founding memory into a kept coordinate: under conformist transmission the founding frequency selects a permanent basin within the model ([[boyd-1982-cultural-transmission-cooperation]]), and in [[young-1993-evolution-conventions]] which convention forms is remembered until noise-driven switching between basins erases it, a different and much slower mechanism than the relaxation within a basin that the four-cycle result proves.

Which remedy is available can depend on size. In Axelrod's model with cultural drift, [[klemm-2003-global-culture-noise-induced-transition]] find that disordered multicultural states are metastable and that drift at a rate small compared with $1/T_N$ carries a finite system to global culture, where the relaxation time is $T_N=N\ln N$; as $N\to\infty$ this time diverges and global polarization persists. The coarsening time $t_{\mathrm{co}}\sim L^2$ of the ordered Axelrod phase plays the same role ([[castellano-marsili-vespignani-2000-axelrod-transition]], Fig. 3 inset, checked against the full text on 2026-09-28).

> [!note] Editorial:
> On this evidence, waiting out the transient is a small-system remedy. In large populations the founding configuration can outlive every accessible time scale and then has to be carried as a parent variable. A four-agent result therefore cannot choose between the remedies for the program's target networks.

## Formed but not permanent: metastability

On longer time scales the question is whether a formed collective persists. Section 5 of [[barrera-2016-abrupt-convergence-small-perturbations]] gives the picture in one dimension: in a double-well potential the law inside a well thermalizes abruptly at logarithmic time, while the choice of well persists for times exponential in the inverse noise, with exponentially distributed, memoryless exit times ([[freidlin-2012-random-perturbations-dynamical-systems]]). The third edition of Freidlin and Wentzell identifies the sub-limiting distribution for a given initial point and time scale with metastability. [[bovier-2015-metastability-potential-theoretic-approach]] make metastable sets and their hitting times precise through the potential theory of reversible Markov processes (see also [[bovier-2004-metastability-diffusions]]). [[bianchi-2016-metastable-states-quasi-stationary-soft-measures]] introduce soft measures that interpolate between the restricted-ensemble measure and the quasi-stationary measure, with an asymptotically exponential exit law, and [[collet-2013-quasi-stationary-distributions]] treat quasi-stationary distributions, laws conditioned on not being killed, together with the Q-process of trajectories that survive forever. Together they give a vocabulary for a formed but impermanent collective: a sub-limiting or quasi-stationary law on a plateau between the $\log(1/T)$ cutoff of the discarded coordinates and an escape of the retained source assignment on a time scale exponential in $1/T$. In this vocabulary the memory-variable remedy corresponds to the transient before the quasi-stationary (Yaglom) limit is reached.

> [!note] Editorial:
> For the four-cycle this is a hypothesis, not a result. The model's windows of order $1/T$ and longer are undecided [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]], no exit-time asymptotics for the source assignment have been proved, and the parent has not been shown to be a quasi-stationary law or a metastable set. Apart from [[barrera-2016-abrupt-convergence-small-perturbations]], the metastability sources were read at abstract or table-of-contents level.

## Entropy versus free energy

The divergence of the discarded relative entropy before the threshold does not mean the parent is energetically expensive.

> [!note] Editorial:
> Multiplying the upper bound by $T$ gives $T\eta(\theta)=\tfrac92\rho_\circ^2e^{-8\theta}+O(Te^{-16\theta})\le\tfrac92\rho_\circ^2\approx4.5\times10^{-4}$ for every $\theta\ge0$, and the event-information term contributes $O(T)$, all up to the factor $1/(1-p_\theta)$; the evaluator-retaining physical loss adds at most $T$ [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]]. At $\theta=\tfrac12\log(1/\epsilon)+1$ the discarded relative entropy is of order $T^{-1/2}$ while $T\cdot\mathrm{KL}$ is of order $T^{1/2}$. At the first completion time the lower bound $T^{-5/8}$ gives $T\cdot\mathrm{KL}\gtrsim T^{3/8}$, which still vanishes; the source does not give a matching upper bound there. So a parent activated early can be nearly free in free energy and still badly wrong about its members in information. Which of the two is the right formation criterion for a collective is a modeling decision the sources do not settle.

A coarse variable can look relaxed while the discarded detail still carries initial-condition memory. Below the Kuramoto threshold the order parameter can decay by Landau damping even though the oscillator density does not relax ([[strogatz-2000-kuramoto-to-crawford]], Sec. 9, checked against the full text on 2026-09-28). The contrast with the four-cycle is that its discarded modes do relax, at rates of at least 4, whereas Kuramoto's decay is phase mixing and the fine-grained information is never lost. None of the social-science sources measures discarded information, so this distinction has no empirical counterpart yet. [[flack-2017-coarse-graining-downward-causation]] describes components acting on estimates of collectively computed coarse variables; in the model, acting on the coarse estimate at the first detector firing would mean acting on a description that still omits a diverging amount of relative entropy at small free-energy cost.

## Cutoff in time versus transition in size

The four-cycle has four agents. Its threshold sharpens in a small-noise limit ($\epsilon\to0$) and in time, not in a thermodynamic limit ($N\to\infty$) and in a control parameter, so it is a cutoff. On the standard reading of [[yang-1952-phase-transitions-condensation]] (verified here at abstract level only), a finite system at fixed temperature is analytic and a sharp, nonanalytic transition requires an infinite-system limit, so a four-agent threshold at $T>0$ could not be a phase transition in that sense. [[fisher-1972-finite-size-scaling-critical-region]] describe how a finite system rounds and shifts a transition by powers of its size; the four-cycle's $O(1)$ window and $O(1)$ offset around $\log(1/\epsilon)$ are a rounding and a shift only by analogy, because its large parameter is $1/T$, not size.

This does not decide whether formation is a crossover or a transition. That question is posed by families of networks of increasing size with an order parameter, and the four-cycle can only ground the mechanism such families would have to reproduce. The social-physics record shows what the large-size objects look like. Axelrod's model has a genuine nonequilibrium transition at a critical trait number $q_c$ in the largest-domain fraction $\langle s_{\max}\rangle/L^2$, first order for $F>2$ and continuous for $F=2$, with a drop that steepens as $L$ grows ([[castellano-marsili-vespignani-2000-axelrod-transition]], Fig. 1). With cultural drift the order–disorder change is instead controlled by $rN\ln N$, and the authors state that it is not a phase transition in the equilibrium sense because it disappears in the thermodynamic limit ([[klemm-2003-global-culture-noise-induced-transition]], footnote [10]). The Kuramoto onset at $K_c=2/(\pi g(0))$ is an $N\to\infty$ statement, and at finite $N$ the coherence fluctuates at $O(N^{-1/2})$ ([[strogatz-2000-kuramoto-to-crawford]], Secs. 3.3 and 5.1). The Vicsek flocking transition, with order parameter $|v_a|\sim(\eta_c-\eta)^\beta$ and $\beta\approx0.45$, is inferred for the thermodynamic limit from finite-size scaling of simulations ([[vicsek-1995-self-driven-particles]]). In growing networks a finite fraction of links can condense onto one source ([[bianconi-2001-bose-einstein-condensation-networks]]). Bounded-confidence compromise produces sharp bifurcations in the number of clusters in the population-density limit ([[ben-naim-2003-bifurcations-compromise]]), while finite populations show slight sample-to-sample variation in that number ([[deffuant2000-bounded-confidence]], Sec. 2.2). In each case the sharp object is an order parameter in a size or growth limit, not a threshold in time.

> [!note] Editorial:
> A formation transition for the program's target networks would need an order parameter for the parent, for example the fraction of agents whose source assignments have condensed into coherent blocks, computed along families of networks of increasing size with $GL(K,\mathbb R)$, $GL(K,\mathbb C)$ or $SU(N)$ transport, together with finite-size scaling of its rounding and shift. The four-cycle result predicts only that, within such a family, the discarded coordinates of a formed block lose their founding imprint at a cutoff. Whether soft formation at every finite size sharpens into a genuine transition as the number of agents grows is open.

The committed-minority experiment of [[centola-2018-tipping-points-social-convention]] shows the distinction in data. Its tipping point is sharp only for large populations and long observation times; the model underlying it has a single absorbing state, a subcritical minority leaves it in a long-lived quasi-stationary state in finite time, and at short times the transition is a smooth crossover. That is analogous to the four-cycle as a limit-sharpened crossover, but the sharp object there is a bifurcation in the committed fraction, not a cutoff in time. On the longest time scales the relevant picture is the metastability of the previous sections: the source assignment can change only by crossing between basins, which in [[young-1993-evolution-conventions]]'s setting takes exponentially longer than the logarithmic transient.

## Open

For the four-cycle, the discarded relative entropy given the whole contrast event at a deterministic late time, the learned/frozen first-block comparison at $\epsilon_L=T^{1/8}$, windows of order $1/T$ or longer, and recursion remain open [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]]. The cutoff literature derives logarithmic forgetting times for unconditioned laws, but no source above treats the relative entropy of a conditioned, discarded law, the profile inside the window, or the choice between the two remedies when the imprint relaxes slowly compared with the slow dynamics. For the program's target networks it is open whether families of increasing size have a formation transition with an order parameter, whether the number of discarded coordinates adds a $\log n$ term to the activation clock, and whether a formed parent is a quasi-stationary law or a metastable set.
