## Results

The boundary-information ceiling grows as $\lambda^2$ to within 2% across four decades
($4.97 \times 10^{-6}$ to $4.86 \times 10^{-2}$ nats at $k = 1$), saturation just beginning
at $\lambda = 100$ — so the top of the grid has genuinely something to bind. The partition
posterior does not care: the modal class is the **singleton partition at every** $\lambda$,
and at $k = 1$ the singleton mass *rises* with coupling ($0.586 \to 0.605$). Per the declared
rule, no formation transition is licensed on this ray: the singleton preference of
[[2026-08-18-mpart-participatory-blocking]] is structural in the derived energy, not a
weak-coupling artifact — the weak-coupling excuse is retracted. $R_{\mathrm{MI}}$ is
flat-to-declining ($0.0230 \to 0.0142$ at $k = 1$): **transmission is kernel-limited, not
signal-limited** — the $t \approx 0.15$ per-edge factor of
[[2026-08-18-minfo-information-retention]] is a property of the Bayes channel, which does not
adapt to signal. The sector gain stays small at every $\lambda$ (+10% to +17%), pricing
enlarged parent alphabets down until the kernel family itself changes.
