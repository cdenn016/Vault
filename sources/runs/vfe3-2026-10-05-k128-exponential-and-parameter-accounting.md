# VFE_3.0 K=128 exponential-chart run and parameter accounting against conventional baselines, October 5, 2026

> [!info] Scope
> A read-only analysis, made for the GL(K) manuscript rework of [[dennis-2026-glk-attention-v2-rework-2026-10-05]], of one VFE_3.0 training run (`VFE_3.0/vfe3_runs/34.48_wikitext-103_K128_block_glk_linear_mlp_s6`), of earlier VFE runs kept in `D:/DATA_KEEP/` (the $K=160$ affine headline `30.14_wikitext-103_K160_n=256_6-layer` and the matched runs `39.77_wikitext-103_K128_n=128` and `52.91_wikitext-103_K64_n=128_4-layers`), and of the two conventional baselines in `D:/DATA_KEEP/10-3-2026_baselines/` (`90.15_wikitext-103_K128_baseline_s6`, `117.63_wikitext-103_K64_baseline_s6`). No training, GPU workload or edit of a run directory was made; numbers come from each run's `config.json`, `summary.json`, `metrics.csv`, `provenance.json` and diagnostics, and the trained-link spectra from CPU float64 loads of the final checkpoints. Full records: `VFE_3.0/docs/manuscript-revision-2026-10-05/claude/headline-run-K128.md`, `headline-run-K160.md`, `baselines-parameter-mapping.md` and `theory-interfaces-links.md` (Section 3.4).

## The K=128 exponential-chart run

The model has width $K=128$ in four $\mathfrak{gl}(32)$ blocks, four heads of width 32 and four layers with independent maps, the `projected_predictive` route with Gaussian-KL routing between full $32\times32$ projected covariances at $\tau=\sqrt{32}$, the exponential content chart $\widetilde O=T^{-1}+O\varphi_1(TO)$ evaluated in float64 with stored $O=0$ at initialization, residual $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$, moment-matched predictive covariance with floor $10^{-4}$, a uniform causal prior with no ALiBi (positions enter only through learned positional frames), one shared LayerNorm, a $128\to1024\to128$ MLP and an untied linear decoder with a unigram prior. It has 227,041,490 parameters and was trained for 150,000 AdamW updates at batch 32 and context 128, that is 614.4M token presentations or 5.26 passes over the 116.8M-token training split, with model seed 6 and data seed 3, on the RTX 5090 in 58,897 s, with no skipped or nonfinite step. Under context 128 and stride 32 the test perplexity is 34.4804 at the final weights (CE 3.5404) and 33.9696 at the best-validation weights (step 140,000); best validation is 32.9439 and final validation 33.4414. The source tree was dirty at launch; the saved archive reproduces the recorded code identity.

In this run the residual is active: the per-token ratio $\|m_i\|/\|\mu_i\|$ has median 0.74, 0.62, 0.74 and 1.05 at layers 0 to 3 on eight validation windows, unlike the $K=160$ and $K=256$ affine runs, where deleting the residual costs almost nothing. Removing the routing costs on one validation batch raises CE from 3.146 to 4.866 nats. The effective content maps $\widetilde O_hT_h$ have largest singular values 1.1 to 4.5, while the stored generator products reach 47 to 150. No bootstrap intervals, seed replicates or full-split frozen interventions exist for this run.

## Trained links are numerically low rank

From the final checkpoint in float64: the sending maps $T$ are well conditioned (condition number 11 to 25), but the generators $L=OT$ have $\operatorname{tr}L=-2610,-1740,-1790,-674$ by layer and $\min\operatorname{Re}\lambda(L)$ between $-123$ and $-39.5$, so $\sigma_{\min}(e^L)\le e^{-39.5}\approx7\times10^{-18}$ in every layer. Between 35 and 79 eigenvalues per layer give gains below $10^{-3}$, and the numerical rank of $e^L$ at relative threshold $10^{-3}$ is 47, 61, 83 and 90 of 128. The group element is invertible in exact arithmetic but behaves as a selective, numerically low-rank channel reached by contraction; reverse edges $U_je^{-L}U_i^{-1}$ have norms up to about $e^{123}$, so inverse-edge holonomy on trained models must be computed in log space from $\operatorname{eig}(L)$. A direct `slogdet` of `matrix_exp(L)` returned negative determinants for two layers, an underflow artifact that was discarded.

For the $K=160$ affine headline the trained transfers $OT$ have negative determinant in four of six layers and 7 to 11 negative real eigenvalues in every layer, so they are not in $\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(K))$ and the exponential chart could not represent them; the token-0 self-block $I+OT$ of the residual layer also has negative real eigenvalues in all six layers, so the reading of the residual layer as the first-order chart of a group element fails at those trained weights. In the same run, replacing the token frames by the identity in every role costs $+6.90$ nats on the full validation split ($[6.78,7.03]$, paired article bootstrap), removing the routing costs $+1.59$, flattening the positional prior $+2.46$, and removing the residual $+0.0009$.

## Parameter accounting and the baselines

The $K=128$ VFE models (34.48 and 39.77) hold 205,852,672 parameters in the token-frame table ($50{,}257\times4\times32^2$, 90.7%), 6,432,896 each in prior means and log-variances and 6,483,153 in the decoder, so 99.2% of the total is vocabulary-indexed; positional frames hold 524,288 and context processing 1,315,584 (attention maps 262,144, MLP 1,053,184, norm 256). The conventional $d=128$ baseline has 14,233,681 parameters, of which 1,317,632 process context. Non-embedding parameters in the Kaplan convention ([[kaplan-2020-scaling-laws]]) therefore match to 0.16% at $K=128$ and 0.31% at $K=64$, and the 16- and 17-fold differences in total parameters sit entirely in per-type and per-position tables: each vocabulary entry stores $K(d_b+2)=4{,}352$ numbers in the VFE against 128 in the baseline. The frame is stored as an embedding but acts as a token- and position-conditioned invertible linear map on the queries, keys, values and outputs of every layer.

The baselines were built by `BaselineConfig.matched_to` from the 39.77 and 52.91 runs, not from the 30.14 headline, which differs from the $d=128$ baseline in tokens (4.0×), context and stride, depth, heads, batch, schedule and total parameters. The protocol-matched pairs (equal tokens, steps, batch, context, stride, depth, heads, MLP expansion, ALiBi prior, unigram decoder prior, tokenizer and test set) give 39.77 against 90.15 at $K=128$ and 52.91 against 117.63 at $K=64$; they still differ in data order, optimizer groups, learning rates, weight decay and clipping, and in total parameters. The 34.48 run has no matched conventional baseline at its 614.4M-token budget and differs from 39.77 in five confounded settings (tokens, chart, prior, covariance contract, learning rates), so 34.48 against 39.77 isolates none of them.

## What is not established

The matched gaps show that the VFE models with their per-type tables beat conventional transformers of equal non-embedding size under one protocol and one seed each. They do not show whether the gain comes from the multiplicative frame structure or from raw per-type capacity: the control that would separate them, a conventional transformer with a per-type read-map table of the frame table's size (`per_type_table='block_read'`), and a frozen-transport VFE arm have not been run. Seed variability for these configurations is unmeasured; the nearest noise floor is the $K=30$ measurement of [[vfe3-2026-10-05-k30-multiseed]].

## Relevance to this research

This record anchors the language-model section of the reworked GL(K) manuscript and answers why a width-128 state reaches about 36 perplexity where conventional width-128 models do not: $K$ is the width of the evolving state, not a measure of capacity comparable to $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ with tied tables. The low-rank trained links qualify the conditioning argument for group links recorded at $K=20$ in [[vfe3-2026-10-03-link-charts-and-channel-receiver]].
