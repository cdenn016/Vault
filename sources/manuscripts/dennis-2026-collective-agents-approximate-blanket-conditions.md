# Communication, Coherence and Collective Agents: misread records, approximate blanket conditions, level-0 belief and Markov error

## Source and scope

The live source is the supplement section "Approximate meta-agents and controlled renormalization" of the manuscript *Communication, Coherence and Collective Agents*: statements in [ext1_apx.tex](../../manuscripts/Collective_Agents/sections/ext1_apx.tex) and proofs in [appext1_apx.tex](../../manuscripts/Collective_Agents/sections/appext1_apx.tex). This note is a companion to [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]], which records three earlier results of the same section. It records, at the author's request of November 11, 2026, the theory of four further results: a bound for a declared filter whose observation model is wrong and whose record is a summary of a finer record; the approximate meta-agent theorem without exact blanket conditions; the excess relative entropy of the belief carried by the average of level-0 members; and a bound on the Markov error of one coordinate of a linear recursion that need not be reversible. It records statements, hypotheses and limits only. The numerical checks that accompany these results in the manuscript are not recorded here. The manuscript is a working draft and its statements may change.

The section "What is not established" of the companion note is superseded in part by the section of the same name below, which says which of its items are now settled, under which hypotheses, and which remain.

## Setting

The notation is that of the companion note: a fine process $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t+\mathfrak n_t$, a retained variable $y_t=Cx_t$, a declared coarse model $(\bar A,\bar B,\bar Q)$ with closure defect $D=CA-\bar AC$, input defect $E=CB-\bar B$ and defect along the path $\delta_t=Dx_t+Eu_t$, the predictive covariance $P^-_{\mathrm b}$, gain $L$ and closed-loop matrix $\Psi'=\bar A(I-L\bar H)$ of the stationary [[Kalman filter]] of the declared model, its contraction factor $\theta_{\mathrm b}<1$ in the metric of $P^-_{\mathrm b}$ and its forgetting time $\tau_{\mathrm b}=(1-\theta_{\mathrm b}^{1/2})^{-1}$. Throughout, $g(x)=x-1-\ln x$, $h(M)=\frac12(\operatorname{tr}M-\dim M-\ln\det M)$, and $\Vert v\Vert_{\mathrm b}=(\mathbb E\Vert v\Vert^2_{(P^-_{\mathrm b})^{-1}})^{1/2}$. The excess relative entropy of a belief is the expected relative entropy from an exact predictive law, the reference, to that belief. All four results are linear and Gaussian.

## A record that the declared model misreads

In the companion's filter bound the record reads the retained variable alone and the declared observation model is exactly right. The new setting has a fine record $z_{t+1}=H_{\mathrm x}x_t+v_t$ with noise covariance $R_{\mathrm x}\succ0$ that reads the whole fine state, a policy that may read the fine record, $u_t=\pi_t(z_{1:t},\omega)$ with a randomization $\omega$, and a meta-agent that reads a summary $s_t=\mathsf Tz_t$, with $\mathsf T$ of full row rank and noise covariance $R_{\mathsf T}=\mathsf TR_{\mathrm x}\mathsf T^\top$, through a declared observation model $s_{t+1}=\bar Hy_t+\hat v_t$, $\hat v_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\bar R)$, with $\bar R\succ0$ and $(\bar A,\bar H)$ detectable. The observation defect is $\mathsf G=\mathsf TH_{\mathrm x}-\bar HC$, with $\varpi_t=\mathsf Gx_t$ along the path, and the noise mismatch is $R_{\mathsf T}-\bar R$. The start is Gaussian given the randomization, with a deterministic covariance. The reference is the exact predictive law $\mathcal N(C\hat x^-_t,P_{\mathrm e,t})$ of $y_t$ given the fine record, and $e_t=C\hat x^-_t-\hat\eta^-_t$ is the error of the mean of the belief $\mathcal N(\hat\eta^-_t,P^-_{\mathrm b})$.

The theorem assumes that $(A,H_{\mathrm x})$ is detectable and that $e_0$ has a finite second moment. Its first part is an exact recursion, $e_{t+1}=\Psi'e_t+\bar A(L_{\mathrm e,t}-L\mathsf T)\iota_t+d_t-\bar AL\hat\varpi_t$, in which $\iota_t$ is the innovation of the fine record, $L_{\mathrm e,t}$ the exact gain of $y_t$ on it, $d_t$ the mean of the closure defect given the fine record through round $t+1$ and $\hat\varpi_t$ the mean of the observation defect given the fine record through round $t$. Its second part is a covariance equation. The innovation term has stationary covariance $\bar A\mathsf W\bar A^\top$, and $\mathsf W$ splits orthogonally into $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}$, the part that no gain on the summary can remove, plus the mismatch between the best gain on the summary and the declared gain. The difference $\mathsf X=P_{\mathrm e}-P^-_{\mathrm b}$ of the predictive covariances obeys $\mathsf X=\Psi'\mathsf X\Psi'^\top+\mathsf N+\mathsf N_{\mathrm{obs}}-\bar A\mathsf W\bar A^\top$, where $\mathsf N$ is the term of the companion's theorem and $\mathsf N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ collects the observation defect and the noise mismatch, so the stationary energy $\mathsf i_{\mathrm b}^2$ of the innovation term in the metric of $P^-_{\mathrm b}$ is known exactly. An enclosure of $\mathsf X$ of the same quadratic form as in the companion's theorem holds with a modified source term. The bound is
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}\Delta\mathcal F_t\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^kg(1+\mathsf y_i)+\frac12\bigl(\tau_{\mathrm b}\bar d_{\mathrm o}+\mathsf i_{\mathrm b}\bigr)^2,$$
where the $\mathsf y_i$ are the eigenvalues of $\mathsf X$ in the metric of $P^-_{\mathrm b}$ and $\bar d_{\mathrm o}$ bounds the upper limit of $\Vert d_t-\bar AL\hat\varpi_t\Vert_{\mathrm b}$, the defect drive. When the defect drive vanishes, as when $D=0$, $E=0$ and $\mathsf G=0$, the limit of the excess exists and equals the right side. A further part bounds the excess at every round by an expression that depends on the start. In the companion's setting, where $\mathsf G=0$, $R_{\mathsf T}=\bar R$ and $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}=0$, the bound reduces to the companion's.

The contraction argument is unchanged because $\Psi'$ and $P^-_{\mathrm b}$ belong to the declared filter alone. The observation defect behaves as the closure defect does: the part that the fine record resolves moves the mean through the declared gain, and the unresolved part is added to the noise of the summary and is correlated with the prediction error of $y_t$. The covariance equation therefore contains the difference between the declared noise and the summary noise augmented by the unresolved defect, and a declared noise that absorbs the unresolved defect removes that term although it differs from $R_{\mathsf T}$. A remark shows that the right side of the bound is at most a constant times the square of the largest of six quantities, namely the root energies of $\delta_t$ and of $\varpi_t$, the norm of $\mathsf N$, the mismatch of the augmented summary noise, the covariance of the unresolved observation defect with the prediction error, and the gain residual $(\operatorname{tr}\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}})^{1/2}$ of the summary, on families of instances on which a stated list of norms stays bounded, whenever that largest quantity is at most one. The constant depends on those bounds and is not uniform in them. A second remark extends the theorem to a fine input that is any square-integrable function of the fine record and a declared input that is another such function, which covers members that act on the exterior one by one while the declared model knows a coarse act.

A proposition isolates what the summary cannot carry. For every coarse record $\mathfrak k_t$ that is a function of the fine record and the randomization and that determines $\hat\eta^-_t$, the excess splits exactly as $\Delta\mathcal F_t=\mathbb E\operatorname{KL}(P(y_t\in\cdot\mid\mathfrak k_t)\Vert\mathcal N(\hat\eta^-_t,P^-_{\mathrm b}))+I_t$, with $I_t=I(y_t;(z_{1:t},\omega)\mid\mathfrak k_t)$, and every belief that is a probability kernel from $\mathfrak k_t$ has an excess of at least $I_t$ relative to the fine-record reference. The information term satisfies $I_t\le\frac12\ln\det(I+P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}\mathsf V_t)$, where $\mathsf V_t$ is the expected conditional covariance of the exact predictive mean given $\mathfrak k_t$, with equality exactly when the law of $y_t$ given $\mathfrak k_t$ is Gaussian with covariance $P_{\mathrm e,t}+\mathsf V_t$. For a deterministic initial mean and a coarse record that contains the summary and the acts, $I_t\le\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T,t})$, where $P_{\mathsf T,t}$ is the predictive covariance of $y_t$ under the exact filter that reads the summary alone. When the policy reads the summary alone and the initial mean of the belief is a function of the randomization, this is an equality, the law given the coarse record is that of the filter of the summary with the acts as known inputs, and the theorem applies to the remaining excess with no insufficiency term when $(A,\mathsf TH_{\mathrm x})$ is detectable; the hypothesis on the initial mean of the belief cannot be dropped. When all model defects vanish and only the summary is insufficient, the excess converges to $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e}^{-1}P^-_{\mathrm b})$, which the bound then equals and which is positive in a scalar example with two independent readings of which the summary keeps one. No bound that vanishes with $D$, $E$, $\mathsf G$, $\mathsf N$ and $R_{\mathsf T}-\bar R$ can therefore hold for a summarized record. When the policy reads the fine record, the acts carry part of what the summary withholds; the law of $y_t$ given the coarse record is then a mixture of Gaussian laws, which is Gaussian for policies that are affine in the fine record and a Gaussian randomization and is not Gaussian for an explicit policy that uses an absolute value.

A second proposition treats a recursion that no filter equation produced: any gain $L$, any claimed covariance $P^-_{\mathrm c}\succ0$ in whose metric $\Psi'$ contracts with factor $\theta_{\mathrm c}<1$, and an additive perturbation $\nu_t$ with finite second moments and arbitrary dependence. The error recursion and the split of $\mathsf W$ hold as before, the relative entropy splits exactly into a covariance part and half the squared error norm, and the upper limit of the error norm is at most $(\bar d_{\mathrm c}+\bar\nu_{\mathrm c})/(1-\theta_{\mathrm c}^{1/2})$ plus the root of the innovation energy. When $\rho(\Psi')<1$, the predictive covariance that the declared model assigns to the gain $L$ is an admissible $P^-_{\mathrm c}$. This is the form in which the argument reaches the members' average under computation noise and the fixed-gain average of level-0 members.

## The approximate meta-agent under approximate blanket conditions

The companion's assembled theorem keeps exact the hypothesis, called (H-suff) in the manuscript, that the coarse sensory state is the information-form statistic of the members' sensors, that the sensors read the coarse exterior $\eta_C$ alone and without memory, and that the exterior reads the members only through the coarse act. The new theorem does not assume it. It keeps the canonical partition, in which no internal and no active variable reads the exterior and the exterior reads no internal variable, per-channel noise, a stable joint process, $\rho(A)<1$, the consensus protocol of the members on a shared declared coarse model $(\bar F,\bar B,\bar Q)$ and a shared declared observation model $(\bar H,\bar R)$ with $(\bar F,\bar H)$ detectable, and the start of the companion's theorem, in which the exterior is Gaussian given the initial collective variables with a covariance that does not depend on them. The coarse sensory state is $s_C=\mathsf P_{\mathrm S}X_{\mathrm S}$ for an arbitrary map $\mathsf P_{\mathrm S}$ of full row rank, whose dimension need not equal that of $\eta_C$.

The reduction is a change of record. The members' record is the sensory channels net of what they read inside the collective, $z_{t+1}=A_{\mathrm{SE}}X_{\mathrm E,t}+\Xi^{\mathrm S}_t$; the coarse sensory state is the summary $\mathsf P_{\mathrm S}z_{t+1}$ plus a term $\mathfrak p_t$ that the collective knows; and the exterior is driven by a fine act that the collective knows. The closure defect $\delta_t$ now contains what the exterior reads of the members beyond the coarse act, and the observation defect $\varpi_t=\mathsf GX_{\mathrm E,t}+\mathfrak p_t$, with $\mathsf G=\mathsf P_{\mathrm S}A_{\mathrm{SE}}-\bar H\mathsf P_{\mathrm E}$, contains what the coarse sensory state reads beyond $\bar H\eta_C$. Five kinds of violation are distinguished, and none enters the hypotheses.

| Kind | Enters through | Error terms affected | Cap proved on the information term when the kind acts alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| (B1) sensors read exterior coordinates outside $\eta_C$ | $\mathsf G$, $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}$ | energy of $\varpi_t$ in the path rate and in the defect drive; innovation energy $\mathsf i_{\mathrm b}$; the credit term of the path identity uses a smaller covariance | $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T,t})$ |
| (B2) coarse sensory state formed with other weights | $\mathsf G$, $R_{\mathsf T}-\bar R$, $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}$ | energy of $\varpi_t$; $\mathsf i_{\mathrm b}$; the noise mismatch term $h(\bar R^{-1}R_{\mathsf T})$; with a matched $(\bar H,\bar R)$ only $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}$, and then, with the true coarse model and noiseless computation, the belief bound is an equality | $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T,t})$ |
| (B3) sensors with memory | $\mathfrak p_t$ | energy of $\varpi_t$ in the path rate and in the defect drive | none |
| (B4) sensors read members' acts or internal states | $\mathfrak p_t$ | as (B3) | none |
| (B5) exterior reads the members one by one, or their sensory channels | $\delta_t$ and the difference of true and declared effects | energy of $\delta_t$ in the path rate and in the defect drive; additional terms in the agency bound | none |

The cap of the last column holds for a record that contains the coarse blanket path and from a start in which the conditional mean $m_0$ of the exterior is deterministic; it is not proved for other starts.

The conclusions follow the five properties of the exact theorem. First, in place of the exact coarse blanket, a filtering statement: the law of the exterior given the paths of all collective variables is $\mathcal N(\hat x^-_t,\Sigma^-_t)$, computed by the exterior filter from the members' record with the fine act as a known input, it is also the law given in addition the internal and active variables of the next round, the covariance is deterministic and does not depend on what the members compute or do, the pair $(A_{\mathrm{EE}},A_{\mathrm{SE}})$ is detectable, and the covariance converges from every start. The pair $(s_C,a_C)$ need not be a coarse path blanket in the weak or in the strong sense, and no bound on its leak is asserted. The statement is not the fine path blanket of the manuscript, which can fail under (B4). Second, the closure of the coarse internal state is exact. Third, for a start in which the coarse exterior has a nondegenerate conditional covariance, the relative entropy between the true path law and a declared law is given by an exact identity, and its rate is at most $h(\bar Q^{-1}Q^y)+\frac12\operatorname{tr}(\bar Q^{-1}\Sigma_\delta)$ when the abstract physics alone is declared and at most that plus $h(\bar R^{-1}R_{\mathsf T})+\frac12\operatorname{tr}(\bar R^{-1}\Sigma_\varpi)$ when the sensing is declared as well, with $\Sigma_\delta$ and $\Sigma_\varpi$ the stationary covariances of the two defects. Under either declared law the remaining collective variables keep their true conditional laws; the first declared law is that of a closed loop when the sensors read the exterior through $\eta_C$ alone, and neither can be simulated by the meta-agent from its coarse model alone. Fourth, the belief $\mathcal N(y^\eta_{C,t+1},P^-_{\mathrm b})$ encoded by the members' average, measured against the law of the coarse exterior given the members' channels, satisfies
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}\Delta\mathcal F^C_t\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^kg(1+\mathsf y_i)+\frac12\bar{\mathsf e}_{\mathrm o}^2,\qquad\bar{\mathsf e}_{\mathrm o}=\tau_{\mathrm b}\bigl(\bar d_{\mathrm o}+\bar\nu_{\mathrm b}\bigr)+\mathsf i_{\mathrm b},$$
where $\bar\nu_{\mathrm b}$ is the computation noise of the companion's theorem and the defect drive $\bar d_{\mathrm o}$ is at most the root energy of $\delta_t$ in the metric of $\bar Q$ plus a constant of the declared filter times the root energy of $\varpi_t$ in the metric of $\bar R$. Under (H-suff), with the declared observation model $\bar H=\bar R=\Lambda^s$, the right sides are at most those of the companion's theorem. Fifth, the stationary law of deliberation is exactly the group posterior of the declared model, and the total-variation bound of the companion's theorem holds with a first-order term that now includes what the exterior reads of the sensory channels and the difference between the true and the declared effect of each member's active channels. The error terms for the path law and the belief are of second order in the violations, in the sense of the remark quoted above.

A proposition compares two reference laws. A coarse record is a function of the paths of the collective variables; examples are the coarse blanket path and the coarse blanket path together with the coarse internal path. For every coarse record that determines the mean of the belief, the excess relative to the members' channels equals the excess relative to the law given the coarse record plus an information term $I_t(\mathfrak k)$, so the latter excess obeys the same bound, and every belief computed from the coarse record pays at least $I_t(\mathfrak k)$. The information term is bounded uniformly in $t$ by $\frac12\ln\det(I+(Q^y)^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_{C,t}))$, it decreases when the coarse record is refined, and for the coarse blanket path it equals $I(\eta_{C,t};\mathfrak z_{0:t}\mid b_{C,0:t})$, which is at most the leak on the window of the strong coarse path blanket for the residual assignment that places every residual with the interior. When that leak vanishes the two reference laws coincide. This inequality is the sense in which the theorem is combined with the leak bound of the manuscript, and the combination is weak, because the leak bound grows linearly in the window while the information term stays bounded. When only (B1) and (B2) are present, $m_0$ is deterministic and the record contains the coarse blanket path, the information term is at most $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T,t})$, with $P_{\mathsf T,t}$ the predictive covariance of the filter that reads the coarse sensory state alone.

A further proposition concerns the limit and the declared input matrix. The excess has the exact form $\Delta\mathcal F^C_t=\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e,t}^{-1}P^-_{\mathrm b})+\frac12(\Vert\mathfrak q_t\Vert^2_{\mathrm b}-k)$, where $\mathfrak q_t=\eta_{C,t}-y^\eta_{C,t+1}$ is the error of the belief mean as a prediction of the coarse exterior, so it converges, and its limit does not depend on the start; the upper limits of the theorem are therefore limits. When only the weights are wrong and every model is declared truthfully, the limit is the insufficiency term $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}=\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e}^{-1}P^-_{\mathrm b})$. For a declared input matrix $\bar B=\mathsf P_{\mathrm E}\mathsf L_{\mathrm E}+\upsilon\mathsf Q$ the limit is $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}$ minus a term linear in $\upsilon$ plus a term quadratic in $\upsilon$, with a derivative at $\upsilon=0$ that is negative for a suitable $\mathsf Q$ whenever $\mathsf K\Sigma_{\mathfrak qa}\ne0$, where $\mathsf K$ is the closed-loop matrix of the declared filter and $\Sigma_{\mathfrak qa}$ the stationary covariance of the error with the current coarse act. In that case declared input matrices arbitrarily close to the true one give a limit below $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}$: the insufficiency is not a floor, because the common update then reads the coarse act as a further observation. When the internal and the active variables read the sensory channels through the coarse sensory state alone, the limit is at least $\mathfrak i_{\mathsf T}$ whatever input matrix is declared, and $\mathsf K\Sigma_{\mathfrak qa}=0$. The least value of the limit over the declared input matrix is not computed.

A remark covers sensors that sense only a part of $\eta_C$, for which the information matrix $\Lambda^s$ of the sensors is singular and which the companion's theorem excludes. With the reduced statistic of dimension $q<k$ as the coarse sensory state and the matching declared observation model, and when $(\bar F,\Lambda^s)$ is detectable, the declared observation model is exact, the summary is sufficient, $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}=0$, the members and the meta-agent are those of the exact theorem with the singular $\Lambda^s$, and the bounds are at most the expressions of the companion's theorem formed with that $\Lambda^s$.

## The belief of a collective of level-0 members

The companion's theorem on level-0 members bounds the memory of their average and says nothing about the average as an estimate. The new proposition treats scalar members with exact code models in the scalar network of the manuscript, with world state $\eta_{t+1}=F\eta_t+w_t$, Gaussian noises that are independent of one another and across rounds, and every channel noise specific to its receiver. The exterior of $C$ is the world state together with the agents outside $C$, each of which hears individual members, so it is driven by the members' messages and not by a coarse act. The record stacks what the members receive from outside: their sensor readings and the messages from the outside senders they attend. The randomization consists of the members' initial means and the noise of the messages inside $C$. The hypotheses are positive variances of the world innovation, of every channel of the record and of the local noise of every outside agent, a Gaussian start, and $\rho(W)<1$ for the update $W$ of the network. Nothing is assumed about $F$, and $\lvert F\rvert\ge1$ is allowed. The collective holds the belief $\mathcal N(\bar m(t),P_{\mathrm c})$ about $\eta_t$, with $\bar m$ the average of the members' means and $P_{\mathrm c}>0$ any claimed variance.

The proposition has five parts. The reference law: the members' means are functions of the record and the randomization, the law of the exterior given what the collective holds is Gaussian with the deterministic covariance of an exterior filter, the filter equation has exactly one fixed point to which the covariance converges from every start whatever the value of $F$, and the exact predictive law of $\eta_t$ is $\mathcal N(\hat\eta^{\mathrm e}_t,P_{\mathrm e,t})$. The exact value: for every $t\ge1$,
$$\Delta\mathcal F^0_t=\tfrac12\ln\frac{\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg},t}^2}{P_{\mathrm e,t}}+\tfrac12g\Bigl(\frac{\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg},t}^2}{P_{\mathrm c}}\Bigr),$$
where $\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg},t}^2$ is the mean square error of the average, which splits as $P_{\mathrm e,t}$ plus the mean square of $e^{\mathrm b}_t=\hat\eta^{\mathrm e}_t-\bar m(t)$. The excess is thus half the logarithm of the inefficiency of the average as an estimator plus the price of a claimed variance that differs from the actual mean square error; it converges, the first term of the limit does not depend on $P_{\mathrm c}$, and the second vanishes exactly for $P_{\mathrm c}=\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg},\infty}^2$. The error of the mean: $e^{\mathrm b}_t$ obeys a recursion with coefficient $\bar\lambda$, the mean row sum of the update of the members, and four drivers, namely the mismatch between the members' pooled fixed gains and the exact gain of the world state on the innovation of the record, the part of the outside senders' errors that the record predicts, which the coarse sensory state reads as if it were the world state, the memory defect $\delta_t$ of the companion's theorem, and the noise of the messages inside $C$. For $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert<1$ this gives a bound $\bar{\mathsf e}_0$ on the upper limit of the root mean square of $e^{\mathrm b}_t$ and $\Delta\mathcal F^0_\infty\le\frac12g(P_{\mathrm e}/P_{\mathrm c})+\bar{\mathsf e}_0^2/(2P_{\mathrm c})$, with equality when no agent is outside $C$ and the column sums of the update are equal. A bound closed up to the outside senders: under $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert<1$, mixing of the deviations, and the small-gain condition $\epsilon_{\mathrm{avg}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\mathcal S_\perp/(1-\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert)<1$, the limiting error of the average is bounded through the heterogeneity of the members and the errors of the outside senders, the exact predictive variance is bounded below by the solution $P_\star$ of a scalar filter equation for the direct readings of the world state, and
$$\Delta\mathcal F^0_\infty\le\frac12\Bigl[\frac{\bar{\mathsf e}_{\mathrm{avg}}^2}{P_{\mathrm c}}-1-\ln\frac{P_\star}{P_{\mathrm c}}\Bigr];$$
when no agent is outside $C$ the right side is a function of the parameters of the collective alone. The coarse record as reference: against the law of $\eta_t$ given the paths of the coarse sensory state and of the average, the excess is smaller by exactly $\frac12\ln(P_{\mathfrak k,t}/P_{\mathrm e,t})$, the information about $\eta_t$ that the members' individual inputs and means carry beyond the coarse record, and every upper bound of the proposition remains an upper bound for it.

The first bound is of second order jointly in its drivers and in $P_{\mathrm e}/P_{\mathrm c}-1$ on families on which $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert$ stays away from one and $P_{\mathrm c}$ from zero. The a priori bound is not of second order, since it charges the whole error of the fixed-gain recursion. A remark discusses the two choices in the definition. The two reference laws differ for level-0 members because the coarse sensory state weights the members' channels by gains and not by precisions and because individual inputs separate the outside senders' errors from the world state better than their weighted sum does; the path of the coarse sensory state alone is not a usable reference, since the average is not a function of it. For the claimed variance, two choices are defined by the network. Reading the closed coarse recursion of the companion's theorem as a stationary Kalman filter with the true innovation variance gives $P_{\mathrm c}=Q/(1-F\bar\lambda)$, under stated conditions on the gains. The members' own claims under literal fusion are $Q/(1-F^2(1-k_i))$ for a member of total gain $k_i$ when $F^2(1-k_i)<1$, and with equal total leaks the claim of every member that attends another member is below the first choice. Neither choice is calibrated in general; the calibrated one requires the actual error of the average.

## The Markov error of one coordinate

The Markov error of a coordinate $y=\phi^\top x$ of a stationary recursion $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+\mathfrak n_t$ is $\Xi^{\mathrm d}=\sup_u\lvert\operatorname{Corr}(y_t,y_{t+u})-\mu_1^u\rvert$, the distance between its autocorrelation and the geometric sequence of the slowest mode. The theorem is stated for a real matrix $A$, a noise of mean zero and covariance $Q\succ0$ that is uncorrelated across rounds, and a unit vector $\phi$, and it uses second moments only. With $\bar\lambda=\phi^\top A\phi$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}$ the norms of the blocks of $A$ that couple the complement of $\phi$ into $y$ and $y$ into the complement, and $A_{\perp\perp}$ the block on the complement, the hypothesis is the one under which the companion's theorem locates the slow mode: a power bound $\Vert A_{\perp\perp}^l\Vert\le M_\perp\alpha_\perp^l$ for every $l$, a gap $\mathsf{gap}_\perp=\bar\lambda-\alpha_\perp>0$, and $4M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}<\mathsf{gap}_\perp^2$; in addition the real simple eigenvalue $\mu_1$ near $\bar\lambda$ is below one. For the update of the members' means and the uniform vector these are the mean row sum and the dispersions of the column and row sums.

The theorem has four parts. A triangular form: in the coordinates $(y,\tilde x)$, with $\tilde x$ the deviations from the slow mode, the recursion is a cascade $\tilde x_{t+1}=A_2\tilde x_t+\mathsf G\mathfrak n_t$, $y_{t+1}=\mu_1y_t+A_{y\perp}\tilde x_t+\phi^\top\mathfrak n_t$, with $\Vert A_2^l\Vert\le M_\perp\alpha_2^l$ for a rate $\alpha_2<\mu_1$, and the difference $\operatorname{Corr}(y_t,y_{t+u})-\mu_1^u$ is an explicit convolution of the two geometric sequences. A bound: $\Xi^{\mathrm d}$ is at most the product of $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$, a factor $(1+\mathsf s_{\mathrm{row}}^2)\mathsf T_2$ with $\mathsf T_2$ a constant of the two rates, a ratio of timescales $(1-\mu_1^2)/(1-\mu_1\alpha_2)$, and a bracket that contains the covariance of the noise of the coordinate with the noise of the deviations, the tilt $\mathsf s_{\mathrm{row}}=M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}/(\mu_1-\alpha_\perp)$ of the slow mode against $\phi$, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$ times the persistence of the deviations, divided by the smallest eigenvalue of $Q$. This part needs no smallness beyond the hypothesis and stability. An a priori form: for isotropic noise and $M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{row}},M_\perp\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}\le\mathsf{gap}_\perp/4$,
$$\Xi^{\mathrm d}\le\frac{4M_\perp^2\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{row}})}{3\mathsf{gap}_\perp^2}\cdot\frac{\mu_1(1-\mu_1^2)}{1-\mu_1\hat\alpha_\perp},$$
with $\hat\alpha_\perp$ the bound of the companion's theorem on the other eigenvalues. Attainment: on a family of two coordinates with isotropic noise the isotropic bound is attained to leading order as the dispersions tend to zero, so it cannot be improved by a constant factor there. For equal column sums the coordinate is an exact first-order autoregression and $\Xi^{\mathrm d}=0$ for every noise. The theorem holds for every unit vector, extends to $-A$ for a negative slow eigenvalue, and for anisotropic noise admits either the general bound or the isotropic bound for the whitened pair. The comparison of its constant with that of the continuous-time theorem of the manuscript is made in a formal small-step limit, which is not proved as a statement in continuous time.

Two propositions show what cannot be improved. On an explicit family of two coordinates with equal row sums, column dispersion $\sigma$ and a diagonal noise of anisotropy $\upsilon$, $\Xi^{\mathrm d}=\sigma\lvert\upsilon\rvert(1-\bar\lambda^2)/(1+\sigma^2+2\bar\lambda\sigma\upsilon)$, so for anisotropic noise the Markov error is of first order in the column dispersion and no bound of the form $K\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{row}})$ holds with a constant that depends only on $M_\perp$, $\alpha_\perp$, $\bar\lambda$ and the condition number of $Q$. The mechanism is a covariance between the noise of the coordinate and the noise of the deviations, which the column dispersion feeds back one round later and which makes the coordinate a moving average of first order on top of its autoregression; a second-order statement remains available in whitened coordinates. On a family of three coordinates whose deviation block is $\alpha_\perp$ times an oblique projection of norm $M_\perp$, the powers of $M_\perp$ in the bound are attained: any bound $K(M_\perp^\beta\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}+M_\perp^\gamma\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}^2)$ for isotropic noise with $K$ depending only on $\bar\lambda$ and $\alpha_\perp$ has $\beta\ge1$ and $\gamma\ge2$, and for anisotropic noise the first-order coefficient grows linearly in $M_\perp$. The amplification is transient growth of the deviations and not slow relaxation, a difference from the continuous-time hypothesis of the manuscript, under which the semigroup of the deviations contracts.

A corollary applies the theorem to level-0 members with the exterior held fixed, that is, to the members' errors driven by their local noise alone, with diagonal noise covariance. The Markov error of the error of the average is then bounded by the column dispersion times the sum of the relative dispersion of the members' local noise variances, the tilt, and the column dispersion weighted by the persistence of the members' disagreement. For equal local noise variances and under the conditions of the a priori form with $\bar\lambda+\varrho_\perp<1$, $\Xi^{\mathrm d}\le\frac{16}{3}M_\perp^2\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{row}})\mathsf{gap}_\perp^{-2}(1-\bar\lambda)/(1-\alpha_\perp-\varrho_\perp)$, where $\varrho_\perp$ is the radius of the companion's theorem within which $\mu_1$ lies. Because the local noise variance of a level-0 member depends on its gains, equal variances are the exception and the Markov error is in general of first order in the column dispersion. A world innovation common to the members can be kept, with a modified variance factor. With this theorem, the discrete-time counterparts of the kernel bound, the location of the slow mode and the Markov error are available without reversibility for one coordinate under a power bound on the deviation block.

## What is not established

Relative to the list of the companion note, the following items are now settled under the hypotheses named. The combination of the approximate meta-agent theorem with violated blanket conditions exists for the canonical partition, per-channel noise, a stable joint process and members that run the consensus filter, with the members' channels as the record and the law of the exterior given those channels as the reference. A singular information matrix of the sensors is covered when the declared drift is detectable through it. Level-0 members have a counterpart of the belief property for scalar members with exact code models, channel noise specific to the receiver, a Gaussian start and a stable network update. The Markov error of the average under a non-reversible update is bounded with the exterior held fixed, for a stable update under the power bound and the gap condition. A bound closed in the parameters of the network exists when no agent is outside the collective, under $\lvert\bar\lambda\rvert<1$, mixing of the deviations and the small-gain condition.

The following remain, by the statements of the manuscript. Under violated blanket conditions no bound on the leak of the coarse blanket through the two defects and the insufficiency is proved, and no bound on the information term that is uniform in the window and vanishes with the defects; the cap by the insufficiency holds only when (B1) and (B2) alone are present, $m_0$ is deterministic and the record contains the coarse blanket path. A bound for the excess relative to a coarse record that omits the insufficiency term is not given, and no proof decides whether that excess tends to zero when the acts carry to the coarse record a part of what the members' channels hold. The declared models are not optimized and the least excess over them is not determined. Members that do not run the consensus filter, noise correlated between the sensory channels and the exterior, a singular noise covariance of the summary or of the declared observation model, a declared observation model with memory, and starts in which the exterior is not Gaussian given the initial collective variables are outside the theorem. All closed forms for beliefs concern the limit; the bounds that hold at every round depend on the start, and none is uniform in the round. For the level-0 belief, the bounds take second moments of the outside senders' errors as data when members hear outside senders, the exact gain and predictive variance are not expressed through the dispersions, the sharp constant for the colored drivers is not known, a bound for the excess against the coarse record that is not also a bound for the excess against the members' inputs is not given, and channel noise shared between a member and an outside listener, inexact code models, vector-valued states, a start that is not Gaussian and performative acts are not treated. For the Markov error, the hypothesis is a power bound and not the precision-based contrast of the manuscript, the joint form for several blocks is open, no bound in a finer measure of non-normality than $M_\perp$ is given, and the case in which the exterior responds is open; applied to the whole network the hypothesis excludes a slow exterior in two cases. From the companion's list, the lower bound on the closure defect is still proved only for channel noise specific to the receiver, members with vector-valued states have no memory bound, the constants of the filter bound are not sharp, and everything is linear and Gaussian. The open problem of the manuscript on an approximate form of the meta-agent theorem is narrowed and not closed.

## Relevance to this research

These results extend the error analysis of the meta-agent of the program, begun in [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]], to the cases that a collective of real agents presents: a coarse sensory state that is not a sufficient summary of the members' channels, sensors and an exterior that do not respect the coarse [[Markov Blanket]], and members that fuse messages literally. They say what a parent of [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] can still be credited with when the blanket conditions fail, namely an exactly closed internal summary and an exact group posterior, and against which law its belief should be measured. They connect [[Coarse Graining]] and filtering ([[kalman-1960-approach-linear-filtering]], [[olfati-saber-2005-distributed-kalman-filter]]) to the notion of a sufficient summary ([[Sufficient statistics]]). The synthesis page is [[Approximate meta-agent theorem]].
