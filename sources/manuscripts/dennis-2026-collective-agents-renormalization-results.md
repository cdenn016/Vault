# Communication, Coherence and Collective Agents: results on renormalization across levels

## Source and scope

The live source is the supplement section of additional results on renormalization of the manuscript *Communication, Coherence and Collective Agents*: statements in [s10c_renormalization.tex](../../manuscripts/Collective_Agents/sections/s10c_renormalization.tex) and proofs in [app10c_renormalization.tex](../../manuscripts/Collective_Agents/sections/app10c_renormalization.tex), together with the conjecture "Scale up, rank down" in [s08_scale.tex](../../manuscripts/Collective_Agents/sections/s08_scale.tex). This note records, at the author's request of November 11, 2026, the theory of four statements of that section and of the conjecture as reworded: the error of the memoryless elimination of internal states, finite-round consensus on contributions across the levels of a hierarchy, the decay of decimated kernels on $\mathbb Z^D$, and an example in which the effective rank is not monotone along a relay. It records statements, hypotheses and limits only. The numerical checks that accompany these results in the manuscript, and the rest of the section, are not recorded here. The manuscript is a working draft and its statements may change.

## Setting

All four results belong to the linear-Gaussian framework of the manuscript. A population of agents has a world-conditional precision $\Pi$ that is diffusive, a leak $\varepsilon^\Pi$ on the diagonal plus a coupling Laplacian with couplings $a^\Pi$, and renormalization means passing to fewer variables: by decimation, which keeps a sublattice and marginalizes the rest, by block averaging, by eliminating internal states, or by letting the units of a hierarchy speak through aggregates or representatives. The main theorems of the section give the cases in which such a step is exact. The statements recorded here treat four places in which it is not. Two of them settle former items of the section's open problem under stated hypotheses, one narrows a current item, and one forces a rewording of a conjecture.

## Error of the memoryless elimination of internal states

The setting is a population of particles with state $x=(x_{\mathrm{ret}},x_{\mathrm{int}})$, the internal states and all others, evolving as the linear diffusion $\mathrm dx=-Mx\mathrm dt+\sqrt2\mathsf N\mathrm d\mathsf B_t$, with every eigenvalue of $M$ in the open right half plane and $\mathsf N$ block diagonal over the particular partition, the setting of [[friston-2023-path-integrals-particular]]. Let $\Sigma$ be the stationary covariance, $\mathsf C_{\mathrm{ir}}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{ir}}\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}^{-1}$ the regression of internal on retained states and $x_\perp=x_{\mathrm{int}}-\mathsf C_{\mathrm{ir}}x_{\mathrm{ret}}$ the residual. A proposition that precedes the new one says what the elimination of internal states drops. The retained states obey the drift $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}=M_{\mathrm{rr}}+M_{\mathrm{ri}}\mathsf C_{\mathrm{ir}}$ plus the term $-M_{\mathrm{ri}}x_\perp$, and the residual, which is uncorrelated with the retained states at stationarity, is a state with dynamics of its own and is not noise. The retained states therefore obey exactly a generalized Langevin equation with the memory kernel $\Gamma_{\mathrm F}(u)=M_{\mathrm{ri}}e^{-M_{\perp\perp}u}M_{\perp\mathrm r}$, the equation of the Mori projection ([[mori-1965-transport-collective-motion]], [[zwanzig-1961-memory-effects]]) with $x_\perp$ as the complement. The elimination keeps the drift $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}$ and replaces the residual term by white noise independent of the past of the retained states, so it drops the kernel, an initial-state term and the color of the force. The retained process is Markov with a transition law that does not depend on the initial law if and only if $M_{\mathrm{ri}}=0$, which fails for every particle whose active states read its internal states.

The new proposition bounds what is dropped and, under a separation of timescales, determines it to first order. The memoryless reduction is the stationary diffusion with drift $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}$ and a noise intensity $\mathsf G_{\mathrm F}$; $\mathsf R(u)$ is the exact stationary autocovariance of the retained states and $\mathsf E_{\mathrm F}(u)=\mathsf R(u)-e^{-\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}u}\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}$ is the error of a reduction with the stationary covariance $\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}$. The hypothesis throughout is that the noise factors $\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}$ and $\mathsf N_{\mathrm i}$ are invertible. First, the noise is forced: $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}+\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}^\top=2\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}^\top$, so $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}$ is stable, the reduction has the stationary covariance $\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}$ if and only if $\mathsf G_{\mathrm F}=\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}^\top$, the noise intensity of the retained states themselves, and the reduced autocovariance then agrees with the exact one in its value and its right derivative at lag zero. Second, the error is exact and bounded for every stable drift, with no asymptotic parameter: $M_{\perp\perp}$ is stable, and
$$
\mathsf E_{\mathrm F}(u)=\int_0^ue^{-\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}(u-w)}\int_0^w\Gamma_{\mathrm F}(w-v)\mathsf R(v)\mathrm dv\mathrm dw,\qquad\sup_{u\ge0}\frac{\Vert\mathsf E_{\mathrm F}(u)\Vert}{\Vert\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\Vert}\le\tau_{\mathrm F}\gamma_{\mathrm F},
$$
where $\tau_{\mathrm F}=\int_0^\infty\Vert e^{-\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}w}\Vert\mathrm dw$ is the relaxation time of the reduction and $\gamma_{\mathrm F}=\int_0^\infty\Vert\Gamma_{\mathrm F}(w)\Vert\mathrm dw$ is the mass of the kernel that it drops. The bound takes norms inside both integrals and is not sharp.

Third, the rate. Let the internal rows of the drift and the internal noise intensity be multiplied by a speed factor $\mathsf s\ge1$, so that the noise matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf N_{\mathrm r},\mathsf s^{1/2}\mathsf N_{\mathrm i})$, and let $M_{\mathrm{ii}}$ and the quasi-static drift $\mathsf A_\infty=M_{\mathrm{rr}}-M_{\mathrm{ri}}M_{\mathrm{ii}}^{-1}M_{\mathrm{ir}}$ both be stable. Then there are $\mathsf s_0$ and $\mathsf c'_{\mathrm F}$, depending on $M$ and $\mathsf N$ only, such that for $\mathsf s\ge\mathsf s_0$ the scaled drift is stable, $\tau_{\mathrm F}\gamma_{\mathrm F}\le\mathsf c'_{\mathrm F}/\mathsf s$, and $\sup_{u\ge0}\Vert\mathsf E_{\mathrm F}(u)-\mathsf s^{-1}\mathsf E_1(u)\Vert\le\mathsf c'_{\mathrm F}/\mathsf s^2$, with $\mathsf E_1(u)=\int_0^ue^{-\mathsf A_\infty(u-w)}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}e^{-\mathsf A_\infty w}\mathrm dw\Sigma^\infty_{\mathrm{rr}}$ and a matrix $\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}$ that is explicit in the solutions of two Lyapunov equations, one for the quasi-static drift and one for the internal block. The relative error over any set of lags, multiplied by $\mathsf s$, therefore converges to a finite coefficient $\mathsf c_{\mathrm F}(\mathcal U)$, the supremum of $\Vert\mathsf E_1(u)\Vert/\Vert\Sigma^\infty_{\mathrm{rr}}\Vert$ over that set, and the first-order expansion of the error is uniform over all lags. The manuscript reads the first-order term as follows: $\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}/\mathsf s$ is the integral of the dropped kernel to leading order, so at this order the exact autocovariance relaxes with the drift $\mathsf A_{\mathrm F}-\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}/\mathsf s$, and the reduction misses this correction of the drift by the memory while it keeps the stationary law.

Fourth, the order of the rate is a property of the instance. $\mathsf E_1$ vanishes identically if and only if $\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}=0$, and then the error is of order $\mathsf s^{-2}$; if $M_{\mathrm{ri}}=0$ the error vanishes for every $\mathsf s$. In the reversible family $M=\Pi$, symmetric and positive definite, with $\mathsf N=I$, one has $\mathsf Z_{\mathrm F}=\Pi_{\mathrm{ri}}\Pi_{\mathrm{ii}}^{-2}\Pi_{\mathrm{ir}}\Sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}^{-1}$ and the trace of $\mathsf E_1(u)$ is positive for $u>0$ unless $\Pi_{\mathrm{ri}}=0$, so the rate is exactly of first order whenever a retained state is coupled to an internal one. Without reversibility the coefficient can vanish although $M_{\mathrm{ri}}\ne0$: with one retained and one internal state this happens when $\mathsf N_{\mathrm i}^2M_{\mathrm{ri}}=2\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}^2M_{\mathrm{ir}}$. Fifth, the scaling of the noise matters. If the matrix $\mathsf N_{\mathrm i}$, and not the intensity $\mathsf N_{\mathrm i}\mathsf N_{\mathrm i}^\top$, is multiplied by $\mathsf s$, the internal fluctuation acts on the retained states as white noise of order one, the exact autocovariance converges to that of a diffusion with drift $\mathsf A_\infty$ and an enlarged noise intensity, and the memoryless reduction, which keeps the intensity $\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}\mathsf N_{\mathrm r}^\top$, has its drift forced to a different limit. If $M_{\mathrm{ri}}\ne0$ its relative error then tends to a positive limit as $\mathsf s\to\infty$.

The first-order rate had first been a numerical observation on random instances, in line with what the expansions in the ratio of timescales of [[vankampen-1985-elimination-fast-variables]] and [[geigenmuller-1983-elimination-fast-variables]] lead one to expect; the manuscript cites these two works from their abstracts. The proposition proves the rate under its hypotheses. The manuscript states that the instances behind the observation were not tested against those hypotheses, so the observation is not a verified case of the proposition.

## Finite-round consensus on contributions across levels

A proposition that precedes the new one treats hierarchies with unlimited rounds. If every level runs literal consensus on posted beliefs, a regular hierarchy counts the common prior once per agent, as flat literal consensus does, and believed precisions diverge at every level; with unequal groups the group whose graph has the largest spectral radius dominates the top-level belief exponentially in the number of lower-level rounds, whatever its information, so persuasion bias ([[demarzo-2003-persuasion-bias-social]]) multiplies across levels. If instead every level runs consensus on information contributions and passes exact sums upward, with the common prior added once at the top, the top-level belief is the union posterior for every hierarchy: the information form is closed under hierarchy and the posterior form is not.

The new proposition removes the assumption of exact sums. The $N$ agents are the units of level $0$; the units of level $l-1$ are partitioned into the units of level $l$, with a single unit at level $L$. Each unit $J$ of level $l\ge1$ has $n_J$ children, a symmetric, doubly stochastic, entrywise nonnegative consensus matrix $\mathcal W_J$ on them whose spectral radius off the consensus direction is $\rho_{\perp,J}<1$, and, below the top, one representative. Agent $j$ enters with its information contribution $(h^s_j,\Lambda^s_j)$. At level $l$ the children of each unit run $n_{\mathrm c,l}$ noiseless rounds of consensus on the pairs they hold and multiply by $n_J$, and the unit enters the next level with the estimate of its representative. The children $I$ of the top unit add the common prior once. The proposition has four parts.

Counts. Every child $I$ of the top unit holds $h_I=\Lambda_\circ m_\circ+\sum_j\mathfrak c_{Ij}h^s_j$ and $\Lambda_I=\Lambda_\circ+\sum_j\mathfrak c_{Ij}\Lambda^s_j$, where the count $\mathfrak c_{Ij}\ge0$ is the product over the levels of $n_J$ times the entry of $\mathcal W_J^{n_{\mathrm c,l}}$ that links the representative of the unit containing $j$ (at the top level, $I$ itself) to the child containing $j$. If all units of each level have the same number of children, the counts of each $I$ sum to $N$. Errors compound multiplicatively. With $\mathsf d_l=\max_J(n_J-1)\rho_{\perp,J}^{n_{\mathrm c,l}}$ over the units of level $l$ and $\mathfrak k_L=\prod_{l=1}^L(1+\mathsf d_l)-1$, every count satisfies $\lvert\mathfrak c_{Ij}-1\rvert\le\mathfrak k_L$, and the bound is attained in a family of hierarchies with equal units and consensus matrices of the form $\rho_lI+(1-\rho_l)\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/n_l$. The recursion behind it, $\mathfrak k_l=(1+\mathsf d_l)\mathfrak k_{l-1}+\mathsf d_l$, says that each level amplifies the error inherited from below by $1+\mathsf d_l$ and adds $\mathsf d_l$. Belief. The precision obeys $\max\{0,1-\mathfrak k_L\}\Lambda^s\preceq\Lambda_I-\Lambda_\circ\preceq(1+\mathfrak k_L)\Lambda^s$, so every such belief is proper, the mean obeys the exact identity $m_I-m^\ast=\Lambda_I^{-1}\sum_j(\mathfrak c_{Ij}-1)(h^s_j-\Lambda^s_jm^\ast)$ relative to the union posterior mean $m^\ast$, and if $\mathfrak k_L<1$ the relative entropy from the belief of $I$ to the union posterior $p^\ast$ is at most
$$
\frac{\mathfrak k_L^2}{(1-\mathfrak k_L)^2}\Bigl[\frac{\dim\eta_C}4+\frac12\Bigl(\sum_j\bigl\Vert h^s_j-\Lambda^s_jm^\ast\bigr\Vert_{(\Lambda^\ast)^{-1}}\Bigr)^2\Bigr].
$$
Dynamic world. When the hierarchy runs the protocol within every world step and each top-level child applies the steady-state member filter to its miscounted sum, its deviation from the same recursion driven by the exact sum obeys a stable linear recursion forced by $\sum_j(\mathfrak c_{Ij}-1)h^s_j(t)$, and is bounded, in mean square and pathwise, by $\mathfrak k_L$ times a constant of the closed-loop filter times the supremum of the summed sizes of the contributions.

To keep $\mathfrak k_L$ below a target $\mathfrak k_\star\le1$ it suffices that $\mathsf d_l\le\mathfrak k_\star/(2L)$ at every level, that is $n_{\mathrm c,l}\ge\ln(2L(n_J-1)/\mathfrak k_\star)/\ln(1/\rho_{\perp,J})$ rounds for every unit with at least two children and $\rho_{\perp,J}>0$. The manuscript notes two properties of flat consensus that are lost. The counts of a flat consensus sum to $N$ for every member, and the proposition guarantees this in a hierarchy only for equal numbers of children per level. The average of the members' estimates obeys the coarse filter pathwise in a flat consensus for every number of rounds, and in a hierarchy with $L\ge2$ it inherits the errors of the representatives of the lower levels: its input equals the exact sum for all channel readings if and only if the product of the count factors below the top level equals one for every agent whose sensor carries information. The information form itself adds no error; with exact sums the counts are one and the earlier statement is recovered. The bound on the relative entropy takes norms term by term and is not claimed to be sharp; the identity for the mean is exact.

## Decay of decimated kernels on $\mathbb Z^D$

On $\mathbb Z$ decimation closes on nearest-neighbor precisions: a theorem of the main text gives the decimated coupling and leak in closed form, with the mass $m$, $\cosh m=1+\varepsilon^\Pi/(2a^\Pi)$, multiplied by the block factor $b$ and the ratio of leak to coupling obeying a Chebyshev recursion whose fixed points are the unstable massless point and the attracting decoupled point. On $\mathbb Z^2$ the nearest-neighbor class is left after one step. An earlier observation of the section, from computations on finite tori, concerned the sign and the decay of the couplings that decimation generates there; it is numerical and is not recorded here. The new proposition proves the sign and the exponential rate.

Let $\Pi$ be the nearest-neighbor precision on $\mathbb Z^D$, $D\ge2$, with $\varepsilon^\Pi>0$ and $a^\Pi>0$, let $b\ge2$, let $\Pi^{\mathsf{Dec}}(x')$ be the entry of its decimation onto $b\mathbb Z^D$ at the coarse displacement $x'$, and let $\Pi^{\mathsf{Dec}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(n)$ be its sum over the hyperplane $\{x'_1=n\}$. For transverse indices $k_\perp\in\{0,\dots,b-1\}^{D-1}$ define $m(k_\perp)>0$ and a rational function by
$$
\cosh m(k_\perp)=1+\frac{\varepsilon^\Pi}{2a^\Pi}+\sum_{d=2}^D\Bigl(1-\cos\frac{2\pi k_d}b\Bigr),\qquad\mathsf H(z)=\sum_{k_\perp}\frac{\sinh bm(k_\perp)}{\sinh m(k_\perp)\bigl(\cosh bm(k_\perp)-z\bigr)},
$$
so that $m(0)=m_1$ is the axis rate of the fine field, and let $m_\ast$ be the next smallest value. The proposition has five parts. The function $\mathsf H$ has exactly one zero in each gap between consecutive poles and no other; with $\mu_i$ the inverse hyperbolic cosines of these zeros in increasing order, the hyperplane sums at $\lvert n\rvert\ge2$ are an explicit combination of the exponentials $e^{-\mu_i\lvert n\rvert}$ with coefficients of one sign. The leading rate satisfies $bm_1<\mu_1<bm_\ast$, with an explicit lower bound on $\cosh\mu_1-\cosh bm_1$, and as the leak tends to zero at fixed coupling $bm_1$ tends to zero while $\mu_1$ has a positive limit. For $b=2$ and $D=2$ there is one zero, $\cosh\mu_1=\frac12[(\varepsilon^\Pi/a^\Pi+4)^2-6]$, the hyperplane sums are a single exponential, and $e^{-\mu_1}\to5-2\sqrt6$ as the leak vanishes. Every entry $\Pi^{\mathsf{Dec}}(x')$ with $x'\ne0$ is nonpositive, and for $\Vert x'\Vert_\infty\ge2$ it is bounded in absolute value by the hyperplane sum at distance $\Vert x'\Vert_\infty$, hence by a constant times $e^{-\mu_1\Vert x'\Vert_\infty}$; the rate cannot be improved, since no larger rate bounds all couplings. The partial Fourier transform of the kernel in the transverse directions has the same structure at every transverse momentum, with rates that are all at least $\mu_1$, and the couplings along each coordinate axis are negative from distance two on and satisfy $\lim_nn^{-1}\ln(-\Pi^{\mathsf{Dec}}(n\mathbf e_d))=-\mu_1$. For the block factor two on $\mathbb Z^2$ the symbol of the decimated precision is given in closed form, the transverse rate obeys $\cosh\mu_1(p_\perp)=\cosh\mu_1+1-\cos p_\perp$ with $p_\perp=0$ its only minimizer modulo $2\pi$ and a nondegenerate one, and the couplings on the two axes carry the factor $n^{-1/2}$ with its constant,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/2}e^{\mu_1n}\bigl(-\Pi^{\mathsf{Dec}}(n\mathbf e_d)\bigr)=\frac{16a^\Pi}{\mathsf x_\Pi}\coth\frac{\mu_1}2\Bigl(\frac{\sinh\mu_1}{2\pi}\Bigr)^{1/2},\qquad\mathsf x_\Pi=\frac{\varepsilon^\Pi}{a^\Pi}+4.
$$

Since decimating $l$ times with factor $b$ is one decimation with factor $b^l$, the proposition covers every level of the flow of the nearest-neighbor precision. The manuscript's reading is that the couplings of the decimated precision are governed by the zeros of the aliased covariance symbol and not by its poles. The hyperplane sums of the covariance of the retained sites decay at the rate $bm_1$ per coarse step, which vanishes with the leak, while the hyperplane sums of the couplings decay at the rate $\mu_1$, which bounds every coupling and stays positive at the massless point, as the picture of a walk killed on the retained lattice suggests.

## Effective rank along a relay and the reworded conjecture

The setting is the abstraction subsection of the scale section. A receiver with prior covariance $P$ that reads a code moves its mean by a gain-code map, and with a common prior metric each noiseless hop is the orthogonal projection $P_{V_j}$ onto the subspace $V_j$ that the code transmits. A theorem of that section shows that cyclic and quasi-periodic relays of fixed codes converge geometrically to the projection $P_\cap$ onto the common ground $V_\cap=\bigcap_jV_j$, the classical setting of alternating projections ([[halperin-1962-product-projection-operators]], [[kayalar-1988-error-bounds-method]], [[badea-2012-rate-convergence-method]]). For independent, identically distributed random hop maps with $\mathbb E\ln^+\Vert A_m\Vert<\infty$ the Lyapunov exponents $\Gamma_1\ge\Gamma_2\ge\cdots$ of the path product exist ([[furstenberg-1960-products-random-matrices]], [[oseledets-1968-multiplicative-ergodic-theorem]]), and $\Gamma_1>\Gamma_2$ for invertible maps whose generated semigroup is strongly irreducible and contracting ([[guivarc-h-1985-frontiere-furstenberg-proprietes]], [[gol-dsheid-1989-lyapunov-indices-product]]); the manuscript cites these results at the level of their records and restatements. The effective rank of the content shared across $n$ hops is the number of singular values of the path product above a fixed fraction of the largest.

The conjecture as now worded says that in relay networks with diversity of codes or frames the effective rank is nonincreasing in the hop distance when the codes share a direction, and tends to the multiplicity of the top Lyapunov exponent of the path cocycle when the singular values that belong to that exponent remain comparable, generically one; a collective of linear size $L$ would then share content of rank about the number of exponents whose gap to the top one is at most $\ell/L$, with a length $\ell$ that the passage does not define. The paragraph after it states where this is known. The limit statement holds for fixed codes in a common metric that share a direction, with limiting rank $\dim V_\cap$, and for independent invertible hop maps under the cited conditions, with rank one. For fixed codes in a common metric the monotonicity clause holds when $V_\cap\ne\{0\}$ and can fail when $V_\cap=\{0\}$, where the limit of the effective rank can also differ from the multiplicity of the top exponent.

A proposition of the renormalization section treats meta-level hops that apply a convex combination $\bar P=\sum_j\mathsf w_jP_{V_j}$ with positive weights, as when each member of the receiving meta-agent relays one member of the sending one and the content is averaged. The map $\bar P$ is symmetric with $0\preceq\bar P\preceq I$, fixes exactly $V_\cap$, and has rank $\dim\sum_jV_j\ge\max_j\dim V_j$. Along a stationary relay of such hops the ratios of singular values are $(\lambda_k(\bar P)/\lambda_1(\bar P))^n$, so the effective rank at any relative threshold is nonincreasing in $n$, and $\Vert\bar P^n-P_\cap\Vert=\lambda_\ast^n$ with $\lambda_\ast<1$ the largest eigenvalue of $\bar P$ on $V_\cap^\perp$. The conjecture therefore holds for stationary averaged relays in a common metric, with limiting rank $\dim V_\cap$ when $V_\cap\ne\{0\}$. In the reading that meta-agents communicate at lower rank than their members it fails at one hop, because averaging parallel paths raises the rank before relay depth lowers it.

The new example shows that the monotonicity clause needs the restriction to codes that share a direction. For $0<\mathsf o<1$, in $\mathbb R^3$, let $\mathbf w=\mathsf o\mathbf e_2+(1-\mathsf o^2)^{1/2}\mathbf e_3$, $\mathbf w'=-(1-\mathsf o^2)^{1/2}\mathbf e_2+\mathsf o\mathbf e_3$ and $\mathbf v=\mathsf o\mathbf e_1+(1-\mathsf o^2)^{1/2}\mathbf w'$, and let the hops be the orthogonal projections onto $V_1=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2)$, $V_2=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf w)$ and $V_3=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf w,\mathbf v)$, in this order. The three planes have no common direction. The path products after one, two and three hops have the nonzero singular values $(1,1)$, $(1,\mathsf o)$ and $(\mathsf o,\mathsf o)$, so at every relative threshold in $(\mathsf o,1)$ the effective rank is $2$, $1$ and $2$. In the cyclic continuation the product after $3(n+1)$ hops has the nonzero singular values $\mathsf o^{2n+1}\mathfrak y_n$ and $\mathsf o^{2n+1}/\mathfrak y_n$ with $\mathfrak y_n=\frac12[(4+n^2(1-\mathsf o^2)^2/\mathsf o^2)^{1/2}+n(1-\mathsf o^2)/\mathsf o]$. Both singular values have the same exponential rate $\frac23\ln\mathsf o$ per hop, while their ratio is at most $1/((1-\mathsf o^2)^2n^2)$ from six hops on, so at every relative threshold the effective rank is one from some hop on. That limit is neither $\dim V_\cap=0$ nor the multiplicity of the top Lyapunov exponent, which is two. The manuscript calls the instance degenerate in this respect: the product over one cycle acts on $V_3$ as a Jordan block with a double eigenvalue, so the two singular values share their exponent while their ratio tends to zero. The limit clause of the conjecture claims the rank one only generically, and where the top exponent is not simple it presupposes that the singular values belonging to it remain comparable, which is why that condition now appears in the wording.

The absence of a common direction is necessary for the failure: if $V_\cap\ne\{0\}$ every path product fixes $V_\cap$ and has largest singular value one, and since $\sigma_k(P_VA)\le\sigma_k(A)$ for every projection, the effective rank at a relative threshold is nonincreasing. What fails without a common direction is monotonicity at a fixed relative threshold, because a hop can shrink the largest singular value by more than the second. Two monotone statements remain true for noiseless hops in a common metric: the singular values themselves do not increase, so the number of singular values above a fixed fraction of the largest singular value of the first hop is nonincreasing, and the effective rank at a relative threshold is nonincreasing along stationary averaged relays. The monotonicity clause could therefore also be read with an absolute threshold.

## What is not established

For the elimination of internal states, the first-order rate is proved only when the noise factors are invertible, $M_{\mathrm{ii}}$ and the quasi-static drift are stable, and the internal noise intensity is scaled with the speed factor; stability of the drift at speed factor one does not imply the two stability hypotheses. The non-asymptotic bound is not sharp. Three further steps of the reduction of a particular partition add errors that the propositions do not cover: the asserted independence of one particle's internal states from the blankets of others, the projection onto the slow eigenmodes of each blanket, and the neglect of correlations among the induced fluctuations. The instances behind the earlier numerical observation of the rate were not tested against the hypotheses of the proposition.

For consensus across levels, the proposition assumes symmetric doubly stochastic consensus matrices, one representative per unit, noiseless rounds and the information form. It gives no statement for noisy rounds, for several representatives, or for directed or unbalanced weights, and its bound on the relative entropy is not sharp.

For decimated kernels, the proposition proves the exponential rate uniformly in the distance $\Vert x'\Vert_\infty$ and along the coordinate axes. It does not prove the correction to the exponential outside the block factor two on $\mathbb Z^2$: for the other block factors and dimensions a factor $n^{-(D-1)/2}$ is a numerical observation in the cases computed, and Laplace's method would give it if the transverse momentum zero were the only minimizer of the transverse rate and a nondegenerate one, of which only the minimizer property is proved for $b\ge3$ or $D\ge3$. The rate along directions other than the axes is not determined, though for $b=2$ on $\mathbb Z^2$ the closed-form symbol reduces it to the asymptotics of a nearest-neighbor covariance on the coarse lattice, a reduction that the manuscript has not carried out. Kernels beyond nearest neighbors are open: the proofs use that the symbol on the axis is a positive combination of terms $1/(Z-\cos p_1)$ with $Z>1$, which has to be checked case by case and fails for a staggered pole when $b$ is odd.

For the rank conjecture, the limit clause is open for networks with many paths, where path products add and ranks can rise, for correlated or learned codes, for heterogeneous priors, where the contraction itself can fail, and for non-invertible pure relays that do not reduce to invertible factors of the code rank, for instance relays whose code ranks differ along the path. The manuscript gives no proof of how typical the failure of monotonicity is among relays without a common direction.

The open problem of the section on renormalization beyond the exact cases keeps six items: (a) a definition of contrast for the non-diffusive laws that block averaging produces; (b) discrete-time closure for non-reversible updates jointly for several blocks; (c) the correction to the exponential decay of decimated kernels in two and more dimensions beyond the one proved case, their rate along other directions, and the rate for kernels beyond nearest neighbors; (d) baselines of nested media embedded in lattices beyond one instance; (e) the characterization of the regions that Laplacian renormalization ([[villegas-2023-laplacian-renormalization-group]]) returns, beyond two equitable blocks and the nested medium, and the limit statement of the rank conjecture for networks with many paths and heterogeneous metrics; (f) a bound on the error of nonlinear and discrete renormalization such as that of renormalizing generative models ([[friston-2025-pixels-planning]]). The manuscript states that the partial results of its section on approximate meta-agents settle none of these items, and that two former items are settled under hypotheses by the propositions on the memoryless reduction and on consensus across levels. Everything recorded here is linear and Gaussian.

## Relevance to this research

These results quantify four things that a theory of levels built from agents has to control, in one tractable class. The construction of [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] pools constituents into a parent and repeats the step scale by scale; the consensus proposition says how miscounting compounds when the pooling at each level is finite, and the propositions on literal reading say why the pooled object should be an information contribution and not a posterior ([[Decentralized Bayesian inference]]). The elimination of internal states is the step by which a particle is replaced by its blanket in renormalization-group readings of the free-energy principle, and its error is now a computable first-order quantity with a stated dependence on the noise convention ([[Coarse Graining]], [[Geometric singular perturbation theory]]). The decay of decimated kernels says that the exactly decimated nearest-neighbor medium on $\mathbb Z^D$, $D\ge2$, has couplings that decay exponentially at every level of the flow, at a rate that keeps a positive limit as the leak tends to zero ([[Renormalization group flow]]). The example on effective rank limits the claim that abstraction, the loss of shared rank with scale, is monotone ([[Effective rank along relays]]). The companion note on the approximate meta-agent results of the same manuscript is [[dennis-2026-collective-agents-approximate-meta-agent]], with its synthesis at [[Approximate meta-agent theorem]]. None of these statements concerns the gauge-theoretic free-energy functional or its implementation.
