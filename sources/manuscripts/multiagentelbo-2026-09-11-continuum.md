## Confirmed results and boundaries

The native critical matter construction takes a specified square-lattice sequence with growing plaquette penalty. Defect probability vanishes and the defect-free sector, with a root sign, maps to the published free critical Ising field. Its normalized order parameter has the cited non-Gaussian continuum limit and domain-dilation exponent. Gauge flux disappears. This is separate from the high-temperature basin.

A different dual sequence retains a finite flux-point process with count generating function $Z(Hz)/Z(H)$. Overdispersion is a positive tilted-magnetization variance term. Wilson parities survive at positive $H$ and actual native $H$-Fisher convergence has explicit derivative assumptions. Flux is empty at $H=0$: this is a near-critical family, not a nontrivial-flux critical fixed point. The cluster construction gives a joint matter/flux law rather than multiplying separately correct marginals.

Other declared families include the finite-jump Z2 interval, Gaussian-curvature U(1) heat-kernel loop laws, compact connected-group planar refinement with ordered products/Haar bridges, and a non-Gaussian gauge encoding of the critical Ising field. The compact inverse limit does not automatically provide a distributional gauge potential. Mesh and topology hypotheses remain explicit.

The non-Gaussian scale law whitens covariance through a fractional transform while retaining non-Gaussian dependence and recoverable matter. Uncorrelated disjoint increments need not be independent. Full-plane examples have rational scale actions on specified common carriers. Domain-changing equality in law, an inverse-limit action and arbitrary continuous scale are different assertions.

Correlated cylinder tilts and normalized sensors give exact VFE within each experiment. Generating nested observations recover full KL monotonically and DQM Fisher through conditional-score convergence under the stated domination/pointwise conditions. Finite quadrature checks do not prove those limits. These families are not one unspecified physical model.
