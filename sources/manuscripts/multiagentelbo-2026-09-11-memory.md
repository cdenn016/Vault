## Confirmed results and boundaries

The R5 observation layer computes complete word probabilities, posterior parent states and next-record predictions. Impossible histories retain zero evidence and undefined conditionals. The parent-sensitive mechanism changes residence-time distributions using local bit flips/merges/splits followed by conditional refresh of the unchanged marked target. It does not establish a physical persistence advantage.

The finite-memory law $Q^{(k)}$ uses exact rows conditional on at most $k$ preceding observations. It preserves the appropriate initial prefixes and retained windows, with forward-KL loss equal to discarded conditional information. Exact rational rows and certified logarithms support finite bounds. Lazy evaluation queries a hidden filter rather than compiling the full alphabet-power table.

For the specified stationary source, fixed memory orders zero, one and two have positive asymptotic forward-KL loss-rate lower bounds. Exact word differences and the finite-chain ergodic theorem imply whole-path TV tends to one for those six approximations. No long simulation substitutes for that proof. Two-sided entropy-rate brackets and synchronized pair-support certificates give controlled finite-duration bounds. The primary path-mergeability theorem applies only after its hypotheses are checked. Explicit logarithmic-memory constants were obtained but are too conservative for useful compression at the tested durations.

All-preparation Markov closure requires the complete next-retained-state/record row to be constant on every source fiber. Stationary or one-time agreement is insufficient. Without closure, retain a sufficient filter or relevant history. An observed controller action may reveal hidden state; condition on it before propagation. Policies using discarded information do not automatically descend.

The formation counterexample agrees on two stationary records and differs at three; an exact hidden-state repair restores closure. Persistence must be measured at a declared informative event clock. Clock slowing alone is not evidence for improved organization.
