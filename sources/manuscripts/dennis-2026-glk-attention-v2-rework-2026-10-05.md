## What the rework states

The introduction now opens with a thesis stated as a hypothesis: neural networks are one instantiation of a more general information geometry and dynamics, non-neural systems also communicate and compute, and the long-term aim is a language architecture that is not neural; this paper builds and tests the communication layer. It positions attention as multi-agent communication (CommNet, DIAL, TarMAC, emergent-communication work) and states the lattice claim precisely: the lattice is the communication graph of agents above a single base point $c_*$, not a lattice in the Eguchi–Kawai sense; invertible links are lattice gauge data, while singular head maps are sheaf or quiver data.

Section 2 begins with the principal $\mathrm{GL}(K)$-bundle over the zero-dimensional base $\{c_*\}$ and the bundle figure (Figure 1). It separates the associated vector bundle, whose fiber $E_i$ is the sample space, from the statistical bundle, whose fiber $\mathbb R^K\times\mathrm{SPD}(K)$ carries the Fisher metric $g=v^\top\Sigma^{-1}w+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}B)$; a belief is a point of the statistical fiber. Agents carry $(q_i,p_i,U_i)$ with frame $u_i=\sigma_0U_i^{-1}$ and reference moments $\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i$, $C_i=U_i^{-1}\Sigma_iU_i^{-\top}$. Section 2.3 explains the four head maps as a 2×2 picture: the sender emits a key $S_K\xi_j$ and content $T\xi_j$; the receiver forms a query $S_Q\xi_i$, which is never transmitted, and writes received content through the receiving map $\widetilde O$. The routing weights $\beta_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_j(-D_{ij}/\tau+\log\pi_{ij})$ are the decision of whom to hear, and $S_Q,S_K$ are what that decision is computed from; none of the maps selects a coordinate subset. A new Proposition (`prop:comparison_link`) states that for square invertible $S_Q,S_K$ the routing divergence is $D_{ij}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij})_*q_j)$ with the group-valued comparison link $\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}=U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1}$; through a map of rank below $K$ the corresponding divergence is infinite, which is why partial channels compare in a query space.

Notation follows the author's choice: $L_{ij}$ is the Lie-algebra generator, $\Theta_{ij}=U_ie^{L_{ij}}U_j^{-1}$ the group link, $\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$ the flat link. Section 3 keeps the endpoint law, the low-rank identity and the split $\Theta=\Omega+U_iO\varphi_1(TO)TU_j^{-1}$ (`prop:lie_split`, flat transport plus a channel term), and adds a central theorem (`thm:routed_transport`): with tiled heads, an invertible stacked sending map, routing outside the exponent and the shared generator $L=OT$, the routed message is
$$m_i=\sum_jU_if(L)\,\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})\,U_j^{-1}\mu_j,\qquad \Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=T^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=\sum_h\beta^{(h)}_{ij}\Pi_h,$$
with $f(L)=L$, $I+L$ or $e^L$ in the affine, first-order and exponential charts and $\Pi_h$ oblique head projectors. In the exponential chart the edge map factors as $\Theta_{ij}U_j\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1}$, a routed oblique projection followed by link transport, and a hopping form $\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Theta_{ij}\mu_j$ holds when the routing is shared across heads. A standard transformer layer is the affine chart with residual coefficient $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$; at fixed routing it is the first-order chart $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ of $\exp(\mathfrak L_\beta)$ on the joint state. One paragraph answers when matrix-valued and group-valued links differ for $G=\mathrm{GL}(K)$: the endpoint law and covariance do not distinguish them; invertibility (a groupoid rather than a monoid, with inverse edges and holonomy), the reachable component $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ and the optimization geometry do. Asked whether $OT\in\mathrm{GL}(K)$ for a standard transformer, the manuscript answers that the question is misplaced: each head's $W_OW_V$ is singular, the stacked product is generically invertible but unconstrained, and the group-valued objects are $e^{OT}$ and $I+OT$. Sections 4 to 6 keep covariance and the reference normal form, Gaussian-KL routing as an exact natural-parameter dot product with its transformer reductions, and the exact source-posterior and predictive-mean status of routing and content.

Section 7 includes a parameter-accounting subsection: $K$ is the width of the evolving state, and each vocabulary entry stores $K(d_b+2)$ numbers (a mean, a variance and a block frame that multiplies queries, keys, values and outputs in every layer). Section 8.2 names what remains neural (LayerNorm, the MLP, the decoder, backpropagation). The receiver inference over the same channels and the latent link generators of [[dennis-2026-dynamic-links]] moved to the supplement (S5, S6), together with the singular boundary, the complement split, selective matrix links and induced comparison links, holonomy diagnostics, routing details, irreducible frame blocks, scope results and the numerical identity checks. The tied key-and-content variant with a Bayesian receiver is not developed in this paper; it is a separate note, [[dennis-2026-minimal-agent-model]], which the paper does not cite.
