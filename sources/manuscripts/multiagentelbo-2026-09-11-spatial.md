## Confirmed results and boundaries

The positive Z2 ladder supplies a normalized fixed-width gauge/matter size family, two exact blocking schemes, full generated actions and a limiting theorem. The flat diamond remains a separate exact interacting witness, $u'=2u^2/(1+u^4)$ with its scalar partition factor. Neither alone is a critical gauge universality theorem.

The two-dimensional family uses dressed edges $z_e=\phi_vu_e\phi_w$, factors $(1+bz_e)$ and $(1+cW_p)$, and explicit boundary conventions. For $0<b<1$, $0<c<1/12$, it constructs a unique infinite-volume law and exact geometric blocking. The early depth-dependent sufficient bound $c<1/(6B)$ is superseded within the declared construction by a depth-uniform every-exterior Gibbs theorem. All generated interactions and reference/scalar conventions are retained. Infinite exterior conditionals are constructed by limits, not by dividing by a zero-probability infinite event.

An open weighted interaction space near the fair-product law retains many-body and long-range characters. The path RG has an actual Fréchet derivative, quadratic remainder, exact two/three/six composition and a local attraction basin. A first-order character survives only if it is a union of complete retained paths; nonlinear combinations can generate further terms.

The finite-amplitude evaluator computes selected rational Taylor coefficients and bounds the complete omitted tail in the same norm. Budget exhaustion returns incomplete output. The basin-entrance proof supplies a computable depth and shrinking same-norm bound for the admitted spatial family; it does not replace the fine model by independent signs.

Geometry-dependent partition factors enter pressure and VFE limits. A term constant within a branch can still alter competition across branches, and dropping it can reverse selection. Strict-gap selection and tied branches need different hypotheses. The high-noise family is not automatically a critical, arbitrary-lattice, or random-geometry construction.
