# Finite auxiliary budget for the admitted continuous Gaussian parent

October 3, 2026. This record removes the additional finite-budget hypothesis for the exact fixed-model Gaussian candidate in [continuous-gaussian-parent-admission.md](continuous-gaussian-parent-admission.md). It proves finiteness, not a small error, a uniform regime estimate, or convergence of the finite-grid implementation. No code, experiment, test, or GPU calculation is part of this derivation.

Companion result: [continuous-grid-moments.md](continuous-grid-moments.md) supplies the separate uniform-integrability argument for the exact-arithmetic implementation on deterministic grids enriched by the independent proposal clock. It gives L1 convergence to the fixed-probe random limit and convergence of expected totals to this continuous budget. It supplies no quantitative finite-grid bias, floating-point error bound, or small retained-law error.

## Target and assumptions

Fix a finite configuration dimension d, a finite assignment set A, a full-row-rank retained projection P, a constant positive-definite diffusion covariance Q, and finitely many frozen affine sheet drifts F_j x+f_j. The native law starts from the actual Gaussian activation N(mu_0,Sigma_0), with Sigma_0 positive definite and the specified independent initial assignment. A rate-one proposal clock draws a complete assignment from the normalized product of the positive quadratic-softmax receiver rows; only assignment changes are visible. The source supports, positive prior weights, positive temperature, receiver order, local hidden coordinate maps, and finite positive moment-exact cubature rules are fixed. These are the assumptions of the admitted candidate and the [continuous parent budget](continuous-parent-error-budget.md), rather than new restrictions on its coefficients.

Let R=PX and X=TR+Zz, with T=P^T(PP^T)^{-1} and orthonormal columns Z spanning ker P. The candidate message (m,S) is initialized by the actual Gaussian conditional and is driven by the native retained history (R,J), with the exact continuous Riccati propagation and uncut sequential source updates in the admission record. Invisible self-proposals change neither its mean nor its covariance. In particular, the message does not receive an observation of a self-proposal time.

For every such fixed model and every finite horizon t_*, the claim is

$$
B_{t_*}=E_P\int_0^{t_*}\left[
\frac12\|Pv_{J_t}(X_t)-\bar v_{J_t}(R_t,m_t)\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2
+\sum_{a\ne J_{t-}}\psi\bigl(b_a(X_t),\widehat b_a(R_t,m_t,S_t)\bigr)
\right]dt<\infty,
 Q_r=PQP^T,
\tag{1}
$$

where psi(x,y)=x log(x/y)-x+y. All characteristics inside an integral use predictable pre-jump values. The consequence is the exact continuous-law entropy identity already proved conditionally in [conditional-characteristic-error.md](conditional-characteristic-error.md), not equality of the finite-grid experiment to this continuous parent.

The user-supplied scope remains the regular-filter problem at fixed c_* in the live Chapter 11, def:rg-regular-finite-filter and open:rg-pointwise-parent-dynamics, and SPEC section 0. That problem asks for small path-law error uniformly over specified windows and separately over growing histories. Finiteness at each fixed model and horizon is strictly weaker. No bound below is asserted to be uniform in network size, hidden dimension, temperature, regime parameter, or horizon.

The proof combines deterministic bounds on every cubature update with a Lyapunov bound for the native-driven continuous prediction error. An ordinary Euclidean Gronwall estimate for the gain can produce an exponential of an exponential in the Poisson count and does not prove integrability. The adjugate weight below controls covariance contraction at a source jump without such an estimate.

## Bounds for the declared cubature coordinates

For one receiver, let H_i be its declared fixed full-row-rank map of the hidden coordinate, and let C=H_i S H_i^T=LL^T with the declared Cholesky factor L. The admission record's local basis convention is the case H_i=V_i^T. The proof also allows other fixed independent local rows, including physical-coordinate rows. It does not replace those rows by an orthogonal basis: finite Cholesky cubature need not be invariant under such a change, so that replacement could define a different candidate.

Define

$$
B=SH_i^TL^{-T}.
\tag{2}
$$

Then BB^T=SH_i^T(H_iSH_i^T)^{-1}H_iS is at most S in positive-semidefinite order. Congruence by S^{-1/2} turns the middle expression into the orthogonal projection onto the columns of S^{1/2}H_i^T. All inverses in (2) exist because S is positive definite and H_i has full row rank.

Let the fixed standardized cubature nodes be xi_l, and put q_i=max_l |xi_l|^2. For any source label, its strictly positive normalized cubature weights w_l define

$$
\bar\xi=\sum_lw_l\xi_l,

\Xi=\sum_lw_l(\xi_l-\bar\xi)(\xi_l-\bar\xi)^T.
$$

They satisfy |bar xi|<=sqrt(q_i) and 0<=Xi<=q_i I, independently of how small the label probability is. The Gaussian update is exactly

$$
m^+=m+B\bar\xi,

S^+=S-BB^T+B\Xi B^T.
\tag{3}
$$

With r_i=max(1,q_i), equations (2)-(3) give

$$
S^+\preceq r_iS,

|m^+-m|\leq\sqrt{q_i}\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}S}.
\tag{4}
$$

The admission proof establishes S^+ positive definite: positive weights on affinely spanning nodes make Xi positive definite, and the two positive-semidefinite summands in (3) cannot share a nonzero null vector. The proof requires no likelihood-weight lower bound uniform over the state space. For a receiver without hidden-dependent logits, take r_i=1 and q_i=0 because no update occurs. For the symmetric rank-r rule with nodes plus or minus sqrt(r) times the coordinate vectors, q_i=r.

There are finitely many stages in a complete source update. Put

$$
\rho=\prod_i r_i,

h_0=\sum_i\sqrt{q_i}\sqrt{\prod_{v<i}r_v}.
\tag{5}
$$

If the model uses a fixed finite catalog of local rules, take maxima of these constants over that catalog. Applying (4) successively proves, for every target assignment and every incoming state,

$$
S^+\preceq\rho S,

|\delta|^2\leq h_0^2\operatorname{tr}S,
 \delta=m^+-m.
\tag{6}
$$

The same bounds, with constants no larger than fixed full-update constants, hold at every hypothetical prefix used to evaluate a source probability. They are uniform over labels and incoming states of this fixed model and do not require native and candidate label probabilities to agree.

## The native-driven prediction error

Assume first k=d-rank(P)>=1. On sheet j use C_j=PF_jZ, D_j=Z^TF_jZ and the admission record's noise blocks Q_z and Q_zr. Define

$$
A_j=D_j-Q_{zr}Q_r^{-1}C_j,
 V_j=C_j^TQ_r^{-1}C_j,
 Q_0=Q_z-Q_{zr}Q_r^{-1}Q_{zr}^T.
\tag{7}
$$

The Schur complement Q_0 is positive definite. The matrices A_j and V_j range over a finite set, V_j is positive semidefinite, and Q_0 is constant. Put e=z-m. Subtracting the candidate's native-driven mean equation from the hidden native equation cancels the affine terms and retained-coordinate forcing. Between visible jumps,

$$
de=(A_j-SV_j)e dt+dM,
 d\langle M\rangle_t=(Q_0+SV_jS)dt,
\tag{8}
$$

$$
\dot S=A_jS+SA_j^T+Q_0-SV_jS.
\tag{9}
$$

Equation (8) retains the correlated observation noise: M is the hidden transformed native noise minus (SC_j^T+Q_zr)Q_r^{-1} times its retained noise. Expanding its covariance gives Q_0+SV_jS. Neither independence of these noises nor equality of S to the true conditional covariance is used.

Set W=I+S, H=W^{-1}, and E=e^THe. Since W>=I, one has 0<H<=I. Ito differentiation, using (8)-(9) and the finite variation of S between jumps, gives

$$
\mathcal L_c E
=e^T\left[H(A_j+A_j^T+V_j-Q_0)H-V_j\right]e
+\operatorname{tr}(HQ_0)+\operatorname{tr}(HSV_jS).
\tag{10}
$$

In particular, the terms containing V_j combine by SH=HS=I-H to give -V_j+HV_jH. Since H^2<=H and SHS<=S, finite model-dependent constants give

$$
\mathcal L_cE\leq c_1E+c_2(1+\operatorname{tr}S).
\tag{11}
$$

Equation (11) alone does not control a source update that makes S much smaller. Define instead

$$
D(S)=\det(I+S),
 L(e,S)=D(S)E=e^T\operatorname{adj}(I+S)e.
\tag{12}
$$

An adjugate eigenvalue is the product of k-1 eigenvalues of W, all at least one. Therefore

$$
L(e,S)\geq |e|^2,
 D(S)\leq(1+\operatorname{tr}S)^k.
\tag{13}
$$

This includes k=1, for which adj(W)=1. Jacobi's determinant identity and (9) give

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\log D(S)
=\operatorname{tr}((I-H)(A_j+A_j^T))
+\operatorname{tr}(HQ_0)-\operatorname{tr}(HSV_jS)
\leq c_3.
\tag{14}
$$

Here I-H is positive semidefinite and at most I, and the final trace is nonnegative before its minus sign. Multiplying (10) by D and adding the finite-variation product term from (14) introduces no quadratic covariation. Thus

$$
\mathcal L_cL\leq c_4L+c_5(1+\operatorname{tr}S)^{k+1}.
\tag{15}
$$

These calculations invert I+S, whose eigenvalues are bounded below by one. They do not assume inverse moments of S.

## The adjugate controls source jumps

If W_1,W_2 are positive definite and W_2<=rho W_1 with rho>=1, then

$$
\operatorname{adj}(W_2)\preceq\rho^{k-1}\operatorname{adj}(W_1).
\tag{16}
$$

Indeed let B=W_1^{-1/2}W_2W_1^{-1/2}. Its eigenvalues lie in (0,rho], so every eigenvalue of adj(B)=det(B)B^{-1} is at most rho^{k-1}. Congruence in the identity

$$
\operatorname{adj}(W_2)
=\det(W_1)W_1^{-1/2}\operatorname{adj}(B)W_1^{-1/2}
$$

proves (16). No uniform lower bound on the eigenvalues of B is needed.

At a visible native assignment change, z stays fixed and e^+=e-delta. Equation (6) implies W^+<=rho W. Applying (16), the quadratic inequality |a-b|_M^2<=2|a|_M^2+2|b|_M^2 for positive-semidefinite M, and (6), gives

$$
L(e^+,S^+)\leq2\rho^{k-1}L(e,S)
+2h_0^2\rho^{k-1}(1+\operatorname{tr}S)^k.
\tag{17}
$$

For the second term use norm(adj(W^+))<=(1+tr S^+)^{k-1}, tr S^+<=rho tr S, and |delta|^2<=h_0^2 tr S. This bound remains valid when the update contracts an eigenvalue to an arbitrarily small positive value. Self-proposals are identity updates.

Put s=1+tr S, p=k+1, and

$$
\mathcal V(e,S)=L(e,S)+s^p.
\tag{18}
$$

Taking the trace of (9) gives dot s<=c_6s, because tr(SV_jS)>=0. At a source jump, s^+<=rho s. Combining these facts with (15) and (17) gives finite fixed-model constants c_c,c_J such that

$$
\mathcal L_c\mathcal V\leq c_c\mathcal V,
\mathcal V(e^+,S^+)\leq c_J\mathcal V(e,S)
\tag{19}
$$

for every visible target assignment. Under the native law, the sum of visible intensities is 1-b_J(X)<=1. The full native-driven generator, including the message resets, consequently satisfies

$$
\mathcal L\mathcal V\leq c\mathcal V,
 c=c_c+\max(c_J-1,0).
\tag{20}
$$

This uses the actual native probabilities. Candidate labels are not assumed to have generated the observed history.

## Localization and moment bounds

Stop the joint native state and message on compact subsets that bound |X|, |m|, tr S, tr S^{-1}, and the visible jump count. The stops tau_n increase to infinity almost surely by the already proved nonexplosion and preservation of positive covariance. Before each stop the continuous coefficients are bounded. There are finitely many continuous target maps, so their images of each incoming compact set are compact as well. A jump that triggers the stop can overshoot the incoming compact set, but its image is bounded and its value is included in the stopped process. Stopped Dynkin therefore contains that terminal jump and has true martingale stochastic integrals.

The actual Gaussian conditional initialization gives deterministic S_0 and e_0 with mean zero and covariance S_0. Hence

$$
E\mathcal V(e_0,S_0)
=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(I+S_0)S_0)
+(1+\operatorname{tr}S_0)^{k+1}<\infty.
\tag{21}
$$

If an initial state is outside the stopped compact set, take its stop to be zero; its initial value remains integrable. From (20), stopped Dynkin, and nonnegativity,

$$
E\mathcal V_{t\wedge\tau_n}
\leq E\mathcal V_0+c\int_0^tE\mathcal V_{u\wedge\tau_n}du
\leq e^{ct}E\mathcal V_0.
\tag{22}
$$

Fatou's lemma at fixed t and then Tonelli's theorem give

$$
E\mathcal V_t\leq e^{ct}E\mathcal V_0,
 E\int_0^{t_*}\mathcal V_tdt
\leq E\mathcal V_0\int_0^{t_*}e^{ct}dt<\infty.
\tag{23}
$$

No equality is needed when removing the stops. Equations (13) and (23) give a finite second moment of z-m and its time integral. The native affine switching equation, whose continuous state does not jump, separately gives E|X_t|^2<=C exp(Ct)(1+E|X_0|^2) by the quadratic Ito bound and localization. Therefore R and m also have finite second moments and time integrals on every finite horizon.

The argument also gives all polynomial moments at this fixed scope. Between jumps, the continuous quadratic variation of L satisfies

$$
\frac{d\langle L\rangle_t}{dt}
=4e^T\operatorname{adj}(W)(Q_0+SV_jS)\operatorname{adj}(W)e
\leq4L\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(W)(Q_0+SV_jS))
\leq C Ls^{k+1}.
\tag{24}
$$

For any integer q>=1, Ito's formula, (15), (24), and Young's inequality show that the continuous drift of L^q is bounded by C_q[L^q+s^{q(k+1)}]. Equation (17) gives the same bound for its post-jump value, and the trace bound controls s^{q(k+1)} both between and at jumps. The preceding localization proof applied to L^q+s^{q(k+1)} proves finite moments of |e|^{2q}, |m|^{2q}, and tr S of every fixed order. Gaussian activation supplies the required initial moments, and the native affine equation supplies its corresponding moments. This is not a discretization estimate or a confidence interval for a finite numerical sample.

## Integrable source log loss at every hypothetical prefix

Each supported negative log probability in a fixed positive-temperature quadratic-softmax row has at most quadratic growth in the retained and sufficient hidden coordinates. The finitely many logits are quadratic polynomials plus fixed log prior weights, so

$$
-\log\beta_{i,a}(y;R)
=\log\sum_b e^{\ell_{i,b}(y;R)}-\ell_{i,a}(y;R)
\leq\log|S_i|+\max_b(\ell_{i,b}-\ell_{i,a})
\leq C_i(1+|R|^2+|y|^2).
\tag{25}
$$

At every hypothetical prefix, (4)-(6) bound its covariance by a fixed multiple of S and its mean displacement by a fixed multiple of sqrt(tr S). Its local node is y=H_i m_prefix+L_prefix xi_l. Since H_i and xi_l are fixed,

$$
|y|^2\leq C(|m|^2+\operatorname{tr}S).
\tag{26}
$$

Equivalently, the hidden fine-coordinate representative at a node is m_prefix+B_prefix xi_l; equation (2) bounds its displacement by a fixed multiple of sqrt(tr S_prefix), and H_i applied to that representative gives the declared y. This respects the actual local coordinate convention and all logits that depend on it.

Combining (25)-(26), every likelihood value in a positive cubature average c_i(a) is at least exp[-C(1+|R|^2+|m|^2+tr S)]. Since its weights sum to one, c_i(a) has that same lower bound. Multiplying the finite receiver rows yields

$$
\max_{a\in A}[-\log\widehat b_a(R,m,S)]
\leq C(1+|R|^2+|m|^2+\operatorname{tr}S).
\tag{27}
$$

The constant is uniform over all labels and hypothetical prefixes of this fixed model. There is no covariance floor, probability clipping, or truncation. Small probabilities remain allowed; their negative logarithms satisfy (27).

Both full mark distributions b and b-hat are normalized. The visible intensity cost obeys

$$
\sum_{a\ne J}\psi(b_a,\widehat b_a)
=D(b\Vert\widehat b)-\psi(b_J,\widehat b_J)
\leq D(b\Vert\widehat b)
\leq\max_a[-\log\widehat b_a].
\tag{28}
$$

The full mark KL bounds the visible cost; it does not equal that cost. The last inequality uses sum_a b_a log b_a<=0. Equations (23), (27)-(28), and the native second-moment bound make the jump part of (1) finite. The retained drift discrepancy is C_J e, and positive-definite Q_r and the finite collection of C_j give

$$
\|C_Je\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2\leq C|e|^2\leq C\mathcal V(e,S).
\tag{29}
$$

Equations (23) and (29) make the drift part finite, proving (1). The higher-moment argument also gives finite polynomial moments of this continuous instantaneous cost, without identifying the distribution of a discretized estimator.

If k=0, the projection retains all continuous coordinates and its inverse reconstructs X. There is no hidden Gaussian message; the retained drift and every source row are evaluated exactly. The budget is zero. This case is not used to set the direction for hidden-coordinate parent channels.

## Consequence and remaining scope

The entropy theorem in [conditional-characteristic-error.md](conditional-characteristic-error.md) now applies to this admitted candidate on every finite horizon under the frozen affine, Gaussian activation, positive quadratic-softmax, and fixed finite cubature assumptions. Its previously additional hypothesis B_t*(S)<infinity follows from the proof above. Thus B_t*=G_t*+D(P_Y||S_Y) holds at this scope. The candidate covariance still is not the true conditional covariance. The proof evaluates neither G nor an absolute numerical KL from the saved finite-grid experiment.

For related continuous Riccati and Kalman-Bucy estimates, see Bishop and Del Moral, [On the Stability of Kalman-Bucy Diffusion Processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04686), Sections 1-2. Those continuous estimates do not directly address these label-dependent cubature resets. The reset bound, adjugate calculation, and native-law localization required here are supplied explicitly above.

O1 still requires a small bound on true retained prediction cost, uniformly in its prescribed fixed-window regime and separately over growing-history horizons, together with the continuous-to-grid link needed to interpret numerical functionals. The constants proved here can grow with dimension, receiver count, inverse temperature, coefficients, and horizon; no vanishing or useful scaling rate follows. Endogenous structural feedback and a second intrinsic formation remain the separate O4 obligation. Both O1 and O4 stay open.
