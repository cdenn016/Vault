# Uniform moments and expected-budget convergence for the frozen grid

October 3, 2026. This record proves uniform polynomial moments for the exact-arithmetic frozen-grid algorithm on each fixed finite horizon. They supply the uniform integrability missing from [continuous-grid-limit.md](continuous-grid-limit.md). With a fixed number of probes, the recorded source estimate still converges to a random probe functional; its expectation converges to the continuous source budget. This is an analytical result, with no new simulation, code change, unit test, or GPU work.

## Scope and the permitted meshes

Fix the finite frozen affine switching model, positive-definite constant diffusion covariance, positive quadratic-softmax rows, Gaussian activation, fixed retained projection, and fixed uncut cubature rules in [continuous-budget-integrability.md](continuous-budget-integrability.md). The local rules are the actual ordered physical-coordinate rules identified in the grid-limit record; no orthogonal rotation of cubature nodes is made. Work on a fixed horizon $T<\infty$. All constants may depend on the model, hidden dimension, receiver count, temperature, and horizon. No uniform regime or growing-horizon estimate is claimed.

The grids in this result are deterministic grids united with the common rate-one Poisson proposal times, or more generally finite meshes measurable from that clock alone, with maximum step $h$. Conditional on the complete clock, the mesh is fixed and Brownian increments on its intervals remain independent centered Gaussians with their specified covariances. Initial variables and proposal-label uniforms are independent of this clock and of future Brownian increments. This additional restriction is needed for the conditional moment argument. The earlier pathwise convergence theorem allowed more general vanishing meshes; an arbitrary Brownian-adaptive mesh is not included in the moment theorem without a separate conditional-increment argument.

For sufficiently small $h$, the claim is a bound on every left-endpoint state of the exact-arithmetic grid, uniform over $h$ and $t\in[0,T]$, of every fixed polynomial moment of the native state, Gaussian message mean, and covariance trace. The theorem applies to the exact formulas for both dense and square-root source maps, which agree under the fixed coordinate convention. It does not establish a fixed-float64 limit or repair the historical dense-backend failure. The implementation's actual coarse/fine tape construction has the required clock-only mesh dependence. The result supplies no evaluated threshold or quantitative bias for its two recorded meshes.

Assume first that the hidden dimension $k$ is positive. Let $e=z-m$ be the actual native hidden state minus the candidate mean, $s=1+\operatorname{tr}S$, and

$$
L(e,S)=e^T\operatorname{adj}(I+S)e,

\Phi_q(e,S)=L(e,S)^q+s^{q(k+1)},
 q=1,2,\ldots.
\tag{1}
$$

As shown in the continuous-integrability record, $L\ge |e|^2$. The Gaussian initialization has finite expectation of every $\Phi_q$. This weight avoids inverse moments of $S$.

## An exact decomposition of one grid step

On a fixed sheet write the Euler step in the admission record's retained and orthonormal hidden coordinates as

$$
\Delta r=\delta(A_r r+Cz+u)+\eta_r,
$$

$$
z'=z+\delta(Er+Dz+v)+\eta_z,

\begin{pmatrix}\eta_r\\\eta_z\end{pmatrix}
\sim N\left(0,\delta\begin{pmatrix}Q_r&Q_{zr}^T\\Q_{zr}&Q_z\end{pmatrix}\right).
\tag{2}
$$

The sheet is held fixed through this interval; its new proposal label, if any, is drawn afterward. Put

$$
K_c=Q_{zr}Q_r^{-1},
A=D-K_cC, V=C^TQ_r^{-1}C,
Q_0=Q_z-Q_{zr}Q_r^{-1}Q_{zr}^T>0.
\tag{3}
$$

The Gaussian decomposition $\eta_z=K_c\eta_r+\eta_0$ makes $\eta_0\sim N(0,\delta Q_0)$ independent of $\eta_r$ and of the incoming state. Substituting the observed increment from (2) gives

$$
z'=Bz+b_\delta(r,\Delta r)+\eta_0,
 B=I+\delta A,
$$

$$
b_\delta(r,\Delta r)
=\delta[(E-K_cA_r)r+v-K_cu]+K_c\Delta r.
\tag{4}
$$

Conditioning the incoming candidate $N(m,S)$ on the linear observation $\Delta r-\delta(A_r r+u)=\delta Cz+\eta_r$ gives

$$
\bar S=(S^{-1}+\delta V)^{-1},
$$

$$
\bar m=m+\bar S C^TQ_r^{-1}
[\Delta r-\delta(A_r r+Cm+u)].
\tag{5}
$$

Equations (4)-(5) reproduce exactly the forecast-then-endpoint-condition update in `gaussian_message.py`:

$$
m'=B\bar m+b_\delta(r,\Delta r),
 S'=B\bar S B^T+\delta Q_0.
\tag{6}
$$

This is an algebraic Gaussian regression identity for the numerical candidate. The actual native hidden law need not be Gaussian or have covariance $S$. Its prediction error nevertheless obeys exactly

$$
\bar e=z-\bar m
=\bar S S^{-1}e-\bar S C^TQ_r^{-1}\eta_r,

 e'=B\bar e+\eta_0.
\tag{7}
$$

The known retained-coordinate terms cancel, and the independent residual noise is retained. The identity $I-\delta\bar S V=\bar S S^{-1}$ proves the first equation directly. This decomposition is used only for the proof; the executed code need not be rewritten in this order.

## The pure measurement step has a one-plus-step moment bound

For fixed incoming $(e,S)$ consider an auxiliary interval $a\in[0,\delta]$ with

$$
\frac{dS_a}{da}=-S_aVS_a,

 de_a=-S_aVe_a da-S_aC^TQ_r^{-1/2}dB_a,
 (S_0,e_0)=(S,e).
\tag{8}
$$

Here $Q_r^{-1/2}$ is the symmetric positive-definite inverse square root, and $B_a$ is a standard retained-dimensional Brownian motion. Since $S_a=(S^{-1}+aV)^{-1}$, direct differentiation shows

$$
e_a=S_aS^{-1}e-S_aC^TQ_r^{-1/2}B_a.
\tag{9}
$$

At $a=\delta$, this has precisely the joint law of $(\bar e,\bar S)$ in (7). The auxiliary construction holds the native old hidden coordinate fixed; it is not an additional physical dynamics or a posterior assumption.

The continuous adjugate calculation applies to (8) with $A=0$ and $Q_0=0$. Its covariance stays positive for every finite $a$ by the explicit inverse above, even though this auxiliary residual process noise is zero. In the notation of the continuous-integrability record, its drift and quadratic variation satisfy

$$
\mathcal L L\le c L+c s^{k+1},

\frac{d\langle L\rangle_a}{da}\le c Ls^{k+1},
 \frac{ds}{da}\le0.
\tag{10}
$$

For integer $q$, Ito differentiation of $L^q$, followed by
$L^{q-1}s^{k+1}\le C_q[L^q+s^{q(k+1)}]$, gives
$\mathcal L\Phi_q\le c_q\Phi_q$. Conditional on the incoming finite $S$, (8) is a linear Gaussian equation with bounded coefficients on this finite interval, so its polynomial Ito expectations are justified directly; alternatively the nonnegative localization argument in that record applies. Integrating the inequality gives

$$
\mathbb E[\Phi_q(\bar e,\bar S)\mid e,S,J,\delta]
\le e^{c_q\delta}\Phi_q(e,S).
\tag{11}
$$

The multiplier is $1+O(\delta)$, uniformly over the incoming state. A fixed factor larger than one at each grid step would not suffice.

## The affine forecast also has a one-plus-step bound

For this paragraph use $(e,S)$ for the input of the forecast alone and put

$$
W=I+S, W'=I+BSB^T+\delta Q_0,
 e'=Be+\eta_0.
\tag{12}
$$

Let $a_* =\max_j\|A_j\|$. Choose a fixed positive $h_0$ such that $h_0a_*\le1/2$, taking any finite $h_0$ when $a_*=0$, and restrict $\delta\le h_0$. Then $B$ is invertible and $BWB^T\succeq BB^T\succeq I/4$. Furthermore,

$$
W'-BWB^T
=\delta(Q_0-A-A^T)-\delta^2AA^T
\preceq\delta(\|Q_0\|+2a_*)I.
$$

Consequently, with a finite model-dependent constant $c$,

$$
W'\preceq(1+c\delta)BWB^T.
\tag{13}
$$

The adjugate-order identity proved in the continuous record gives

$$
B^T\operatorname{adj}(W')B
\preceq(1+c\delta)^{k-1}\det(B)^2\operatorname{adj}(W)
\preceq e^{c'\delta}\operatorname{adj}(W).
\tag{14}
$$

For the last inequality use $|\det B|\le\|B\|^k\le(1+a_*\delta)^k$. Thus the deterministic affine factor changes $L$ by at most $e^{c'\delta}$, including when $S$ is badly conditioned. Separately,

$$
s'=1+\operatorname{tr}(BSB^T+\delta Q_0)
\le(1+c_s\delta)s.
\tag{15}
$$

To include the additive Gaussian noise without introducing a fixed factor of two, keep $M=\operatorname{adj}(W')$ fixed and set $\xi_a=Be+Q_0^{1/2}B_a$ for $0\le a\le\delta$. Write $\ell_a=\xi_a^TM\xi_a$ and $r_M=\operatorname{tr}(MQ_0)$. The exact polynomial Ito formula gives the following expression for $q\ge2$; for $q=1$ omit its second term:

$$
\frac{d}{da}\mathbb E\ell_a^q
=q r_M\mathbb E\ell_a^{q-1}
+2q(q-1)\mathbb E[\ell_a^{q-2}\xi_a^TMQ_0M\xi_a]
\le q(2q-1)r_M\mathbb E\ell_a^{q-1}.
\tag{16}
$$

The matrix inequality used here is $MQ_0M\preceq r_M M$: conjugate by $M^{1/2}$ and bound a positive-semidefinite matrix by its trace. Young's inequality bounds the right side by $c_q\mathbb E\ell_a^q+c_q r_M^q$, with the same conclusion for $q=1$ directly. Since

$$
r_M\le k\|Q_0\|(s')^{k-1}\le C s^{k-1},
$$

integration of (16), followed by (14), yields

$$
\mathbb E L(e',S')^q
\le e^{c_q\delta}L(e,S)^q
+C_q\delta e^{c_q\delta}s^{q(k-1)}.
\tag{17}
$$

Combining (15)-(17), and increasing the constant in the exponential, proves

$$
\mathbb E[\Phi_q(e',S')\mid e,S,J,\delta]
\le e^{C_q\delta}\Phi_q(e,S).
\tag{18}
$$

There is no mesh-independent additive multiplier at each step. Applying (18) after (11), using the independent residual noise in (7), proves this same form of estimate for the complete numerical propagation-and-observation step.

## Native source resets and conditioning on the clock

The deterministic cubature bounds in the continuous record hold at numerical states as well. For every target assignment,

$$
S^+\preceq\rho S,
|m^+-m|^2\le h_*^2\operatorname{tr}S,
$$

$$
L(e^+,S^+)\le c_J L(e,S)+c_J s^k,
 s^+\le\rho s.
\tag{19}
$$

Raising to a fixed power once per source update gives

$$
\Phi_q(e^+,S^+)\le J_q\Phi_q(e,S)
\tag{20}
$$

for a finite fixed constant $J_q\ge1$. This inequality is uniform over the label chosen by the native softmax and its dependence on the just-observed native configuration. No equality between native and candidate mark probabilities is needed. A self-proposal uses the identity and satisfies the same bound.

Condition now on the complete proposal clock on $[0,T]$, with count $N_T=n$. Every mesh interval has a fixed length under this conditioning, and its Brownian increment is independent of the incoming sigma field. Iterate (18) across its intervals and (20) at its proposals. Their time lengths sum to at most $T$, so at any grid endpoint,

$$
\mathbb E[\Phi_q(e^h_t,S^h_t)\mid\text{clock}]
\le e^{C_qT}J_q^n\mathbb E\Phi_q(e_0,S_0).
\tag{21}
$$

The same bound applies to the left-endpoint state at any fixed time because its mesh index is determined by that clock. It does not require stopping at a random numerical state. Since a rate-one Poisson count satisfies

$$
\mathbb E J_q^{N_T}
=e^{-T}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(TJ_q)^n}{n!}
=e^{T(J_q-1)},
$$

we obtain

$$
\sup_{0<h\le h_0}\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\mathbb E\Phi_q(e^h_t,S^h_t)
\le e^{T(C_q+J_q-1)}\mathbb E\Phi_q(e_0,S_0)<\infty.
\tag{22}
$$

The notation takes the supremum over the declared clock-only mesh family, not over arbitrary state-adaptive grids. Enormous dimension-dependent reset constants are permitted; the assertion is finiteness and uniformity in the time discretization alone.

The native Euler state has the analogous bound on $(1+|X|^2)^q$. Each affine deterministic update changes $1+|X|^2$ by at most $1+C\delta$, uniformly over the finite sheets; the same Gaussian polynomial calculation as (16) controls its additive noise. Its continuous state does not jump at proposals. Conditioning on the clock and iterating therefore gives uniform native moments of every fixed order. Since $R=PX$, $z=Z^TX$, $m=z-e$, and $L\ge|e|^2$, equation (22) supplies the claimed uniform polynomial moments of $R,m,e$, and $\operatorname{tr}S$ as well.

## Uniform integrability of the recorded costs

Write $d_h(t)$ for the left-endpoint drift discrepancy and $c_h(t)$ for the exact finite-assignment visible source rate. The previous deterministic source-log bound applies at every numerical state and hypothetical prefix, giving

$$
0\le d_h(t)\le C|e^h_t|^2,

0\le c_h(t)\le C A_h(t),

A_h(t)=1+|R^h_t|^2+|m^h_t|^2+\operatorname{tr}S^h_t.
\tag{23}
$$

Let $G_h(t,V)$ be the actually sampled native-prefix row-KL sum minus its exact current-assignment correction. The row sum is nonnegative and bounded by $CA_h(t)$. Also, for $0<p,q\le1$,

$$
0\le\psi(p,q)=p\log(p/q)-p+q\le-\log q+1.
$$

The same source-log bound therefore gives the absolute bound

$$
|G_h(t,V)|\le CA_h(t)
\tag{24}
$$

for every prefix draw, including those for which the corrected estimate is negative. In particular (22) with $q=2$, together with native fourth moments, bounds the second moments of all rates in (23)-(24), uniformly over $h$ and $t$.

The stored drift integral is $D_h=\int_0^T d_h(t)dt$. Jensen's inequality gives

$$
\mathbb E D_h^2\le T\int_0^T\mathbb E d_h(t)^2dt\le C_T.
\tag{25}
$$

For the stored source estimate use $M\ge1$ independent uniform times and independent native-prefix draws, separate from the simulated path and mesh, exactly as in the producer:

$$
\widehat I_h=\frac{T}{M}\sum_{a=1}^M G_h(U_a,V_a).
$$

Jensen's inequality over the finite average, followed by the uniform-time expectation, yields

$$
\mathbb E\widehat I_h^2
\le\frac{T^2}{M}\sum_{a=1}^M\mathbb E G_h(U_a,V_a)^2
\le T\int_0^T C\mathbb E A_h(t)^2dt\le C_T.
\tag{26}
$$

The bound is uniform even over probe counts $M\ge1$. Consequently the exact source integral, the recorded drift integral, and the recorded total $D_h+\widehat I_h$ have uniformly bounded second moments in $h$. The same reasoning applies to the full-mark and self-correction outputs. It requires no clipping of a negative realized visible estimate.

## What converges in mean

Under the common Brownian, clock, proposal-uniform, and probe coupling, the preceding grid-limit record gives, for each fixed $M$,

$$
D_h+\widehat I_h\longrightarrow
D_0+\widehat I_0
=\int_0^T d(\mathcal Z_t)dt
+\frac{T}{M}\sum_{a=1}^M G(\mathcal Z_{U_a},V_a)
\text{almost surely}.
\tag{27}
$$

The square bounds just proved upgrade (27) to $L^1$ convergence. Explicitly, Fatou also bounds the second moment of the limit. For any cutoff $K>0$, the expectation of $\min(|Y_h-Y_0|,K)$ tends to zero by bounded convergence, while the remainder is bounded by $\mathbb E|Y_h-Y_0|^2/K\le C/K$. First let $h\downarrow0$, then $K\uparrow\infty$. This proves the claimed mean convergence without inferring it from continuous-budget finiteness alone.

Conditional expectation over the independent uniform-time and native-prefix probes gives $\mathbb E[\widehat I_0\mid\mathcal Z]=\int_0^T c(\mathcal Z_t)dt$. All terms are integrable by (23)-(26) and the continuous moment result. Hence

$$
\lim_{h\downarrow0}\mathbb E[D_h+\widehat I_h]
=\mathbb E\int_0^T[d(\mathcal Z_t)+c(\mathcal Z_t)]dt
=B_T.
\tag{28}
$$

The same result holds for exact source quadrature. At fixed $M=2$, the random variable in (27) generally remains different from the realized continuous budget integral. Its residual probe variance was identified in the grid-limit record. Equation (28) concerns expected functionals, not removal of that finite-probe randomness and not a statistical guarantee for sixteen saved paths.

For full retention, $k=0$, the hidden message is absent, the drift discrepancy and source discrepancy vanish, and the asserted bounds are immediate. This trivial case does not determine the hidden-coordinate research direction.

The strongest result is finite-horizon $L^1$ convergence of the actual idealized fixed-probe output to its continuous random-probe limit, and convergence of its expectation to the admitted continuous auxiliary budget. The proof uses the exact split of the implemented endpoint-conditioning step, the adjugate weight, and the Poisson reset count. It does not give a finite-grid bias bound, a confidence interval, floating-point stability, an absolute numerical retained-path KL, small error in the two Chapter 11 regimes, or endogenous recursive formation. Those remain separate obligations; O1 and O4 are not closed.
