# Fixed-projection approximation with unrestricted smooth filter complexity

October 3, 2026. This record gives a finite-horizon existence result for the frozen switching class with a fixed projection. A three-coordinate deterministic filter can make the retained path relative entropy arbitrarily small when its coefficient functions, density dictionary and derivative bounds may depend on the requested horizon and accuracy. Those functions can be extremely large and sharp. This establishes no efficient implementation, uniform complexity bound, formation mechanism, or result for assignment-dependent projections.

Fix a finite d, a finite assignment set A, a constant positive-definite Q, affine drifts v_j(x)=F_j x+f_j, and strictly positive quadratic-softmax product mark probabilities b_k(x) on A. The native process has continuous configuration X, diffusion covariance Q and a rate-one whole-assignment redraw clock. Only assignment changes are observed. Fix a full-row-rank projection P with hidden dimension k=d-rank(P)>0. Set

$$
R=PX, B=P^T(PP^T)^{-1}, X=BR+Zz,
$$

where the columns of Z are orthonormal and span ker P. The native initial joint law of (X,J) is supplied and has a finite fourth moment in X. It need not be a product law or Gaussian. This moment condition, fixed P and finite horizon are explicit assumptions of this theorem. All coefficients are frozen during the trajectory. The native law, retained map, assignments, initialization and noise are unchanged by the construction.

For every finite H>0 and tolerance eta>0, there is a parent S on (R,J,theta), with theta in R^3, such that theta is a deterministic retained-history functional, its initialization is continuous on each assignment stratum, its jump map is the identity, its between-jump coefficients are locally Lipschitz and time-homogeneous, and its decoded hidden density is C1 into L1(dz). It starts with the exact native retained initial law and satisfies

$$
D(P_{R,J}^{[0,H]}\Vert S_{R,J}^{[0,H]})<\eta.
\tag{1}
$$

The parent characteristics are induced by that density under the actual native drift and source channel. An unrelated auxiliary decoder is not used. The dimension three does not depend on H or eta; the coefficient functions and finite density library do.

## 1. Native moments and a predictable hidden density

Finite affine sheets have a common linear-growth bound. Ito's formula for |X|^4, stopped on compact balls, bounds its expected drift by C(1+E|X|^4). Assignment changes do not change X. Integration and Fatou give

$$
\sup_{0\le t\le H} E|X_t|^4<\infty.
\tag{2}
$$

There are finitely many proposals on each finite interval, and the affine diffusion on each intervening sheet is nonexplosive.

To identify a common hidden dominating measure, compare the fine path law with a reference having zero configuration drift, the same constant Q and full initial law, and uniform assignment redraws at rate one. On compact stops the drift change is bounded and all active source probabilities and their ratios are bounded away from zero and infinity. The stopped Brownian and counting likelihood therefore gives equivalence. Both complete laws are nonexplosive. For a full-path null event, intersect with the events that the compact stopping time equals H; stopped equivalence and their increasing union extend absolute continuity to the full finite horizon. The reverse argument also applies. This argument is for the full fine state, before projection.

Under the reference, conditional on the retained history and initial J, the hidden coordinate at t>0 is an initial conditional hidden variable plus the known correlated-noise shift and an independent Gaussian of covariance

$$
t Q_0,
Q_0=Z^TQZ-Z^TQP^T(PQP^T)^{-1}PQZ>0.
\tag{3}
$$

Future reference assignment redraws are independent of the Brownian motion. Thus the hidden conditional law has a Lebesgue density even if the supplied hidden initialization is singular. Disintegration of the absolute-continuity statement transfers this property to the native law. Apply the same disintegration on the product with an independent uniform time in (0,H), conditioning on the retained predictable sigma-field. This gives a jointly measurable predictable version nu_t(z) on native path times Lebesgue time. No density is asserted at time zero. The candidate starts with the actual retained initial law, while its initial decoded hidden density may approximate rather than equal a singular native hidden initialization; there is no initial retained entropy cost. Deterministic grid times and actual jump times have zero product measure, so choices there do not affect the integrals below.

Let

$$
\mathcal B=L^1((1+|z|^2)dz).
$$

Conditional Jensen and (2) give

$$
E\int_0^H\|\nu_t\|_{\mathcal B}^2dt
=
E\int_0^H
[1+E(|z_t|^2\mid\mathcal H_{t-})]^2dt
\le E\int_0^H(1+|z_t|^2)^2dt<\infty.
\tag{4}
$$

This is a separable Banach space; simple functions on rational boxes form a dense set. All density approximations below use this weighted norm, which controls hidden means as well as probability.

## 2. Finite predictable density libraries

Use refining dyadic time partitions of [0,H]. On a time bin beginning at s, retain quantized R and exact J samples at all points of that partition through s, together with the bin index. Choose nested finite spatial partitions with growing boxes and shrinking cells. Their boundary offsets may be chosen to avoid atoms of every sampled coordinate distribution: there are only countably many deterministic samples, levels and forbidden offsets. The outside-box cell is kept.

These data generate a finite sigma-field G_n on the product probability space of native paths and uniform time in [0,H]. The fields can be chosen increasing. Continuity of R, the left limits of J and density of the sampling times imply that their union generates the retained predictable sigma-field modulo product-null sets. In particular every finite retained cylinder at times strictly before t is recovered in the limit; the usual augmentation changes only null sets.

Take conditional expectation of the density kernel on this product space:

$$
q_n=E[\nu\mid G_n].
\tag{5}
$$

Each q_n is a finite table of probability densities, indexed by time bin and quantized past. Conditional Fubini preserves nonnegativity and unit integral. Cells of zero probability receive a fixed Gaussian density. The finite-moment property of every positive-probability cell follows from (4).

The convergence needed here is

$$
E\int_0^H\|q_n-\nu_t\|_{\mathcal B}^2dt\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
$$

For completeness, approximate nu in this L2 Banach norm by a finite simple function sum_i u_i 1_{A_i}, measurable in the limiting sigma-field. For each scalar indicator, conditional expectations onto the increasing G_n converge in L2: the orthogonal projections onto their increasing scalar L2 subspaces converge to the projection onto the closure of their union, which is the full limiting L2 space. The finite sum therefore converges. Conditional Jensen makes conditional expectation a contraction in L2(B), so the two simple-function approximation errors bound the remainder and prove (6).

The table densities can themselves be replaced by finite positive Gaussian mixtures. To see density of that family in B, truncate and normalize a target density on a large ball, losing arbitrarily small weighted mass. Convolve it with a narrow Gaussian; weighted translation continuity gives convergence in B. The map from the compact set of Gaussian centers into B is uniformly continuous, so replacing the convolution integral by positive cell masses at finitely many centers gives a normalized finite Gaussian mixture with arbitrarily small B error. Since each table is finite, its entries can all be approximated to any requested common tolerance. Choose these tolerances tending to zero. Equation (6) still holds for the resulting table, denoted g_n. This uses finite dictionaries of ordinary Gaussian densities rather than storing unspecified infinite-dimensional density functions.

## 3. A common positive density component controls source entropy

Fix a Gaussian density q_0 with mean m_0, and 0<delta<1. Replace every prototype by

$$
p_{n,\delta}=(1-\delta)g_n+\delta q_0.
\tag{7}
$$

The decoded conditional mean is m_{n,delta}=integral z p_{n,delta}(z)dz. For j=J_{t-}, write the projected drift and full conditional mark probabilities as

$$
a_t^*=A_jR_t+C_jm_t^*+d_j,
p_t^*(l)=\int b_l(BR_t+Zz)\nu_t(z)dz,
\tag{8}
$$

where A_j=PF_jB, C_j=PF_jZ, d_j=Pf_j and m_t^*=integral z nu_t(z)dz. The candidate uses these same formulas with p_{n,delta} in place of nu. Only l different from j is a visible channel.

For every supported complete assignment l, finite quadratic logits and positive prior weights imply

$$
b_l(B r+Zz)\ge
\exp[-C(1+|r|^2+|z|^2)]
\tag{9}
$$

with one finite constant for the fixed model. Integrate (9) under q_0 and apply Jensen to the exponential. Its second moment is finite, so after increasing a constant,

$$
b_{0,l}(r):=\int b_l(B r+Zz)q_0(z)dz
\ge e^{-C_0(1+|r|^2)}.
\tag{10}
$$

Consequently every mixed prototype, and every convex mixture of them and q_0, gives

$$
\widehat b_l(r)\ge\delta b_{0,l}(r),
-\log\widehat b_l(r)\le\log(1/\delta)+C_0(1+|r|^2).
\tag{11}
$$

This bound is independent of the number of prototypes or their moments. It is the reason a tail-localization constant need not grow with the density library.

Let psi(a,b)=a log(a/b)-a+b, with its continuous convention at a=0. For normalized full marks, the sum of visible psi terms is the full-mark KL minus the nonnegative self psi. Therefore its value is bounded above by

$$
D(p_t^*\Vert\widehat b_t)
\le\log(1/\delta)+C_0(1+|R_t|^2).
\tag{12}
$$

For fixed delta, (6) implies convergence of hidden means in L2 and of full mark vectors in L2 on native path times time: integration against bounded b_l is an L1 contraction, while the weighted norm controls means. Here is an explicit source-risk continuity bound, avoiding a subsequence restriction. Write c(p,pi;j) for the visible psi sum and D_delta(r)=log(1/delta)+C_0(1+|r|^2). On |R|<=L both mixed mark vectors have every entry at least kappa_L=delta exp[-C_0(1+L^2)]. Since the derivative of psi(p,y) in y is 1-p/y, integration of the full mark channel is an L1 contraction and gives

$$
E\int_0^H|c(p,\pi;j)-c(p,\pi';j)|dt
\le (1+\kappa_L^{-1})\sqrt H\|q-q'\|_{L^2(Pdt;\mathcal B)}
+2E\int_0^H D_\delta(R_t)1_{\{|R_t|>L\}}dt.
$$

First choose L large to control the integrable tail, then make the density approximation error small. This proves convergence along the whole sequence for fixed delta. The same estimate applies to the smooth finite-library approximants below.

The limiting density is p_delta=(1-delta)nu+delta q_0. Its full marks are (1-delta)p_t^*+delta b_0(R_t). Hence

$$
D(p_t^*\Vert(1-\delta)p_t^*+\delta b_0(R_t))
\le-\log(1-\delta).
\tag{13}
$$

Its drift cost is delta squared times a finite constant,

$$
K_H=\frac12 E\int_0^H
\|C_{J_{t-}}(m_t^*-m_0)\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2dt<\infty,
 Q_r=PQP^T.
\tag{14}
$$

For the finite-table policy, the actual conditional-characteristic risk C_H therefore satisfies

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_H(p_{n,\delta})
\le\delta^2 K_H+H[-\log(1-\delta)].
\tag{15}
$$

The result concerns true predictive characteristics. It has no candidate-independent hidden-information term.

## 4. Smooth causal realization and admission

Fix delta and a sufficiently accurate finite table in (15). Apply [the smooth finite-history lemma](smooth-finite-history-realization.md) to its finite Gaussian-mixture prototypes and q_0. A scalar clock and two alternating registers implement the table on high-probability safe quantization cells outside short capture gates. Initialization smoothly encodes the quantized (R_0,J_0). At every actual assignment change, both registers and the clock have identity jump updates. The observation paths remain continuous, and their assignment changes remain at their actual random times.

The decoder is a finite convex mixture of the prototypes and q_0 with smooth clock/code weights. It is C-infinity into B and hence C1 into L1. Every decoded density retains at least delta q_0, so (11) remains valid for all filter states. The lemma gives L2(B) convergence to the chosen table as the exceptional probability and total gate length vanish. Repeating the drift L2 and localized source-cost arguments from Section 3 shows convergence of C_H to the finite-table risk. Thus one can first choose delta, then the table, then the gate parameters so that

$$
C_H(\widehat q)<\eta.
\tag{16}
$$

The parent's retained drift is A_jr+C_j integral z q_hat_theta(z)dz+d_j, and its full mark law is integral b_l(Br+Zz)q_hat_theta(z)dz. These are the actual channels evaluated under the decoder. Means of the finite mixture library are bounded. Each integrated source rate is locally Lipschitz in r because derivatives of the quadratic-softmax law have at most polynomial growth and Gaussian-mixture prototypes have all moments. Clock and register coefficients are smooth, finite for the selected design, and autonomous in the augmented state.

The registers stay in a compact interval, the clock increases at unit speed, the retained drift has global linear growth, and the total assignment-change rate is at most one. Concatenating the unique diffusion/ODE solutions between finitely many proposals yields a unique nonexplosive parent. The register ODE is driven only by the actual retained path, so its solution is a deterministic functional of that path. A decoded hidden law has density with respect to the fixed dz coordinates on X=Br+Zz. This establishes the stated regular admission conditions for a three-dimensional filter.

## 5. From the conditional risk to retained path KL

Use the actual native retained initial law for the parent. Its auxiliary fine-lift budget is finite: projected native drift and candidate drift grow at most linearly in X and R, while (11) bounds all source negative log probabilities quadratically in R. Equation (2) supplies the required moments.

The likelihood argument in [conditional-characteristic-error.md](conditional-characteristic-error.md), sections “Predictable retained characteristics” and “Entropy equality under the finite-budget hypothesis,” applies to this parent. Its hypotheses are now explicit: same retained covariance Q_r>0 and initialization, positive visible rates, deterministic adapted internal state, local uniqueness and nonexplosion, and finite auxiliary budget. The auxiliary lift corrects the fine drift by QP^T Q_r^{-1}(u-Pv) and uses the candidate's source rates; its drift has linear growth because the finite library's means are bounded. Compact stopped Brownian/count likelihoods, uniqueness and finite-entropy localization identify the retained law. Conditional projection then removes the candidate-independent hidden-information term and yields

$$
D(P_{R,J}^{[0,H]}\Vert S_{R,J}^{[0,H]})
=
E\int_0^H
\left[
\frac12\|a_t^*-\widehat a_t\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2+
\sum_{l\ne J_{t-}}\psi(p_t^*(l),\widehat b_t(l))
\right]dt
=C_H(\widehat q).
\tag{17}
$$

The representation depends on the retained Brownian innovation and actual visible counting processes. It does not infer path TV from proximity of a grid-interpolated path. Equations (16)–(17) prove (1), and Pinsker's inequality gives TV less than sqrt(eta/2).

## 6. Consequence and remaining scope

For any specified family of finite horizons H_epsilon, choose tolerance eta_epsilon tending to zero and apply the construction separately at each epsilon. The filter dimension stays three. Its codebook, number of Gaussian components, derivative bounds, gate widths and numerical precision can depend on epsilon and H_epsilon. Every fixed-window marginal contained in [0,H_epsilon] inherits the same TV bound by projection. If a finite switching time s_epsilon is defined and H_epsilon is at least s_epsilon plus the requested window length, this controls those window placements for this fixed-projection subclass. A specified growing history horizon can also be used. No bound on computational cost follows.

The quantifier proved is “for each finite requested horizon and accuracy, there exists a parent.” It is not “one fixed coefficient set approximates every horizon.” Time homogeneity is obtained on the augmented state including the elapsed-time coordinate. A restriction excluding that coordinate, requiring a single horizon-independent design, or imposing uniform regularity would define a stronger problem. None is inserted into the live manuscript here.

Assignment-dependent projections may create continuous-coordinate jump marks with support constraints. Their approximation and conditional-density admission require a separate argument. This record also does not provide the efficient Gaussian-message predictor used in the N512/N1024 studies, a useful numerical bound for those existing budgets, a uniform large-N limit, learned structural feedback or intrinsic recursive formation. Those obligations remain. The result establishes that fixed dimension with individual smoothness alone does not bound the approximation capacity of this fixed-projection parent class.

No simulation, numerical test, GPU calculation, active-profile change or theory-manuscript edit was performed. The next substantive O1 step is to resolve the moving-projection jump kernel while preserving its exact support, and to distinguish this unrestricted existence result from an effective parent with controlled resources. O4 is unaffected.
