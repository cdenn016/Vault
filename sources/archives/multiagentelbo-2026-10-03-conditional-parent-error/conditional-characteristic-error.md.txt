# Retained-history error and the hidden-information part of the budget

October 3, 2026. One bounded analytical continuation of the existing actual-law budget. The result below is conditional on a finite auxiliary budget for a fixed finite model and horizon. It supplies an exact error identity at that scope, not a small-error estimate, an asymptotic parent theorem, or a numerical evaluation of the exact filter.

## Predictable retained characteristics

Define the native projected drift a_t=P v_{J_{t-}}(X_t) and visible fine intensity lambda_t(k)=1_{k different from J_{t-}} b_k(X_t). Their retained predictable projections are

$$
a_t^*=E_P[a_t\mid H_{t-}],\qquad
\lambda_t^*(k)=E_P[\lambda_t(k)\mid H_{t-}].
\tag{1}
$$

Here and below conditional expectations denote jointly predictable versions for dt times P almost every (t,path), rather than an arbitrary separate version at each t. Native affine growth gives E integral |a_t|^2 dt finite on every fixed horizon, and the finite visible rates are at most one.

The process R_t-R_0-integral_0^t a_s^* ds is an H martingale: testing an increment against a bounded H_s-measurable random variable reduces the claim to the fine Brownian martingale and the conditional-expectation identity. Its quadratic variation is Q_r t, so its whitening is an H Brownian motion by the continuous martingale characterization. Testing the native compensated visible counts against bounded H-predictable processes similarly gives compensators lambda_t^*(k)dt. Thus these are the actual retained characteristics even though Y need not be Markov.

In particular,

$$
\lambda_t^*(k)=1_{\{k\ne J_{t-}\}}
 E_P\left[\prod_i\beta_{i,k_i}(X_t)\mid H_{t-}\right].
\tag{2}
$$

It is generally not a product of conditional row means. Conditioning couples rows through their common hidden state and observed history. Absence of a visible jump also belongs to this history: on sheet j it weights the hidden law over dt by 1-[1-b_j(x)]dt. After normalization its contribution to the conditional expectation of a test function f is Cov(f,b_j)dt. The Gaussian candidate omits this survival update; the identity below charges the resulting prediction error.

## Conditional decomposition

Let u_t and nu_t(k) be a candidate's H-predictable retained drift and visible intensities, evaluated along the actual retained history, with the same Q_r and support. For the admitted candidate they are the drift and sequential source rates from the earlier construction. Set psi(x,y)=x log(x/y)-x+y. Use psi(0,y)=y, psi(0,0)=0, and psi(x,0)=infinity for x>0.

The auxiliary fine cost is

$$
B_T(S)=E_P\int_0^T
 \left[\tfrac12\|a_t-u_t\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2
 +\sum_k\psi(\lambda_t(k),\nu_t(k))\right]dt.
\tag{3}
$$

Both self-channel rates are zero and its summand is zero. Conditional quadratic expansion and the fact that u is H-predictable give

$$
E[\|a-u\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2\mid H]
=E[\|a-a^*\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2\mid H]
 +\|a^*-u\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2.
\tag{4}
$$

For a nonnegative intensity lambda with conditional mean l, expansion of x log x gives

$$
E[\psi(\lambda,\nu)\mid H]
=E[\psi(\lambda,l)\mid H]+\psi(l,\nu).
\tag{5}
$$

The cross term is (lambda-l) log(l/nu), whose conditional expectation is zero. One may first restrict the H-measurable l and nu away from zero, then extend by monotone restriction of H events; when l=0, lambda=0 conditionally almost surely, giving the stated conventions directly. The finite native intensity set makes lambda log lambda integrable. The resulting identity is in extended nonnegative arithmetic, without subtracting two infinite numbers.

Integrating the nonnegative terms yields

$$
\boxed{B_T(S)=G_T+C_T(S)},
\tag{6}
$$

where

$$
G_T=E_P\int_0^T
 \left[\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}
 (Q_r^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}(a_t\mid H_{t-}))
 +\sum_k\psi(\lambda_t(k),\lambda_t^*(k))\right]dt,
\tag{7}
$$

$$
C_T(S)=E_P\int_0^T
 \left[\tfrac12\|a_t^*-u_t\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2
 +\sum_k\psi(\lambda_t^*(k),\nu_t(k))\right]dt.
\tag{8}
$$

G_T depends on the native law and retained filtration, not on the candidate. Its drift part is bounded by the native second-moment integral. Its jump part is bounded by T log|A|: adding the nonnegative self channel gives the conditional information between the hidden state and a full proposed mark. Hence G_T is finite at this fixed finite scope. It may depend strongly on network size and parameters. This is a conditional prediction-information functional; no equality to mutual information of the entire fine and retained paths is asserted.

If x=Tr+Zz and a=A_jr+C_jz+f_j, then the covariance in (7) is C_j Cov_P(z_t|H_{t-}) C_j^T. The candidate's Gaussian covariance S_t is not this true conditional covariance. Subtracting its trace from the saved squared error would not evaluate G_T and need not leave a nonnegative quantity.

For normalized full marks b and q, visible cost is D(b||q)-psi(b_j,q_j). Its survival compensation is b_j-q_j. The same exclusion must be used in B, G and C. The full-mark KL may bound visible cost, but dropping the self term changes the functional.

## Entropy equality under the finite-budget hypothesis

For the admitted continuous candidate,

$$
\boxed{D(P_Y^{[0,T]}\Vert S_Y^{[0,T]})=C_T(S),
\qquad B_T(S)=G_T+D(P_Y^{[0,T]}\Vert S_Y^{[0,T]}).}
\tag{9}
$$

The following argument identifies the likelihood and its integrability. It does not infer equality merely from matching local characteristics.

First, the earlier fine-lift proof gives D(P_Y||S_Y)<=B_T(S)<infinity, so P_Y is absolutely continuous with respect to S_Y and has finite entropy. No unproved uniqueness of the true conditional filter is needed for this step.

Second, establish the martingale representation for S_Y. As a reference take the same Y_0 law, a Brownian retained coordinate with covariance Q_r, and an independent finite-state chain with rate one for each allowed transition k different from j. For smooth bounded cylinder payoffs, conditional expectations evolve between observation times by the product heat and finite-chain semigroup. Ito's formula represents each resulting martingale by the Brownian coordinate and compensated jump counts. Cylinder payoffs are dense in L2 of the canonical path sigma-field; the orthogonality and isometries of these integrators extend the representation to square-integrable martingales, with initial H_0-measurable values allowed.

The admitted candidate message is a deterministic functional of R,J. Its Riccati evolution, finite-variation integration-by-parts formula for the shared observation noise, and smooth jump maps construct it for every continuous R path and finite-jump J path, with finite mean and positive finite covariance on each compact time interval. Stop when |R|+|m|+tr S+tr S^{-1} or the jump count reaches n. These stops exhaust [0,T] under the reference and candidate. On a stopped compact set the candidate drift is bounded and every active candidate rate has a positive lower bound. Bounded likelihood exponentials relative to the reference are true martingales, as follows from the same stopped square-moment estimate used in the earlier budget proof. The stopped candidate equations have uniqueness, so the weighted reference law is that stopped candidate law. Local equivalence and the Bayes product rule transfer the reference representation to S_Y; localization gives the representation for local martingales. No claim of independent retained Brownian and count innovations under S_Y is needed.

For the unbounded density martingale, first approximate its terminal density by bounded terminal truncations. Doob's inequality gives convergence uniformly in probability of their conditional-expectation martingales, so the density is continuous between label jumps and its jumps are predictable functions of the old history and new label. Bayes' compensator identity gives jump ratio lambda^*/nu. On each candidate compact set this ratio is bounded by 1/inf nu, since native intensities are at most one. Stopping at an upper density level therefore has bounded overshoot. The bounded representations patch over these stops, with an additional positive lower density stop before logarithms or divisions.

Put Z_t=E_S[dP_Y/dS_Y|H_t]. On local intervals where Z is positive, represent its stochastic differential as

$$
\frac{dZ_t}{Z_{t-}}=
 \theta_t^T dW_t^S+
 \sum_k(\rho_t(k)-1)[dN_t(k)-\nu_t(k)dt].
\tag{10}
$$

The Brownian and count characteristic changes obtained by multiplying test martingales by Z are u+Q_r^{1/2}theta and rho nu. Uniqueness of predictable characteristics under P, proved in (1), therefore identifies theta=Q_r^{-1/2}(a^*-u) and rho=lambda^*/nu. Inactive self channels are omitted. Density-zero sets are S sets of zero P weight; localize also on positive lower and finite upper density levels before extending.

Ito's formula for f(Z)=Z log Z then has predictable compensator

$$
\int_0^t Z_{s-}
 \left[\tfrac12|\theta_s|^2+
 \sum_k\nu_s(k)(\rho_s(k)\log\rho_s(k)-\rho_s(k)+1)\right]ds.
\tag{11}
$$

The conditional split already gives C_T(S)<=B_T(S)<infinity. Bayes' rule and Tonelli turn the expected compensator in (11) into C_T(S). The compensator is an integrable increasing process, hence its stopped values are uniformly integrable. Finite terminal entropy and conditional Jensen give uniform integrability of the stopped values of f(Z): f is bounded below by -1/e and f(Z_tau)<=E_S[f(Z_T)|H_tau]. Their difference, the local martingale in Ito's formula, is consequently a true martingale. Taking expectations, with Z_0=1 because the initial laws agree, proves (9). This last step excludes an unaccounted entropy loss at removal of the stops.

The ordinary Markov activation-lift comparator also has this representation: its drift is affine in R on finitely many sheets and its positive rates are smooth and bounded. Its finite-horizon auxiliary budget is finite because source negative log rates grow at most quadratically in the native state and its affine lift, and native second moments are finite. For the Gaussian-message candidate the finite-budget condition remains explicit; the completed numerical study alone does not prove it.

## Paired risks and what can be measured

For two candidates A and B with finite budgets and matched retained initialization, (6) and (9) give

$$
B_T(A)-B_T(B)=C_T(A)-C_T(B)
=D(P_Y\Vert S_A)-D(P_Y\Vert S_B).
\tag{12}
$$

The fine-path integrand of this difference can be evaluated without the true conditional filter:

$$
(u_B-u_A)^TQ_r^{-1}a
+\tfrac12(\|u_A\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2-\|u_B\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2)
+\sum_k[\lambda_k\log(\nu_{B,k}/\nu_{A,k})+\nu_{A,k}-\nu_{B,k}].
\tag{13}
$$

Equivalently its expectation is the retained candidate log-likelihood ratio

$$
\log\frac{dS_B}{dS_A}(Y)=
\int_0^T(u_B-u_A)^TQ_r^{-1}dR
-\tfrac12\int_0^T(\|u_B\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2-\|u_A\|_{Q_r^{-1}}^2)dt
+\sum_{t:\Delta J_t\ne0}\log\frac{\nu_{B,t}(J_t)}{\nu_{A,t}(J_t)}
-\int_0^T\sum_k(\nu_{B,k}-\nu_{A,k})dt.
\tag{14}
$$

Compensation under the native law changes (14) into the expectation of (13). Finite budgets justify the expectations; in particular finite drift energies and integrable intensity log ratios follow from the nonnegative costs and bounded rates. The true hidden-information term cancels only in matched differences. A small signed difference does not bound either absolute KL.

The [declared posthoc calculation](frozen-lift-comparison-CONTRACT.md) compares the evolving Gaussian message with an activation-lift Markov parent along every saved path of the completed frozen study. Its finite-grid functionals are approximations to (3), with prefix and time-probe sampling in the Gaussian mark term. Neither (9) nor (12) turns these saved finite-grid values into continuous-time KL estimates without a discretization argument. The mesh includes invisible proposal times, which are not observations of the limiting parent. These boundaries are retained explicitly.

## Primary-source placement and remaining obligation

Jiao, Venkat and Weissman, [*Mutual Information, Relative Entropy and Estimation Error in Semi-martingale Channels*](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.05199), Lemma4 and Theorem2, give the general Bregman and mismatched-estimation relationships. Their Assumption1 imposes local uniqueness and an almost-sure deterministic energy bound. The unbounded Gaussian switching model does not automatically satisfy that bound. The conditional algebra above specializes their orthogonality principle; the reference-density argument proves the stated finite-budget application rather than importing their theorem without its hypotheses. This is continuity with established information-estimation theory, not a novelty claim for the principle.

Léonard, [*Girsanov Theory under a Finite Entropy Condition*](https://leonard.perso.math.cnrs.fr/papers/2012-Leonard-Girsanov%20theory%20under%20a%20finite%20entropy%20condition.pdf), Theorems2.4 and2.11, separately develops continuous and jump likelihood/entropy formulas and the role of uniqueness. It supports the finite-entropy approach; the mixed retained process still requires the model-specific argument above.

The sharper target for O1 is C_T(S), with a controlled small value uniformly on the required windows and on the separate growing history horizons. G_T need not vanish. No exact conditional moments, useful upper confidence radius, uniform epsilon estimate, endogenous feedback or second formation has been supplied here. The finite-dimensional regularity requirement is unchanged: naming the exact infinite-dimensional conditional law does not construct an admitted finite filter. O1 and O4 remain open.
