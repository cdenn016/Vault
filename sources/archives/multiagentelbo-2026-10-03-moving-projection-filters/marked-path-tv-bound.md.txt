# Retained marked-path total variation from native characteristic error

October 3, 2026. This record gives a finite-horizon comparison for a retained switching diffusion with assignment-dependent projections. The jump mark includes the new retained coordinate. The argument compares its exact supported transition measures, without adding noise to a coordinate jump and without assuming relative entropy between hidden conditional densities.

The source target is def:rg-regular-finite-filter and open:rg-pointwise-parent-dynamics in Research/manuscripts/MultiAgentELBO_Theory/c11_network_coarsegraining.tex, lines 1288–1346. The fixed-projection predecessor is [fixed-projection-filter-approximation.md](fixed-projection-filter-approximation.md), and the conditional-characteristic precedent is [conditional-characteristic-error.md](conditional-characteristic-error.md). The present proof supplies the path-TV step needed when coordinate jumps make the finite-mark logarithmic bound inapplicable. Baselines are code e1ac92b and Research 91f2519. It is a direct derivation; no external general marked-semimartingale inequality is imported.

## Statement and assumptions

Fix a finite horizon H and a finite set of assignment strata A. The retained state is Y=(J,R), where R belongs to R^{r_j} when J=j. Strata of dimension zero are allowed. Between jumps its continuous local martingale has covariance Q_j dt, with Q_j positive definite on each positive-dimensional stratum. There is no boundary or killing. J and R jump together to a destination mark e=(k,r') in the disjoint union of destination strata; only changes k different from j are included in the jump measure M(dt,de).

The drift convention throughout is the between-jump drift with raw, uncompensated finite-activity jumps. Embed each coordinate stratum into one fixed Euclidean space by an injection iota_j. Then the native coordinate obeys

$$
\overline R_t=\overline R_0+
\int_0^t\iota_{J_{s-}}a_s ds+M_t^c+
\sum_{s\le t}\Delta\overline R_s,
$$

where the continuous local martingale has covariance iota_j Q_j iota_j^T dt and the raw jump marks have compensator K_t(de)dt. The candidate has the same form with u and Khat. A canonical characteristic defined using compensated or truncated jumps would require the corresponding jump-compensator drift correction; it is not the convention used in (1) or the likelihood below.

Let P be the nonexplosive native retained path law in its usual natural filtration. Its predictable continuous drift is a_t, and its jump compensator is K_t(de)dt. Let S be a candidate with the same initial retained law and covariance on every stratum. A finite-dimensional internal state theta is permitted when it is a specified deterministic causal functional of the retained path. Along a native path this functional gives the candidate drift u_t and jump kernel Khat_t(de). Both total visible jump rates are at most one.

The following are the introduced comparison hypotheses. Predictable kernel versions are chosen on the canonical retained path space. For P times dt almost every history, Khat_t is absolutely continuous with respect to K_t; write r_t(e)=dKhat_t/dK_t(e). The candidate has a unique nonexplosive law from its declared initialized state, and a measurable continuation law from every stopped augmented state used below. Its coefficients and internal-state rule continue to be the same after such a restart. The native drift discrepancy has finite energy

$$
A_H=E_P\int_0^H
\|u_t-a_t\|_{Q_{J_{t-}}^{-1}}^2dt<\infty.
\tag{1}
$$

The dimension-zero stratum contributes zero to this integral. No global equivalence of P and S, martingale representation property, finite hidden-density entropy, or pointwise lower bound on the mark density is assumed.

For a signed finite measure eta, let ||eta||_var denote its full variation mass. For probability laws use TV(P,S)=sup_B|P(B)-S(B)|, which is one half of the full variation mass. Define

$$
e_H=E_P\int_0^H\left[
\frac18\|u_t-a_t\|_{Q_{J_{t-}}^{-1}}^2+
\frac12\int(\sqrt{r_t(e)}-1)^2K_t(de)
\right]dt.
\tag{2}
$$

Then the complete retained histories satisfy

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{TV}(P^{[0,H]},S^{[0,H]})
\le \min\{1,2\sqrt{e_H}+16e_H\}.}
\tag{3}
$$

In particular, writing

$$
V_H=E_P\int_0^H\|\widehat K_t-K_t\|_{\mathrm{var}}dt,
$$

one has e_H <= A_H/8+V_H/2. Thus A_H tending to zero and V_H tending to zero suffice for full-path TV tending to zero. The quantities in this assertion are expectations under the native law, rather than under an unknown intermediate law.

## 1. A stopped likelihood with integrable overshoot

Write beta_t=Q_j^{-1/2}(u_t-a_t) on a positive-dimensional stratum. The whitened continuous innovation W is defined between successive jumps; all stochastic integrals below concatenate these intervals. Its dimension may change with j. The continuous scalar martingale integral beta dot dW has quadratic variation |beta|^2dt. Independence between the continuous innovation and the jump measure is not required.

Consider the nonnegative local likelihood L with L_0=1 and

$$
dL_t=L_{t-}\beta_t^T dW_t+
\int L_{t-}(r_t(e)-1)[M(dt,de)-K_t(de)dt].
\tag{4}
$$

Equivalently, up to the first zero factor it is the continuous exponential multiplied by

$$
\exp\left(\int_0^t[K_s(E)-\widehat K_s(E)]ds\right)
\prod_{s\le t:\Delta J_s\ne0} r_s(e_s).
\tag{5}
$$

A zero jump ratio sets L to zero thereafter. Ratios are finite K-almost everywhere. Both rates are at most one, so integral |r-1|K <= 2. The integral of |r-1| against the native jump measure has finite expectation on [0,H], and hence is finite almost surely. Equation (1) gives almost surely finite continuous energy. Consequently the displayed local likelihood is well defined. It need not be a true martingale without stopping.

Let tau_e be the first time L leaves the open interval (1/4,4), and tau=tau_e min H. The exit event is B={tau_e<=H}. The jump that causes exit is included. In particular L_tau can overshoot 4 by an arbitrarily large factor; the proof does not truncate or remove that jump.

Before and at the left limit of tau, L_{t-}<=4. Therefore the continuous martingale part of the stopped equation has expected quadratic variation at most 16 A_H. Its jump integrand satisfies

$$
E_P\int_0^\tau\int
L_{t-}|r_t(e)-1|K_t(de)dt\le8H.
\tag{6}
$$

The compensated jump integral is a true integrable martingale: the expectations of the total variations of its jump sum and compensator are both bounded by the left side of (6). Together with the square-integrable continuous part, this proves that L stopped at tau is a true uniformly integrable martingale on the bounded time interval. In particular

$$
E_P L_\tau=1.
\tag{7}
$$

This establishes the needed change of probability directly. It does not use a Novikov condition or a bounded likelihood overshoot.

## 2. The square-root identity

Apply the continuous Ito formula between the finitely many visible jumps, and add the exact jump increments of sqrt(L). Before exit L is at least 1/4, so differentiation occurs away from zero. If the final jump has ratio zero, its square-root increment is simply evaluated at zero and the stopped process is constant afterward.

With

$$
h_t=\frac18|\beta_t|^2+
\frac12\int(\sqrt{r_t(e)}-1)^2K_t(de),
$$

the stopped square-root equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d\sqrt{L_t}
={}&\frac12\sqrt{L_{t-}}\beta_t^T dW_t\\
&+\int\sqrt{L_{t-}}(\sqrt{r_t(e)}-1)
[M(dt,de)-K_t(de)dt]
-\sqrt{L_{t-}}h_tdt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

All terms here stop at tau. For the continuous martingale, its expected quadratic variation is at most A_H. For the jump martingale use

$$
(\sqrt r-1)^2\le r+1,
E_P\int_0^\tau\int
L_{t-}(\sqrt r-1)^2K_t(de)dt\le8H.
\tag{9}
$$

Thus both martingale terms in (8) are square integrable. Also sqrt(L_{t-})<=2 before the stopping time. Taking expectations, and using (7), gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_H
&:=E_P(\sqrt{L_\tau}-1)^2\\
&=2E_P\int_0^\tau \sqrt{L_{t-}}h_tdt
\le4e_H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

This is a Hellinger square for the stopped change of law, derived with the exit overshoot included.

Define the probability law Q by dQ=L_tau dP on the whole native path space through H. The elementary factorization |L-1|=|sqrt(L)-1|(sqrt(L)+1) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)
&=\frac12 E_P|L_\tau-1|\\
&\le\frac12
\sqrt{D_H E_P(\sqrt{L_\tau}+1)^2}
\le\sqrt{D_H}\le2\sqrt{e_H}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

The penultimate inequality uses E_P sqrt(L_tau)<=1 and E_P L_tau=1.

On B, L_tau<=1/4 or L_tau>=4. At either extreme

$$
L_\tau\le4(\sqrt{L_\tau}-1)^2.
$$

It follows that

$$
Q(B)=E_P[L_\tau 1_B]\le4D_H\le16e_H.
\tag{12}
$$

This direct bound controls the exit under the changed probability, including arbitrarily large upward jumps.

## 3. Identifying the candidate up to exit and continuing it

The continuous martingale product identity applied to L and the retained continuous local martingale adds Q_j^{1/2}beta=u-a to the drift under Q, before and including the stopped interval. For a bounded predictable jump test f(t,e), the jump product contributes L_{t-}(r-1)f K_t(de)dt. Consequently the changed jump compensator on that interval is rK_t(de)dt=Khat_t(de)dt. These product identities, first localized and then extended by the integrability bounds above, are the local change-of-characteristics calculation needed here.

The deterministic candidate internal state is already the specified causal functional along this retained prefix. Thus under Q the retained prefix through tau has exactly the candidate augmented dynamics. There is no assertion that Q has the candidate dynamics after tau.

Sample a path under Q. If tau_e>H, retain it unchanged. Otherwise keep its prefix through tau, including any exit jump and the resulting internal state, then sample an independent candidate continuation from that augmented state. This is a measurable construction by the stated continuation hypothesis. It solves the candidate dynamics throughout [0,H]: the prefix has the candidate characteristics, and the continuation has its declared conditional transition law. There is no added jump at the joining time. Uniqueness in law identifies the resulting full path law with S.

This construction couples Q and S so that their paths agree whenever B does not occur. Hence TV(Q,S)<=Q(B). Combining (11) and (12) by the triangle inequality proves (3). No global candidate/native likelihood was required; the only probability tilt was the proven true stopped likelihood.

## 4. Application to exact moving-projection jump channels

For current assignment j, write X=B_jr+Z_jz, with P_jB_j=I and columns of Z_j spanning ker P_j. Let nu_t(z) be the native predictable hidden density and q_t(z) the decoded candidate density along the same retained history. The exact channels are

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_t(k,C)
&=\int b_k(B_jr+Z_jz)
 1_{\{P_k(B_jr+Z_jz)\in C\}}\nu_t(z)dz,\\
\widehat K_t(k,C)
&=\int b_k(B_jr+Z_jz)
 1_{\{P_k(B_jr+Z_jz)\in C\}}q_t(z)dz,
 k\ne j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

Assume nu_t and q_t are strictly positive with respect to the same hidden-coordinate measure, for P times dt almost every history, and the supported b_k are strictly positive. The measures in (13) are then mutually absolutely continuous, even if their destination-coordinate support is a proper affine subspace. No ambient Lebesgue density for the destination mark is needed. The geometric construction must establish the native strict positivity and state the common measure; existence of a hidden density alone would not imply the required reverse absolute continuity.

The latent-to-visible subprobability channel contracts full variation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat K_t-K_t\|_{\mathrm{var}}
&\le
\int\sum_{k\ne j} b_k(B_jr+Z_jz)
|q_t(z)-\nu_t(z)|dz\\
&\le \int|q_t(z)-\nu_t(z)|dz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

For affine v_j(x)=F_jx+f_j, the continuous drift difference is

$$
u_t-a_t=P_jF_jZ_j
\int z[q_t(z)-\nu_t(z)]dz.
\tag{15}
$$

Finiteness of the assignment set and positivity of each retained Q_j give a finite model constant C such that

$$
\|u_t-a_t\|_{Q_j^{-1}}^2
\le C\left(\int(1+|z|^2)|q_t(z)-\nu_t(z)|dz\right)^2.
\tag{16}
$$

Thus weighted L2 approximation of decoded densities under P times dt makes A_H tend to zero. Equation (14) and Cauchy-Schwarz in native path times time make V_H tend to zero. Finally,

$$
\int(\sqrt r-1)^2K_t(de)
\le\int|r-1|K_t(de)
=\|\widehat K_t-K_t\|_{\mathrm{var}},
\tag{17}
$$

so (2) tends to zero and (3) supplies complete retained-path TV convergence.

All comparisons in (13)–(17) use the same current retained history. The moving affine support generally prevents TV continuity of K as r itself varies. Such continuity is neither assumed nor used. Candidate existence and continuation should be constructed through the latent mark (k,z) and the exact affine update r'=P_k(B_jr+Z_jz), rather than inferred from TV-Lipschitz continuity of the pushed-forward kernel.

## Scope and remaining obligation

The result is a finite-horizon path-law comparison. It establishes no relative-entropy convergence, mixing rate, invariant distribution, Lyapunov decrease, useful computational complexity, or uniform bound as network size changes. Its error is measured for the continuous native system, not a numerical time discretization.

The [moving-projection kernel record](moving-projection-kernel.md) addresses the common strictly positive predictable hidden density and exact affine support. The [combined parent construction](moving-projection-filter-approximation.md) assembles these inputs with weighted-density approximation, smooth causal realization and candidate continuation, retaining its own horizon and initialization quantifiers. Given these hypotheses, continuous-coordinate jump marks create no additional path-TV obstruction: the supported kernel error is controlled directly by (14). No relative-entropy conclusion or resource bound follows from this lemma. No simulation, GPU calculation or test was run for this derivation.
