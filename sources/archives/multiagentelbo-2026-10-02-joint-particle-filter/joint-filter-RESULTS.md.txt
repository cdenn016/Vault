# Joint retained particle-filter study

October 2, 2026. The bounded implementation and its declared replay are complete. Both tested particle filters perform worse than the published static-lift baseline, and carrying the 64-particle history performs worse than resetting it in these cases. This phase therefore supplies an exact filtering target and an executable approximation with a recorded limitation; it does not establish a useful reduced parent or close the renormalization program.

## Construction and runtime path

The [contract](joint-filter-CONTRACT.md) derives the hidden conditional law given the complete observed history of block means and source assignments. Each predictive row is the normalized mixture of the existing global source-refresh kernel and the projected Gaussian transition. Both likelihoods enter the posterior weights. Independent multinomial ancestor draws are followed by exact Gaussian conditioning onto the next observed block means. Shared Gaussian noise in the conditional formula retains the within-block correlations; no dense network-sized covariance is materialized.

The standalone profile is experiments/gaussian_joint_filter.py. Its RUN, THEORY and NUMERICS dictionaries pass directly to run_joint_filter_study and then particle_joint_scores. The active run_lab.py selection remains neighborhood. The profile selects the first 128 saved native paths at each of N=512 and 1,024, K=2, block size 16, 64 microsteps of 0.015625 over horizon one, particle counts 16 and 64, and a 64-particle reset control. The native model seed, actual activation, rate-one global refresh and structural schedule come from the prior passing joint-parent metadata. Each path receives a separately seeded algorithmic random tape, independent of its native path. No native trajectory or learning schedule was regenerated.

The prior best lifted-source/recognition-drift baseline is evaluated at the actual next observation with its Gaussian normalizer. Its earlier analytically integrated row score cannot substitute for this pointwise mixture comparison. Every new row is scored before assimilating that row's observation; the prefix test checks this ordering.

## Measured result

All entries below are nats per complete path, mean +/- empirical paired standard error over 128 native-path/tape pairs. Positive means the named arm has larger negative log loss. Steps within a path are not independent replicates. The comparisons concern expected path KL conditional on the supplied tape, not the KL of a tape-marginalized parent. They provide no absolute retained-path KL estimate.

| 512 | filter64 | 35.5919 +/- 2.9571 | 3.3380 +/- 1.1297 |
| 512 | reset64 | 32.2539 +/- 2.8501 | reference |
| 1024 | filter16 | 97.5648 +/- 7.7971 | 16.7123 +/- 2.2580 |
| 1024 | filter64 | 85.8359 +/- 7.0453 | 4.9834 +/- 1.8801 |

Increasing the cloud from 16 to 64 reduces loss: N=512: -8.8328 +/- 1.6137; N=1024: -11.7289 +/- 2.2755. That improvement does not remove its disadvantage relative to the static-lift baseline. The reset control also underperforms that baseline. Consequently the present approximation supplies no empirical history advantage. This does not imply that the optimal conditional predictor, another approximation, or a larger cloud has the same behavior.

The global assignment likelihood is especially selective when the observed assignment changes. The following effective sample sizes are measured before ancestor resampling, only at those observed changes. An observed change is not an observation of the latent refresh coin: the kernel still includes refreshes to the same assignment.

Most rows are hold rows, so the all-step median ESS obscures this concentration. The near-one change-step ESS is evidence of severe weight concentration in these finite clouds. It is a diagnostic consistent with approximation error, not a proof that concentration alone causes the score deficit or a dimension-uniform impossibility result. Network and schedule variation, additional particle counts, independent replicate tapes per native path, and continuous-time refinement were not studied.

## Validation and retained evidence

Eight distinct tiny CPU checks pass in two disjoint JUnit packets: four block-conditioning checks and four filter/scoring checks. The independent dense Gaussian checks cover conditional mean, conditional covariance from basis noise, normalized projected density, and identity retention. The filter packet covers weighted source/observation coupling, exact native density with full retention, tape/prefix replay, and the normalized baseline against an independent multivariate-normal calculation. Final packet times are 1.819 and 3.827 seconds. Initial fixture-import/missing-API RED results and the superseded systematic-resampling check are retained as history, not counted as additional final passes. Multinomial resampling matches the finite-history consistency argument in the contract.

One declared study completed with status pass in 616.507218 seconds on CPU float64. Here pass means finite recorded quantities and conditional-projection error within the declared 1e-10 tolerance; it is not a favorable scientific outcome. The maximum projection residual was 2.77556e-16. There was no study retry, GPU workload, full suite, historical heavy replay, or active configuration edit.

[Metadata](joint-filter-evidence/metadata.json), per-case JSON, raw pointwise score/ESS arrays, [executed source archive](joint-filter-evidence/source.zip), study log and [JUnit record](joint-filter-evidence/tests/validation.json) are retained. The native input hashes and source hashes are diagnostic metadata. The saved input paths remain artifacts/gaussian-joint-parent/20261002T161127396348Z plus the activation/schedule locations named in metadata; those large predecessor inputs are local dependencies, not newly reproduced calculations. The source archive preserves the bytes executed before subsequent documentation edits.

## Remaining obligation and requested pause

The contract supplies a finite-history weak-consistency argument as particle count tends to infinity under its stated assumptions. It supplies no finite-particle error budget or uniform-integrability argument for expected log-score convergence. The empirical hidden cloud is singular relative to the continuous exact hidden law, and its direct hidden-law KL is not small. A normalized observation predictor does not cure that distinction.

This scheduled, random, fine-configuration-sized cloud has not been admitted to Chapter 11's regular finite-filter class. Absolute parent error, autonomous/endogenous structural feedback, intrinsic recursive formation and the general size/depth limit remain open. The existing O1 and O4 obligations remain the next research obligations when work resumes; no new phase is launched here. At the user's request, execution pauses after this checkpoint is ingested, committed, pushed, merged and fast-forwarded in both canonical checkouts.
