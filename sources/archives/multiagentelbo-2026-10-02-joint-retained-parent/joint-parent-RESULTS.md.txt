## Measured predictor differences

The normalized source kernel mixes one whole-network hold atom with one product redraw; refreshes that reproduce the same assignment contribute to both terms. Log-domain scoring preserves that mass. The four parents combine recognition or lifted pointwise source rows with recognition-weighted or observed-assignment configuration forces. Their initialization is the actual projected activation and independent initial assignment law. Every configuration force includes sender terms.

The conditional relative-entropy identity in the contract makes expected paired scores equal joint retained-path KL differences. Each absolute score contains an unknown candidate-independent information gap. Sample averages and empirical standard errors are neither absolute path KL nor certified upper confidence bounds.

After inspecting the first N=512 source contrast, a [posthoc fixed-mean control](joint-parent-evidence/fixed-mean-control.json) was scored on exactly the existing assignment paths. It evaluates the pointwise source posterior at the fixed activation mean, using the same moving source priors and links. This separates the contribution of observed block fluctuations from replacing a recognition expectation with a point evaluation. No path was resampled and no coefficients were refit.

The fixed-mean source already improves on recognition source by 4.480715 +/- 0.295433 nats at N=512 and 9.528724 +/- 0.487509 at N=1,024. Most of the original source contrast therefore does not isolate the value of retained configurations. The matched lifted-minus-fixed-mean contrasts show a smaller additional improvement in these samples.

For this static activation lift, conditioning the drift formula on the observed assignment worsens its score in all six cases. Observing information cannot worsen the optimal predictor, but a particular plug-in formula can. These results do not rule out assignment-aware filtering; they expose a limitation of this simple lift, which ignores how assignment history changes the hidden conditional distribution.

Both the source and configuration contrasts use the same trajectories and source schedules within each network. There is one seeded graph/link instance per N. The results do not establish graph-ensemble generality, a size limit, a causal effect of learning, or superiority over every parent. The six joint output laws differ by projection dimension, so their absolute information gaps need not agree. They also differ from the earlier configuration-only, eight-observation law.
