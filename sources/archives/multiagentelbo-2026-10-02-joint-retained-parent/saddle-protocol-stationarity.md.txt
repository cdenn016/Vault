# Stationary comparison for the same capped saddle-selection protocol

October 2, 2026. Bounded round 3 of the fixed-energy recursive candidate. This is an analytical comparison for each fixed even N>=512 and fixed K. It changes no model, simulator, initial-condition requirement, or manuscript. The conclusion is a failure of the declared protocol's universal nonequilibrium-surplus route, not a negative solution of the general hierarchy problem.

## Exact target, sources, and scope

Fix the temperature-independent coefficients, pair partition, vectors d_u,d_v, sufficiently small k>0, and all event tolerances of [recursive-formation-construction.md](recursive-formation-construction.md) and [recursive-handoff-experiment.md](recursive-handoff-experiment.md). The [nonlinear exit result](nonlinear-saddle-exit.md) supplies its uniform two-branch and finite-continuation probabilities. DIRECTION, the current STATUS entry, these three full records, live SPEC R14/R15, Chapter 12 sec:full-graph-vfe-target, and Chapter 13 def:rg-formation-cycle-experiment and the same-event comparison in prop:rg-formation-endogenous-cycle(a) were consulted. The repository checkpoint supplied for this round is b114476 and the vault checkpoint is f3be90c; neither is changed here.

The actual process is the reflected diffusion on S=[0,1] x R^{D_u+D_v+D_c}, with D_u=mK, D_v=(m-1)K, D_c=K and m=N/2,

$$
dZ_t=-M_N\nabla E_N(Z_t)dt+\sqrt{2TM_N}dB_t+dL_t,
\qquad M_N=\operatorname{diag}(N^{-1},I,I,I),
$$

$$
E_N=N W(a)+\tfrac12|u-(1-a)d_u|^2
       +\tfrac{k}{2}|v-(1-a)d_v|^2+\tfrac12|c|^2,
\qquad W(a)=a^4-\tfrac{11}{5}a^3+\tfrac{13}{10}a^2.
$$

B is standard Brownian motion, T tends to zero, and L is inward reflection at a=0,1. The stationary probability is rho_T proportional to exp(-E_N/T). No discretization is involved. All constants below may depend on this fixed N and the fixed coefficients. No joint N,T limit or uniform finite-temperature threshold is asserted.

The claim proved is this: there exists a fixed smooth probability density mu, supported in a small full-dimensional neighborhood of the unformed minimum, with finite second moment and independent of T, for which the laws of all declared post-entrance protocol outcomes under mu and rho_T have total variation distance tending to zero. This includes E_1, E_0, F, their joint indicators, and the retained trajectory measured relative to saddle entrance. The same first-entrance, exit deadline, handoff, and future-window rules are used on both sides.

This does not assert total variation convergence of the original full paths including their initial states, the pre-entrance histories, or the absolute waiting times. Those are not the observables in the declared E_1/E_0/F events. Earlier history remains part of the actual path; it is not erased physically. The first waiting cap is included and controlled explicitly below.

## 1. A strongly convex comparison energy agreeing on the low strip

Put s=1/10 and extend W on its existing domain [0,1] by

$$
W_-(a)=\begin{cases}
W(a),&0\le a\le s,\\
W(s)+W'(s)(a-s)+\tfrac12W''(s)(a-s)^2,&s<a\le1.
\end{cases} \tag{1}
$$

This function is C^2, its derivative is Lipschitz, and

$$
W_-''(a)\ge W''(s)=7/5,
\qquad W_-(0)=W_-'(0)=0. \tag{2}
$$

Indeed W''(a)=12a^2-(66/5)a+13/5 decreases from 13/5 to 7/5 on [0,s]. Define E_- by replacing only W with W_- in E_N. This is a comparison process used in the proof, not a change to the candidate. It agrees with E_N for every u,v,c whenever a<=s.

Use whitened coordinates y=M_N^{-1/2}Z, with domain D=[0,sqrt(N)] x R^{NK}, and write F_-(y)=E_-(M_N^{1/2}y). Its unique minimum is y_0=(0,d_u,d_v,0). The only coupled Hessian block, in the parallel unit directions, is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}W_-''+1+k&1&k\\1&1&0\\k&0&k\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For a vector (q,p,r), its quadratic form is W_-'' q^2+(p+q)^2+k(r+q)^2. Since

$$
q^2+p^2+r^2\le5q^2+2(p+q)^2+2(r+q)^2,
$$

all Hessian eigenvalues, including transverse matter coordinates, are at least

$$
\lambda=\min\{7/25,1/2,k/2\}>0. \tag{3}
$$

The Hessian is also globally bounded above on D. Thus the reflected comparison diffusion has a globally Lipschitz drift, normal reflection on a convex domain, and a normalized stationary density rho_-T proportional to exp(-F_-/T). Its minimum is unique. Integration by parts gives zero stationary flux; local Lipschitz coefficients and the quadratic moment bound give a unique nonexplosive reflected solution.

## 2. Elementary contraction and one-unit total variation smoothing

Couple two comparison solutions X,Y synchronously. Monotonicity of normal reflection on the convex domain gives

$$
\langle X-Y,dK_X-dK_Y\rangle\le0.
$$

Strong convexity therefore gives the pathwise estimate

$$
|X_t-Y_t|\le e^{-\lambda t}|X_0-Y_0|. \tag{4}
$$

A short drift-change argument converts this Wasserstein contraction into total variation. For deterministic initial points x,y, run X naturally from x and drive Y from y by the same Brownian motion with the additional adapted drift

$$
v_t=\frac{X_t-Y_t}{1-t},\qquad 0\le t<1,
$$

up to their meeting, and zero thereafter. The distance estimate is now

$$
|X_t-Y_t|\le(1-t)e^{-\lambda t}|x-y|,
\qquad |v_t|\le e^{-\lambda t}|x-y|. \tag{5}
$$

Construct the reflected solutions first on each [0,1-epsilon]; (5) provides a continuous limit at 1 with X_1=Y_1. The added drift stays bounded. With theta_t=v_t/sqrt(2T), the exponential

$$
\exp\left[-\int_0^1\theta_t\cdot dB_t
                 -\tfrac12\int_0^1|\theta_t|^2dt\right]
$$

has mean one because theta is bounded. Under that density, B plus the time integral of theta is Brownian and Y has its original reflected equation started at y. The relative entropy of the controlled underlying law against this changed law is

$$
\tfrac12 E\int_0^1|\theta_t|^2dt
 =\frac1{4T}E\int_0^1|v_t|^2dt
 \le\frac{|x-y|^2}{4T}.
$$

Passing to the terminal Y marginal and using the entropy inequality TV(P,Q)<=sqrt(D(P||Q)/2) yields

$$
\|P^-_1(x,\cdot)-P^-_1(y,\cdot)\|_{TV}
\le\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt{8T}}. \tag{6}
$$

Here TV is sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|. The bounded-control density, covariance factor 2T, and reflection law have all been retained; no log-Sobolev theorem is needed.

At stationarity, applying the comparison generator to |y-y_0|^2 and using the nonpositive reflection term gives

$$
E_{\rho_{-T}}|y-y_0|^2\le Td/\lambda,
\qquad d=NK+1. \tag{7}
$$

For any fixed compactly supported mu, couple its start with a rho_-T start, use (4) until time t and then (6), and integrate. This gives

$$
\|\mu P^-_{t+1}-\rho_{-T}\|_{TV}
\le\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\sqrt{8T}}
\left(E_\mu|y-y_0|+\sqrt{Td/\lambda}\right). \tag{8}
$$

Take

$$
t_T=1+\lambda^{-1}\log(1/T).
$$

The right side of (8) is O(sqrt(T)). Mixing is established for the comparison diffusion on this specified logarithmic time, not assumed for the bistable original diffusion.

## 3. The actual process stays in the agreement region during mixing

Fix R=s sqrt(N) and 0<R_0<R/2. Choose mu to be any smooth density supported in a small ball contained in {0<a<s, |y-y_0|<R_0}; such a full-dimensional ball exists. This law is fixed independently of T and has all moments finite.

For the comparison process put r=|y-y_0| and V_T=exp(eta r^2/T), where 0<eta<=lambda/2. Direct differentiation gives

$$
\frac{{\cal L}_-V_T}{V_T}
\le2\eta d+(-2\eta\lambda+4\eta^2)r^2/T
\le2\eta d. \tag{9}
$$

The reflection contribution is nonpositive: at the lower face r has zero normal derivative, and at the upper face the reflection points inward. Stopping the nonnegative local supermartingale exp(-2 eta d t)V_T and localizing yields, for the first radius-R exit tau_R,

$$
P_\mu^-(\tau_R\le t)
\le\exp\left[2\eta d t-\eta(R^2-R_0^2)/T\right]. \tag{10}
$$

At t=t_T this is a power of 1/T times exp(-c/T), hence tends to zero. The a-coordinate cannot reach s without r reaching R. The original and comparison equations agree until that strip exit and can be coupled with exactly the same Brownian motion. Consequently

$$
\|\mu P_{t_T}-\mu P^-_{t_T}\|_{TV}=o(1),
\qquad P_\mu\{\tau_{G_T}\le t_T\}=o(1). \tag{11}
$$

The saddle ball G_T is disjoint from the low strip for small T.

The stationary densities rho_T and rho_-T have exactly the same conditional density on A={a<=s}, since their energies agree there. Their a-marginals are proportional to exp(-NW(a)/T) and exp(-NW_-(a)/T). The common normalization integral on [0,s] is bounded below by c_N sqrt(T). On a>=s,

$$
W(a)=a^2[(a-11/10)^2+9/100]\ge9s^2/100,
\qquad W_-(a)\ge(7/10)a^2.
$$

It follows that both probabilities of A^c are at most C_N T^{-1/2} exp(-c_N/T). The identical conditional law on A therefore gives

$$
\|\rho_T-\rho_{-T}\|_{TV}=o(1). \tag{12}
$$

Combining (8), (11), and (12) proves for the actual bistable process

$$
\delta_T:=\|\mu P_{t_T}-\rho_T\|_{TV}\longrightarrow0. \tag{13}
$$

An early saddle hit is negligible also from rho_T. To check the path claim rather than infer it from stationary occupation, note that strong convexity and the bounded Hessian give

$$
\rho_{-T}\{|y-y_0|>R_0\}\le C_Ne^{-c_N/T}.
$$

For this bound, use F_->=lambda r^2/2, bound the numerator by exp(-lambda R_0^2/(4T)) times a Gaussian integral, and lower-bound the normalizer on a radius-sqrt(T) half-ball at y_0 by c_N T^{d/2}. Split this small tail from the initial law and apply (10) on its complement. Initial-law total variation (12), followed by the same strip coupling, then gives

$$
P_{\rho_T}\{\tau_{G_T}\le t_T\}=o(1). \tag{14}
$$

This proof does not infer an entrance-time bound from the small stationary saddle occupation.

## 4. The same stopped-protocol outcomes lose their initialization dependence

First remove only the first waiting cap, leaving the same exit deadline L_T=C_exit log(1/T), common handoff eta+h, first-stage conditions, and future window [D,D+1]. The mean-entrance bound already established in the original candidate is

$$
E_z\tau_{G_T}\le C_N(1+|X(z)|^2)e^{C_N/T},
\qquad X=(u,v,c). \tag{15}
$$

Thus the entrance is almost surely finite. Let K_T(z,\cdot) be the law, from z, of the actual post-entrance path on the fixed interval [0,L_T+h+D+1], measured relative to that entrance, together with its exit and event indicators. This is one common Markov kernel. All the uncapped E_1/E_0/F outcomes are measurable functions of it. The starting point and path before entrance are not resampled.

For any initialization nu, compare its first entrance with the first entrance searched for after t_T on the very same path. On {tau_{G_T}>t_T} the two selected path segments agree, up to the irrelevant absolute origin of time. The latter segment has law (nu P_{t_T})K_T. Therefore

$$
\|\operatorname{Law}_\nu(O_T^{\infty})
                  -(\nu P_{t_T})K_T\|_{TV}
\le P_\nu\{\tau_{G_T}\le t_T\}. \tag{16}
$$

Stationarity gives rho_T P_{t_T}=rho_T. Kernel contraction of total variation, (13), (14), and (16) imply

$$
\|\operatorname{Law}_\mu(O_T^{\infty})
       -\operatorname{Law}_{\rho_T}(O_T^{\infty})\|_{TV}
\le P_\mu(\tau_{G_T}\le t_T)+P_{\rho_T}(\tau_{G_T}\le t_T)
      +\delta_T=o(1). \tag{17}
$$

Now restore the same cap H_T=exp(2C_N/T) for both laws. Their X-second moments at initialization are bounded uniformly in T; for rho_T this follows directly from its conditional Gaussian laws and 0<=a<=1. Markov's inequality and (15) give

$$
P_\nu\{\tau_{G_T}>H_T\}\le C_N(1+E_\nu|X|^2)e^{-C_N/T}=o(1),
\qquad \nu\in\{\mu,\rho_T\}. \tag{18}
$$

The capped and uncapped outcome records can differ only on that event. Any missing-entry record can be assigned a distinguished failure symbol, or the original deadline continuation can be retained: the discrepancy is still bounded by (18). Hence the actual capped protocol satisfies

$$
\boxed{\quad
\|\operatorname{Law}_\mu(O_T)-\operatorname{Law}_{\rho_T}(O_T)\|_{TV}
\longrightarrow0.
\quad} \tag{19}
$$

In particular, the probability differences for E_1, F, E_1 intersect F, and every other measurable declared outcome event tend to zero. The convergence is uniform over such event sets, including T-dependent ones. Conditional comparisons given E_1 or E_0 also converge because their probabilities are bounded below by the existing branch theorem. Absolute waiting times, pre-entrance path observables, or an additional condition of no coherence anywhere since time zero are outside (19); they are not silently identified with this protocol.

Equation (19) refutes a positive limiting probability surplus for every fixed initial law for this candidate's declared protocol: the smooth fixed mu above is an admissible counterexample. It does not refute the actual same-process high-versus-low facilitation margin. Nor does it imply that every initial law mixes on this time scale.

## 5. A stationary entrance/completion bound for the horizon comparison

The following bound concerns actual repeated appearances of the same qualified lineage motif, with no reset of the diffusion. It supplies an operational entrance convention rather than inferring recurrence from occupation.

Starting from the actual current state, wait for G_T, follow the same exit deadline and handoff rule, and inspect E_1 intersect F through the end of its future window. After a failed inspection, start the next search from the actual endpoint. On each trial the waiting rule is the first entrance after that trial starts; every post-entrance rule and event tolerance is identical to the original protocol. This is a nonoverlapping search for occurrences, not a change to its one-trial probability experiment. Use an uncapped wait for this upper-bound search; its expectation is finite by (15).

There is p_0>0 such that, conditional on every actual entrance point in G_T, the trial succeeds with probability at least p_0 for all sufficiently small T. This combines uniform high-branch probability, fixed continuation, and high future success. If B_T=L_T+h+D+1, every inspection after entrance finishes within B_T. At the exit-or-deadline time eta, the state is in the compact closure of O. After the fixed additional h+D+1, the stable linear matter equations with bounded a-forcing give a uniform bound

$$
E[1+|X_{\mathrm{end}}|^2\mid\mathcal F_{\mathrm{entrance}}]\le C_N. \tag{20}
$$

This bound is unconditional over success and failure; division by a possibly small failure probability is unnecessary. Trial j is reached with probability at most (1-p_0)^{j-1}. For j>=2, conditioning at the preceding entrance and retaining the failure indicator gives

$$
E[1_{\{\mathrm{trial}\ j\ \mathrm{reached}\}}
          (1+|X_{\mathrm{start},j}|^2)]
\le C_N(1-p_0)^{j-2}. \tag{21}
$$

Apply (15) at each actual trial start and sum the nonnegative expected waiting costs using (21). Sum the inspection durations by the same geometric bound. The first successful completion time R_T then obeys

$$
E_\nu R_T
\le C_N'(1+E_\nu|X|^2)e^{C_N/T}+B_T/p_0. \tag{22}
$$

The exponential constant C_N is the one already used in (15); retries change prefactors and add a logarithmic term, not this exponent.

Take nu=rho_T(.|A_T), for any declared measurable A_T with rho_T(A_T)>=a_0>0. Its second moment is bounded by a_0^{-1}E_rho|X|^2, uniformly in T. In particular a fixed sufficiently small low-well neighborhood has stationary mass tending to one. Thus

$$
\frac{E_{\rho_T(\cdot\mid A_T)}R_T}{H_T}
\le C_{N,a_0}e^{-C_N/T}+\frac{B_T}{p_0H_T}
\longrightarrow0. \tag{23}
$$

This is an entrance/completion-time statement from a set of stationary mass bounded below. It is stronger than an occupancy estimate and uses the actual process between attempts. The chosen full horizon H_T is much longer, not shorter, than this stationary waiting scale. Markov's inequality also gives a stationary successful completion before H_T with probability tending to one for the stated repeated-occurrence search. The same repeated-occurrence probability tends to one from mu, so this search supplies no limiting probability surplus either.

The horizon compared in (23) includes the original saddle wait. A horizon declared only after an already selected handoff is a different scope and is not refuted by (23); it cannot be substituted silently for the full-protocol horizon. The entrance convention also matters. A one-shot protocol that reports permanent failure after its first low exit has no finite 'first successful completion time' on that failure event; calling its mean infinity is not a stationary recurrence estimate. The repeated search above defines recurrence of the same local lineage motif. It is not used to replace the one-shot event in the exact probability comparison (19).

For the reciprocal-mean first-completion rate under this entrance convention, both rates tend to zero: early saddle entrance probabilities vanish on t_T, completion cannot precede saddle entrance, and therefore E_nu R_T>=t_T(1-o(1)) for nu=mu,rho_T. Consequently their reciprocal-mean rates cannot differ by a positive limiting absolute margin. This specifies a rate statistic; it does not claim equality of waiting-time distributions or a universal statement about every rescaled rate.

## Result and stopping point of this candidate loop

For one fixed smooth admissible low-well initialization, the declared capped stopped-protocol outcome law is asymptotically the same as under stationary initialization. Its probability-surplus branch of R15 therefore fails the every-initial-law requirement. For the operational entrance convention in Section 5, the chosen full waiting horizon also fails the short-horizon alternative: actual stationary qualified-lineage completion has mean at most C exp(C_N/T), whereas the chosen horizon is exp(2C_N/T). These are fixed-N results for every allowed even N, not extrapolations from a small network.

The high-versus-low handoff facilitation and first/second restoring estimates remain valid. What fails is using this particular saddle-selection protocol to turn them into a universal nonequilibrium formation claim. The earlier selected-handoff surplus q_sigma(g_T)-rho_T(g_T) remains a correct comparison of differently selected preparations; it cannot replace the same-protocol calculation (19).

This closes the named stationary comparison for the existing candidate and protocol at the stated entrance convention. It does not prove a general no-go theorem for the fixed-energy model class, other protocols, other event definitions, or the Chapter 12 hierarchy target. A materially different answer would require a separately constructed protocol or mechanism that satisfies a matched stationary alternative, together with the restoring and facilitation requirements, from every declared initial law; merely appending partition or GL labels does not address this obstruction. This bounded candidate loop stops here. No new simulation, numerical test, GPU calculation, or Lean work was run.
