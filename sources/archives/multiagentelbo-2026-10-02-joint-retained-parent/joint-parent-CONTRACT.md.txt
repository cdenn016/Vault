# Joint source-assignment and configuration parent study

October 2, 2026. This bounded continuation retains the actual source assignments required by the Chapter 11 parent-dynamics question. The previous switching experiments discarded them and scored only configuration projections. This is a new observed law and comparison, not a replay of those results or a solution of the continuous-time problem.

## Law and candidates

Use the exact saved learn-both activations and eight-interval structural schedules from the stiffness refinement. Keep the 64 microsteps of delta=1/64, horizon one, global refresh rate one, source temperature 0.5, K=2, N=512 and 1,024, sparse cycle/stride-17 support, and full sender forces. The all-assignment curvature condition is unchanged. Every microstep is now observed through (R_t,J_t), R_t=P X_t. Blocks 16, 32 and 64 use the same paths. The label is a receiver-row source index, with its node identity supplied by the carried graph. The physical refresh coin is marginalized out.

For p=1-exp(-delta), the normalized source transition is

$$
k_t(j'|x,j)=(1-p)1_{j'=j}+p\prod_i\beta_{i,j'_i}(x).
$$

The hold event includes refreshes that redraw exactly the same complete assignment. Multiplying separate receiver hold mixtures would define a different law. Log-softmax and log-add-exp evaluate this global mixture without multiplying exponentially small probabilities.

Compile a fixed lift from the actual product-Gaussian activation,

$$
\widehat x(r)=\mu_0+\Sigma_0P^T(P\Sigma_0P^T)^{-1}(r-P\mu_0).
$$

This is an activation conditional mean, not the actual evolving hidden filter. Four scheduled Markov parents on (R,J) combine two source rows (recognition beta_q, or pointwise beta at the lift) and two configuration drifts (recognition-weighted force, or the newly observed assignment force). Each uses the normalized global-refresh source mixture and a Gaussian configuration row with mean P[hat x-delta M g(hat x)] and covariance C=2 delta P M P^T. Their initial law is the exact projected activation times its independent initial assignment law. All structural schedules and lift coefficients are supplied controls; these parents are not autonomous inherited closures. Positive noise makes every configuration row normalized and full support.

## Score identity

Write H_t=(R_0:t,J_0:t), Z_t=(X_0:t,J_0:t), and Y=(J_{t+1},R_{t+1}). Under the declared fine model, the conditional Y row given Z_t is k_t times a projected Gaussian with mean m_t(X_t,J_{t+1}) and covariance C. For any declared parent s_t(Y|H_t), conditional relative-entropy decomposition gives

$$
E D(P(Y|Z_t)\Vert s_t(Y|H_t))
=E D(P(Y|H_t)\Vert s_t(Y|H_t))
 +I(Y;Z_t|H_t).
$$

Summing in t and using the common initial law yields U(S)=D(P_retained||S)+G, with G a nonnegative sum of conditional information independent of S. Factor each row into its source and subsequent Gaussian factors. An unbiased per-trajectory score is the sum of log k_t(J_next|X_t,J_t)-log s_J(J_next|H_t), plus the analytically integrated Gaussian conditional KL given X_t,J_next. This Gaussian contribution is one half the squared C-inverse distance of the two means. Integrating the Gaussian noise reduces variance without changing the expected score. Source contributions can be negative on individual paths; no clipping is allowed.

Thus paired differences of expected U are exact retained joint-path KL differences. Absolute sample means are estimates of an upper functional, not computed absolute retained-path KL or certified confidence bounds. This identity concerns the fine-grid joint observed law. Its information gap differs from that of the earlier eight-observation configuration-only study.

## Execution and boundaries

The study uses 1,024 independent paths per N in batches of 128 with declared independent seed streams, reusing each path across all four parents and three block sizes. It saves projected histories, every assignment, native source-row log probabilities, separate source/drift scores, and per-trajectory contrasts. No learning run is repeated. Tiny independent checks cover normalized source mixtures, exact capture parity, activation conditional lifting, full-retention identity recovery, and Gaussian/log-density scores before one large run. The new standalone profile is experiments/gaussian_joint_parent.py; the active neighborhood launcher is unchanged.

Report empirical means and paired standard errors over trajectories. These are two fixed network instances, not replicated graph ensembles. Finite scores and normalized laws are acceptance conditions; an improvement is not required. This study provides a baseline joint predictor with growing retained assignment storage. It establishes neither a fixed-dimensional regular filter, vanishing error, long-window closure, continuous-time accuracy, learned path feedback, nor recursive formation.
