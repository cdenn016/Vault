## Conditional construction

Fix K>=2. Cells i in Z each contain two agents r_i,u_i in R^K. There are three parallel inverse-paired links from u_i to r_i with marks I,A_i,B_i, plus an identity link between r_i and r_(i+1). Identity edges form the connecting forest; A_i and B_i are based fundamental-cycle holonomies. This is an abstract agent graph at c_*, not base geometry.

Let the cell pairs (A_i,B_i) be independent and identically distributed across i, with independent A and B within each cell (their two fixed marginals may differ), and proper compactly supported densities in GL^+(K,R), bounded away from singularity. Their supports contain nonisometries and noncommuting pairs, for example small neighborhoods of diag(2,1/2) and [[1,1],[0,1]] for K=2. A pair commutes only on a measure-zero algebraic subset, so almost every environment has noncommuting holonomy. Reverse links are inverses, not independent draws. These are independent nonflat link variables in a noncompact structure group, but the bounded-support assumption is substantive. This is not a Haar or full-support GL prior.

Condition on the full environment Theta. Freeze m^2,kappa,gamma,eta>0 and a uniform bound max(||A_i||,||B_i||)<=L. Set x_i=(r_i,u_i), and define the quadratic form initially on finite-support sequences:

$$
2E(x)=m^2\sum_i(|r_i|^2+|u_i|^2)
+\kappa\sum_i|r_i-u_i|^2
+\gamma\sum_i(|r_i-A_i u_i|^2+|r_i-B_i u_i|^2)
+\eta\sum_i|r_{i+1}-r_i|^2.
$$

The form defines a bounded precision H_Theta on l^2(Z;R^(2K)); it does not claim that infinite typical samples have finite E. Local factors can be realized by proper Gaussian node priors and normalized Gaussian comparison records conditioned at zero, with supplied labels. This static multi-record model is a new conditional specialization, not the October 2 one-record-per-receiver model.

Endpoint Euclidean metrics are declared data. Carry them under passive GL re-expression: H'=G^(-T)HG^(-1), C'=GCG^T, with all comparison maps and reference measures transformed. Holding the identity-metric formula fixed after a nonorthogonal change changes the model. A second belief/model channel may be a copied independent Gaussian sector with identical links and identity cross-channel morphisms. This gives an admitted two-channel specialization, not learned model-channel dynamics.

Define

$$
\Lambda=m^2+2\kappa+2\gamma(1+L^2)+4\eta,\qquad
q=1-m^2/\Lambda<1.
$$

The estimates ||[I,-A]||^2<=1+L^2 and ||discrete difference||<=2 give

$$
m^2I\le H_\Theta\le\Lambda I,\qquad
\Lambda^{-1}I\le C_\Theta=H_\Theta^{-1}\le m^{-2}I.
$$

The inverse is the norm-convergent series

$$
C_\Theta=\Lambda^{-1}\sum_{p\ge0}Q_\Theta^p,\qquad
Q_\Theta=I-H_\Theta/\Lambda,\qquad ||Q_\Theta||\le q.
$$

For each finite interval D, set C_D=(C_Theta)_(D,D) and let mu_D^Theta=N(0,C_D). Its finite action, Lebesgue density coordinate, and partition function are

$$
E_D(x_D)=\tfrac12 x_D^T C_D^{-1}x_D,\qquad dx_D,\qquad
Z_D=(2\pi)^{K|D|}\det(C_D)^{1/2}.
$$

Lebesgue measure here is only a density coordinate. For the normalized measure-pair tier, additionally carry the probability reference rho_D=N(0,m^(-2)I) and the finite raw measure nu_D=Z_D mu_D. Its density relative to rho_D is f_D=Z_D (d mu_D/d rho_D). At every blocking step push forward both nu_D and rho_D; the evidence mass Z_D stays fixed along that depth tower. The normalized orthonormal block rows preserve the white reference covariance m^(-2)I. In other frames, push its covariance and density Jacobian with the physical law. A newly assigned Lebesgue or Gaussian reference is not substituted at a later depth. Different volumes have their own finite evidence masses; projectivity is claimed for the normalized physical and reference laws, not for raw masses across volumes.

These are transparent boundary conditions: the precision is obtained by eliminating the exterior, not by truncating H. For D contained in E, Gaussian marginalization gives (pi_(E,D))_*mu_E^Theta=mu_D^Theta exactly. A direct infinite realization is X_a=sum_b(C_Theta^(1/2))_(a,b)xi_b for independent standard normal xi_b. Rows are square summable, so each series converges in L^2; the countable coordinates have covariance C_Theta. This constructs a normalized cylinder law without an infinite Lebesgue density or infinite partition function. Integrating Theta gives a proper joint law, generally a Gaussian mixture. Its conditional finite Gaussian laws use the entire quenched environment through the boundary precision.

## Exact coarse map and closure

First take z_i=(r_i+u_i)/sqrt(2), keeping rooted holonomies and dressed boundary copies. For b>=2 and B=b^ell define

$$
Y_j^{(\ell)}=B^{-1/2}\sum_{i=jB}^{(j+1)B-1}z_i.
$$

In another frame use the corresponding rooted tree transports. All blocks are connected subtrees of the chosen forest. Retain every A_i,B_i in each block, based at its root, and all microscopic boundary copies. No averaged group element replaces parallel copies or multiple holonomies. Structural storage grows with block size; bounded interface size is not asserted.

A subsequent step is b^(-1/2) times the sum of its b child features, so

$$
b^{-s/2}\sum_{a=0}^{b^s-1}
 b^{-r/2}\sum_{c=0}^{b^r-1}z_{j b^{r+s}+a b^r+c}
=b^{-(r+s)/2}\sum_{d=0}^{b^{r+s}-1}z_{j b^{r+s}+d}.
$$

The underlying averages have multiplicative weights; the square-root factors are the identification/rescaling. Relabel coarse cells by their block indices, carrying original multiplicity, roots, ordered marks, and forest data. The disjoint union of these typed finite states is a common standard Borel carrier. On lengths divisible by each requested ratio, the deterministic kernels obey K_(b^r)K_(b^s)=K_(b^(r+s)). For total kernels add one isolated absorbing state. K_(b^r) sends a length not divisible by b^r, or shorter than b^r, to that state and fixes it; K_1 is the identity. Divisibility by b^(r+s) is equivalent to the two successive admissibility conditions, so the same composition law holds on the completed common carrier. The physical limit below uses only admissible lengths. In the canonical orthonormal coordinates the rescaled linear right inverse is P^T, not the unscaled replication map; in other frames it is carried with both endpoints.

For geometry the required identity is Theta_(A<-I)Theta_(I<-i)=Theta_(A<-i). Twice dressing an edge therefore gives exactly Theta_(A<-i)Theta_e Theta_(A'<-j)^(-1), the direct dressing. Composite forest trees prove the identity after every passive frame change. Ordered mark concatenation is associative. The two copied channels and identity morphisms commute with the same linear map. This matches the hypotheses of the source forest-composition proposition and also supplies its separate weight condition.

If P is the finite block matrix, the exact covariance is P C_D P^T and the precision is (P C_D P^T)^(-1). Gaussian closure is exact in the class of all positive precision matrices, including its determinant constant. A nearest-neighbor or fixed-edge precision family is generally not closed. Marginalizing random links also leaves the Gaussian family. No precision, bandwidth, or mark truncation is used here.

## Two-index theorem and proof

Cell range one implies (Q^p)_(i,j)=0 for p<|i-j|. Thus

$$
||C_{ij}||\le\Lambda^{-1}\sum_{p\ge|i-j|}q^p
=m^{-2}q^{|i-j|}.
$$

Write S=[I,I]/sqrt(2), with SS^T=I, C^z_(ij)=SC_(ij)S^T, and

$$
f_i(\Theta)=\sum_{h\in Z}C^z_{i,i+h},\qquad
\chi=\mathbb E_\Theta f_0(\Theta).
$$

The sums converge absolutely, uniformly in Theta. Stationarity and transposition pair h with -h, so chi is symmetric.

For a direct almost-sure averaging argument, truncate the Neumann series after R and sum its finite-range row to obtain f_i^(R). This variable depends only on link marks within R+1 cells of i, is uniformly bounded in R by Lambda^(-1)sum_p(2p+1)q^p, and satisfies

$$
||f_i-f_i^{(R)}||
\le\Lambda^{-1}\sum_{p>R}(2p+1)q^p
\le c_q(R+1)q^R.
$$

Variables f_i^(R),f_j^(R) are independent when |i-j|>2R+2. Entrywise, their B-cell average has variance at most c(R+1)/B. Choose R=ell, B=b^ell. The probability of an epsilon deviation from its mean is at most c(ell+1)/(epsilon^2 b^ell), a summable sequence. The probability of any deviation after L is bounded by its tail sum, which vanishes. Taking rational epsilon>0 and finitely many entries gives almost surely

$$
B^{-1}\sum_{i=jB}^{(j+1)B-1}f_i(\Theta)\longrightarrow\chi.
$$

This works for every fixed integer j, on a common probability-one set. It uses only independence, a variance bound, and the union bound.

The omitted row sums outside one block contribute at most

$$
\left\|\operatorname{Var}_\Theta(Y_j^{(\ell)})
-B^{-1}\sum_{i=jB}^{(j+1)B-1}f_i(\Theta)\right\|
\le\frac{2q}{m^2(1-q)^2B}.
$$

The cross-covariance between distinct blocks is at most q/[m^2(1-q)^2B] for neighboring blocks, and smaller for separated blocks. Normalized disjoint block rows are orthonormal on microscopic coordinates, hence their covariance bounds imply

$$
\Lambda^{-1}I\le\chi\le m^{-2}I.
$$

For every finite set of distinct indices j_1,...,j_s, almost surely in Theta,

$$
(Y_{j_1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,Y_{j_s}^{(\ell)})
\longrightarrow \mathcal N(0,I_s\otimes\chi)
$$

in total variation. Indeed the positive covariance matrices converge; their Gaussian densities converge pointwise. Dominated convergence for the minimum with the limiting density then makes the L^1 distance vanish. Integration over Theta gives the same annealed finite-observable limit.

Now take D_n={0,...,b^n-1}, with N_n=2b^n agents, and 0<=ell<=n after initial cell formation. At fixed ell, coarse finite laws are exact cylinder restrictions of an infinite coarse law. Set ell(n)=floor(n/2). Both block size b^ell(n) and number of coarse cells b^(n-ell(n)) diverge. Every fixed finite set of nonnegative coarse indices belongs to D_n eventually, and exact transparent marginals remove finite-boundary errors. The preceding proof therefore applies to the diagonal. Volume-first, then depth, gives the same finite-observable limit. At maximal depth only one cell remains, so a many-cell limit is not asserted there.

For a finite study, chi may be estimated using the same coupled environment. The theorem supplies explicit covariance/boundary bounds; it does not supply an unproved finite-sample confidence interval for an arbitrary estimator of chi.

## Scaling conclusion and boundaries

Increasing ell means larger blocks; logarithmic scale is varsigma=ell log b. The limiting matter law is independent Gaussian with covariance chi, fixed under normalized sums of independent copies. For covectors transported by t'_a=g_(r_a)^(-T)t_a, the gauge-invariant characteristic observable has limit

$$
\mathbb E\exp\left(i\sum_a t_a^T Y_{j_a}\right)
\longrightarrow
\exp\left(-\tfrac12\sum_a t_a^T\chi t_a\right).
$$

The limit chi is independent of integer b and deterministic block origin: the proof uses only interval length and finite dependence. This is a specific scheme comparison. It does not cover arbitrary trees, weighted blocks, or graph families. Microscopic mass and links affect chi; whitening by its carried covariance gives a standard Gaussian limit within this massive class.

No continuous beta function, critical point, interacting perturbation spectrum, or general nonlinear universality is proved. The O(B^-1) boundary/cross-covariance estimate is an actual error bound, not a classification of all operators. The source's independent-product Hermite exponents cannot be imported as gauge-interaction exponents.

The exact finite state retains all holonomies, but their growing list is not shown to converge as a complete state. No infinite ELBO density, autonomous generator, learned-link path law, or endogenous recursive formation is obtained. Identity forest links are a real restriction despite the independent noncommuting cell holonomies. Full-support unbounded GL priors and general noncompact forest products need additional tail/coercivity control.
