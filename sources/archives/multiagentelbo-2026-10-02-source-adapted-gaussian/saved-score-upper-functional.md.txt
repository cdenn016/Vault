# Recovering the existing upper functional from saved scores

October 2, 2026. Follow-through of the upper-functional identity already recorded in [joint-filter-CONTRACT](joint-filter-CONTRACT.md), following [trajectory-construction](trajectory-construction.md). This is an analytical observation about saved outputs, not a new simulation or a regular-filter closure theorem.

## Identity and assumptions

For every fixed finite native model, declared H-step schedule and full-row-rank retention P, let Z_t=(X_0:t,J_0:t), H_t=(R_0:t,J_0:t), R_t=PX_t, and Y_next=(J_next,R_next). The unchanged finite-grid law has joint conditional density

$$
p(Y_{t+1}|Z_t)=
k_t(J_{t+1}|X_t,J_t)
\phi_{C_t}(R_{t+1}-Pa_{t,J_{t+1}}(X_t)),
\qquad C_t=2\delta_t PM_tP^T>0.
\tag{1}
$$

The assignment likelihood marginalizes the one global hold/refresh coin, including refreshes to the same assignment. It is not the refresh-only product. For the current saved study C_t=C is deterministic and constant in t.

Let s_t be any normalized, positive, nonanticipating candidate joint predictor. It need not be the exact integrated Gaussian decoder: the normalized sequential cubature predictor in [source-adapted-CONTRACT](source-adapted-CONTRACT.md) qualifies. Its source row is scored before its assignment-conditioned Gaussian observation row, which is scored before conditioning on that observation. Both candidate and native path laws use the same actual initial retained law; otherwise add their initial KL.

Conditional on Z_t and J_next, the innovation in (1) is exactly N(0,C_t). Therefore, with m=rank P,

$$
\mathbb E_P[\log\phi_{C_t}(R_{t+1}-Pa_{t,J_{t+1}}(X_t))
\mid Z_t,J_{t+1}]
=-h_t,
\qquad
h_t=\frac12[m\log(2\pi e)+\log\det C_t].
\tag{2}
$$

No unknown conditional mean occurs on the right. Integrating (2) and retaining the candidate's actual observed log score proves

$$
\boxed{
W_S=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}
\left[\log k_t(J_{t+1}|X_t,J_t)-\log s_t(Y_{t+1}|H_t)-h_t\right],
\qquad
\mathbb E_P W_S=\mathcal U(S).
}
\tag{3}
$$

The existing conditional-divergence decomposition gives

$$
\mathcal U(S)=
\sum_t\mathbb E_P D(P(Y_{t+1}|Z_t)\Vert s_t(\cdot|H_t))
=D(P_{R,J}\Vert S)+
\sum_t I_P(Y_{t+1};Z_t\mid H_t).
\tag{4}
$$

Thus the expectation in (3) is an absolute upper functional for retained joint-path KL. It is not the KL itself unless the information gap vanishes. An individual W_S, or its finite-sample mean, need not be nonnegative or be an upper bound on the unknown KL.

For a particle predictor with a tape U independent of the entire native path, replace s_t by s_t,U. The same calculation gives E_(P,U)W=E_U D(P_RJ||S_U)+G, with the same G as (4). Independent tape/path pairs estimate this tape-conditioned risk; fixing one independently supplied tape estimates its conditional risk. Neither is an unbiased estimate of the marginalized-parent KL. That latter KL is bounded above by the tape-averaged risk, but the two generally differ.

Replacing only the native Gaussian log density by its known expectation preserves unbiasedness. It is not a conditional expectation of the complete log-ratio statistic, because the candidate log score still depends on the same innovation. Consequently no general variance-reduction claim follows.

## Integrability and applicability to these files

Native finite-horizon states have moments of every order by the uniform affine-sheet growth bound and Gaussian innovations. Supported source priors have a positive finite minimum, so -log k_t grows at most quadratically in X_t. The particle contract already establishes finite expected log loss for each fixed cloud and horizon.

For the deterministic Gaussian implementation, finite log-score moments can also be checked without assuming the exact hidden law Gaussian. In one positive spherical cubature update of local rank r, every node offset from the original, untilted cubature mean has outer product at most r C_local. The tilted covariance equals the weighted outer product of these original offsets minus the tilted mean-shift outer product, so it too is at most r C_local; the regression identity implies Sigma_new<=max(1,r)Sigma. Each mean shift has squared norm at most r||Sigma||. These bounds do not divide by a small label probability. Propagation is affine and adds fixed positive noise; Gaussian observation conditioning decreases covariance, while its gain is bounded by the finite forecast covariance and the positive innovation floor P Q P^T. Induction over the finite receiver/step count gives a finite deterministic covariance upper bound and at most linear mean growth in the finite observed history. Source negative log probabilities and Gaussian observation negative log densities consequently have at most quadratic growth in that history. The cutoff freezes updates and preserves these bounds. Finite variance follows from native fourth moments. Constants can be enormous; this establishes integrability, not a useful numerical confidence radius. It concerns the mathematical formulas; a nonfinite numerical run still fails.

The recorded native field log_source_kernel has exactly the first term of (3); source inspection confirms that the refresh coin was marginalized before saving. gaussian_joint_scores records log_source plus the fully normalized Gaussian observation log density in log_joint. The old particle/baseline log_joint arrays have the corresponding pointwise scores. They are suitable inputs, unlike the earlier analytically integrated Gaussian-drift comparison scores.

Using the first 32 or 128 saved paths is valid when the native path indices, all 64 steps, activation, projection, source schedule, and candidate scores match. The subsets were declared without outcome selection. Any candidate fitted or selected on those same outputs introduces a separate selection question: the selected candidate's in-sample mean is not automatically an unbiased out-of-sample risk estimate.

## Stable computation and uncertainty

For arithmetic blocks of size b and independent node mobility blocks M_i,

$$
C_{\rm block}=\frac{2\delta}{b^2}\sum_{i\in{\rm block}}M_i,
\qquad
\log\det C=\sum_{\rm blocks}2\sum_a\log L_{{\rm block},aa},
\tag{5}
$$

where L_block is its Cholesky factor. Here M_i is the saved activation Sigma_i, not the current learner covariance or the candidate predictive covariance. The retained dimension is m=(N/b)K. Differential entropy may be negative; replacing h by its absolute value gives the wrong sign.

Use float64, compute row residuals log_k_native-log_joint_candidate-h, and accumulate each complete path with compensated summation such as math.fsum. Avoid exponentiating likelihoods or forming determinants directly. Inspect agreement with a separately ordered compensated accumulation and report any material discrepancy; agreement is a roundoff diagnostic, not a proof of exact saved density values. Large entropy cancellation does not invalidate (3), but input and summation errors must remain small relative to the reported residual. Floating-point stored scores provide numerical approximations to the mathematical unbiased statistic.

For L independent complete paths, report mean(W), sample standard deviation divided by sqrt(L), and L. Steps within one path are not independent replicates. For candidates A,B, calculate their paired difference directly:

$$
W_A-W_B=\sum_t[\log s_{t,B}-\log s_{t,A}],
\tag{6}
$$

which cancels both native assignment likelihood and entropy pathwise. Use the same 32-path subset when comparing the new Gaussian arms with earlier 128-path particle outputs. The standard error of an absolute W and that of a paired difference answer different questions.

A mean plus a reported standard error is descriptive, not a rigorous upper confidence certificate. Such a certificate needs a justified variance/tail bound (for example a proved V>=Var(W), giving U<=mean(W)+sqrt(V/(L alpha)) with probability at least 1-alpha) or another valid concentration argument. Replacing V by the empirical variance does not prove that statement. The existence of finite variance alone supplies no known numerical radius.
