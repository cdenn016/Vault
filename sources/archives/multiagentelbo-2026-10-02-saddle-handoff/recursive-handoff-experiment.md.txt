## 1. Exact conditional input needed from the exit result

Let Z=(a,u,v,c), s_N be the actual saddle, G_T the declared thermal ball about it, and O a fixed bounded saddle neighborhood. Let Gamma_+,Gamma_- be disjoint outgoing sections, all fixed before execution except the radius of G_T. Assume the following uniform assertion, strengthening the bare two probability inequalities in equation (20) by stating the required path concentration explicitly.

There exist p>0, C_exit<infinity, fixed k>0, fixed h>1, full-dimensional compact handoff sets K_+,K_-, and deterministic outgoing branch segments gamma_+,gamma_- on [0,h], such that for all sufficiently small T and every x in the closure of G_T, each sign has a good-path event A_± with probability at least p-epsilon_T, epsilon_T tending to zero. On A_±, the actual process exits O through Gamma_± by C_exit log(1/T), then remains in a fixed strict-margin tube about the corresponding segment for duration h. The high segment satisfies the first-coherence corridor throughout its last unit interval and ends in the interior of K_+; the low segment avoids the declared pair-coherence set from saddle entry to handoff and ends in the interior of K_-.

It suffices that the probability of each sign is bounded below by p and the conditional tube failure given that sign tends to zero uniformly over entrance points. Mere lower bounds for exit signs do not imply this path assertion. The saved nonlinear-saddle-exit.md supplies the uniform tube conclusion and compatible sections, including fixed finite path conditions with strict margins. Choose O small enough that |u|>r_1 sqrt(N) throughout O; this also controls the pre-exit portion of the low event. All constants can depend on fixed N. No joint N,T limit is inferred.

Such corridors are consistent with the source deterministic construction: at k=0 the high branch approaches a=1,U=0 while V=7/20; the low branch approaches a=0,U=1 with that same V. Choose sufficiently late finite segments so U d_max<r_1/4 and 1-a<r_1/(8d_max) over the final unit interval, where d_max is defined below. Then choose a sufficiently small fixed positive k so strict margins persist by finite-time continuous dependence. This is the same existence construction as the exit note with tighter, fixed pairwise tolerances. The calculation below also gives an explicit smallness requirement on k for actual first-stage restoration. It does not supply a temperature-dependent parameter.

## 2. Coherence events and a common stopping-time handoff

Let the declared first partition be the m=N/2 pairs used to define u. Write u=(u_b) over those m pairs, with u_b in R^K, and let d_max=max_b |d_{u,b}|. Because |d_u|^2=N and m=N/2, d_max>=sqrt(2). Choose a fixed per-pair resolution 0<r_1<1/20. The first-stage corridor is

$$
\mathcal C_1=\{0\le1-a\le r_1/(4d_{max}),\quad \max_b|u_b|\le r_1,
\quad (3/10)\sqrt N\le|v|\le(2/5)\sqrt N\}. \tag{1}
$$

Every declared pair now satisfies its own fixed tolerance r_1. The earlier global norm criterion |u|<=r_1 sqrt(N) would control only an RMS quantity and is not used to claim all-pair coherence. Whole-network disagreement remains visibly positive on C_1.

Choose K_+ as a full-dimensional closed tube around a compact portion of the high branch inside C_1, and K_- as a full-dimensional closed tube around the low branch inside the source's invariant low corridor, with strict margins. In particular K_- has a<1/10, u near U d_u with U>9/10, and v near V d_v with V in (3/10,2/5). The c-coordinate may be restricted to any fixed ball about zero for compactness; it is independent of the other dynamics and does not affect either future coherence event. The branch-concentration input fixes the tubes so the high final unit remains inside C_1 and low paths have |u|>r_1 sqrt(N) throughout the episode.

Let tau_T=inf{t>=0:Z_t lies in closure(G_T)}. Fix the first waiting cutoff H_T=exp(2C_N/T) supplied by the entrance estimate and L_T=C_exit log(1/T). To define a finite handoff on every history, put

$$
\widehat\tau_T=\tau_T\wedge H_T,
\quad \eta_T=\inf\{t\ge\widehat\tau_T:Z_t\notin O\}
\wedge(\widehat\tau_T+L_T),
\quad \sigma_T=\eta_T+h. \tag{2}
$$

If tau_T>H_T, this is a failure-prefix handoff, not a fabricated saddle entrance. All times in (2) are stopping times for the completed continuous-path filtration; adding a fixed positive h preserves the stopping-time property. The rule is identical for both signs and all starting laws. The process is not restarted or modified at any of these times.

Let Q_T be the prefix event that tau_T<=H_T, eta_T is a genuine exit before its deadline, and the exit point lies on one of the two declared sections. Define the qualified first-stage event

$$
E_{1,T}=Q_T\cap\{Z_{\sigma_T}\in K_+\}
\cap\{Z_s\in\mathcal C_1\text{ for all }s\in[\sigma_T-1,\sigma_T]\}. \tag{3}
$$

It is measurable from the actual history up to sigma_T. Its partition is the declared pair partition. The high-state requirement is part of the event declared in advance, not a posterior exclusion of an unsuccessful first-stage handoff. This is a qualified lineage subevent; it does not claim to include every spontaneous coherence episode of the diffusion.

Define a counterfactual prefix event

$$
E_{0,T}=Q_T\cap\{Z_{\eta_T}\in\Gamma_-\}
\cap\{Z_{\sigma_T}\in K_-\}
\cap\{|u_s|>r_1\sqrt N\text{ for all }s\in[\tau_T,\sigma_T]\}. \tag{4}
$$

Thus E_0 is disjoint from E_1 and has no declared all-pair coherence episode since the episode start tau_T: if all m pair norms were at most r_1, their joint norm would be at most r_1 sqrt(m)<r_1 sqrt(N). The stronger low-event condition in (4) is retained. Arbitrary initial laws may have had earlier coherence episodes; that past remains part of the history and is not erased. A claim of no first-stage formation anywhere before sigma_T would be a different event and is not proved for arbitrary initial laws.

Predeclare the handoff class as the entire state space

$$
\mathcal H=[0,1]\times\mathbb R^{D_u}\times\mathbb R^{D_v}\times\mathbb R^{D_c}
$$

and include any independent partition mark if it is present. This class contains every actual first-stage handoff, every noisy atypical handoff, all deadline failures, and all E_0 handoffs. K_+ and K_- are subsets used to prove the event's margin; neither is falsely called the full handoff class.

## 3. Positive weights from one actual process

For a fixed mu with finite second moment, the source entrance estimate gives

$$
P_\mu(\tau_T\le H_T)\ge1-a_T(\mu),\qquad a_T(\mu)\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
$$

Conditioning on the actual entrance position and using the uniform input of Section 1 gives

$$
P_\mu(E_{1,T})\ge(1-a_T(\mu))(p-\epsilon_T),
\quad
P_\mu(E_{0,T})\ge(1-a_T(\mu))(p-\epsilon_T). \tag{6}
$$

The strong Markov property used here has a precise role: for a nonexplosive pathwise-unique reflected SDE driven by Brownian motion, future Brownian increments after a finite stopping time are independent of the past, and the future solution is the same measurable solution map started at the stopped state. Local Lipschitz drift, interval reflection, and the source moment estimate provide that setting. This conditional-law identity does not perform a physical reset.

The two events therefore have positive weights in one process under one preparation. Neither an injected sign coin, a replacement Gaussian at the saddle, nor a different initial law supplies the counterfactual.

## 4. One future event and the actual high-minus-low margin

Choose fixed 0<r_2<1/20, a delay D, and the whole-network coherence set

$$
\mathcal C_2=\{N(1-a)^2+|u|^2+|v|^2\le Nr_2^2\}. \tag{7}
$$

Its block is the whole network, hence a union of at least two first-stage pair blocks. The c-coordinate is an irrelevant global location and is not constrained. Let

$$
F_T=\{Z_{\sigma_T+t}\in\mathcal C_2\text{ for every }t\in[D,D+1]\},
\quad g_T(z)=P_z\{Z_t\in\mathcal C_2\text{ for every }t\in[D,D+1]\}. \tag{8}
$$

The same D and same event are used after both handoff types. The source high-box decay z+2U+2V<=F(0) exp(-kt/2), transverse contraction, and a sufficiently small full-dimensional K_+ give a finite D for which every high deterministic path lies strictly inside C_2 over [D,D+1]. Choose D slightly larger than needed for separate r_2/4 margins in each coordinate. The low corridor keeps V>=1/4 and hence |v|>=sqrt(N)/4, strictly outside C_2. The stopped finite-window small-noise estimate then supplies b_T tending to zero with

$$
\inf_{z\in K_+}g_T(z)\ge1-b_T,
\qquad \sup_{z\in K_-}g_T(z)\le b_T. \tag{9}
$$

For the actual conditional handoff laws

$$
q_{+,T}=\operatorname{Law}_\mu(Z_{\sigma_T}|E_{1,T}),
\qquad q_{-,T}=\operatorname{Law}_\mu(Z_{\sigma_T}|E_{0,T}),
$$

the Markov conditional-law identity and (9) give

$$
P_\mu(F_T|E_{1,T})=q_{+,T}(g_T)\ge1-b_T,
\quad P_\mu(F_T|E_{0,T})=q_{-,T}(g_T)\le b_T,
$$

$$
q_{+,T}(g_T)-q_{-,T}(g_T)\ge1-2b_T. \tag{10}
$$

Also P_mu(E_1 intersect F)>=P_mu(E_1)(1-b_T)>=p-o(1). Because the full handoff class contains K_- and g_T there tends to zero, g_T does not tend uniformly to one on that class. Thus the R14 supplied-scale override is avoided for this qualified same-process statistic. This is facilitation, not suppression. It depends on the supplied bistable-memory mechanism and supplied pair partition; it does not establish that the mechanism or partition was selected endogenously.

## 5. First-stage restoration while V is approximately 0.35

The source's restoring estimate near the fully formed minimum cannot alone justify the first stage, where v is still of order 0.35 sqrt(N). The actual equation for each within-pair contrast is

$$
du_b=-(u_b-(1-a)d_{u,b})dt+\sqrt{2T}dB_b.
$$

On the actual high-a corridor 0<=1-a<=r_1/(4d_max), the center of every pair has norm at most r_1/4. At |u_b|=r_1,

$$
\mathcal L|u_b|^2=-2|u_b|^2+2(1-a)\langle u_b,d_{u,b}\rangle+2TK
\le-\tfrac32 r_1^2+2TK. \tag{11}
$$

The inward radial restoring rate is at least 3/4 at this boundary, and its rate times squared resolution divided by T diverges for fixed N,r_1. This holds with the actual moving a; it does not freeze a favorable value by hand. The event and branch concentration require that actual a remains in its corridor.

The coupled fast stability supporting that corridor is also explicit. On a>=19/20, W''(a)>=89/100. In mobility coordinates Y=(sqrt(N)(1-a),u), with actual V=<v,d_v>/N as input, the fast Hessian is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}W''(a)+1+k&-d_u^T/\sqrt N\\-d_u/\sqrt N&I\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its nontrivial 2-by-2 block has determinant W''(a)+k and trace W''(a)+k+2. Therefore its smallest eigenvalue is at least determinant/trace>=89/289>3/10; transverse eigenvalues equal one. The full high-a strip is strongly convex in these fast coordinates even when V=0.35. At Y=0 the remaining forcing has size sqrt(N)k|V|. Set lambda_f=3/10. Up to exit from the strip and an actual bound |V|<=V_max, radial dissipation and Young's inequality give

$$
\mathcal L|Y|^2\le-\lambda_f|Y|^2
+Nk^2V_{max}^2/\lambda_f+2T(D_u+1). \tag{12}
$$

Reflection at a=1 contributes no positive radial term. Choose k fixed and small enough that k V_max d_max/lambda_f<=r_1/8, together with the source corridor requirements. Since W''>=89/100 on this strip, the fixed-V equilibrium relation below gives z_*(V)<=k V_max/(89/100+k), hence z_*(V)d_max<r_1/4. This places every pair's equilibrium bias below its declared resolution; actual path containment still comes from the stated corridor event and concentration. The kV force is retained reciprocally in the a equation; dropping it would change the descent model. At fixed V the fast equilibrium has U=1-a=z_*(V), where -W'(1-z_*)+kz_*=kV and z_*=O(kV), so u need not be exactly centered at zero during the first stage.

For the second stage, all of a,u,v in C_2 are near the formed minimum in the relevant coordinates, and the source positive-Hessian restoring estimate applies with a fixed lambda_N>0. Both episodes therefore have actual restoring mechanisms at fixed positive resolutions above the thermal scale. This alone does not settle their R15 horizon clauses.

## 6. The exact stationary distinction

There are two mathematically different experiments.

First, consider the common future-window functional g_T of (8). Starting directly from stationary rho_T at an ordinary time,

$$
\rho_T(g_T)\le\rho_T(\mathcal C_2)
\le\rho_T\{|v|\le r_2\sqrt N\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{13}
$$

This uses stationarity at time D and the source's explicit stationary Gaussian/energy estimate. It does not infer an entrance rate from occupancy. Combining (9) with (13) proves q_+,T(g_T)-rho_T(g_T)>=1-o(1). For the unconditional actual handoff law q_sigma,T, (6) similarly gives q_sigma,T(g_T)-rho_T(g_T)>=p-o(1). These are valid comparisons of exactly the same future path functional over the same duration, under an actual selected handoff law and an unselected stationary initial state.

This establishes a handoff-law probability surplus. It is the probability inequality for a separately declared future-window experiment initialized from its actual handoff law. It must be stated together with the fact that the handoff law was selected by waiting for a rare saddle entrance. It is not excess prevalence or excess occurrence rate of the full selection protocol in a stationary population.

Second, apply the entire same rule (2), the same first-stage qualification (3), and the same future event (8) to a process initialized with rho_T. This is the stationary counterpart of the complete stopped experiment. The stationary family has uniformly bounded second moments for fixed N and T in a bounded interval: its conditional Gaussian means are bounded and its conditional variances are proportional to T. Thus the source entrance estimate applies uniformly to this family. Equations (6) and (9) then also give

$$
P_{\rho_T}(E_{1,T})\ge p-o(1),\quad
P_{\rho_T}(F_T|E_{1,T})\ge1-b_T,
\quad P_{\rho_T}(E_{1,T}\cap F_T)\ge p-o(1). \tag{14}
$$

In particular, both conditioned high-handoff future probabilities lie in [1-b_T,1], so

$$
|P_\mu(F_T|E_{1,T})-P_{\rho_T}(F_T|E_{1,T})|\le b_T\longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
$$

There is no positive stationary excess for this matched, first-stage-conditioned stopped protocol. For the unconditioned stopped protocol or the joint lineage event, the present estimates give positive lower bounds for both starts and do not prove any strict surplus. Replacing P_rho(F after its own selected sigma) by rho(g) changes the initialization/selection protocol. That replacement is invalid if the claimed event is the entire wait-exit-handoff experiment.

Consequently the source statement “positive handoff success therefore satisfies R15” needs this scope distinction. The valid conclusion is (13)'s handoff-law surplus for a fixed future functional. The live Chapter 13 proposition explicitly compares the same event under stationary initialization, so the conservative R15 target here remains the matched full protocol. The complete stopped-protocol R15 horizon requirement remains unresolved, and its matched first-stage-conditioned probability-surplus version is ruled out by (15). These statements are compatible: a rare stationary selection can create a highly nonstationary conditional handoff law.
