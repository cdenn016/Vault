# Uniform nonlinear saddle exit from thermal-size entrance laws

October 2, 2026. Bounded round 2 of the [recursive formation construction](recursive-formation-construction.md). This note proves its local estimate (20) for the stated fixed finite-dimensional saddle, including uniformity over the actual thermal entrance ball and concentration sufficient for fixed-time continuation along either outgoing branch. It uses one verified primary nonlinear exit theorem and supplies the uniform-in-initial-state extension explicitly. No simulation, GPU calculation, or numerical saddle trajectory was run.

## Target and scope

For each fixed even \(N\ge512\), let the mobility-whitened process near the candidate's saddle solve
\[
 dY_t=b(Y_t)dt+\sqrt{2T}dW_t,\qquad
 b(0)=0,\quad Db(0)=A,
\]
where \(b\) is \(C^2\), \(A=A^\top\), one eigenvalue \(\lambda>0\) is simple, and all other eigenvalues are negative. Fix any \(c<\infty\). The target is uniform over every deterministic entrance point \(|Y_0|\le c\sqrt T\), hence over every possibly history-dependent entrance distribution supported there. The equivalence used is equality of stopped laws with the original reflected model before exit from an interior neighborhood, followed by probability bounds and convergence in probability. No equality of reduced parent path laws is claimed.

User-supplied requirements retained are fixed \(c_*\), fixed model coefficients as \(T\to0\), no restart at the handoff, and actual-law counterfactual comparisons. Introduced assumptions are those of the candidate already written in the first record, the smooth index-one saddle, and a fixed interior exit neighborhood. Constants may depend on \(N\), the coefficients, the exit neighborhood and \(c\). No bound uniform in network size is asserted.

## Primary nonlinear input

The external input is [Yuri Bakhtin, *Exit asymptotics for small diffusion about an unstable equilibrium*, arXiv:math/0701569v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701569v3), Theorem 1, printed pages 3-4. Its hypotheses include a \(C^2\) drift, a simple leading positive eigenvalue, a \(C^2\) unstable curve, and transverse exits \(q_\pm\). At initial state zero, for noise amplitude \(\epsilon\), it implies
\[
 H_\epsilon\Longrightarrow\tfrac12\delta_{q_+}+
             \tfrac12\delta_{q_-},\qquad
 \frac{\tau_\epsilon}{\log(1/\epsilon)}
       \longrightarrow\lambda^{-1}
       \quad\hbox{in probability}.                         \tag{1}
\]
This is the nonlinear theorem. Its Lemma 8, printed pages 9-11, controls the quadratic remainder up to the logarithmic departure time; Section 5 then transfers to the transverse exit. Its initial-state hypothesis alone does not cover a thermal ball. The separate corollary below supplies that uniformity; no novelty claim is made. The PDF and the cited proof sections were inspected directly on October 2, 2026.

## Checking the geometry and the candidate hypotheses

At the saddle of the first record, whiten coordinates by
\[
 Y=M_N^{-1/2}(Z-Z_*),\qquad
 b(Y)=-M_N^{1/2}\nabla E_N(Z_*+M_N^{1/2}Y).
\]
Then the noise is isotropic with amplitude \(\epsilon=\sqrt{2T}\), and
\(A=-M_N^{1/2}\nabla^2E_N(Z_*)M_N^{1/2}\) is symmetric. The parallel Hessian computed there has Schur complement \(W''(13/20)=-91/100\) and a positive lower block. All transverse Hessian blocks are positive. Thus \(A\) has exactly one positive eigenvalue, which is necessarily simple; no zero eigenvalues occur for fixed \(k>0\). The drift is polynomial near the saddle. The point \(a=13/20\) is interior to \([0,1]\), so a sufficiently small neighborhood does not encounter reflection.

For completeness the needed \(C^2\) local unstable curve can be constructed directly. Split \(Y=(u,z)\) so
\[
 \dot u=\lambda u+f(u,z),\qquad
 \dot z=Bz+g(u,z),\qquad B=B^\top\le-\beta I,
\]
where \(f,g\) vanish with their first derivatives at zero. Choose
\(\lambda/2<\gamma<\lambda\). For a prescribed unstable coordinate \(\xi\) at time zero, solve on \(t\le0\)
\[
 u(t)=e^{\lambda t}\xi-\int_t^0e^{\lambda(t-s)}
                                      f(u(s),z(s))ds,
\]
\[
 z(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t e^{B(t-s)}
                                      g(u(s),z(s))ds.     \tag{2}
\]
Use the norm \(\sup_{t\le0}e^{-\gamma t}(|u(t)|+|z(t)|)\).
On a ball of radius \(R\), the quadratic terms have norm at most
\[
 C R^2\left((2\gamma-\lambda)^{-1}
                      +(2\gamma+\beta)^{-1}\right);
\]
the difference of two images has the same bound with \(R^2\) replaced by
\(2R\) times their norm difference. A sufficiently small \(R\), with
\(|\xi|\le R/2\), makes (2) a contraction into that ball. Its unique solution defines \(h(\xi)=z(0)\). Applying the estimate on a ball of radius \(2|\xi|\) gives \(h(\xi)=O(\xi^2)\). The first and second derivatives with respect to \(\xi\) solve linear fixed-point equations whose inverse is the convergent series \(\sum_{j\ge0}(D\mathcal T)^j\); the same integral bounds control their forcing. Hence \(h\) is \(C^2\). Uniqueness after a small time shift proves local invariance.

Choose \(\delta>0\) so that the cylinder
\[
 G=\{ |u|\le\delta,\ |z|\le\delta\}
\]
is inside the reflection-free coordinate neighborhood, contains no other critical point, and \(|h(u)|<\delta/4\) for \(|u|\le\delta\). Its boundary is piecewise smooth. The curve intersects only the two end caps at
\[
 q_\pm=(\pm\delta,h(\pm\delta)).
\]
Since \(\dot u=\lambda u+O(u^2)\), these intersections are transverse for sufficiently small \(\delta\). There is no other equilibrium in \(G\): invertibility of \(A\) and \(b(y)=Ay+O(|y|^2)\) exclude one after shrinking \(\delta\). This matches all hypotheses used in (1). Write \(O=\operatorname{int}G\).

Take a smooth cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of \(G\), supported inside a larger reflection-free coordinate neighborhood, and extend the resulting drift to a globally \(C^2\), globally Lipschitz drift \(\widetilde b\). Let its Lipschitz constant be \(L\). It agrees with the original process until exit from \(O\). The extension only makes the following comparison of complete path measures well-defined; no coefficient inside the exit problem is changed.

## A uniform entropy bridge

Let \(P_x^T\) be the law on continuous paths of
\[
 dX_t=\widetilde b(X_t)dt+\epsilondW_t,\quad X_0=x,
 \qquad \epsilon=\sqrt{2T}.
\]
Let \(X^0\) have law \(P_0^T\). For deterministic \(x\), define
\[
 r_x(t)=x(1-t)_+,\qquad Y_t^x=X_t^0+r_x(t),
 \qquad Q_x^T=\mathop{\rm Law}(Y^x).
\]
Both \(P_x^T\) and \(Q_x^T\) start at \(x\). Under \(Q_x^T\), the drift is
\[
 \widetilde b(y-r_x(t))+r_x'(t)
 =\widetilde b(y)+v_x(t,y),
\]
\[
 |v_x(t,y)|\le (L+1)|x|1_{[0,1]}(t).                     \tag{3}
\]
The derivative at the single corner \(t=1\) is immaterial.

Here is the precise change-of-drift fact being used. For two diffusions with identical constant nondegenerate noise \(\epsilon I\), identical deterministic initial state, and bounded adapted drift difference \(v\) supported on \([0,1]\), the exponential density with integrand \(v/\epsilon\) changes one drift into the other. Its expectation is one because the integral of the squared integrand is deterministically bounded. Taking its logarithm and expectation under the changed law gives
\[
 D(Q\Vert P)=\frac1{2\epsilon^2}\mathbb E_Q
                       \int_0^1|v(t,X_t)|^2dt.
\]
Changing back gives the analogous formula for \(D(P\Vert Q)\). The common transition kernels after time one imply that these are also the entropies on the entire infinite path space. Thus there is no factor proportional to the exit horizon.

In the present application, for every \(|x|\le c\sqrt T\), (3) yields
\[
 D(Q_x^T\Vert P_x^T)\le K,\qquad
 D(P_x^T\Vert Q_x^T)\le K,\qquad
 K=\frac{(L+1)^2c^2}{4}.                                  \tag{4}
\]
This is uniform in the entrance point, in \(T\), and in every future time horizon. The bridge is an analytical comparison of measures; it is not a control or a reset applied to the physical process.

We will use two elementary consequences of the binary entropy identity. For any event \(E\), put \(p=P(E),q=Q(E)\). Decomposing relative entropy over \(E,E^c\) gives
\[
 D(Q\Vert P)\ge q\log(1/p)-h_b(q).
\]
Therefore
\[
 q\ge a>0,\quad D(Q\Vert P)\le K
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p\ge\exp[-(K+\log2)/a].                                  \tag{5}
\]
In the other direction,
\[
 D(P\Vert Q)\le K
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P(E)\le\frac{K+\log2}{\log(1/Q(E))},\qquad 0<Q(E)<1.       \tag{6}
\]
If \(Q(E)=0\), absolute continuity gives \(P(E)=0\). A uniformly vanishing \(Q\)-probability therefore implies a uniformly vanishing \(P\)-probability. Bounded entropy does not force nonvanishing branch probabilities to approach one half.

## The bridge does not create an early exit

Let \(d_0=\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial O)>0\). Since \(\widetilde b(0)=0\),
\[
 \sup_{t\le1}|X_t^0|
 \le\epsilon e^L\sup_{t\le1}|W_t|.
\]
For sufficiently small \(T\), \(c\sqrt T<d_0/2\). On
\(\{\sup_{t\le1}|X_t^0|<d_0/2\}\), both \(X^0\) and \(Y^x\) stay in \(O\) until time one, simultaneously for every \(|x|\le c\sqrt T\). After time one these coupled paths are identical. Consequently their exit times and exit points agree unless this early exceptional event occurs.

The Brownian maximal second-moment bound gives
\[
 e_T:=\Pr\{\sup_{t\le1}|X_t^0|\ge d_0/2\}
 \le\frac{16d\epsilon^2e^{2L}}{d_0^2}=O(T),               \tag{7}
\]
where \(d\) is the fixed dimension. Thus every event determined by the exit time and point has \(Q_x^T\)-probability differing from its zero-start probability by at most \(e_T\), uniformly in the thermal ball.

## Uniform nonlinear exit theorem

Let \(\tau^T\) be first exit from \(O\) and \(H^T=X_{\tau^T}\). For any disjoint neighborhoods \(U_\pm\) of \(q_\pm\), and any
\[
 C>\frac1{2\lambda},
\]
there are \(T_0>0\) and a positive constant
\[
 p_*=\exp[-3(K+\log2)]
\]
such that, for \(0<T<T_0\),
\[
 \inf_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}
 P_x^T\{H^T\in U_\pm,\ \tau^T\le C\log(1/T)\}
 \ge p_*.                                                \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}
 P_x^T\{\operatorname{dist}(H^T,\{q_+,q_-\})>\eta\}
 \longrightarrow0,                                      \tag{9}
\]
\[
 \sup_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}
 P_x^T\left\{\left|\frac{\tau^T}{\log(1/T)}
                          -\frac1{2\lambda}\right|>\eta\right\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{10}
\]

Proof. Apply (1) with \(\epsilon=\sqrt{2T}\). The zero-start probability of either event in (8) tends to one half, and the zero-start probabilities of the exceptional events in (9)-(10) tend to zero. Equation (7) gives the same conclusions under \(Q_x^T\), uniformly in \(x\). For small \(T\), each branch event has \(Q_x^T\)-probability at least \(1/3\); (5) proves (8). For a bad event \(B_T\), let
\(a_T=P_0^T(B_T)+e_T\to0\). Equation (6) gives the explicit uniform bound
\[
 \sup_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}P_x^T(B_T)
       \le\frac{K+\log2}{\log(1/a_T)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves (9)-(10). The cutoff agrees with the original model until exit, so these are its stopped-law statements as well. This completes the nonlinear estimate requested in equation (20) of the preceding record.

The logarithmic-time nonlinear control comes from the verified nonlinear theorem (1), not from replacing the original dynamics by its linearization. The new comparison spends entropy only on a fixed initial interval; its cost remains bounded when the exit time diverges.

## Concentration on a branch and fixed-time continuation

Fix small disjoint branch neighborhoods \(U_\pm^0\). Their probabilities are bounded below by (8). For every smaller radius \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}
 P_x^T\{|H^T-q_\pm|>\eta\mid H^T\in U_\pm^0\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{11}
\]
Indeed the numerator is bounded by a bad event in (9), while the denominator is at least \(p_*\) for small \(T\). The neighborhoods are chosen small enough that a point in \(U_+^0\) cannot be close to \(q_-\), and conversely.

Let \(\phi_t\) be the deterministic flow of the original model. Suppose a fixed \(h<\infty\) places \(\phi_h(q_\pm)\) strictly inside desired handoff regions \({\cal H}_\pm\), and the finite paths \(\phi_{[0,h]}(q_\pm)\) have a fixed positive margin inside a compact region on which the coefficients are Lipschitz. For a prescribed path tolerance \(\delta>0\), continuous dependence first supplies a radius \(\eta\) such that deterministic paths from \(|y-q_\pm|<\eta\) stay within \(\delta/2\) of these paths. The strong Markov property at \(\tau^T\), followed by the stopped finite-time Gronwall estimate, gives
\[
 \sup_{|y-q_\pm|<\eta}
 P_y^T\{\sup_{t\le h}|X_t-\phi_t(y)|>\delta/2\}
       \le C_{h,\delta}T.                                \tag{12}
\]
Reflection, if needed along the continuation, is treated with its interval reflection map; for the candidate one can instead choose compact finite branch segments wholly inside the endpoints.

Combining (8), (11), and (12) yields
\[
 \inf_{|x|\le c\sqrt T}
 P_x^T\{H^T\in U_\pm^0,\ X_{\tau^T+h}\in{\cal H}_\pm,
                 \ \tau^T\le C\log(1/T)\}
 \ge p_*/2                                               \tag{13}
\]
for small enough \(T\). The same argument controls every declared finite path condition with a strict margin, not only the endpoint. Conditional on either branch event, failure of that continuation tends to zero uniformly. A branch probability of one half is not asserted for thermal-ball starts.

## Matching the previously constructed high and low corridors

For the candidate, the parallel drift is
\[
 \dot a=-W'(a)-(U-1+a)-k(V-1+a),\quad
 \dot U=-(U-1+a),\quad \dot V=-k(V-1+a).
\]
At \(k=0\), the two strong unstable branches have \(V=7/20\). The high branch enters the invariant rectangle
\(0\le1-a,U\le7/20\), whose only lower-energy critical point is
\((a,U)=(1,0)\). The low branch enters
\(0\le a\le13/20,\ 7/20\le U\le1\), whose only lower-energy critical point is
\((0,1)\). Energy decreases strictly on the nonconstant branches, and the squared-gradient integral is finite on these compact rectangles. Their limit points are therefore critical; since the energy has already fallen below the saddle's, convergence is to the indicated minimum. This restates the elementary branch argument with its compactness and exclusion conditions.

Choose one finite \(h\) large enough that both endpoints have strict margins:
on the high branch \(1-a,U<1/40\), on the low branch \(a<1/20,U>19/20\), while \(V=7/20\). The local strong unstable curve and its transverse exit points depend continuously on sufficiently small \(k\ge0\). This follows from the contraction (2): at \(k=0\) the complementary spectrum is nonpositive, and the positive eigenvalue is still separated from it; the same weighted integral estimates remain contracting with the stable denominator \(2\gamma+\beta\) replaced, if necessary, by \(2\gamma\). Differentiating the fixed-point equation or its convergent iteration gives parameter continuity. Finite-time ODE continuity then preserves the endpoint margins and \(3/10<V<2/5\) for all sufficiently small fixed positive \(k\).

Choose such a \(k\), additionally \(k\le7/100\), once and for all. The high endpoint is inside the first record's box \({\cal H}_+\), and the low endpoint inside \({\cal H}_-\). The high box obeys \(F(t)\le F(0)e^{-kt/2}\), \(F=(1-a)+2U+2V\); the low box keeps \(V\ge1/4\). Small full-dimensional neighborhoods and finite path corridors retain these strict margins. Therefore (13) supplies the required local stochastic bridge into both handoff regions for this fixed-\(N\), fixed-coefficient matter candidate.

This is an existence choice of a sufficiently small positive \(k\) and finite \(h\), determined by strict deterministic inequalities. No particular numerical \(k\), finite-temperature threshold, or experimentally useful lower bound is claimed.

## Actual stopping-time entrance laws

Let \(\sigma_T\) be any stopping time of the original process such that, on its entrance event, the whitened displacement from the saddle belongs to \(\overline G_T=\{|y|\le c\sqrt T\}\). Conditional on the full history at \(\sigma_T\), the future law is the original law started at the actual entrance state. Uniformity of (8)-(13) therefore permits integration against this conditional distribution. The two branch lower bounds survive without assuming a Gaussian entrance law, a symmetric entrance distribution, independence from prior history, or a reset.

In original Euclidean coordinates, an entrance condition \(|Z-Z_*|\le c\sqrt T\) implies the whitened condition with \(c\) replaced by \(c\|M_N^{-1/2}\|\). This is still a fixed constant at fixed \(N\). It can worsen the lower bound and is not a network-size-uniform assertion.

The independent partition factor in the first record does not change this continuous process. A new coordinate-dependent mark factor or an interacting link law can change the drift or its switching semantics. Such changes require a new hypothesis check; this proof does not automatically cover them.
