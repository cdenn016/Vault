## 1. K. G. Wilson, Confinement of quarks, Phys. Rev. D 10 (1974)

**Status: EXISTING, key `WilsonConfinement1974`.** Its fields (vol. 10, no. 8, pp. 2445-2459, 1974) match Crossref. A `doi = {10.1103/PhysRevD.10.2445}` could be added.

**Verified locator.** I read the APS-original PDF (a copy served at `hep.physics.uoc.gr/Erasmus-IP-2013/files/lectures/Rabinovici/Wilson.pdf`) from page images. The non-Abelian plaquette action is Eq. (3.29), p. 2452, in Sec. III.B (Quantization), in the paragraph that extends the lattice quantization to non-Abelian groups after Eq. (3.24). It reads $A_B=\frac{1}{2g^2}\sum_{n\mu\nu}\mathrm{Tr}\,U_A(b_{n\mu}\,b_{n+\hat\mu,\nu}\,b_{n+\hat\nu,\mu}^{-1}\,b_{n\nu}^{-1})$. In words: the action is a sum over elementary lattice squares of the trace of the ordered product of the four link group elements around the square, with $U_A$ the adjoint representation (Wilson remarks that any representation will do), and it is invariant under the lattice gauge transformations (3.25)-(3.28). Because the sum runs over ordered pairs $(\mu,\nu)$, each square enters twice, once per orientation, which the factor $1/2$ compensates (my reading of the normalization). Wilson states that in the classical continuum limit ($b_{n\mu}$ an infinitesimal group element $g a A_\mu$) it reduces to the Yang-Mills action. The U(1) version is Eq. (3.9), p. 2449, Sec. III.A (Classical action on a lattice): $A_B=\frac{1}{2g^2}\sum e^{if_{n\mu\nu}}$ with the lattice field strength $f_{n\mu\nu}=B_{n\mu}+B_{n+\hat\mu,\nu}-B_{n+\hat\nu,\mu}-B_{n\nu}$ (Eq. 3.8); its small-$f$ expansion is (3.10)-(3.11).

**Convention to keep in mind.** Wilson's $A$ is the exponent of the Boltzmann weight (the averages in Eqs. 3.13 and 3.34-3.35 contain $e^{A}$), so $A_B=-S_{\rm Euclid}$ up to an additive constant. The positive sign of the plaquette sum in (3.29) is therefore not a sign error.

**Not verified.** Nothing outstanding for this locator.

## 2. J. Kogut and L. Susskind, Hamiltonian formulation of Wilson's lattice gauge theories, Phys. Rev. D 11 (1975)

**Status: EXISTING, key `KogutSusskind1975`.** Fields (vol. 11, no. 2, pp. 395-408) match Crossref. A `doi = {10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395}` could be added.

**Locator: NOT CONFIRMED.** The paper has no open copy (APS and ADS were not retrievable or list only the publisher link; OSTI offers no full text; Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex report closed access; the INSPIRE record has no attached document), so I could not read it and cannot give a section number for the time-like-plaquette or transfer-matrix derivation. I will not guess one. What is established: the ADS abstract says the paper presents Wilson's model as a canonical Hamiltonian theory of coupled rigid rotators, with gauge-invariant states built from strings of electric flux. Creutz's textbook (item 3, Chapter 15, first paragraph) describes the paper as keeping time continuous with space discrete and working in the temporal gauge $A_0=0$ to define the Hamiltonian directly. Creutz's 1977 abstract (INSPIRE record 110460) says that paper uses the transfer matrix to relate the Wilson and Kogut-Susskind approaches and extracts the Kogut-Susskind Hamiltonian from the transfer matrix in the gauge $A_0=0$ as the time-like lattice spacing goes to zero. This suggests, but does not prove, that the transfer-matrix derivation of the electric term is Creutz's rather than Kogut and Susskind's.

**Verified substitutes for the claim "the electric term comes from the time-like plaquettes via the transfer matrix in temporal gauge":**

Wilson 1974, Sec. III.B, Eqs. (3.17)-(3.24) (pp. 2450-2451), for U(1): the partition function is $Z=\mathrm{Tr}\,V^{2N+1}$ (3.17), the operator $V=\int\prod dB_{n0}\,e^{U}$ (3.18) acts on functions of the spatial link angles, and its exponent $U$ (3.19)-(3.21) contains the spatial plaquettes shared between adjacent time slices together with the time-like plaquettes $f_{ni0}$ that couple the two slices; the temporal links $B_{n0}$ are integrated inside $V$; the Hamiltonian is defined by $V=e^{-aH}$ (3.23)-(3.24). This is the fixed-spacing version; Wilson does not take the continuous-time limit.

Creutz 1977, key `Creutz1977`, already in the bib: the abstract quoted above. I have not read its sections.

Creutz 1983, Chapter 15 (item 3): the full derivation, with equation numbers.

**Recommendation.** Cite Kogut-Susskind for the Hamiltonian formulation itself; cite `Creutz1977` and Creutz 1983 Ch. 15 (and Wilson Sec. III.B for U(1)) for the transfer-matrix derivation of the electric term, unless the section of the 1975 paper is confirmed from a copy.

## 3. M. Creutz, Quarks, Gluons and Lattices (Cambridge University Press, 1983)

**Status: EXISTING, key `Creutz1983`.** The hardback ISBN in the entry (978-0521244053) matches a trade listing of the 1983 hardback. The book is in Cambridge Monographs on Mathematical Physics (vol. 8) and was reissued open access in 2022 (CC BY-NC-ND, DOI 10.1017/9781009290395); the reissue is the original text, and its chapter pagination agrees with the table of contents of the 1985 paperback.

**Verified locator.** I read the open-access reissue (PDF served by INSPIRE, record 205398). The chapter on the Wilson action is **Chapter 7, "Lattice gauge theory", pp. 34-38**. It introduces a group element $U_{ij}\in G$ on each nearest-neighbor bond with $U_{ji}=U_{ij}^{-1}$ (7.1)-(7.2), writes $U_{ij}=e^{iga A_\mu}$ (7.3), and states that Wilson's action is a sum over all elementary squares (7.5), with the action on each square (7.6)
$S_\square=\beta\,[\,1-\tfrac1n\,\mathrm{Re\,Tr}(U_{ij}U_{jk}U_{kl}U_{li})\,]$,
$n$ being the dimension of the matrices, the trace allowed in any representation, and the additive constant chosen so the action vanishes at the identity. Eqs. (7.7)-(7.10) show the classical continuum limit to $\int \mathrm{Tr}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$, Eq. (7.11) gives $\beta=2n/g_0^2$, and Eq. (7.12) is the partition function $Z=\int(dU)e^{-S(U)}$ with the invariant measure and no gauge-fixing term.

The Kogut-Susskind Hamiltonian is derived from the Wilson action in **Chapter 15, "The Hamiltonian approach", pp. 101-107**: separate time spacing $a_0$ and couplings $\beta_s,\beta_t$ on space-like and time-like plaquettes (15.1)-(15.8); temporal gauge, so that a time-like plaquette becomes a coupling between two space-like links at consecutive times, with action (15.9); transfer operator $T$ with $Z=\mathrm{Tr}\,T^N$ (15.10) and kernel (15.16); the limit $a_0\to0$ in (15.20)-(15.40) gives $T=K\exp(-a_0H)$, and Eq. (15.41) is the Kogut-Susskind Hamiltonian, whose first term (quadratic Casimirs $I_{ij}^2$ of the link generators) is the electric-field term arising from the time-like plaquettes and whose second term (space-like plaquettes) is the magnetic term. Gauss's law appears as Eq. (15.45).

(2)-(4):** integrating out $\Phi$ gives $\int DU\,D\Phi\,e^{-S}\propto\int DU\,e^{-S_{\rm ind}[U]}$ (2); the exact induced action is $S_{\rm ind}[U]=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}\ln\big(\delta_{x,y}-m_0^{-2}\sum_\mu U_\mu(x)\otimes U_\mu^\dagger(x)\,\delta_{x+\mu,y}\big)$ (3); and its expansion in $1/m_0$ is a sum over lattice loops $\Gamma$ of a scalar particle in the gauge background, $S_{\rm ind}[U]=-\tfrac12\sum_\Gamma|\mathrm{tr}\,U[\Gamma]|^2/(l[\Gamma]\,m_0^{2l[\Gamma]})$ (4), with $l[\Gamma]$ the loop length. **Eq. (9)** gives the induced Yang-Mills coupling in the local limit in four dimensions, $1/g_0^2\to (N/96\pi^2)\ln(\Lambda^2/m^2)$ (Eqs. 5-8 are the derivation; the trace of $\Phi$ is kept as a dynamical variable, footnote 1).

**Caveat stated by the authors.** The induced action is a sum over all closed loops with the adjoint-type character $|\mathrm{tr}\,U|^2$, not the elementary-plaquette Wilson action. The authors remark (just after Eq. 10) that the loops not being elementary plaquettes seems unimportant, since the same ambiguity exists in Wilson's theory, and that what matters is that the loops are small, which corresponds to heavy inducing fields. The leading non-constant term at large $m_0$ is the shortest nontrivial loop, the elementary plaquette (my deduction from Eq. 4, not stated in the paper).

**Verified locator** (arXiv v3, the Annals reprint; text read, every numbered theorem located and the statements of Theorems 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and the open questions read). The Laplacian on a vector bundle with connection $\phi$ over a finite graph is $(\Delta f)(v)=\sum_{v'\sim v}\big(f(v)-\phi_{vv'}f(v')\big)=(d^*d f)(v)$ (Sec. 3.2). A cycle-rooted spanning forest (CRSF) is a spanning subgraph each of whose components has as many edges as vertices (a tree plus one edge, a unicycle) (Sec. 4.1).

- **Theorem 1 (Sec. 4.2)**, due to Forman and reproved by Kenyon with Cauchy-Binet: for a line bundle (connection with values in $\mathbb C^*$) on a connected finite graph, $\det\Delta=\sum_{\rm CRSFs}\prod_{\rm cycles}(2-w-1/w)$, where $w,1/w$ are the monodromies of the two orientations of a cycle. It holds for any $\mathbb C^*$ connection, unitary or not; for unitary ones $\Delta$ is Hermitian positive semidefinite and the weights give a probability measure (Sec. 5, determinantal by Theorem 2).
- **Theorems 5, 6, 7 (Secs. 7.1, 7.2, 8)**: the same with edge conductances (extra factor $\prod c(e)$); Forman's version on directed graphs with oriented CRSFs; and the Dirichlet-boundary version (essential CRSFs).
- **Theorem 9 (Sec. 9.2)**: for a $\mathbb C^2$-bundle with $SL_2(\mathbb C)$ connection, $\mathrm{Qdet}\,\Delta=\sum_{\rm CRSFs}\prod_{\rm cycles}\big(2-\mathrm{tr}(w)\big)$, where $\mathrm{tr}(w)$ is the trace of the monodromy of the cycle. The determinant here is the quaternion-type determinant Qdet of a self-dual matrix (defined in Sec. 9.1; Theorem 8, attributed to Mehta's Random Matrices, 3rd ed., gives $\mathrm{Qdet}(M)=\mathrm{Pf}(Z\hat M)$, where $\hat M$ is the associated $2n\times2n$ complex matrix and $Z$ is block diagonal with blocks $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$; Theorem 10 is the noncommutative Cauchy-Binet formula used in the proof). Self-duality is exactly the condition $\phi_{vv'}^{-1}=\widetilde{\phi_{vv'}}$ (adjugate), which holds for $SL_2$ and fails for $GL_2$ in general. From Theorem 8 and $\det Z=1$, the ordinary determinant of the $2n\times2n$ matrix equals $(\mathrm{Qdet})^2$; this last remark is my deduction, not a statement of the paper.
- **Other groups:** not covered. Sec. 14, open question 2, asks whether the vector bundle Laplacian has any combinatorial meaning for higher-rank bundles. So there is no CRSF determinant formula in the paper for $GL(K)$ or $SU(N)$ with $K,N\ge3$.

## 7. W. J. Culver, On the existence and uniqueness of the real logarithm of a matrix, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 17 (1966)

**Status: EXISTING, key `culver1966existence`.** Fields (vol. 17, no. 5, pp. 1146-1151, 1966) match Crossref. A `doi = {10.1090/S0002-9939-1966-0202740-6}` could be added.

**Verified locator** (AMS PDF read in full). **Theorem 1, p. 1149 (Sec. 4):** for a real square matrix $C$, there is a real $X$ with $C=e^X$ if and only if $C$ is nonsingular and each elementary divisor (Jordan block) of $C$ belonging to a negative eigenvalue occurs an even number of times. The proof of necessity uses that a real $X$ has complex Jordan blocks in conjugate pairs and that blocks of $e^X$ at a negative eigenvalue come from non-real eigenvalues of $X$; sufficiency constructs a real $Y$ with the required elementary divisors and a similarity. **Theorem 2, p. 1150:** the real solution is unique if and only if all eigenvalues of $C$ are positive real and no elementary divisor of $C$ belonging to any eigenvalue appears more than once. The Corollary on p. 1151 separates the countable and uncountable cases of non-uniqueness.

**Not verified.** Nothing outstanding.

They are used in Sec. 2: the stationarity conditions of the Gaussian free energy are Eq. The introduction illustrates the method with $\exp\begin{pmatrix}-A^\top&Q\\0&A\end{pmatrix}t=\begin{pmatrix}F_1&G_1\\0&F_2\end{pmatrix}$, the standard route to the weighted integral $\int_0^\Delta e^{A^\top s}Qe^{As}ds$, and states that the integrals (1.1)-(1.4) of the report are read off from submatrices of $\exp(Ct)$ at $t=\Delta$.
