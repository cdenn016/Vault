## open:glink-higher-rank-forests (g11 lines 800–811; ledger row gb_backmatter 143)

**Verdict: R16 VIOLATED as posed. The editor's concern (1) is right.** The "no identity" branch is met by a degree count for every $K\ge2$ and every multigraph, and the editor's triangle formula is correct.

Derivation 1 (degree count). With $A_i=a_i\mathsf G$, $W_e=w_e\mathsf G$, $\Lambda(\mathring\omega)$ is a homogeneous linear function of the scalars $(a_i,w_e)$ with matrix coefficients, so $\det\Lambda$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $NK$ in the scalars, nonzero for $a_i>0$. A cycle-rooted spanning forest of the root-augmented multigraph ($N+1$ vertices) has $N+1$ edges (an essential one, $N$), so each term of the posed right-hand side has degree at most $N+1$ in the scalars. For $K\ge2$ and $N\ge2$, $N+1<NK$; for $N=1$ (loops only) and $K\ge3$, $2<K$. Two polynomials of different degrees that agree for all positive scalars at fixed links are both zero; $\det\Lambda>0$. Hence no identity of the posed form exists for any $K\ge2$ and any multigraph in the class, with no computation beyond this count. The branch is met by a trivial construction, which R16 excludes.

Derivation 2 (triangle, $K=3$, confirming the editor). Isometric links in $\GL^+(3)$ are $\mathsf G^{1/2}$-conjugates of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$; vertex-wise isometric gauge transformations preserve isotropic data and $\det\Lambda$, so set the two tree links to $I$ and the third to the holonomy $\mathring\Theta_p\sim\operatorname{diag}(1,R(\phi))$. $\Lambda$ splits into the axis line bundle (trivial holonomy, determinant $\alpha=\det(\operatorname{diag}(a)+L^{\mathrm{scalar}})$) and the plane, whose real $6\times6$ determinant is the square of the complex line-bundle determinant with holonomy $e^{\mathrm i\phi}$; by Cauchy–Binet the latter is $\alpha+\beta(2-2\cos\phi)$ with $\beta=w_{e_0}w_{e_1}w_{e_2}$ (the three-edge row set gives $\lvert1-e^{\mathrm i\phi}\rvert^2\beta$, every other row set is a rooted forest with unimodular determinant). With $\mathfrak Q_p=3-\Tr\mathring\Theta_p=2-2\cos\phi$: $\det\Lambda=\alpha(\alpha+\beta\mathfrak Q_p)^2$. Probe D1: exact agreement for random $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ links. Quadratic in the single cycle invariant with the cross term $2\alpha^2\beta\mathfrak Q_p$: not a single forest sum with one cycle function, independently of the degree count.

What survives. (a) Cycle rank one is settled in a $K$-tuple form: the single holonomy $H\in\mathrm O(K)$ (after congruence) is diagonalized over $\mathbb C$ by a global unitary, $\Lambda$ splits into $K$ line-bundle Laplacians with holonomies $\lambda_k$, $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert=1$, and Kenyon's conductance and Dirichlet versions (citations.md item 6 records Theorems 5 and 7; the combined statement must be checked before citing) give $\det\Lambda=\prod_k(\alpha+\beta_pq_k)$, $q_k=2-\lambda_k-\bar\lambda_k$, $=\sum_j\alpha^{K-j}\beta_p^je_j(q)$, each $e_j$ a class function of $H$. (b) The posed wording "cycle-rooted spanning forests of the augmented multigraph" admits cycles through the root, whose "holonomy" (a single link) is not conjugation-invariant; the forests must be essential (components meeting the root are trees). (c) By Cauchy–Binet the coefficient of a scalar monomial $\prod_ew_e^{m_e}\prod_ia_i^{m_i}$ ($0\le m\le K$, $\sum m=NK$) depends only on the links in its support and is invariant under vertex-wise isometric gauge, so a first-fundamental-theorem argument for simultaneous orthogonal conjugation (Procesi 1976 is the expected source; not checked here) would make it a polynomial in Wilson loops of the support. The forest indexing and explicit coefficients are the open content.

Recommended re-posing (both branches nontrivial). Class: connected multigraphs of cycle rank at least two, self terms $a_i\mathsf G$, weights $w_e\mathsf G$, $a_i,w_e>0$, isometric links, rank $K\ge3$. Settled by an identity $\det\Lambda(\mathring\omega)=\sum_{(F_1,\ldots,F_K)}\prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{e\in F_k}\mathrm c_e\,\Phi_{F_1\cdots F_K}(\mathring\omega)$ over $K$-tuples of essential cycle-rooted spanning forests of the root-augmented multigraph, $\mathrm c_e$ the scalar of the edge ($w_e$ or $a_i$), each $\Phi$ a polynomial in the traces of holonomies of closed walks supported on the cycles of $F_1,\ldots,F_K$, equal to one for cycle-free tuples; or a multigraph in the class and a $K\ge3$ at which the coefficient of some scalar monomial of $\det\Lambda$ is nonzero but is not realized by any $K$-tuple of essential cycle-rooted spanning forests with the monomial's multiplicities. The cycle-rank-one exclusion is justified by (a); the degree count is built in; Kenyon's Theorems 1 and 9 cover $K\le2$ only; the "no" branch needs a cycle-rank-two computation at $K=3$ that was not attempted here. Text, ledger row 143, and the chapter's scope sentence must change together. Fallback if the author declines the $K$-tuple form: delete the open problem and its ledger row; the scope paragraph already declines a forest expansion in rank three or higher, which then stands as the declined claim.

## open:glink-unanchored-normalization (g11 lines 813–824; ledger row gb_backmatter 144)

**Verdict: R16 VIOLATED as posed. The editor's concern (2) is right:** at $K=1$ the "integrable" branch holds for every multigraph in the class, by a short Cauchy–Binet argument, so the problem "at a stated $K\ge1$" is settled by it.

Derivation ($K=1$). Let the multigraph be connected with cycle rank $c\ge2$, $w_e>0$, $A_i=0$, $\mathsf h=0$, $\nu$ Gaussian on $\R^{E_+}$ with positive-definite covariance. Then $\Lambda=\delta^\top W_\oplus\delta$ with rows $\delta_e=\mathrm e_i-e^{\mathring\omega_e}\mathrm e_j$, and Cauchy–Binet gives $\det\Lambda=\sum_{S\subseteq E_+,\lvert S\rvert=N}\prod_{e\in S}w_e(\det\delta_S)^2$, every term nonnegative. Fix a spanning tree $T$ and the non-tree copies $f_1,\ldots,f_c$. For $S=T\cup\{f_k\}$ the subgraph is unicyclic; expanding $\det\delta_S$ along a leaf column gives a factor $1$ or $-e^{\mathring\omega_e}$ and removes the leaf, and the cycle that remains has determinant $1-h_k$ by eq:glink-cycle-determinant at $K=1$, with $h_k=e^{s_k}$, $s_k=(\mathbf C\mathring\omega)_k$ the fundamental-cycle sum. Hence $\det\Lambda\ge w_{\min}^Ne^{-2\sum_e\lvert\mathring\omega_e\rvert}\sum_{k=1}^c(1-e^{s_k})^2$, and $(1-e^s)^2\ge e^{-2}\min(s^2,1)$ for real $s$, so $\det\Lambda^{-1/2}\le\mathrm c_0e^{\sum_e\lvert\mathring\omega_e\rvert}\max(\lvert s\rvert^{-1},1)$ with $s=\mathbf C\mathring\omega\in\R^c$. The cycle map is surjective (identity on the non-tree coordinates), so in linear coordinates $\mathring\omega\leftrightarrow(s,t)$ the Gaussian density is bounded by $\mathrm c_1e^{-\kappa(\lvert s\rvert^2+\lvert t\rvert^2)}$ and the exponential factor by $e^{\mathrm c_2(\lvert s\rvert+\lvert t\rvert)}$; the integral is finite exactly because $\int_{\lvert s\rvert\le1}\lvert s\rvert^{-1}ds=\lvert S^{c-1}\rvert\int_0^1r^{c-2}dr<\infty$ for $c\ge2$. For $c=1$ the same bound diverges, consistent with cor:glink-unanchored-cycle. Probe E1 (theta graph, $K=1$): the lower bound never exceeds $\det\Lambda$ over 2000 samples (ratio at most $0.83$).

Status of $K\ge2$. The singular set is the set of generator families whose fundamental-cycle holonomies share a nonzero fixed vector; its generic stratum (one-dimensional common fixed space) has codimension $(c-1)K+1\ge K+1$, the stratum with $f$-dimensional fixed space codimension $f((c-1)K+f)$, and where $\det\Lambda$ vanishes to order $2f$ the integrand $\det^{-1/2}\sim\operatorname{dist}^{-f}$ is locally integrable since $f<f((c-1)K+f)$. This count favors integrability for every $K$ at $c\ge2$, but it assumes the Łojasiewicz exponent $2f$ along each stratum and says nothing about the singular set at infinity in the non-compact group, so neither branch at $K\ge2$ is met by an existing statement or a trivial construction. This is my assessment, not a result.

Recommended re-posing. Record the $K=1$ result as an ESTABLISHED proposition (for instance after cor:glink-unanchored-cycle, with the derivation above as proof) and restrict the open problem to "at a stated $K\ge2$" with the settling condition otherwise unchanged: for connected multigraphs of cycle rank at least two with positive-definite weights, zero self terms, zero information vector, and a product Gaussian base law with positive-definite covariances, a proof that $\int\nu(d\mathring\omega)\det\Lambda(\mathring\omega)^{-1/2}<\infty$ for every such multigraph and law at a stated $K\ge2$, or a multigraph and law in the class at which it diverges. Update ledger row 144, the forward pointer at g11 line 591 ("Whether more cycles restore integrability") to say that for $K=1$ they do, and the scope sentence at lines 38–39.

## open:glink-chart-infimum (g11 lines 826–838; ledger row gb_backmatter 145)

**Verdict: R16 VIOLATED as posed. Derivation. Write $\mathbf l(\mathbf M)=\E\mathsf E_e(\cdot;\mathbf M)$, strictly convex quadratic with unique minimizer $\mathbf M^\star$, singular, $\Tr\mathbf M^\star<0$ (prop:glink-sample-record(iii)); the chart links $\mathscr C=\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(K))$ are the real invertible matrices in which every Jordan block of every negative eigenvalue occurs an even number of times (Culver, Theorem 1, already cited at g11 line 281 and ca_proofs 462). Suppose $\mathbf M\in\mathscr C$ attains $\inf_{\mathscr C}\mathbf l$. Step 1: $\mathbf M$ has a negative eigenvalue; otherwise every real matrix near $\mathbf M$ has no eigenvalue in $(-\infty,0]$, lies in $\mathscr C$, so $\nabla\mathbf l(\mathbf M)=0$, $\mathbf M=\mathbf M^\star$, contradicting invertibility (the first case of the $K=2$ proof). Step 2 ($K=3$): $\det\mathbf M>0$ and Culver leave only the spectrum $\{\mathrm c',\mathrm c',\lambda\}$ with $\mathrm c'<0<\lambda$ and two $1\times1$ blocks at $\mathrm c'$ (a $2\times2$ block would occur once), so $\mathbf M=\mathbf S\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm c',\mathrm c',\lambda)\mathbf S^{-1}$, as the editor states. For $\mathbf E=\mathbf S\widetilde{\mathbf E}\mathbf S^{-1}$ the eigenvalues of $\mathbf M+t\mathbf E$ near $\mathrm c'$ are $\mathrm c'+t\nu(t)$, where by the Schur complement against the $\lambda$ block $\nu$ solves $\det\bigl(\nu I_2-\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{11}-t\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{12}(\mathrm c'+t\nu-\lambda-t\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{22})^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{21}\bigr)=0$; when $\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{11}$ has non-real (hence distinct, simple) eigenvalues, the implicit function theorem continues them to non-real $\nu(t)$ for small $\lvert t\rvert$ of either sign, while the eigenvalue near $\lambda$ stays real and positive. So on $U=\{\mathbf E:(\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{11}-\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{22})^2+4\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{12}\widetilde{\mathbf E}_{21}<0\}$ (the inequality of the $K=2$ proof applied to the $\mathrm c'$ block), open, nonempty, invariant under $\mathbf E\mapsto-\mathbf E$, the matrices $\mathbf M+t\mathbf E$ are chart links for all small $t\ne0$. Minimality gives $\langle\nabla\mathbf l(\mathbf M),\mathbf E\rangle=0$ on $U$, hence $\nabla\mathbf l(\mathbf M)=0$, the contradiction of Step 1. Probe F1 confirms the two-sided chart-link property on a random instance. Step 3 (every $K$, semisimple negative spectrum): with eigenspaces $V_l$ of even dimension at the negative eigenvalues $\mathrm c'_l$, take $U=\{\mathbf E:$ every compression $P_l\mathbf E\lvert_{V_l}$ has distinct non-real eigenvalues$\}$ and the same Schur reduction; the conclusion is identical. Step 4 (what remains): a minimizer would need a negative eigenvalue with a Jordan block of size at least two occurring an even number of times, which needs $K\ge4$; there the first-order argument fails (Puiseux branches), and leading-order Lidskii-type perturbation theory suggests the same conclusion but is not carried out here.

Recommended re-posing. Then restrict the open problem to the class $K\ge4$, $W_e,\Sigma_j\succ0$, $\Tr\mathbf M^\star<0$, settled by a proof that no chart link having a negative eigenvalue with a Jordan block of size at least two minimizes $\mathbf l$ over the chart links (which with the semisimple case gives non-attainment for every $K$), or by data in the class and such a minimizing chart link. Neither branch is met by an existing statement; the "no" branch needs a perturbation lemma at a non-semisimple boundary point of $\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(K))$, the "yes" branch a construction.
