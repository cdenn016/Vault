## Independent integral

Write local configuration y=m+Lz with standard normal z and residual maps R_0,R_1. Let D=(R_1^T H R_1-R_0^T H R_0)/(2 tau). The label-zero logit is Z=z^T A z+b^T z+c, where A=L^T D L, b=2L^T Dm, and c=m^T Dm+log(pi_0/pi_1). A tiny comparison with source_log_posterior checks sign and indexing.

For eigenvalues lambda of A and rotated b denoted gamma, completing each Gaussian square gives
phi_Z(t)=exp(itc-(1/2)sum log(1-2it lambda_k)-(t^2/2)sum gamma_k^2/(1-2it lambda_k)).
Each individual principal logarithm is continuous from zero; the principal log of their product is not interchangeable with their sum.

For independent standard logistic L_g, E sigmoid(Z)=P(Z-L_g>=0). Substituting u=sigmoid(x) in its density integral gives the beta integral B(1+it,1-it)=pi t/sinh(pi t). Continuous-distribution characteristic-function inversion at zero therefore yields
E sigmoid(Z)=1/2+integral_0^infinity Im(phi_Z(t))/sinh(pi t) dt.
The removable value at zero is (c+trace A)/pi. Finite first moments justify interchange near zero; exponential decay and |phi_Z|<=1 control the tail. Beyond cutoff 12 the absolute tail is bounded by (2/pi)atanh(exp(-12pi)).

Use SciPy adaptive quadrature with absolute/relative tolerance 1e-10 and at most 200 subdivisions. Its error estimate is reported separately from the analytic tail bound; it is not a rigorous enclosure. Stop on integration warnings, reported error above 1e-9 or probability outside (0,1); do not clip or silently retry rows. Extreme probabilities may suffer cancellation, so no relative-accuracy claim follows.

The inversion formula is equation (21) in section 3.1 of Witkovsky, Wimmer and Duby, [Computing the aggregate loss distribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08299), citing the original [Gil-Pelaez (1951), Biometrika 38, 481-482](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/38.3-4.481). The Gaussian/logistic specialization is derived here, not attributed to their actuarial application.
