## TL;DR

The paper computes the entropy production rate $e_p$ of stationary diffusions on $\mathbb{R}^d$ under the single structural requirement that the time-reversed process is again a diffusion, which admits degenerate and hypoelliptic noise and locally Lipschitz coefficients. When the time-irreversible part of the drift lies in the range of the volatility almost everywhere (plus an integrability or martingale condition), the forward and backward path measures are equivalent and $e_p=\int b_{\mathrm{irr}}^\top D^- b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho dx$ with $D^-$ the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse; when the range condition fails on a set of positive stationary measure, the paper states that the path measures are mutually singular and $e_p=+\infty$. Under this unflipped definition underdamped Langevin dynamics has infinite entropy production.

## Problem & setting

The entropy production rate of a stationary Markov process is the relative entropy per unit time between the path measures of the process and of its time reversal, and it vanishes exactly at detailed balance. For an Itô equation $dx_t=b(x_t)dt+\sigma(x_t)dw_t$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $\sigma:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$ and $D=\sigma\sigma^\top/2$, Jiang, Qian and Qian (the paper's reference [1], Chapter 4) obtained $e_p=\int b_{\mathrm{irr}}^\top D^{-1}b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho dx$ for globally Lipschitz coefficients and uniformly elliptic noise. The paper removes ellipticity and global Lipschitz continuity. Its standing assumptions are local Lipschitz continuity of $b$ and $\sigma$ with non-explosion (Assumption 3.3), and a stationary density $\rho$ whose distributional derivative $\nabla\cdot(D\rho)$ is a function in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ (Assumption 3.4), which Lemma 3.2 shows is equivalent to the time reversal being a diffusion, following Millet, Nualart and Sanz. It builds on the time-reversal theorems of Haussmann and Pardoux, the Helmholtz decomposition of Graham and Nelson's stationary probability current, Girsanov's theorem, the Stroock-Varadhan support theorem, Villani's hypocoercivity and the GENERIC formalism.

## Method

The entropy production rate is defined as $e_p=\frac{1}{t}H[P_{[0,t]}\mid\bar P_{[0,t]}]$ for any $t>0$ (Definition 2.6, eq. 2), which Theorem 2.5, quoted from Jiang, Qian and Qian, shows is constant in $t$. Propositions 2.8 and 2.10 rewrite it as a stationary average over deterministic initial conditions and as the small-step limit $e_p=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}E_{x\sim\mu}[H[p_\varepsilon(\cdot,x)\mid\bar p_\varepsilon(\cdot,x)]]$ of relative entropies between forward and backward transition kernels. Theorem 3.5 identifies the time-reversed process as a diffusion with the same diffusion tensor and drift $\bar b=-b+2\rho^{-1}\nabla\cdot(D\rho)$ where $\rho>0$ (eq. 6). Proposition 3.7 (eq. 9) states that $\rho$ is stationary if and only if $b=b_{\mathrm{rev}}+b_{\mathrm{irr}}$ with $b_{\mathrm{rev}}=D\nabla\log\rho+\nabla\cdot D$ on $\{\rho>0\}$ and $\nabla\cdot(b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho)=0$, and that $\bar b=b_{\mathrm{rev}}-b_{\mathrm{irr}}$. For smooth strictly positive $\rho$, Proposition 3.10 gives $\nabla\cdot(b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho)=0$ if and only if $b_{\mathrm{irr}}=Q\nabla\log\rho+\nabla\cdot Q$ for a smooth antisymmetric matrix field $Q$. Section 3.5 restates the split at three levels: the generator $L=A+S$ with $A=b_{\mathrm{irr}}\cdot\nabla$ antisymmetric and $S=b_{\mathrm{rev}}\cdot\nabla+D\nabla\cdot\nabla=-\Sigma^*\Sigma$ symmetric in $L^2_\mu$, where $\sqrt2\Sigma f=\sigma^\top\nabla f$ (eq. 16 and the display after it, Proposition 3.12); the backward Kolmogorov equation in the hypocoercive form $\partial_t f_t=(A-\Sigma^*\Sigma)f_t$ (eq. 17); and the Fokker-Planck equation in pre-GENERIC form $\partial_t\rho_t=W(\rho_t)-M_{\rho_t}(dH[\rho_t\mid\rho])$ with $W(\rho_t)=\nabla\cdot(-b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho_t)$ and $M_{\rho_t}(\xi)=-\nabla\cdot(\rho_t D\nabla\xi)$ (eq. 19, Proposition 3.13), so that relative entropy to $\rho$ is conserved by the irreversible part and dissipated by the reversible part. The entropy production theorems come from a change of measure: when $b_{\mathrm{irr}}=\sigma\sigma^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}$, Girsanov's theorem with $\log Z_T=-2\int_0^T\langle\sigma^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}(x_t),dw_t\rangle-2\int_0^T|\sigma^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}(x_t)|^2dt$ (eq. 20) turns the forward drift into the backward drift, and in the opposite case the Stroock-Varadhan support theorem is applied to the Stratonovich form of the two equations.

## Key results

Theorem 4.1 (p. 15, proved in Appendix A.3.1 by localization and Girsanov) states that under Assumptions 3.3 and 3.4, if $b_{\mathrm{irr}}(x)\in\mathrm{Range} \sigma(x)$ for $\rho$-almost every $x$, $\sigma^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}$ is Borel measurable and $\int b_{\mathrm{irr}}^\top D^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho dx<\infty$, then $P_{[0,T]}\sim\bar P_{[0,T]}$ and $e_p=\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}b_{\mathrm{irr}}^\top D^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}\rho(x)dx$. Theorem 4.2 (pp. 16-17, proved) reaches the same conclusion with the integrability condition replaced by $E_P[Z_T]=1$ (eq. 20), and Proposition 4.3 (p. 17, proved) lists six sufficient conditions including Novikov's and Kazamaki's criteria and exponential tails of $|\sigma^-b_{\mathrm{irr}}|^2$ under $\rho$. Theorem 4.4 (p. 17, proof in Appendix A.3.2) states that for $\sigma\in C^2$, if the range condition fails on a set of positive $\rho$-measure then $P_{[0,T]}\perp\bar P_{[0,T]}$ and $e_p=+\infty$. For linear diffusions with drift $-Bx$ and stationary law $N(0,\Pi^{-1})$, where $B=(D+Q)\Pi$, Section 5.1 derives $e_p=-\mathrm{Tr}(D^-BQ)$ when $\mathrm{Range} Q\subseteq\mathrm{Range} \sigma$ (eq. 23) and $e_p=+\infty$ otherwise (eq. 24); Lemma 5.1 (eq. 26, proved) gives the entropy production of an exact simulation with step $\varepsilon$, the analytic limit is omitted by the authors, and agreement is shown by simulation in Figures 3 and 4. Underdamped Langevin dynamics has $e_p=+\infty$ (eq. 29, from Theorem 4.4 and eq. 12), while an exact simulation at positive step has finite entropy production in a quadratic potential, which the authors conjecture also holds for nonlinear potentials (footnote 5). The Euler-Maruyama discretization of underdamped Langevin has infinite entropy production at every step size (eq. 31, argued from the supports in Lemma 5.2), and the BBK scheme satisfies $e_p^{\mathrm{BBK}}\le O(\varepsilon)$ (eq. 33, cited from Katsoulakis, Pantazis and Rey-Bellet, with a binned numerical estimate in Figure 7 reported as consistent). Section 6.2 introduces a generalized entropy production with a phase-space involution $\theta$; with the momentum flip it is zero for underdamped Langevin, and Example 6.1 (p. 27) gives $+\infty$ for the perturbed dynamics when $Q_1\neq0$ and $-\gamma^{-1}\beta\mathrm{Tr}(Q_2M^{-1}Q_2)$ when $Q_1=0$, obtained by following the earlier proofs and not proved separately. The link between mutual singularity and sampling efficiency (Section 6.1) is a stated conjecture, and the extension to manifolds (Section 6.3) is asserted to be straightforward.

## Limits

The authors stress that all theorems concern Definition 2.6, with no involution applied to the reversed path measure, and are physically informative only where that definition is the appropriate one (Remark 2.7, Section 6.2); the authors do not know whether an analog of Theorem 2.5 holds for the generalized rate (footnote 3). The state space is $\mathbb{R}^d$, Proposition 3.10 needs a smooth strictly positive density, and the GENERIC computation assumes $\rho_t,\rho>0$. My observations: the proof of Theorem 4.4 passes from non-inclusion of the supports of $P^x$ and $\bar P^x$ to their mutual singularity without argument, and footnote 2 (singular if and only if not equivalent) is not a valid general statement; non-inclusion of supports does give failure of absolute continuity, which suffices for $e_p=+\infty$, so the entropy conclusion stands while the singularity claim would need more. The step from differing tangent sets at $t=0$ to differing supports is also terse.

## Relevance to this research

> [!note] Editorial: relation to the gauge-theoretic free energy program
> Editorial judgment. This paper gives a rigorous statement of the reversible and irreversible drift split that the particular-partition formalism of Bayesian mechanics takes as given, and its bearing on the gauge-theoretic multi-agent model is confined to the dynamical sector. For Hamiltonian belief dynamics and belief inertia it supplies a concrete constraint: underdamped Langevin dynamics, with noise on the momenta only, is hypoelliptic, its Hamiltonian part is exactly $b_{\mathrm{irr}}=Q\nabla\log\rho$ with $Q=-\beta^{-1}J$, where $J$ is the canonical symplectic matrix of Hamilton's equations $\dot z=J\nabla H$ (Example 3.11 prints $Q$ as $\beta^{-1}$ times the block matrix with $-\mathrm{Id}_n$ upper right and $\mathrm{Id}_n$ lower left), and its unflipped entropy production is infinite, so a dissipation or steady-state claim for inertial belief dynamics would have to be phrased with the momentum-flipped quantity of Section 6.2. It also gives the operator-level reading (antisymmetric plus symmetric generator, GENERIC form with relative entropy as Lyapunov function) that a free-energy descent with a conservative component would need to match. The mapping from a stationary diffusion on $\mathbb{R}^d$ to beliefs on statistical fibers is interpretive, since the paper contains no Fisher metric, no bundle or connection, no Markov blanket, no agents and no coarse-graining. The link is moderate for the Hamiltonian sector and absent for transport, blankets and meta-agents.
