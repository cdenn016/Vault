# Unitary quantum theory as a formal framework for a theory of consciousness

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2016) Unitary quantum theory as a formal framework for a theory of consciousness. In: P. Saran Satsangi and S. Hameroff (Eds.) Consciousness: Integrating Eastern and Western Perspectives. New Delhi: New Age Books (pp. 127-146).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2016-unitary-quantum-theory-as-a-formal-framework-for-a-theory-of-consciousness.pdf)

## Summary

Fields proposes unitary quantum theory as a formal setting for reportable experience. The chapter argues that system boundaries and the assignment of classical information depend on chosen decompositions and quantum reference frames, while the universal description remains unitary. It emphasizes a distinction between dynamical correlations and the semantic attribution of those correlations to particular bounded objects. Reports and memories are treated as classical encodings whose interpretation depends on the observer and context.

The consciousness proposal goes further: Fields suggests identifying awareness with quantum dynamics or entanglement, and viewing experiences as encodings on observer-environment boundaries. He presents this as a candidate framework motivated by a perceived similarity between quantum information and phenomenological reports. The closing discussion calls for experimental techniques connecting entanglement to experience. Broad assertions about ubiquitous observers, noncausal semantics, and physical quantum effects in cognition belong to this interpretation; the chapter supplies neither an operational measure of consciousness nor experimental validation of that identification.

## Relevance to this research

The useful connection to [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]] is the demand to state how a record acquires an observer-relative interpretation. [[Quantum reference frames]] also provides a comparison point for changing descriptions. The research program's classical normalized laws and gauge transports do not inherit entanglement, a Born rule, or phenomenal awareness from this conceptual resemblance; a physical correspondence would require additional explicit structure and evidence.
