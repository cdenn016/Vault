# On recognizing an object with a partition

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2013) On recognizing an object with a partition. Logique et Analyse 56 (224): 391-403.

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2013-on-recognizing-an-object-with-a-partition.pdf)

## Summary

Fields analyzes Smith and Brogaard's proposal that a partition can resolve reference by locating an object within one of its cells. He argues that adequate recognition requires both completeness, including all relevant variants of an enduring object, and exclusivity, excluding aggregates that are not that object. On an objective reading, deciding which variants belong already requires the natural mereotopology that the partition was intended to recover. The resulting appeal is circular in his analysis.

On an epistemic reading, a finite history of observations cannot exclude all larger hidden-state machines compatible with the same observations. Fields applies this unrestricted black-box argument to the possible constituents and histories of objects, and then discusses consistent histories and quantum measurements. The paper explicitly retains the practical usefulness of approximate partitions while rejecting their promotion to completely objective truthmakers. Its strongest quantum-ontological conclusions depend on additional interpretation; they are not required for the finite-observation point. Nor does that point rule out identification when a model class, observational protocol, or error tolerance has been specified.

## Relevance to this research

The argument is relevant to [[Quantum reference frames]] and to operational equivalence in [[Multi-agent variational free energy]]. A declared partition supplies a mathematical domain; it does not by itself certify unique physical reference. This motivates stating which records and distinctions a quotient preserves without claiming to recover an observer-independent decomposition of everything.
