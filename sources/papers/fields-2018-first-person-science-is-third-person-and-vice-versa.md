# First-person science is third person and vice versa

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2018) First-person science is third person and vice versa. Constructivist Foundations 13: 240-241.

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2018-first-person-science-is-third-person-and-vice-versa.pdf)

## Summary

Responding to Kordes and Demsar's account of retrospective experience, Fields argues that science cannot be divided cleanly into first-person and third-person enterprises. Recall is reconstructive, so a feeling of confidence is itself a present experience rather than an independent guarantee about the past. Yet experiments, reports, and even introspective inquiry depend on remembered procedures and outcomes. He frames the resulting tension as a limit paradox: generalizing the constructive character of memory can undermine the evidential practices used to establish it.

The proposed response is methodological. Appeals to historical constraints, bodies, calibration standards, public records, and other people function as third-person reference frames even within first-person science. Internal criticism also coordinates several perspectives within an individual. Fields describes scientific knowledge as negotiated, revisable coordination among these perspectives. This is a conceptual commentary, not a theorem that consensus guarantees truth or that every scientific claim is equally unreliable.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]], the paper clarifies why persistent records and comparison procedures are operational commitments. It motivates separating a receiver's current belief from the protocol that produced a shared transcript. The analogy with [[Quantum reference frames]] concerns reference dependence; it does not identify these social or introspective frames with unitary frame transformations, or establish exact inheritance of a collective history.
