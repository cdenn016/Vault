## Contribution and scope

The authors reformulate active inference using finite quantum systems, holographic interfaces, and quantum reference frames that assign operational meaning to observed bit strings. Their analysis distinguishes prediction error from inaccessible or mismatched boundary sectors and error from insufficient learning within shared sectors. The asymptotic argument explicitly assumes learning operators capable of perfect learning, then argues that removing residual mismatch aligns the agents' reference frames. Perfect prediction of an arbitrary partner quantum process is connected to no-cloning, motivating the claimed transition from separability to entanglement in an isolated composite. The paper describes this as asymptotic agreement between its free energy principle and unitarity. This is a quantum-formalism construction with strong limiting assumptions; it does not derive quantum theory from a classical ELBO or prove convergence of every finite agent's learning dynamics.

## Relevance to this research

This is a priority comparison for [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] and [[Quantum reference frames]]. It shares the focus on prediction across an interface, while the project's normalized joints and explicit recognition families permit different, classical statements. The authors' schematic split of prediction error should not replace an exact KL decomposition without a specified law. Claims about biological use of coherence are proposed consequences and research directions, not established mechanisms for the project's agents.
