## Contribution and scope

This reply clarifies Fields and Levin's view that objects and processes are complementary descriptions of persistence, rather than separate ontological kinds. It answers commentators who defend the pragmatic usefulness of the distinction and accepts that both descriptions can be useful tools. Memory and stable informational structure provide the proposed common language. The reply discusses quantum separability and a classical path-based account of conditional independence, but does not reproduce their technical derivations. It also states an often-missed boundary of the agent interpretation: an isolated universe has no environment or interface and therefore is not an active-inference agent in this account. Claims about generic entangling interactions and persistence remain tied to the modeling assumptions of the underlying papers.

## Relevance to this research

This is relevant to [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], especially the distinction between specifying an agent boundary and explaining its persistence. The project's probability laws and retained records can be compared with this relational framing without importing a universal quantum separability claim. The reply is philosophical clarification; it does not itself construct a dynamical agent hierarchy.
