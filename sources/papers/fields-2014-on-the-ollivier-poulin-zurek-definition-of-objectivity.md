## Contribution and scope

This critique examines whether the Ollivier–Poulin–Zurek criterion can define objectivity without prior knowledge or agreement about the observed system. Fields argues that agreement about a property is insufficient if observers have not established that the same physical referent produced their records. Under decompositional equivalence, an environmental fragment can be consistent with many system/environment decompositions, so its records do not uniquely fix the intended source. The paper concludes that an unrestricted reading of the criterion presupposes the identification it seeks to explain. Its stronger conclusion that decoherence cannot explain classicality is an author-attributed foundational position. The argument addresses the selection and interpretation of a system boundary; it does not negate calculations of decoherence or redundant encoding conditional on an explicitly supplied decomposition.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]], the paper separates agreement in outcomes from agreement in reference. This matters when a group of agents is said to establish a shared world or a parent carrier. The project's explicit record kernels and admission of supplied relational structure avoid claiming assumption-free identification. Its formation standard should therefore be assessed on its own declared probes and selection requirements, rather than equated with either OPZ objectivity or Fields's universal critique.
