# Motion, identity and the bias toward agency

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2014) Motion, identity and the bias toward agency. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 8: 597 [13 pp.] (doi: 10.3389/fnhum.2014.00597).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2014-motion-identity-and-the-bias-toward-agency.pdf)

## Summary

Fields proposes that the human tendency to explain events through agency is maintained by mechanisms for recognizing an individual across gaps in observation. Re-identification requires a plausible account of how the remembered individual acquired its current features and location. The proposed fictive causal history supplies this missing interval. If agents are the earliest available representations of hidden causes, agency becomes a default component of such histories.

The paper reviews developmental, behavioral, and neurocognitive literature and proposes that mechanistic explanations become available through analogical structure mappings implemented by the premotor action-planning system. Learning that mechanisms exist would then be insufficient: mechanism-based histories must participate in ordinary re-identification and compete successfully with agent-based histories. Proposed tests include manipulating object histories, comparing mechanistic reasoning with analogy measures, and distinguishing neural contributions to different causal interpretations. These are tests of the proposed architecture, not experiments performed in this paper. Extensions to developmental conditions and the evolutionary origin of social cognition remain hypotheses.

## Relevance to this research

This provides cognitive context for [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] when an observer attributes identity, goals, or agency to another process. It distinguishes the observer's causal representation from the target's actual mechanism. It does not establish that every inferred agent minimizes free energy, or that agency attribution identifies a physical subsystem or a stable parent agent.
