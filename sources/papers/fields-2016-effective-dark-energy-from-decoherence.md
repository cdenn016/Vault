## Contribution and scope

Fields proposes that the thermodynamic cost of redundantly encoding stellar positions could contribute an effective dark-energy density. The model requires each spatial voxel to encode whether each of a specified population of stars occupies it, so its bit count scales with both object number and voxel number. Applying a Landauer cost at the cosmic microwave background temperature then yields an energy density that depends strongly on the chosen spatial resolution. Kilometer-scale voxels are reported to produce an order of magnitude comparable to the observed dark-energy density; Planck-scale voxels give an enormous excess. This is a conditional encoding-cost estimate. The particular redundant database representation, its asserted minimality, and the identification of encoding cost with a cosmological energy density require additional justification. No cosmological stress tensor, equation of state, or dynamical fit is derived.

## Relevance to this research

The paper is relevant to [[Emergent spacetime and holography]] as a speculative example of physicalizing information costs. It should be cited as a proposal, not evidence that information processing explains dark energy. For [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]], it emphasizes why an information quantity needs a specified physical implementation, units, and dynamics before being interpreted as energy or mass.
