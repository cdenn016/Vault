## Contribution and scope

Hartl and colleagues propose a common description of cognition in terms of remapping a problem into an embedding space and navigating within that space by iterative error correction. Their review connects metabolic regulation, gene expression, anatomical repair, and ordinary movement with latent representations in transformers, diffusion models, and neural cellular automata. A remapping must preserve the task-relevant relations that make subsequent computation useful; their commuting-diagram formulation separates changing a representation from implementing a function on represented data. Coarse-graining is therefore selective: constraints can be encoded by the geometry or organization of the embedding without being represented individually. The comparison is an organizing proposal supported by examples from existing research. The authors explicitly retain differences between biological development and artificial models, including embodiment, persistent learning, asynchronous dynamics, and metabolic maintenance. Their suggestion that near-criticality is a general operating regime remains a candidate principle.

## Relevance to this research

This offers a vocabulary for comparing representation changes with inference dynamics in [[VFE Transformer Program]] and [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. A useful comparison must specify which relations an encoding preserves and which predictions its update improves. The review does not establish that an arbitrary remapping preserves a normalized joint law, Fisher geometry, or an agent's complete operational behavior. Its biological analogies should remain distinct from those mathematical obligations.
