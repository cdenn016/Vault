# Regularity properties and pathologies of position-space RG transformations

> [!info] Citation
> A.C.D. van Enter, R. Fernández, A.D. Sokal (1993). "Regularity properties and pathologies of
> position-space renormalization-group transformations: Scope and limitations of Gibbsian
> theory." *Journal of Statistical Physics* **72**(5–6), 879–1167.
> DOI: [10.1007/BF01048183](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01048183).

## TL;DR

The definitive treatment of when real-space RG maps are even well defined. Where the map exists
it is single-valued and Lipschitz on its domain, but in large regions of parameter space —
including at low temperature for standard block transformations — the renormalized measure is
**not Gibbsian for any reasonable Hamiltonian**: the map does not produce a theory of the same
type at all. Extends the peculiarities first exhibited by [[griffiths-1979-rg-transformations]].

## What it establishes

Position-space RG transformations can fail to have well-defined renormalized interactions; the
image of a Gibbs measure need not be Gibbs. Real-space blocking is therefore not guaranteed to
be a map on any fixed space of Hamiltonians, and every real-space scheme owes an existence
argument before its flow can be discussed.

## Relevance to this research

The strongest external calibration for the laboratory's C3 refutation: even in classical
statistical mechanics, real-space blocking away from special structure fails to close on a
declared theory family, so `Theory/07b`'s autonomous-semigroup claim was stronger than the
standard lore ever warranted, and its failure (composition defect 0.204 against a $10^{-10}$
criterion) is the expected situation rather than an anomaly of the gauge-VFE setting. See
[[Staged hierarchy formation and RG composability]] and [[Renormalization group flow]].
