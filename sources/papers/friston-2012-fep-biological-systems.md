## TL;DR

Friston states the free energy principle for an ergodic random dynamical system whose states split into external states exposed to environmental fluctuations and internal states that evolve deterministically. He defines an active system as one whose internal map minimizes the entropy of external states, shows this is equivalent to a least-action condition on surprisal, proves that minimizing a variational free energy over internal states is sufficient for it, and argues that the path integral of a reduced free energy coincides with a reduced information bottleneck criterion.

## Problem & setting

The problem is how a biological system immersed in a fluctuating environment can confine itself to a small set of states. The setting is the theory of random dynamical systems (Crauel and Flandoli; Arnold): a measure-preserving base flow on a probability space stands for the environment, and a cocycle flow on $X\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ stands for the system. The standing assumptions are that the system has a random attractor that is ergodic, hence a unique stationary ergodic density, and that the state space factors as $X=R\times S$, where internal states $R$ do not depend directly on the base flow and external states $S$ do; a subset $A\subset R$ of active states carries the influence of internal on external states. External states are identified with sensory states in this paper, and the term Markov blanket does not appear. The paper builds on the author's earlier free energy papers, variational Bayes (Feynman, Hinton and van Camp, MacKay, Beal), Haken's synergetics and nonlinear Fokker-Planck treatments of circular causality, the good regulator theorem of Conant and Ashby, Jaynes's maximum entropy principle, and the information bottleneck method of Tishby, Pereira and Bialek.

## Method

The system is a random dynamical system $m=(\mathbb{R}^d,\varphi)$ generated by a Langevin equation $\dot x(t)=f_X(x)+g_X(x)\omega(t)$ with $x(0)=x_0$ (Eq. 1). The partition $X=R\times S$ shields internal states from the fluctuations: $\dot s(t)=f_S(r,s)+g_S(r,s)\omega(t)$ and $\dot r(t)=f_R(r,s)$ (Eq. 3). Under ergodicity the entropy of the ergodic density equals, almost surely, the long-run time average of surprisal $\mathcal{L}(x(t))=-\ln p(x(t)\mid m)$ (Eq. 4). An active system is one whose internal map satisfies $\varphi_R^*=\arg\min_{\varphi_R}H(S\mid m)$ (Definition 1, Eq. 7). Action in the mechanical sense is the path integral $\mathcal{S}=\int_0^T\mathcal{L}(s(t))dt$ (Eq. 8). A generative model is introduced by writing the evidence as a marginal over fictive variables, $p(s\mid m)=\int_\Psi p(s,\psi\mid m)d\psi$ (Definition 2, Eq. 10); a proposal density $q(\psi\mid\mu)$ is parameterized by internal states $\mu\subset r$ (Definition 3, Eq. 11); and free energy is $\mathcal{F}(x(t))=\int_\Psi q(\psi\mid\mu)\ln\frac{q(\psi\mid\mu)}{p(s,\psi\mid m)}d\psi$ (Definition 4, Eq. 12), which rearranges to $\mathcal{L}(s)+D_{KL}[q(\psi\mid\mu)\Vert p(\psi\mid s,m)]$ (Eq. 14). Section 4 writes an information bottleneck criterion as $\mathcal{S}_B=I(R;\Psi)-I(S;R)$ (Eq. 19), expands it into entropies and drops two terms to obtain the reduced criterion $\mathcal{S}_B^*=H(R\mid m)+H(S\mid R,m)$ (Eq. 20); a point-mass proposal density gives the reduced free energy $\mathcal{F}^*(t)=-\ln p(\mu(t)\mid m)-\ln p(s(t)\mid\mu(t),m)$ (Eq. 21). Section 5 specializes to generalized filtering, a gradient descent of the form $\dot{\tilde\mu}=\mathcal{D}\tilde\mu-\partial_{\tilde\mu}\mathcal{F}(\tilde s,\tilde\mu)$ in generalized coordinates (Eq. 23), with a hierarchical dynamic generative model (Eq. 24) and a Gaussian proposal density under the Laplace assumption, which yields generalized predictive coding (Eq. 25).

## Key results

Stated with short proofs: Lemma 1 (ergodic entropy, Eq. 5, journal page 2107) bounds the entropy of the ergodic density by the log Lebesgue measure of the attractor, $H(X\mid m)\le\ln\lambda(\mathcal{A}(\omega))$, the uniform density being the maximizer. Lemma 2 (principle of least action, Eq. 8, page 2108) states that the internal states of an active system minimize surprisal pointwise, equivalently $\delta_r\mathcal{S}=0$, with the converse; the proof uses the ergodic identification of entropy with time-averaged surprisal (Eq. 9) and the fundamental lemma of variational calculus. Proposition 1 (the free energy principle, Eq. 13, pages 2109 to 2110) states that if internal states minimize free energy then $\delta_r\mathcal{S}=0$ and the system is active; the proof uses Eq. 14 and Gibbs' inequality and assumes the divergence can be driven to zero, with the general case described as approximate Bayesian inference in the remarks.

Corollaries, each a rearrangement of free energy (pages 2110 to 2111): Corollary 1 (Bayesian inference, Eq. 16), the optimal proposal density is the posterior $p(\psi\mid s,m)$; Corollary 2 (active inference, Eq. 17), action maximizes expected log likelihood (accuracy) under the proposal density; Corollary 3 (maximum entropy principle, Eq. 18), free energy equals negative entropy of the proposal density minus expected log joint, so its minimization maximizes that entropy under the constraint of explaining sensory states; Corollary 4 (entailment), a definition of what it means for a system to entail a generative model.

Argued under simplifying assumptions (Section 4.2, page 2113): with a point-mass proposal density and ergodicity, $\mathcal{S}_F^*=\int dt\mathcal{F}^*(t)=H(R\mid m)+H(S\mid R,m)$ (Eq. 22), which equals the reduced bottleneck criterion of Eq. 20. This is the formal equivalence announced in the abstract.

Asserted or reviewed without derivation here: the predictive coding scheme and its neurobiological correlates (Section 5, Tables 1 and 3, Figure 2), and the closing claim that any biological system up to a social structure encodes a model of its milieu (page 2117). The paper contains no simulations.

## Limits

The author states that the proof of Proposition 1 assumes the proposal density can match the posterior exactly and that otherwise free energy is only a bound; that how sensory states depend on active states is left unspecified; and that the bottleneck equivalence rests on a point-mass (maximum a posteriori) reduction under ergodic assumptions. My observations: internal states are deterministic and external states are identified with sensory states, so there is no separate hidden external state and no Markov blanket. Lemma 2 moves from stationarity of time-integrated surprisal to a pointwise condition $\partial_r\mathcal{L}=0$, although sensory states depend on internal states through the dynamics and not instantaneously. The bottleneck criterion of Eq. 19 is not in Tishby's standard Lagrangian form, the reduction to Eq. 20 discards terms by verbal argument, and Eq. 22 equates a time integral with entropies without the factor $T$ that Eq. 9 carries. The equivalence is heuristic.

## Relevance to this research

> [!note] Editorial: relation to the gauge-theoretic free energy program
> The link to the vault's program is weak to moderate and mostly historical. The paper supplies the variational half of what later became Bayesian mechanics in a clean form: internal states $\mu$ parameterize a proposal density $q(\psi\mid\mu)$ over fictive variables, and free energy is a function of system states only. That is the single-agent statement that internal states are coordinates on a family of beliefs, which the program's statistical fibers generalize, but the paper equips this family with no metric, no natural gradient and no geometry at all. Its least-action language concerns the time integral of surprisal and has no kinetic term, so it does not inform Hamiltonian belief dynamics or belief inertia. There is no Markov blanket, no second agent and no transport. Two side results touch coarse-graining to meta-agents only by analogy: the synergetic reading of internal states as order parameters that enslave fast external variables, and the bottleneck reading of internal states as compressed summaries. Both correspondences are interpretive, and the bottleneck one is not established rigorously in the paper. The maximum entropy corollary is an exact identity and connects the lineage to Jaynes at the level of the proposal density.
