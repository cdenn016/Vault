# The evolution of conventions

> H. P. Young (1993). "The evolution of conventions." *Econometrica* **61**(1), 57–84. doi:10.2307/2951778.

## Verified content (abstract)

Players drawn from a large finite population best-respond to a sample of past play. For coordination games and common-interest games the process converges almost surely to a pure-strategy Nash equilibrium, interpreted as the "conventional" way of playing the game. When players occasionally make mistakes, society occasionally switches between conventions. As the probability of mistakes goes to zero, only the stochastically stable equilibria keep positive probability; they are found by least-resistance paths between equilibria, a special case of a general graph-theoretic theorem on perturbed Markov processes.

## Relevance to this research

> [!note] Editorial:
> Not stated in the abstract: in Young's framework the expected exit time from a basin scales like $(\text{mistake rate})^{-r}$ for resistance $r$, so the logarithm of the switching time is proportional to $r\log(1/\text{mistake rate})$. Founding memory held in the basin choice therefore outlasts the logarithmic within-basin transient by an exponential factor. This inference was checked by the verifier against the framework, not against the paper's text.
