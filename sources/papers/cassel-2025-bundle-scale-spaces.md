## TL;DR

Node features on a graph are modelled as **sections of associated vector bundles**, so that equivariance under a *local* action of a symmetry group $G$ — one that may act on the feature at each node by its own individual transformation — holds **by construction** rather than by architectural constraint. Processing architectures are then obtained as mappings of bundle sections, specifically the mappings induced by **diffusion PDEs on vector bundles associated to a generalized Laplacian**. The resulting **bundle scale space** differs from the ordinary Gaussian scale space in that it possesses *non-trivial fixed points*, namely the **harmonic sections** of the associated bundle.

## Problem & setting

Graph neural networks and related node-feature architectures operate on data $x\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$ indexed by graph vertices. Ordinary architectures presuppose a fixed common basis for the $d$-dimensional feature space at every node. Local symmetry — the possibility that each node's feature vector carries its own, independently chosen frame — is not respected by that presupposition. The bundle formulation makes the choice of frame explicit and quotients it out: a feature is an equivalence class $[\xi_i,x_i]$ of a frame and a coordinate vector, and admissible processing maps are exactly those that respect the equivalence.

The classical scale-space picture (Gaussian scale space, heat diffusion) is the standard multiscale mechanism for such data. Its defect from this viewpoint is that its only fixed points are the constants; diffusing forever destroys the signal. The paper's move is to replace scalar heat diffusion by diffusion generated by a **generalized (bundle) Laplacian** acting on sections, whose kernel is generally larger and geometrically meaningful.

## Method

- **Data model.** Node features are sections of a vector bundle associated to a principal $G$-bundle over the graph; the group acts locally, i.e. node-wise, and equivariance under this local action is built into the representation. (The discrete version of this construction, with $\Xi=\mathrm{SO}(d)^N\subset\mathrm{GL}(X)$ and $\Gamma=(\Xi\times X)/\Xi$, is set out in full in the follow-up [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]], §2, which credits and generalises this paper.)
- **Architecture generation.** Processing layers are not designed directly but *derived* as the flow maps of **diffusion PDEs on the associated bundle**, generated by a generalized Laplacian. Layers are thus time-slices of a geometric flow, in the same spirit as the assignment-flow / neural-ODE construction the group uses elsewhere ([[gonzalez-alvarado-2025-patch-assignment]], [[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]]).
- **Bundle scale space.** The family of these diffusion maps indexed by scale defines the bundle scale space. Its fixed points are the sections annihilated by the generalized Laplacian — the **harmonic sections** — which for a non-flat connection form a non-trivial space, in contrast to the Gaussian scale space whose fixed points are only the constant signals.

## Key results

As stated in the published abstract:

- Local $G$-equivariance of the architecture holds **by construction** from the associated-bundle representation, rather than being imposed as a constraint on learned weights.
- The bundle scale space contains **non-trivial fixed points corresponding to harmonic sections** of the associated vector bundle, "in contrast to the basic Gaussian scale space."

No experimental claims, complexity results or benchmark comparisons are recorded here, because the full text could not be retrieved; see the *Unverified fields* callout. The theoretical content is developed at much greater length, with proofs, in [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]], which should be treated as the citable source for the technical statements.

## Relevance to this research

This is the paper that supplies the **gauge half** of the vault's own construction, and its data model is close to identical to the owner's at the level of objects.

**What coincides.** "Node features as sections of associated vector bundles, with local $G$-equivariance built in" is, up to the substitution of a finite graph for a smooth base manifold and of $\mathbb R^d$ for a statistical fibre, the same statement as [[Agents as fibre-bundle sections]]: agents are smooth local sections of associated bundles $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{state}},\mathcal E_{\mathrm{model}}$ over a base of contexts, and observables must be covariant under the local action of the structure group. The "harmonic sections as fixed points" statement is the bundle-valued version of the fixed-point structure the owner's covariant Dirichlet term would have, and it is the exact object the sigma flow of [[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]] lacks (there the target statistical manifold is fixed and global, not a transported fibre).

**Where the constructions genuinely differ.**
1. **Fibre.** Here the fibre is a Euclidean feature space $\mathbb R^d$ with $G\subseteq\mathrm{SO}(d)$ acting by rotations; the owner's fibres are **statistical manifolds** of Gaussian distributions carrying the [[Fisher information metric|Fisher-Rao metric]], with the structure group acting by congruence and every transport acting as a fibre isometry. A linear diffusion on a vector bundle is not the same object as a gradient flow on a bundle of curved statistical manifolds, and the generalized-Laplacian machinery does not transfer unchanged.
2. **Base.** Here the base is a finite graph and the connection is a discrete graph voltage; the owner's base is a smooth manifold of contexts with a connection one-form and, in Regime II, an independent gauge field.
3. **Objective.** Here processing *is* the diffusion; there is no variational free energy, no belief/prior pair, no KL coupling, and no evidence-lower-bound reading. The owner's dynamics descend a [[Variational free energy]] whose belief-coupling term $\beta_{ij}\,\mathrm{KL}(q_i\Vert\Omega_{ij}q_j)$ is a gauge-covariant discrepancy between neighbouring sections — a divergence-valued, not quadratic, covariant Dirichlet term.
4. **Curvature.** This paper (per the abstract) does not introduce a curvature energy; that appears in [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]].

**What can be imported.** The "layers as time-slices of a bundle diffusion flow" design pattern, and the harmonic-section characterisation of the scale-space fixed points, are directly usable framing for the owner's covariant Dirichlet sector. Together with [[cassel-2025-yang-mills-data]] this group has published the associated-bundle-plus-gauge-equivariance half and, in [[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]], the Fisher-Rao harmonic-map half, within eighteen months and with overlapping authorship and shared funding (DFG SPP 2298; Heidelberg STRUCTURES EXC 2181/1). Neither paper combines the two, and neither carries a variational free energy — but the prior-art position for any of the individual ingredients should be assumed to be occupied.
