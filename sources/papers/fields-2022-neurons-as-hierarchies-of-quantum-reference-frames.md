## Contribution and scope

Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin model neuronal organization as hierarchies of quantum reference frames, extending the same description from synaptic interactions to dendritic branches, neurons, and local networks. Each reference frame is a physically implemented standard for measuring or acting, and successive boundaries encode classical information exchanged by components. The paper distinguishes two kinds of memory: relatively stable executable organization and sequences of recorded measurement outcomes. Dendritic remodeling is then interpreted in terms of the information available from correlated synaptic inputs and the trophic resources supporting those inputs. The authors predict relationships among branch activity, remodeling, and reward, while leaving detailed models of particular cell types to future work. Their account explicitly proposes quantum coherence as a computational resource in bulk cellular processing. That biological proposal is stronger than the mathematical use of quantum reference-frame notation and is not independently established by the representation itself.

## Relevance to this research

The two memory roles connect directly with [[Cue-result generative memory]]: retained structure can encode how to respond, while a record stores a particular outcome. The hierarchy also informs [[Quantum reference frames]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]. Its neural and quantum commitments should remain distinct from a classical probabilistic implementation, which needs its own update equations and evidence for any claimed biological correspondence.
