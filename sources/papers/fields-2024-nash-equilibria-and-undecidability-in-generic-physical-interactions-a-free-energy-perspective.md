Published article, Games 15 (2024), 30, published August 26, 2024. Read PDF pages 1, 9–10, and 18, introduction, Section 3 limits, Section 4.1 convergence, and Section 7 conclusions. PDF page numbers include cover material.

## Contribution and scope

Generic physical interaction is represented as a game in which each system seeks to reduce prediction error at its boundary. The authors combine their quantum-reference-frame formulation of the free energy principle with black-box limits on identifying another system's computation. They argue that a finite player cannot completely determine its own or another player's strategy and payoff function from observed exchanges, so generic interactions resemble games of incomplete information. The discussion connects imitation games, good regulation, synchrony, Bayesian games, and quantum LOCC protocols. Equilibrium existence, convergence, and a player's ability to recognize convergence are treated as different questions. The wide application of Nash and undecidability language depends on the declared physical-to-game representation; a universal convergence theorem for arbitrary interacting inference systems is not supplied.

## Relevance to this research

The paper provides a comparison for interacting agents whose predictions and policies are only partially observable. It supports keeping shared protocols and generative assumptions explicit in [[Multi-agent variational free energy]]. Its payoff interpretation does not establish that independently minimized local free energies descend one normalized global ELBO. The project's distinction among global, receiver-owned, and reciprocal objectives remains necessary, as does a separate proof of any claimed synchronization or equilibrium-selection dynamics.
