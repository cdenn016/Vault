## Contribution and scope

Part I assembles Chu-space and Channel-Theory tools for representing semantic information flow. A Chu space couples objects and attributes through a satisfaction relation; infomorphisms relate classifications while preserving that relation. The survey develops connections to formal concept analysis, local logics, ontology alignment, sampled spaces, simplicial constructions, cones, and colimits. An integration example uses a colimit to characterize a channel through which participating agents' classifications can be coordinated. Here optimality is a universal mapping property, not minimization of an information-theoretic loss. The paper primarily supplies an organized mathematical toolkit, with the object-identification application developed in Part II.

## Relevance to this research

This is background for [[Quantum reference frames]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], especially when comparing local descriptions that share only selected distinctions. It offers explicit compatibility conditions instead of assuming every receiver uses the same semantics. The satisfaction relation and categorical arrows do not specify probabilities, Fisher geometry, or dynamics on their own. To connect the construction to the project, those structures and their preservation under the maps must be separately defined.
