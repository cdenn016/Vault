## Verified content

Eq. (2), $q' = q + A\,q(1-q)(2q-1)$ (p. 330), is the frequency-dependent transmission rule: with $A=0$ transmission leaves trait frequencies unchanged (the authors' "linear transmission rule"), and with $A>0$ it increases the frequency of the more common variant (conformist transmission). Eq. (3) adds a direct-bias term $B\,q(1-q)$ (p. 332). With migration and direct bias, Fig. 3 (p. 337) shows three subpopulation equilibria $q_C$, $q_T$, $q_D$, with $q_T$ unstable: a subpopulation whose initial frequency exceeds $q_T$ reaches a stable cooperator equilibrium, and one starting below $q_T$ reaches a stable defector equilibrium (p. 339). In the linear (genetic-like) case, selection and dispersal constantly destroy the variability between groups (p. 338); as long as conformist transmission is strong enough to create two subpopulation equilibria, cooperation goes to fixation once it is established (p. 342).

## Relevance to this research

Conformist bias is a named mechanism that discards within-group variance. Unlike the linear restoring force on the four-agent cycle of [[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]], its nonlinearity makes the founding frequency's basin permanent within the model (extinction and recolonization are treated separately in the paper), so memory of the founding becomes a discrete, slow coordinate rather than a decaying transient. This is a clean contrast with the proved result: under linear consensus coupling the within-pair imprint is forgotten, while under conformist nonlinearity part of the founding would become a kept coordinate of the parent, like the source assignment. The mapping is interpretive. See [[Soft formation and the activation clock]] and [[Cultural evolution and social learning]].
