## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin propose viewing the history of terrestrial life as the development of a continuously branching cell lineage. This change of scale makes evolution and development related descriptions of interacting processes, rather than entirely separate explanatory domains. They discuss how variation, selection, inheritance, competition, and cooperation can depend on the scale at which a system is described. The perspective also emphasizes inherited organization outside DNA, including membranes, cytoplasm, and bioelectric patterns, and treats larger biological structures as environments that constrain their components. Predictive coding and active inference are offered as languages for representing these reciprocal relationships. The article is a conceptual program supported by examples from existing biology. Its proposal to interpret biological structures as memories and processes as computations is not a derivation that all such structures implement one probability model or that evolutionary and developmental mechanisms become identical after rescaling.
