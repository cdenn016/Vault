# This boundary-less world

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2014) This boundary-less world. In: D. Chopra (Ed.) Brain, Mind, Cosmos: The Nature of Our Existence and the Universe. Carlsbad, CA: Chopra Foundation. Ch. 13.

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2014-this-boundary-less-world.pdf)

## Summary

This accessible chapter connects decompositional equivalence with finite observation and the perception of persistent objects. Fields defines decompositional equivalence as invariance of the world's behavior under changes in how an observer groups or names its components. Finite records leave alternative physical sources indistinguishable, so repeated observations do not by themselves certify that their causes are the same individual system. He then connects this identification problem to visual motion processing and to constructed causal histories linking present perceptions with remembered encounters.

The chapter extends these arguments to an ontology in which boundaries, identity, and experienced time are observer constructions, and finally advocates a panpsychist interpretation. These are philosophical extensions of the discussion. Associativity of a mathematical decomposition does not alone establish that every decomposition is equally useful dynamically or that interaction-defined structures are physically irrelevant. Likewise, finite observational ambiguity does not itself prove that persistent objects do not exist. The chapter's summaries of quantum no-go results are informal analogies rather than derivations, and its proposed connection to universal consciousness is not an empirical result.

## Relevance to this research

This supplies historical context for [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]] and [[Quantum reference frames]]. Its emphasis on identity through records is relevant to observer-relative agent descriptions. It does not construct an information clock, derive spacetime, or show that a chosen local partition can be removed while preserving all operational predictions.
