## Contribution and scope

This preprint asks whether an operational protocol can certify the absence of entanglement without assuming the classical resources needed to run the protocol. The authors argue that local observations and communicated marginal data do not establish the complete joint state or the separability of the communication carrier itself. Theorem 4 invokes their earlier boundary-observation no-go results to conclude that classicality is necessarily pragmatic. The central concern is unrestricted self-certification of the protocol's physical assumptions. It should be distinguished from testing separability within a specified finite-dimensional model using known measurements and preparation assumptions. The text also uses a product-state equation when discussing separability; mixed-state separability is more general than a product state. These distinctions limit how broadly the title can be quoted.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Quantum reference frames]] and [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]], this sharpens the need to declare what a classical record channel assumes. The live project explicitly supplies normalized preparation and transcript laws, so it makes conditional mathematical claims rather than certification of all its own physical premises. The preprint does not invalidate conditional independence within such laws, nor establish that every useful classical interface must be derived from the authors' holographic-screen formalism.
