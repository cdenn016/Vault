# Science generates limit paradoxes

> [!info] Citation
> Dietrich, E. and Fields, C. (2015) Science generates limit paradoxes. Axiomathes 25: 409-432 (doi: 10.1007/s10516-015-9267-x).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/dietrich-2015-science-generates-limit-paradoxes.pdf)

## Summary

Dietrich and Fields examine scientific findings that appear to undermine assumptions required for their own evidential use. Their examples are apophenia, which challenges confidence in perceived patterns, and quantum boundarylessness, which challenges the identification of systems needed to describe experiments. They organize this diagnosis as a Ladder Schema: a science uses a body of accepted propositions to produce a discovery that conflicts with some of those propositions. Ordinary revision remains manageable when the conflict is restricted; a limit paradox is claimed when the discovery applies to the entire relevant domain.

The authors map this schema to Priest's Inclosure Schema and argue for a dialetheic interpretation of science. The universal extension of the examples and the treatment of practical commitments as knowledge are substantive premises of that philosophical argument. The conclusion advocates epistemic modesty and pragmatic scientific practice. This source should therefore be read as a structured argument for a position, not as an empirical demonstration that all scientific theories contain true contradictions.
