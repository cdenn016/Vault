## Contribution and scope

This commentary proposes a quantum-information interpretation of Markov blankets in response to a morphogenesis-as-inference model. It decomposes a closed system into two open components, assumes local Hamiltonian interactions, and represents a finite interaction through binary-valued measurement operators. A model boundary register alternately exchanges classical records, with thermodynamic efficiencies governing what each side can retain. The authors argue that internal decompositions remain hidden behind the interaction and interpret the resulting surface as providing the conditional independence associated with a blanket. They then relate good prediction of the partner's observable effects to regulation and variational free energy. The discussion is a short formal proposal. Its conditional-independence conclusion should not be imported into an arbitrary probability model solely because a physical boundary or tensor product has been named.

## Relevance to this research

This is an early source for the bridge between [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] and holographic interfaces. It makes the receiving agent's information access central, which matches the project's explicit communication concerns. The project's normalized joint laws require conditional independence to be established for their actual random variables and kernels. A boundary analogy or hidden internal Hamiltonian is not a replacement for that proof, and the commentary gives no general identity between its interaction description and the project's population ELBO.
