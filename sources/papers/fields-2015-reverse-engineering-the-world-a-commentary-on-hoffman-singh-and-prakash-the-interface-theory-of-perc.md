# Reverse engineering the world: A commentary on Hoffman, Singh and Prakash, "The interface theory of perception"

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2015) Reverse engineering the world: A commentary on Hoffman, Singh and Prakash, "The interface theory of perception". Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 22: 1526-1529 (doi: 10.3758/s13423-014-0742-y).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2015-reverse-engineering-the-world-a-commentary-on-hoffman-singh-and-prakash-the-interface-theory-of-perc.pdf)

## Summary

Fields interprets the interface theory of perception through two ideas from computer science: finite observations do not determine the complete behavior of an unrestricted black box, and the same virtual machine can have many physical implementations. Perceptual tokens therefore identify useful patterns without uniquely identifying all of their physical causes. A virtual-machine hierarchy can improve functionality by concealing implementation details, so informational abstraction need not be a failure of perception.

The commentary applies this perspective to Hoffman, Singh, and Prakash's evolutionary models. It proposes that more elaborate organisms may hide more lower-level physical detail while exposing more useful affordances, and that metabolic and cognitive organization may form corresponding abstraction hierarchies. These are hypotheses suggested by the comparison, not new evolutionary experiments or quantitative results reported here. The concluding quantum discussion is interpretive and does not derive quantum dynamics from the interface argument. The finite-observation limitation also presupposes an unrestricted class of possible hidden systems; it does not rule out identification within a specified model class.

## Relevance to this research

This is background for [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]] and the distinction between an agent's operational interface and a proposed physical realization. It motivates retaining explicit observational and memory maps. It supplies neither an exact coarse-graining kernel nor a derivation that a hierarchy of semantic abstractions forms autonomous parent agents.
