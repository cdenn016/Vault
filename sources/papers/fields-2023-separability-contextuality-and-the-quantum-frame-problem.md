## Contribution and scope

The authors formulate a quantum version of the frame problem: deciding whether an action changes environmental entanglement and hence what remains unaffected by it. Their setting assumes finite interacting systems, a separable joint state over the interval of interest, and finite reference frames represented by cone–cocone diagrams. Theorem 3.1 argues that a finite agent cannot decide this problem from its available measurements, including when it is decomposed into components using different frames. Corollaries deny unrestricted certification of separability and complete isolation of an action's causal consequences. The analysis connects preparation and measurement contextuality to basis choices, thermodynamic exchange, and supplied causal models. The universal undecidability interpretation relies on the paper's modeling restrictions; this note records the argument without treating it as an independently checked no-go theorem for every possible experimental model.

## Relevance to this research

This is a useful limit case for [[Quantum reference frames]] and agent-relative contextual descriptions. It warns against equating a chosen task boundary with proof that all relevant interactions have been isolated. The project's exact retained-response results are scoped to a declared family and observable algebra, so they need not claim the unrestricted certification targeted here. Quantum contextuality also remains distinct from ordinary dependence on a contextual base coordinate.
