## TL;DR

The paper characterizes every diffusion that preserves a smooth positive measure $P$ on an arbitrary manifold $M$, using only the pair $(M,P)$: the drift must be $\mathrm{curl}_P(A)+P^\sharp(\gamma)+\frac12\mathrm{div}_P(Y_i)Y_i$ for an antisymmetric bi-vector field $A$, a twisted de Rham class $\gamma$ of degree $n-1$ and the Stratonovich noise fields $Y_i$. This generalizes the Euclidean complete recipe of Ma, Chen and Fox, removes its integrability assumption, and identifies the missing term on topologically nontrivial spaces. The paper also treats reversibility, a volume-free subclass of Langevin-like diffusions, the decay of relative entropy, and underdamped Langevin dynamics on vector bundles and Lie groups.

## Problem & setting

Ma, Chen and Fox (the paper's reference [121]) showed that on $\mathbb{R}^n$ a diffusion preserving $P\propto e^{-H}dx$ can be written as $dZ_t=-(Q\nabla H+D\nabla H)dt+\nabla\cdot(Q+D)dt+\sqrt{2D}dW_t$ with $Q$ antisymmetric and $D$ positive semi-definite (eq. 1), and proved completeness under an integrability assumption by a Fourier construction. The authors object that this recipe depends on Cartesian coordinates and the Lebesgue reference measure, cannot incorporate symmetries or constraints, and does not extend to manifolds, where samplers for molecular dynamics, directional statistics and lattice gauge theory operate. Their standing assumptions are an arbitrary smooth $n$-manifold $M$ with no orientability, connectedness or compactness, a smooth and globally supported positive measure $P$, and diffusions given as Stratonovich equations $dZ_t=X dt+Y_i\circ dW^i_t$ with smooth vector fields, which need no connection. The construction draws on de Rham's twisted forms, Koszul's rotationnel, Weinstein's modular vector fields of Poisson geometry, Graham's covariant Fokker-Planck theory, the non-degenerate compact case in Ikeda and Watanabe, and results from the first author's doctoral thesis.

## Method

The measure defines musical isomorphisms $P^\flat(X)=i_XP$ from $k$-vector fields to twisted $(n-k)$-forms, with inverse $P^\sharp$, and the $P$-rotationnel $\mathrm{curl}_P=P^\sharp\circ d\circ P^\flat$ lowering multi-vector degree by one (Definition 3.2). It is independent of the normalization of $P$, equals $\mathrm{div}_P$ on vector fields, and on a bi-vector field $A$ equals the modular vector field $X^P_A:f\mapsto\mathrm{div}_P(X^A_f)$ with $X^A_f=A(df,\cdot)$ (Theorem 3.3). Since $\mathrm{curl}_P\circ\mathrm{curl}_P=0$, it defines homology groups $H^k_P(M)$, and $P^\flat$ identifies $H^\ell_P(M)$ with the twisted de Rham cohomology $H^{n-\ell}_{dR}(M)$ (Theorem B.1). Lemma 3.4 writes the Fokker-Planck operator of the Stratonovich equation as $L^*P=-\mathcal{L}_XP+\frac12\mathcal{L}_{Y_i}\mathcal{L}_{Y_i}P=\mathrm{div}_P(J(P))P$ with current $J(P)=\frac12\mathrm{div}_P(Y_i)Y_i-X$ (eq. 13), so that $P$ is stationary if and only if $\mathrm{div}_P(J(P))=0$ (eq. 14). Divergence-free fields are locally of the form $\mathrm{curl}_P(A)$ by a measure-twisted Poincaré lemma and globally of the form $\mathrm{curl}_P(A)+P^\sharp(\gamma)$, which yields the recipe $dZ_t=(\mathrm{curl}_P(A)+P^\sharp(\gamma)+\frac12\mathrm{div}_P(Y_i)Y_i)dt+Y_i\circ dW^i_t$ (eq. 2). The potential $A$ is defined up to $A\mapsto A+\mathrm{curl}_P(V)$ for a 3-vector field $V$, which the authors compare to electromagnetic gauge freedom. For $P=e^{-H}\mu_M$ with a reference measure $\mu_M$ the terms split as $\mathrm{curl}_P(A)=\mathrm{curl}_{\mu_M}(A)+X^A_H$ and $\mathrm{div}_P(Y_i)Y_i=\mathrm{div}_{\mu_M}(Y_i)Y_i-Y_i(H)Y_i$ (after Theorem 3.7; Proposition A.5 with a factor $\beta$), into volume-preserving and density-preserving parts. Section 2 reaches the same class heuristically by replacing each Euclidean term with a bracket (eqs. 7 and 11, Theorem 2.4). The noise fields define a symmetric dissipative bracket $S=Y_i\otimes Y_i$, and the pair of $A$ and $S$ is called a thermodynamic bracket.

## Key results

Theorem 3.7 (p. 11) is the main result: $P$ is stationary for $dZ_t=X dt+Y_i\circ dW^i_t$ if and only if around every point $X=\mathrm{curl}_{\iota_U^*P}(A)+\frac12\mathrm{div}_{\iota_U^*P}(Y_i)Y_i$ for a local bi-vector field $A$; the class is globally complete if and only if the twisted cohomology $H^{n-1}_{dR}(M)$ is trivial, and in general the drift carries the extra term $P^\sharp(\gamma)$. It is proved from Lemma 3.4 and Theorem B.1, the latter taken from the first author's thesis with a proof reproduced in an appendix titled as an unpublished result. Corollary 2.5 (p. 8, proved in A.3) recovers the Euclidean recipe, and the text argues that the integrability assumption of Ma, Chen and Fox is unnecessary because the cohomology of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is trivial. Theorem 4.1 (p. 13) represents the obstruction by a harmonic $(n-1)$-form on compact orientable Riemannian manifolds. Corollary 5.3 (p. 15) splits the generator as $Lf=X^P_A(f)+P^\sharp(\gamma)(f)+\frac12X^P_S(f)$ (eq. 21), with the first two terms antisymmetric and the last symmetric and non-positive in $L^2(P)$, and reversibility holding exactly when the Fokker-Planck current vanishes; Corollary 5.5 identifies the carré du champ with the bracket $S$, and Corollary 5.6 (p. 16) gives reversibility up to a target-preserving diffeomorphism $R$ with $R_*A=-A$ and $R_*S=S$, generalizing the momentum flip. Section 6 (pp. 17-20) argues, for pointwise linearly independent noise fields and up to obstructions, that diffusions with no reference-measure drift are $dZ_t=X^{\mathrm{curl}_{\mu_M}(V)}_Hdt-\frac12X^{\mathrm{curl}_{\mu_M}(A_i)\otimes\mathrm{curl}_{\mu_M}(A_i)}_Hdt+\mathrm{curl}_{\mu_M}(A_i)\circ dW^i_t$ (eq. 26); this is not stated as a theorem. Theorem 7.1 (p. 21) gives the elliptic case with Riemannian Brownian noise, $dZ_t=-X^A_{\log p_\infty}dt-\nabla\cdot A dt+\frac12\nabla\log p_\infty dt+dB_t$ (eq. 28). Section 8 (p. 23) derives $\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{KL}(\mu_t\|P)=-\frac12\int\{\log\frac{dP}{d\mu_t},\log\frac{dP}{d\mu_t}\}_S\mu_t\le0$ when Stokes' theorem applies, so only the dissipative bracket contributes, and Proposition 8.1 extends this to general functionals. Theorem 9.1 (p. 25) constructs underdamped Langevin diffusions on $TM$ from a Langevin pair with vertical noise, whose fiber process is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process preserving a Gaussian on each tangent space, and Section 9.3 specializes to Lie groups as an algorithm with no numerical experiments. Section 10 discusses ergodicity informally and asserts, citing Ichihara and Kunita, that strong ellipticity suffices for uniqueness of the invariant measure while positive definiteness of $S$ does not, contrary to a claim in Ma, Chen and Fox.

## Limits

The theory assumes a smooth, globally supported target and smooth vector fields on a finite-dimensional manifold, and it characterizes invariance only; the authors leave open which choices of $A$ and $Y_i$ satisfy Hörmander's condition, defer infinite-dimensional diffusions and sampler design to future work, and report no experiments. The relative-entropy decay requires Stokes' theorem to hold, and the volume-free characterization requires linearly independent noise fields and ignores topological terms. The central Theorem B.1 comes from a thesis and is labeled unpublished. My observations: existence and non-explosion of solutions are not addressed; the splitting of divergence-free fields into a curl part and a cohomological part depends on a choice of representative $\gamma$, so it is canonical only up to that choice; the inverse temperature $\beta$ appears in some displays and is silently set to one in others; and Corollary 5.5 looks inconsistent by a factor of two with the definition in Remark 5.4.

## Relevance to this research

> [!note] Editorial: relation to the gauge-theoretic free energy program
> Editorial judgment. This is the coordinate-free form of the Helmholtz decomposition that Bayesian mechanics uses in flat coordinates, and it is the natural reference for writing a steady-state flow on a statistical manifold. For Hamiltonian belief dynamics and belief inertia, Theorem 9.1 gives the general shape of inertial dynamics on a vector bundle: a conservative bracket flow plus an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process on the fibers that preserves a Gaussian $N(0,M^{-1}(q))$ determined by a metric; reading that metric as the Fisher metric is an interpretive step the paper does not take, although it cites Riemannian Langevin samplers built from information metrics. Theorem 7.1 likewise shows that with noise bracket equal to a co-metric the reversible part is a Riemannian gradient flow plus Riemannian Brownian motion, a stochastic counterpart of natural-gradient descent only when the co-metric is an inverse Fisher metric. The cohomological term matters only if belief or frame variables live on non-contractible spaces such as rotation groups; Gaussian parameter spaces are contractible. The gauge freedom $A\mapsto A+\mathrm{curl}_P(V)$ is a redundancy of a potential for the probability current, not a connection transporting beliefs between agents, so that correspondence is weak. The paper has nothing on Markov blankets, multiple agents or coarse-graining.
