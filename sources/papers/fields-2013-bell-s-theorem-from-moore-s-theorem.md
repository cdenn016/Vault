## Contribution and scope

Fields compares restrictions on finite observations of classical black boxes with restrictions associated with no-cloning, Kochen–Specker, and Bell results. Moore's theorem supplies the central underdetermination: a finite input/output record cannot uniquely identify an unrestricted finite-state machine. Applied to physical measurement devices, the paper argues that records cannot establish the complete degrees of freedom on which a POVM acts or the independence of two presumed sources. The discussion of Bell's theorem emphasizes the later physical communication needed to compare distant records. The paper itself distinguishes this epistemic limitation from a dynamical claim that classical systems are nonseparable. Consequently, its title should not be read as deriving Bell inequalities, their quantitative violations, or quantum correlations from automata theory alone.

## Relevance to this research

This is useful for [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] when distinguishing a protocol's assumed causal structure from structure inferred through messages. The project's conditional statistical results can remain exact after a model is supplied, even though finite data do not uniquely identify that model among all possibilities. The paper gives an operational warning against unexplained source identity, not a substitute for an explicit normalized joint law or a proof that classical inter-agent communication has Bell nonlocality.
