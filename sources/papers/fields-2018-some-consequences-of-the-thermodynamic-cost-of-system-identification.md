## Reading basis

Published article, Entropy 20 (2018), 797, published October 17, 2018. Read PDF pages 1, 9–10, and 13, thermodynamic setting, Section 4/Theorem 1, and concluding Section 6 discussion. PDF page numbers include cover material.

## Contribution and scope

Fields treats system identification as a search performed through finite-resolution observations that consume thermodynamic resources. Stable reference observables identify a carrier, while pointer observables track its state. Theorem 1 argues that refining identification toward a complete objective description eventually makes the search interaction disturb the reference conditions required for re-identification. Later arguments connect temporal and multi-observer identification to Leggett–Garg and Bell/CHSH tests. The paper's strongest claim is that quantum violations become necessary for demonstrating particular shared-identity relations under its assumptions. That conclusion is an author-specific foundational argument, not a general rule that ordinary classical tracking requires a Bell violation. The analysis distinguishes a practically identifiable system from an exact observer-independent collection of degrees of freedom.

## Relevance to this research

This source helps formulate the resource and resolution limits of declared interfaces in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. A finite observation history may identify a carrier only up to an equivalence class, and stronger identification can change the experiment. It therefore complements exact recovery statements scoped to admitted observations. Its thermodynamic and quantum claims do not supply a derivation of the project's statistical Fisher geometry, nor establish that its classical message exchanges inherit Bell or Leggett–Garg violations.
