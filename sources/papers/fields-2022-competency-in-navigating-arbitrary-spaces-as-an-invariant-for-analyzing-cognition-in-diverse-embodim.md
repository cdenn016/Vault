## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin propose competent navigation in a problem space as a way to compare cognition across unfamiliar embodiments. Metabolic, transcriptional, physiological, morphological, and ordinary three-dimensional movement spaces are treated as different domains in which systems can maintain or pursue preferred states. The review gathers examples of adaptive repair and problem solving, including changes that reuse existing biological machinery under new conditions. Its operational emphasis is on testing what interventions and goals a system can manage, rather than deciding intelligence from its substrate or outward resemblance to an animal. The authors connect this perspective with active inference and multiscale competency architectures, where components have their own problem spaces and also participate in larger-scale behavior. The proposed invariant is a conceptual comparison framework, not a numerical measure proven to be identical across all these settings. Biological examples support specific competencies; they do not erase differences in mechanisms or evidence.

## Relevance to this research

This helps formulate observable tasks for [[Collective active inference]] and [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] without assuming that agent identity follows directly from physical size. A useful comparison should specify the accessible states, actions, preferences, and success criteria. For [[VFE Transformer Program]], representational navigation is an interpretation to test against actual predictive behavior, not a substitute for the model's normalized probability law.
