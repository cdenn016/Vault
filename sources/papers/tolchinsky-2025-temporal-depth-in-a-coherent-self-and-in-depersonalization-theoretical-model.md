## Contribution and scope

Tolchinsky and colleagues propose temporal depth as a common organizing variable for dissociative experience. Temporal depth concerns the accessible remembered past and anticipated future, together with the distinctions that make planning across different horizons possible. The self is modeled as a dynamic component of a generative model, with nested bodily, autobiographical, and other functional components. A coherent self requires coordination across these components and timescales. The authors describe dissociation using changes in an attractor landscape: persistent fragmentation can follow a transition away from a regime supporting an integrated autobiographical self. Their term collapse includes loss of usable distinctions between temporal horizons, rather than only a literal decrease in a numerical duration. The paper combines clinical literature, active inference, and nonlinear dynamical language. It explicitly assigns empirical support, computational testing, and therapeutic development to subsequent work, so the proposed common mechanism is not established here by new clinical data or a validated simulation.

## Relevance to this research

This is contextual material for memory, anticipation, and persistence in [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]. It motivates distinguishing an agent's planning horizon from its update rate or an information-geometric clock. The paper supplies no derivation identifying temporal depth with accumulated KL divergence, Fisher distance, or a collective clock slowdown. Its clinical interpretation should not be imported as an established mechanism into the mathematical model.
