## Contribution and scope

This article preserves a multi-author conversation about defining life, including disagreements about thermodynamics, agency, constraint closure, and observer dependence. Fields's contributions use a system/environment decomposition to frame interaction as information exchange, and emphasize that persistence requires a criterion for recognizing the same system through change. His examples include the different identity questions raised by a broken rock and a divided, regenerating planarian. Other participants challenge the utility of taking the environment to be the rest of the universe and question whether such general physics discriminates living systems. The conclusion explicitly retains disagreements; the conversation supplies neither a shared definition of life nor a derivation of active inference accepted by all contributors.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], this is a record of the identity and boundary questions that a formal construction must answer. It supports tracking the chosen scale, environment, and persistence criterion explicitly. Fields's statements that particular physics principles follow from decomposition should be read as positions in a discussion, with technical justification sought in the cited research articles. The dialogue does not establish an endogenous parent-formation law.
