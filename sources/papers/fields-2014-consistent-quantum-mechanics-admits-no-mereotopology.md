## Contribution and scope

The paper asks whether a mereological partition into enduring objects can be represented consistently in quantum mechanics. It uses consistent-history language and system/environment projections to argue that nontrivial partitions require mutually noninteracting components. The universal conclusion is not supported by the argument around Equation (15). Projectors acting on distinct tensor factors commute on every joint state, including entangled states, so the displayed commutation condition is not equivalent to state factorization. Also, an interaction need not entangle every particular product input. These distinctions leave intact the practical question of how a chosen decomposition is identified, but they prevent using this paper as a general theorem that interacting quantum systems lack well-defined subsystem Hilbert spaces. The conclusion should be recorded as the author's proposal with this explicit mathematical limitation.
