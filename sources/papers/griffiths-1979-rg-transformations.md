# Mathematical properties of position-space renormalization-group transformations

> [!info] Citation
> R.B. Griffiths, P.A. Pearce (1979). "Mathematical properties of position-space
> renormalization-group transformations." *Journal of Statistical Physics* **20**(5), 499–545.
> DOI: [10.1007/BF01012897](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01012897). Companion announcement:
> "Position-Space Renormalization-Group Transformations: Some Proofs and Some Problems,"
> *Physical Review Letters* **41**, 917 (1978).

## TL;DR

The first rigorous study of cell-type (Niemeijer–van Leeuwen, Kadanoff) real-space RG maps in
the thermodynamic limit. At large field / small activity the image interactions exist, have
finite norm, decay exponentially with cluster size, and depend analytically on the object
interactions — but the paper also exhibits the "peculiarities": parameter regions where the
renormalized Hamiltonian's existence is in doubt, later shown by
[[van-enter-1993-rg-pathologies]] to be genuine non-Gibbsianness.

## What it establishes

Sufficient conditions under which a real-space blocking map is a well-defined analytic map on a
space of interactions, and the first evidence that outside those conditions no such map need
exist.

## Relevance to this research

Together with [[van-enter-1993-rg-pathologies]], the historical proof that a real-space coarse
map's closure on a theory family is a theorem-by-theorem matter, never a default. The finite
categorical laboratory sidesteps the existence pathology (finite state spaces, strictly positive
weights make the coarse action always exist) but inherits the closure question, which is exactly
what its Moebius truncation residual and C4 projection agreement measure. See
[[Staged hierarchy formation and RG composability]].
