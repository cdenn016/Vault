## Contribution and scope

Fields asks whether observers restricted to separate environmental fragments can establish that they observe the same macroscopic system without prior agreement about its boundary. Matching pointer records are compatible with different sources. The paper considers whether additional quantum specifications or direct preparation operations can remove that ambiguity and argues that those routes either presuppose detailed system information or alter the original observational setting. Its conclusion is that quantum Darwinism explains persistence of classical behavior for an already selected system more directly than it explains the assumption-free selection of that system. This is a critique of the scope of an emergence claim. It does not disprove redundant environmental encoding or decoherence calculations when a particular system, coupling, and observation protocol have been explicitly supplied.
