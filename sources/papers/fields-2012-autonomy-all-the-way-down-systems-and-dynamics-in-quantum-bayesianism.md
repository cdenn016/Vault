## Contribution and scope

Fields critiques the account of systems and autonomous action presented in an early QBist program statement. The recurring issue is diachronic identification: if an observer's action is represented by a POVM and the system responds with an outcome, what makes that outcome refer to the same system across changes in its properties or capacity? The discussion challenges the relation among fixed Hilbert-space dimension, changing experimental objects, identification operators, and inter-observer agreement. It compares this difficulty with the subsystem-selection problem in quantum Darwinism. The conclusion argues that removing objective external boundaries is one possible consequence. This is a philosophical analysis of a particular QBist formulation, not an experimental refutation of QBism or a mathematical impossibility theorem covering every relational interpretation and every operational definition of system identity.

## Relevance to this research

For [[QBism]] and [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]], the paper makes the assumed identity of an agent or observed carrier a substantive modeling question. The project's records and communication mechanisms can specify how identities are maintained within a declared model without claiming an absolute decomposition. Its formation question is stronger: a new parent must preserve admitted behavior and satisfy selection requirements. This critique does not construct that parent or establish equivalence between QBist agents and the project's statistical bundle sections.
