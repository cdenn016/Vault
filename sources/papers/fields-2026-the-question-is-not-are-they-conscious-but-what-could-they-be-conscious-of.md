## Contribution and scope

Fields and Glazebrook redirect debate about artificial consciousness toward the discriminations a system can make and the information to which it can respond. They treat attribution of computation as an interpretation of measured physical behavior, and distinguish Turing computability from a particular von Neumann architecture. The commentary links this operational position to finite measurement resolution, observer-dependent reference frames, and the free-energy principle. Its examples range from text-processing systems to organisms with many sensory dimensions. The paper is a conceptual response to Seth's biological naturalism, not an experiment or a new computational architecture. Differential responsiveness is the authors' proposed way of investigating possible experiential content; it does not independently demonstrate phenomenal experience in a laptop, language model, or rock.

## Relevance to this research

This clarifies the operational meaning of an agent in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]: specify its accessible inputs, retained state, and possible responses before assigning higher-level interpretation. It also helps separate [[Consciousness and the hard problem]] from mathematical claims about inference. Calling an interaction a computation does not supply a normalized generative law, nor does a successful inference model establish consciousness.
