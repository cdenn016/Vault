## Contribution and scope

This early critique focuses on the inference from equal apparatus readings to redundant encodings of one source. Two observers can obtain matching pointer values either because their apparatus sample the same system or because different systems produce indistinguishable values. Fields argues that tracing the apparatus interactions far enough to establish the source uniquely requires interventions that change the redundancy being assessed. He concludes that unrestricted identification of the common source cannot be obtained from the pointer records alone. The text also acknowledges that theoretical and practical considerations can provide strong evidence for a selected encoding. The claim should therefore be understood as a challenge to conclusive, assumption-free certification within the stipulated observation setting, not as evidence that redundant environmental records cannot exist or cannot be tested under an explicit model.

For [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]], this is a concise precursor to the distinction between record agreement and common-cause identification.
