## Contribution and scope

This short commentary compares the causal-state taxonomy of a classical free-energy-principle model with the authors' quantum boundary representation. In the classical account, sensory, active, internal, and external states admit different arrow patterns. Fields and Glazebrook instead assume weakly interacting separable quantum systems and represent their interface by an ancillary holographic screen. Because the screen is not another physical component of the original state space, they argue that the distinctions among inert, active, and strange particles collapse in this representation. Effects of an action are learned through the subsequent environmental response rather than through direct access to the partner's internal state. The commentary then invokes earlier limits on measuring one's own boundary and separability. Its conclusion is about this change of formal representation and its assumptions, not a demonstration that arbitrary classical causal models are inconsistent.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Multi-agent variational free energy]], the paper highlights a modeling choice: is an interface a set of actual state variables in a normalized joint, or an ancillary description of interaction? These alternatives cannot be silently interchanged. The project's explicit records, evaluators, and transcript kernels give one concrete answer. No mathematical identity between those classical objects and the commentary's quantum screen is established, and the authors themselves leave the emergence of classical blanket states as a question.
