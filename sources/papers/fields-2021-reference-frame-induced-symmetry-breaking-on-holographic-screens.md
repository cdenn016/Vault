## Contribution and scope

The paper studies reference frames implemented by finite systems exchanging information across an ancillary holographic screen. Selecting sectors for stable system identification, variable pointer readings, and thermodynamic support breaks the permutation symmetry of the undifferentiated screen. The authors interpret decoherence, contextuality, classical memory, and observable entanglement through relations among these reference frames. In the entanglement discussion, they argue that comparing outcomes requires a previously shared frame and formulate a strong claim that sharing entanglement requires prior entanglement. That claim belongs to their particular account of frame sharing and should not be substituted without qualification for standard quantum communication resource theory. The conclusion explicitly marks a third emergent spatial dimension and an interpretation of inertial mass as speculative, and leaves complete reconstruction of physical symmetries unresolved.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Quantum reference frames]], this supplies a concrete operational vocabulary for frame-dependent identification and finite information access. It is also relevant to [[Emergent spacetime and holography]] as a proposed progression from screen symmetries to perceived structure. The project's passive frame covariance is not automatically this thermodynamic symmetry breaking, and neither construction establishes the other. Shared frame calibration, statistical agreement, and quantum entanglement must remain distinct when relating these ideas to interacting classical agents.
