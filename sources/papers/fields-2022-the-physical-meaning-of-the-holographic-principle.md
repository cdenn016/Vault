## Contribution and scope

This review assembles the authors' communication-centered quantum framework around a generalized holographic principle. For a finite closed bipartite system with separable state, information available across the interaction is represented by a finite boundary encoding. The review develops measurement, local reference frames, sequential observations, time, and scattering in that language, and compares the interface with Markov blankets and computational black boxes. Its starting restriction concerns access through a specified interaction, while its geometric interpretation introduces the conventional Planck-area density when the ancillary boundary is read as a physical screen. These are different levels of structure. The article's conclusion that the principle unifies broad scientific domains is an interpretive synthesis of the authors' constructions, not a new experimental finding or an unrestricted derivation of gravitational area laws from arbitrary finite channels.

## Relevance to this research

This is the most convenient overview for [[Emergent spacetime and holography]] and the quantum side of [[Quantum reference frames]]. It helps identify the common operational question: what can one agent learn through an interface? The project's classical conditional kernels, Fisher geometry, and normalization requirements should be compared at that interface level first. Neither finite bandwidth nor a statistical boundary alone supplies Planck units, Lorentzian geometry, or the gravitational dynamics required to identify the models physically.
