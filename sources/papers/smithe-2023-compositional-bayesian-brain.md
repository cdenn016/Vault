## TL;DR

The thesis gives a category-theoretic account of approximate Bayesian inference and predictive coding. It defines Bayesian lenses (a forward channel paired with a prior-dependent backward channel), proves that exact Bayesian inversions compose by the lens rule, attaches loss functions to lenses to obtain fibrations of statistical games whose strict or lax sections include relative entropy, marginal likelihood, free energy and its Laplace approximation, and defines functors (approximate inference doctrines) from such games to open dynamical systems that recover the standard predictive coding update equations. It does not reach a compositional theory of active inference or of Markov blankets, which it sketches as future work.

## Problem & setting

Hierarchical predictive coding models under the free energy principle have an evident compositional structure (each level contributes a term to a summed free energy), yet their equations of motion are rederived by hand for each model, and the free energy framework's claims to universality are stated informally. The thesis aims to supply functorial semantics: a syntax of statistical models and a semantics in open dynamical systems, connected by structure-preserving functors. The standing setting is a copy-discard (Markov-type) category of stochastic channels, concretely finitely supported kernels or s-finite kernels. Bayesian inversions are treated under a full-support assumption; the loss examples assume densities with respect to a common measure; the predictive coding examples assume Gaussian channels between finite-dimensional spaces, posteriors with small variance, and a mean-field (diagonal covariance) form. The work builds on categorical probability (Cho and Jacobs, Fritz), lenses, optics and open games (Hedges and coauthors), polynomial functors and categorical systems theory (Spivak, Myers), and the predictive coding literature (Rao and Ballard, Friston, Bogacz, Buckley et al.). Only perception is treated; action is deferred.

## Method

Definition 4.3.2 introduces the indexed category of state-dependent channels over a monoidal category $\mathcal{C}$, with $\mathrm{Stat}(X)(A, B) := \mathbf{Set}(\mathcal{C}(I, X), \mathcal{C}(A, B))$: a morphism is a channel $A \to B$ that depends on a state (prior) on $X$. Bayesian lenses are the Grothendieck lenses of $\mathrm{Stat}$ (Definition 4.3.8): a lens $(X, A) \to (Y, B)$ is a pair $(c, c^\dagger)$ of a channel $c: X \to Y$ and a state-dependent channel from $B$ to $A$, and the composite of $(c, c^\dagger)$ with $(d, d^\dagger)$ has backward part $\pi \mapsto c^\dagger_\pi \bullet d^\dagger_{c \bullet \pi}$. A lens is exact when the backward channel is a Bayesian inversion of the forward one (Definition 4.3.13). Section 5.1 introduces copy-composition, which composes channels while retaining the intermediate variable, so that relative entropy obeys its chain rule functorially. A statistical game (Definition 5.3.6) is a lens with a loss, a state-dependent effect assigning a number to each prior and observation; under composition the losses add, the loss of the second lens being reindexed along the first forward channel and the loss of the first averaged under the second backward channel. Loss models are lax sections of the projection from games to lenses (Definition 5.3.15). The examples are $\mathrm{KL}(c, c')_\pi(y) := D_{KL}(c'_\pi(y), c^\dagger_\pi(y))$ (Proposition 5.3.22), $\mathrm{MLE}(c, c')_\pi(y) := -\log p_{c \bullet \pi}(y)$ with the coparameter of $c$ discarded (Definition 5.3.24), and $\mathrm{FE} := \mathrm{KL} + \mathrm{MLE}$ (Definition 5.3.26), which for trivial coparameter equals $D_{KL}(c'_\pi(y), \pi) - \mathbb{E}_{x \sim c'_\pi(y)}[\log p_c(y \mid x)]$ (Remark 5.3.27). Proposition 5.3.31 defines the Laplacian free energy $\mathrm{LFE}(\gamma, \rho)_\pi(y) := E_{(\gamma, \pi)}(\mu_{\rho_\pi}(y), y) - S[\rho_\pi(y)]$ (eq. 5.7), with energy $E_{(\gamma, \pi)} = -\log p_\gamma - \log p_\pi$ evaluated at the posterior mean and posterior covariance equal to the inverse Hessian of the energy there (eq. 5.8). On the semantic side, open dynamical systems are coalgebras of polynomial functors, and cilia (Definition 6.3.8) are systems that emit Bayesian lenses and receive a prior and an observation. An approximate inference doctrine (Definition 7.3.1) is a pseudofunctor from parameterized channels to cilia factoring through an inference system and, in the differential case, through a loss model, a gradient functor and an integrator.

## Key results

Theorem 4.3.14 (thesis p. 164) is the central proved result: for composable exact Bayesian lenses and any prior $\pi$, $(d \bullet c)^\dagger_\pi$ and $c^\dagger_\pi \bullet d^\dagger_{c \bullet \pi}$ are almost surely equal with respect to $d \bullet c \bullet \pi$, so Bayesian inversion is an almost sure section of the fibration of lenses (Corollary 4.3.15). Remark 4.3.18 observes that this section is not monoidal: inverting a tensor of channels under a joint prior differs from tensoring inversions under the marginals unless the prior is a product. Proposition 4.3.20 and Section 4.3.4 show that exact Bayesian lenses with causal forward channel satisfy the GetPut lens law with respect to states but not PutGet or PutPut, because updating mixes prior and observation. Theorem 5.3.8 (p. 194) shows that statistical games form a bicategory. Proposition 5.3.22 (p. 200) proves that relative entropy is a strict loss model, with $\mathrm{KL}$ of a composite equal to $\mathrm{KL}(d, d')_{c \bullet \pi}(z) + \mathbb{E}_{(y, n) \sim d'_{c \bullet \pi}(z)}[D_{KL}(c'_\pi(y), c^\dagger_\pi(y))]$, which is the chain rule; Proposition 5.3.25 shows that likelihood is a lax loss model, so free energy is lax; Proposition 5.3.33 states the same for the Laplacian free energy. Corollary 5.4.13 (p. 221) shows that free energy is lax monoidal with laxator $\lambda^{FE}(c, d)_\omega(y, y') = \mathbb{E}[\log(p_{\omega_X \otimes \omega_{X'}}(x, x') / p_\omega(x, x'))]$, the expectation taken under the product of the two approximate posteriors; this measures the error of inverting two channels independently under a correlated prior. Proposition 7.3.7 (p. 276) shows that the mean-field Laplace assignment is a strongly functorial but non-unital inference system under copy-composition, Proposition 7.3.9 gives the gradient functor, and Corollary 7.3.11 (p. 280) assembles the Laplace doctrine $\mathsf{L}_\lambda := \mathrm{Euler}_\lambda \circ \nabla \circ \mathrm{LFE}$, whose update map (Proposition 7.3.4) is $x \mapsto x + \lambda \partial_x \mu_c(x, y)^T \eta_c(x, y) - \lambda \eta_\pi(x)$ with precision-weighted errors $\eta_c(x, y) := \Sigma_c(x)^{-1} \epsilon_c(y, x)$ and $\eta_\pi(x) := \Sigma_\pi^{-1} \epsilon_\pi(x)$. Corollary 7.3.16 adds a Hebbian weight update. Chapter 8 contains conjectures only: that the claim that every random dynamical system performs inference should be an adjunction between systems and statistical games (Section 8.3.1), that polynomial interfaces formalize Markov blankets (Section 8.2.1), and that sheaves formalize Bayesian sensor fusion, the forming of consensus from overlapping local views (Section 8.1.1). The thesis reports no simulations.

## Limits

The author lists three shortcomings at the start of Chapter 8: the coparameter machinery behind copy-composition is inelegant, the generative models are static so only recognition dynamics are obtained, and the doctrines are not connected to biological circuit models. Action is not treated. Remark 7.3.2 says that 2-cells are ignored in the doctrines and the inference systems are non-unital; Remark 7.3.8 says that copy-composition is used inconsistently with the earlier state-dependence; the monoidal bicategory structure is only sketched (Proposition 5.4.1); and footnote 14 leaves open whether optimizing the factors of a composite game jointly has the same fixed points as optimizing the composite. My observation: the gradient step treats the posterior entropy as independent of the mean, although the covariance is defined as an inverse Hessian evaluated at the mean, which is exact only when that Hessian is constant.

## Relevance to this research

> [!note] Editorial: relation to the gauge-theoretic free energy program
> The thesis informs the fiber picture at the categorical level. State-dependent channels form an indexed category over priors, Bayesian lenses are its Grothendieck construction, exact inversion is a section, and the author describes the fibration of statistical games as a kind of categorified fibre bundle. There is no structure group, connection or Fisher metric, so the correspondence with statistical fibers and gauge transport is interpretive. Three points are more concrete. The failure of inversion to be monoidal, with the laxator of Corollary 5.4.13, quantifies what is lost when systems placed side by side infer independently under a correlated joint prior, which is the kind of term a multi-agent free energy with inter-agent coupling has to account for. The Laplace doctrine yields Gaussian belief dynamics as Euclidean gradient flow on the mean with precision-weighted errors, without natural gradient or inertia, so it is a baseline and not a precedent for Hamiltonian belief dynamics. Remark 7.3.17 and Section 8.1.2 state as future work that fast states should be bundled over slow parameters with a notion of parallel transport, naming the structure without building it. The proposals on Markov blankets as polynomial interfaces and on societies of systems are sketches. Overall the link is moderate and structural.
