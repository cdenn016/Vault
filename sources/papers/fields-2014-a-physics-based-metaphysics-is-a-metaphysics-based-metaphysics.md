# A physics-based metaphysics is a metaphysics-based metaphysics

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2014) A physics-based metaphysics is a metaphysics-based metaphysics. Acta Analytica 29: 131-148 (doi: 10.1007/s12136-013-0204-6).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2014-a-physics-based-metaphysics-is-a-metaphysics-based-metaphysics.pdf)

## Summary

Fields argues that appeals to physics in metaphysics inherit assumptions already embedded in physical theories and experimental practice. The paper compares positions associated with Tegmark, Fuchs, and Zurek, then focuses on the assumption that identifiable systems persist through time. It uses Hilbert-space factorization, interaction, and entanglement to criticize metaphysical arguments about bounded objects and monism. Its methodological proposal is to state the underlying assumptions before treating a physical formalism as independent support for an ontology.

The argument makes stronger claims than this methodological point warrants. In particular, PDF pp. 13-14 treat entanglement as making subsystem Hilbert spaces and boundaries undefined. Entangled states on a specified tensor product still admit reduced density operators on its factors; the absence of separate pure states does not eliminate the chosen subsystem structure. The paper's further movement from entanglement to the absence of proper parts is therefore an ontological interpretation, not a consequence of entanglement alone. This note records the critique without adopting that inference.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]], the useful requirement is explicit accounting for system definitions, observational access, and metaphysical commitments. The gauge/VFE program should distinguish a model's stipulated factorization from an observer's ability to infer it. This paper does not establish that interacting statistical agents or their normalized marginal laws are mathematically unavailable.
