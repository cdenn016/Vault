## Contribution and scope

Fields develops an account of compartmentalization from the symmetries of an interaction boundary between physical systems. The proposed mechanism begins when a permutation symmetry among boundary degrees of freedom is broken, so that different sectors acquire different functional roles. Within the paper's quantum formulation of the free-energy principle, subsequent prediction-error reduction amplifies this differentiation and favors specialized internal processing. Distinct quantum reference frames and the need for classical communication between them then motivate internal boundaries, division of labor, and hierarchical organization. The discussion extends this picture from cells to multicellular systems and larger communities. It also emphasizes an opposing asymptotic tendency: the same dynamics can restore the symmetry associated with an entangled joint state. The result is presented as a general theoretical mechanism under the chosen boundary representation and dynamical interpretation; the paper does not report an experiment demonstrating that every persistent system must form compartments.

## Relevance to this research

This is relevant prior work for [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Markov Blanket]], particularly the idea that interface differentiation and internal organization develop together. It does not by itself construct a normalized classical parent law or prove exact closure after agents are grouped. Applying the mechanism to [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] requires an explicit account of the symmetry, its breaking, and the dynamics that create each new interface.
