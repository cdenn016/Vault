## Reading basis

Author manuscript dated January 12, 2025; 42 PDF pages, distinct from journal pagination. Read PDF pages 1–2, 4–5, 22, 25, and 28 (setting, physical assumptions, memory examples, and conclusion). This is a targeted ingestion reading, not a full proof audit.

## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin organize biological persistence around the complementarity of objects and processes. They assume standard quantum theory, use a system/environment split, and interpret boundary interactions as information exchange. The resulting perspective emphasizes memory as an ongoing interpretative activity: an external record or engram affects an agent through another interaction, and its meaning depends on the reader's current organization. Reproduction is discussed as redundancy generation and memory construction, while a cognitive light cone describes the spatial and temporal reach of an agent's concerns. The authors explicitly acknowledge that a persistent boundary is stipulated over the interval being described. Their broad biological synthesis does not derive how long such a boundary survives or how a new one forms.

## Relevance to this research

This is a central comparison source for [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]]. It closely matches questions about retained records, agent identity, and communication across scales. A reader-dependent memory interpretation is compatible with explicit cue/result kernels, but does not determine those kernels. The cognitive light cone is a functional description of reach; identifying it with a Fisher information clock or emergent spacetime requires additional mathematics.
