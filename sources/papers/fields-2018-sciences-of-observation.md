# Sciences of observation

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2018) Sciences of observation. Philosophies 3: 29 (doi: 10.3390/philosophies3040029).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2018-sciences-of-observation.pdf)

## Summary

Fields surveys observation across quantum information, developmental biology, and cognitive science, asking what an observer must physically do to obtain, interpret, and retain an outcome. Finite resources and finite-resolution records motivate a distinction between interactions with an environment and identifying a particular system within that environment. Reference observables and expected reference outcomes identify the system; pointer observables report its changing state. Memory therefore includes the operational competence needed to recognize a system, not only a collection of previously reported values. The paper connects this account with signal transduction, predictive coding, and reconstructive recall, treating observation as an active, context-sensitive process.

The later ontology of interfaces, the proposed informational interpretation of unitarity, and the suggestion that awareness is primitive are philosophical extensions of this synthesis. The conclusion explicitly leaves formal consequences of the proposed boundary ontology for future investigation. The review does not supply a single experimentally established mechanism covering every biological and quantum example.

## Relevance to this research

This supplies questions for [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]] and [[Quantum reference frames]]: which reference tests identify a communicating peer, which records persist, and which changes are meaningful to the receiver? It supports keeping model, record, and identification rule explicit. Its thermodynamic discussion does not equate statistical variational free energy with energy consumption or derive a normalized joint model for coupled peer beliefs.
