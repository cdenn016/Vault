# The Quotient Bayesian Learning Rule

> [!info] Citation
> Mykola Lukashchuk, Raphaël Trésor, Wouter W. L. Nuijten, İsmail Şenöz, and Bert de Vries. “The Quotient Bayesian Learning Rule.” *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 38*, 2025. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/0ce1eb87dbb03fdfa872a93d15cfe333-Abstract-Conference.html); [doi:10.52202/085713-0298](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-0298).

## TL;DR

For marginal families obtained from suitably regular exponential families, the paper treats marginalization fibers as equivalence classes, proves an induced Riemannian quotient construction, and shows that the quotient metric agrees with the marginal Fisher metric. Natural-gradient updates can then be computed as horizontal projected updates in the covering family.

## Problem & setting

Marginal distributions can be difficult to parameterize directly even when a richer joint exponential family is tractable. Different joint parameters may induce the same marginal, producing redundant directions that a direct optimizer should ignore.

## Method

Under the paper’s minimality, regularity, and moment-parameterized marginalization assumptions, equal marginals define fibers in the covering statistical manifold. The authors construct vertical and horizontal spaces, identify the quotient metric, and derive a quotient Bayesian learning rule. Student-$t$ examples demonstrate the procedure.

## Key results

- The specified marginal family inherits a smooth Riemannian quotient structure.
- The induced quotient metric is the Fisher information metric of the marginal family.
- The quotient natural gradient is represented by the horizontal projection of the covering-space natural gradient.

## Relevance to this research

QBLR is a precise model for eliminating redundant inference coordinates without pretending they are identifiable. It suggests an implementation test: project gradients away from fiber directions and verify that equivalent covering parameters induce the same marginal update.

## Scope limits

The quotient is generated by marginalization fibers under specific exponential-family hypotheses. It is not automatically a quotient by a free and proper noncompact $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ group action, and it does not resolve stabilizers, singular strata, Hausdorffness, or completeness in a passive-frame model. It also differs from the factor reparameterizations of [[ahn-2017-gauging-variational-inference]]. Any transfer requires an explicit equivalence relation and proof that the objective and Fisher geometry descend.
