# If physics is an information science, what is an observer?

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2012) If physics is an information science, what is an observer? Information 3: 92-123 (doi: 10.3390/info3010092).

[Author-hosted or author-linked full text](../pdfs/fields-2012-if-physics-is-an-information-science-what-is-an-observer.pdf). [Primary source](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/observer-published-120217.pdf).

## Reading basis

Published article, Information 3 (2012), 92–123, published February 16, 2012. Read PDF pages 1, 12–13, and 28, introduction, minimal-observer architecture, and Section 7 conclusions. PDF page numbers include cover material.

## Contribution and scope

The paper replaces an abstract point of observation with a physically implemented observer that already stores identification criteria, recognition rules, a control structure, and memory. Its minimal observer is described as a classical virtual machine implemented by quantum degrees of freedom. POVMs identify candidate systems, select relevant pointer readings, and produce reportable records through conditional control flow. The broader argument claims quantum-like observational structure from assumptions including a suitable linear state space, deterministic time-symmetric dynamics, decompositional equivalence, and counterfactual definiteness. The conclusion distinguishes conserved physical information from pragmatic information defined relative to an interpretation. This is a proposed reconstruction under substantial structural assumptions; the architectural account should not be presented as a derivation of the whole quantum formalism from bare finite data or as an empirically established model of every observer.

## Relevance to this research

This is a foundational precursor for the later Fields communication program. It directly informs the question of what [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] must include beyond a probability distribution: source recognition, an evaluator, record memory, and an actual update mechanism. Its virtual-machine semantics are a useful comparison to retained operational descriptions, but the project's normalized laws and exact conditional identities require separate definitions. Neither the paper's assumptions nor its Born-rule interpretation establishes a quantum realization of GL(K) statistical agents.
