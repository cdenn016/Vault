# Quantifying social group evolution

> [!info] Citation
> G. Palla, A.-L. Barabási, T. Vicsek (2007). "Quantifying social group evolution." *Nature*
> **446**(7136), 664–667. DOI: [10.1038/nature05670](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05670).

## TL;DR

Tracking overlapping communities through time in collaboration and phone-call networks with
clique percolation, the paper finds that groups grow by gradual member accretion rather than
wholesale formation, and that the persistence mechanism depends on size: small groups persist
through a stable core of strong ties, while large communities persist through continuous
membership turnover.

## What it establishes

Empirical dynamics of group formation and survival at scale: gradual accretion, size-dependent
persistence strategies, and quantitative lifetime statistics for overlapping communities.

## Relevance to this research

Direct empirical support for the staged-aggregation picture: real social meta-agents are built
by accretion, not de novo, which is the kinetic phenomenon the laboratory can now probe from
inside via free-energy comparison of staged versus direct blocking paths
([[Staged hierarchy formation and RG composability]]). The size-dependent persistence result is
also a target observable for [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]: the mark-carrying
parent's retention price and the partition posterior give the model's own account of when a
block persists.
