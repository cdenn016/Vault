## Contribution and scope

Dobson and Fields construct an agent-dependent account of episodic time from events and the classifications assigned to objects within them. Their framework represents observed events using relational structures, then uses presheaves and sheaf-inspired constructions to express consistency across local descriptions. Remembering includes constructing unobserved events that connect observed ones; different supporting observations can therefore generate different reconstructions of the same apparent episode. The condensed formulation relocates this construction to the present rather than treating an episode as a fixed record stored on an objective timeline. The paper proposes a common time-symmetric constructive operation for episodic recall and prospective planning, with predictions about shared engram resources, interference, and source monitoring. These neuroscientific consequences are hypotheses for testing. The categorical constructions are the paper's modeling apparatus; this ingestion does not independently certify its mathematical arguments or identify them with a physical law of time.

## Relevance to this research

This is a direct conceptual neighbor of [[Cue-result generative memory]]: a cue can organize a present construction rather than retrieve a complete historical state. It also distinguishes event order, reconstructed narrative, and external clock labels. Connecting the proposal to [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] would require an explicit probability law for construction and a demonstrated relation to information-geometric time; neither follows from the shared use of memory or categories.
