## Contribution and scope

The paper proposes that no physically preferred subsystem decomposition, together with the thermodynamic cost of records, explains why observation has quantum structure. A finite black-box thought experiment develops observer-relative time, inaccessible internal mechanisms, phase representations, and a pragmatic Born-rule interpretation. The double-slit discussion emphasizes the difference between an apparatus specification and the observer's available input/output data. The claimed full reconstruction is not established by the displayed argument. In Equation (3), normalization of two scalar coefficients is used to claim unitarity of their phase-weighted projector sum; for orthogonal projectors, the product with its adjoint retains the squared coefficient weights and is not generally the identity. Section 5.6 also explicitly treats the Born rule as a useful inferential rule rather than a theorem obtained by the observer.

## Relevance to this research

This is useful historical context for [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]] and for asking which subsystem structure communication presupposes. It should not be cited as a valid derivation of quantum theory from information costs. The project's classical gauge/VFE framework can preserve the operational distinction between accessible records and assumed implementation while keeping its own mathematical results independent of this reconstruction claim.
