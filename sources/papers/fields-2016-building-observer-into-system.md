# Building the observer into the system: Toward a realistic description of human interaction with the world

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2016) Building the observer into the system: Toward a realistic description of human interaction with the world. Systems 4: 32 [29 pp.] (doi: 10.3390/systems4040032).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2016-building-observer-into-system.pdf)

## Summary

Fields models an embedded observer as a finite system exchanging finite bit strings with an otherwise inaccessible environment. The observer retains the input-output history, but no finite history identifies the complete transition table when alternative internal state spaces remain admissible. This black-box setting makes explicit the difference between a useful predictive model and certain knowledge of the mechanism that generated its records.

The paper's no-boundary argument further removes an a priori guarantee that a selected external degree of freedom is independent of the box. It then treats the entire observable environment as a black box. System identification requires reference properties held fixed while other properties vary, including conventions for recognizing communicating observers. Fields extends this account toward classical analogues of quantum no-go restrictions, superposition, and holographic boundary descriptions. These extensions are the paper's proposed foundational interpretation; finite-data underdetermination alone is not here treated as an independently verified reconstruction of quantum theory.

## Relevance to this research

This directly informs [[Quantum reference frames]] and [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] by separating declared observer boundaries and identification rules from what a transcript can establish. A model may stipulate a normalized preparation and trusted protocol without claiming they were uniquely inferred from communication. The source does not prove that such stipulated models are impossible, or supply the missing dynamics for endogenous parent selection.
