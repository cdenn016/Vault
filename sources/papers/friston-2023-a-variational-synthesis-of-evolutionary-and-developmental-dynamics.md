## Contribution and scope

Friston and colleagues formulate a relationship between fast phenotypic dynamics and slower phylogenetic change using particular partitions, Bayesian mechanics, and path probabilities. The central construction expresses adaptive fitness through a path integral over phenotypic histories, so that inference within a lifetime and learning across generations can be described at different scales of a variational account. The derivation relies on a nonequilibrium steady-state setting, the selected partition into states, separation of relevant timescales, and a specified relation between genotype and phenotype likelihoods. It also treats populations as interacting heterogeneous kinds rather than requiring every member to be an instance of the same type. The paper includes illustrative material, but explicitly limits its main contribution to mathematical apparatus and ideas, leaving a developed process theory and further numerical studies to future work. Its evolutionary interpretation is therefore conditional on the proposed probabilistic construction.

## Relevance to this research

This provides a concrete comparison for multiscale inference in [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. The useful question is which variables are marginalized, what trajectory law is normalized, and how slow parameters constrain fast dynamics. A resemblance between evolutionary selection and free-energy descent does not establish exact closure of an arbitrary coarse-graining or a shared objective for independently specified peer interactions.
