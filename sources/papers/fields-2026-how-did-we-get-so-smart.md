# How did we get so smart?

## Reading basis

Author manuscript of the 2026 commentary. Read PDF pages 1–2 (the commentary body). This is a targeted ingestion reading, not a full proof audit.

## Contribution and scope

Fields comments on Walker's account of human cognitive evolution by connecting receptivity to novelty with analogy and relational communication. The proposed mechanism is that selection can act on neural structures supporting relation abstraction and structure mapping, broadening the range of unexpected events an organism can usefully learn from. Tool demonstration and social learning supply examples of communicating ordered, causal relations. Fields further suggests links to imagination and the default-mode network, and argues that relative safety would allow exploratory cognition to develop. These are explanatory hypotheses assembled from existing work, not a new evolutionary experiment or a quantitative derivation of the zone of bounded surprisal.

## Relevance to this research

The paper is supporting context for [[Collective active inference]] and communication across agents: useful information can concern relations and action sequences rather than isolated features. It suggests questions about which messages enlarge an agent's usable predictive repertoire. It does not identify a zone of bounded surprisal with a KL radius, a Fisher metric ball, or a threshold in the project's dynamics; such correspondences would require an explicit model and evidence.
