# Object permanence

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2017) Object permanence. In: T.K. Shackelford and V.A. Weekes-Shackelford (Eds.), Encyclopedia of Evolutionary Psychological Science, Entry 2373 (doi: 10.1007/978-3-319-16999-6_2373-1).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2017-object-permanence.pdf)

## Summary

This encyclopedia entry treats object permanence as an inference that an individual remains the same through time, particularly across gaps in observation. Fields separates brief occlusion tasks, where trajectory continuity strongly guides identity judgments, from re-identification after longer intervals, which draws on episodic memory, semantic knowledge, and causal expectations. Similar appearance alone cannot settle whether an object is the previously encountered individual. The entry reviews developmental, comparative, and neural approaches and discusses the experienced self as a special persistent reference point.

Object permanence and temporal experience are presented as mutually dependent concepts: identifying change requires a persisting object, while persistence requires distinguishing now from then. The mechanistic connection between time perception and permanence is explicitly left unresolved. The conclusion suggests an interface-theoretic interpretation in terms of recurring fitness consequences, alongside a research program using developmental robotics and neuroimaging. This is a review and conceptual synthesis, not new experimental evidence for a universal mechanism.

## Relevance to this research

The entry motivates treating agent or object identity as a declared inference carried by records rather than an automatic consequence of matching outputs. This bears on [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]] and on identifying persistent participants in multi-agent histories. Its discussion of time gives no quantitative information-clock law, universal parent-clock slowdown, or proof that objective physical systems cannot be defined.
