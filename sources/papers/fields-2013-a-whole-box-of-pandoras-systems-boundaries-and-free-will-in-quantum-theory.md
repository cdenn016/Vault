# A whole box of Pandoras: Systems, boundaries and free will in quantum theory

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2013) A whole box of Pandoras: Systems, boundaries and free will in quantum theory. Journal of Experimental and Theoretical Artificial Intelligence (Special Issue on Paradox and Mind) 25: 291-302 (doi: 10.1080/0952813X.2013.782981).

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2013-a-whole-box-of-pandoras-systems-boundaries-and-free-will-in-quantum-theory.pdf)

## Summary

Fields proposes treating observed systems as semantic structures selected by measurements of the universe, instead of assuming observers can identify their exact physical boundaries. A sequence of recorded outcomes can then represent a classical virtual machine whose physical implementation need not be uniquely known. The paper uses this perspective to criticize both realist and QBist assumptions about repeated system identification and to reinterpret the emergence of classicality as a question about physical encoding and semantics.

The proposal retains universal unitary quantum dynamics. Its discussion of outcome probabilities invokes the Born rule for the universal state; it does not independently derive that rule from ignorance of boundaries. The mechanism by which physical dynamics produces semantic mappings, observers, and classical memories is explicitly left open. A final argument describes future experience as unpredictable from an observer's limited information and calls this semantic freedom. This epistemic claim and the associated panpsychist interpretation should not be treated as a demonstrated ability to control outcomes or as a general theorem excluding probabilistic prediction.

## Relevance to this research

This is a precursor to the operational-observer perspective in [[Participatory realism and quantum foundations]] and [[Quantum reference frames]]. It motivates distinguishing a record sequence from its physical realization. For the present program, the missing construction remains consequential: a semantic description alone does not specify a normalized record-generating law or establish causal local inheritance by a parent agent.
