# Yang-Mills Meets Data

## TL;DR

A systematic development of **discrete vector-bundle gauge theory as a framework for data science**. Node features on a finite graph are represented as sections of a *discrete* vector bundle $\Gamma=(\Xi\times X)/\Xi$ with $\Xi=\mathrm{SO}(d)^N$; discretised gauge fields are identified with **graph voltages**; and the distinguished gauge-invariant feature transformations are **gauged heat kernels** $K_\chi=e^{-tL_\chi}$ generated by a **gauged Laplacian** $L_\chi$, closely related to but distinct from the graph connection Laplacian and the Laplacian of a cellular sheaf. The paper then imports **Yang-Mills curvature analysis**: it defines a **discrete Yang-Mills energy** for graph voltages and shows that this scalar detects whether a voltage is *synchronizable*, which in turn controls the nullspace of $L_\chi$ and hence the data-transformation behaviour of the heat kernel. Everything is stated in finite graph theory, with an appendix reconstructing the discrete objects from smooth bundles.

## Problem & setting

The paper's premise is that several existing strands of geometric data processing — coordinate-independent / gauge-equivariant CNNs, vector-bundle PDE methods for images, vector diffusion maps and graph connection Laplacians, cellular-sheaf neural networks, twisted Hodge theory for synchronization — are the *same* construction seen from different angles, and that vector-bundle gauge theory is the organising first principle that unifies them. The explicit target is to remove the differential-geometric overhead: results are formulated "solely in terms of finite graph theory," with the smooth theory relegated to Appendix A.

The data model (Fig. 1 of the paper): a finite connected weighted graph $(\eta,\kappa)$ with $N$ nodes; features $x\in X=\mathbb R^{N\times d}$; gauge-invariant features $\phi_i=[\xi_i,x_i]$ with $\xi_i\in\mathrm{SO}(d)$; interaction along edges mediated by graph voltages $\alpha_{jk}\in\mathrm{SO}(d)$ and the induced gauged Laplacian $L_\chi$.

## Method

**Gauge invariance as a modelling assumption (§2).** One postulates an abstract space $\Gamma\cong X$ representing *all* bases of $X$ simultaneously, with no canonical isomorphism, and requires data to be represented by $\phi\in\Gamma$ and transformed by maps $\mathcal F:\Gamma\to\Gamma$. The concrete model is $\Gamma=(\mathrm{GL}(X)\times X)/\mathrm{GL}(X)$.

**A sharp negative result, then the fix.** Prop. 2.3 characterises gauge-invariant transformations as equivariant $F:\mathrm{GL}(X)\times X\to X$ with $F(gh^{-1},hx)=hF(g,x)$; Cor. 2.4 identifies them one-to-one with maps $X\to X/\mathrm{GL}(X)$. Since $X/\mathrm{GL}(X)$ has only two orbits (zero and non-zero), **full $\mathrm{GL}(X)$-gauge invariance restricts the expressible transformations to binary classifiers** $f:X\to\{0,1\}$. Restricting to the subgroup $\Xi=\mathrm{SO}(d)^N$ of factor-preserving isometries enlarges the orbit space to $\mathbb R^N_{\ge0}$, giving $f:X\to\mathbb R^N_{\ge0}$. This is the paper's justification for local, node-wise structure groups.

**Discrete vector bundles (§2.3).** Def. 2.6 defines a discrete vector bundle as a vector space $\Gamma=\bigoplus_{i=1}^N\Gamma_0$ with a compatible product metric; Def. 2.9 gives the space of discrete bundle sections $\Gamma=(\Xi\times X)/\Xi=\big((\mathrm{SO}(d)\times\mathbb R^d)/\mathrm{SO}(d)\big)^N$. Remark 2.10 spells out that this is the discrete counterpart of the **associated vector bundle** $E=(P\times V)/G\to M$ of a principal $G$-bundle. Remark 2.11 notes the theory holds for any compact connected Lie group $G$ with a $d$-dimensional orthogonal representation.

**Graph voltages and the gauged heat kernel (§3).** A voltage assigns $\alpha_{ij}\in\mathrm{SO}(d)$ to each oriented edge with $\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{ji}^{-1}$; gauge transformations act on voltages by node-wise conjugation, and a *gauge-invariant voltage graph* is a triple $\chi=(\eta,\kappa,\omega)$ with $\omega\in\Omega=(\Xi\times\mathcal A)/\Xi$. A generalized heat kernel is $K^t=e^{-tL}$ with $L$ positive semi-definite self-adjoint; the **gauged heat kernel (GHK)** is generated by the gauged Laplacian $L_\chi$ built from $\kappa$ and $\omega$. The paper relates $L_\chi$ explicitly to graph connection Laplacians (Singer-Wu), cellular-sheaf Laplacians (Hansen-Ghrist) and discrete twisted Hodge Laplacians (Gao-Brodzki-Mukherjee), and shows these operators *arise naturally* once gauge invariance is imposed. §3.3 gives an **axiomatic characterisation**: stated axioms (locally Laplacian operators with associated gauges) single out these heat kernels among all gauge-invariant data transformations.

**Nullspace and synchronizability (§4).** A voltage is *synchronizable* if some $\xi\in\Xi$ gauges it to the identity on every edge; synchronizability is a gauge-invariant property (Prop. 4.10) and trees are always synchronizable (Lemma 4.11). Analysing $\ker L_\chi$ via a spanning-tree gauge yields **Cor. 4.15: $\omega$ is synchronizable on $\eta$ if and only if $\dim\ker L_\chi=d$**, with a tensor-decomposition corollary (4.16). Remark 4.7 identifies the corresponding statement about parallel sections as a discrete version of the classical **holonomy principle** (parallel sections $\leftrightarrow$ holonomy-invariant vectors).

**Yang-Mills curvature analysis (§5).** Holonomy for graph voltages (§5.1), holonomy as an obstruction (§5.2), the **discrete Yang-Mills energy** (§5.3), and topological obstructions to synchronizability via graph homology, the holonomy of flat voltages, and an **extended Yang-Mills energy** (§5.4.1-5.4.3).

## Key results

- **Gauge invariance under the full general linear group is nearly vacuous for data science** (Cor. 2.4): it admits only binary classifiers. Local subgroup invariance ($\Xi=\mathrm{SO}(d)^N$) is what makes the framework expressive. This is a clean, quotable obstruction result.
- **The gauged Laplacian is forced, not chosen** (§3.3): stated axioms uniquely identify the gauged heat kernel operators among all gauge-invariant transformations, which in turn explains why connection Laplacians and sheaf Laplacians keep reappearing across the literature.
- **Synchronizability is exactly a spectral statement**: $\omega$ synchronizable $\iff\dim\ker L_\chi=d$ (Cor. 4.15); the nullspace structure is governed by a spanning-tree isomorphism, so the *topology* of the graph shapes what the heat kernel can and cannot do to data.
- **A discrete Yang-Mills energy detects synchronizability by scalar quantities** (§5, asserted in the abstract and in the §1.4 roadmap), connecting curvature of the discrete gauge field to the data-transformation characteristics of the heat kernel, with topological obstructions expressed through graph homology.
- **Unification claim**: the framework is presented as interpolating between smooth and discrete vector-bundle approaches, subsuming steerable/coordinate-independent kernels, vector-bundle PDE methods, vector diffusion maps and cellular sheaves under gauge theory. Appendix A derives discrete bundles from smooth ones, graph voltages from parallel transport, and discrete Laplacians from covariant derivatives.

## Relevance to this research

This paper occupies the **gauge and curvature sector** of the vault's own construction, on a discrete base, and it is the single most directly overlapping piece of external prior art the programme has.

**What coincides with the owner's construction.** Data as sections of an associated bundle with a node-wise structure group; transport along edges by group-valued link variables; the demand that all observables be gauge-covariant; curvature of the discrete connection as the obstruction to trivialising it; and a **Yang-Mills-type energy** built from that curvature. The owner's transport $\Omega_{ij}=e^{\phi_i}e^{-\phi_j}$ is, in this paper's vocabulary, a **synchronizable (flat) voltage** — it is a coboundary by construction, so its holonomy is trivial and its Yang-Mills energy vanishes. This is the same fact the manuscript records as **Regime I flatness** ($F=0$ by Maurer-Cartan, `PIFB2.tex` §"Connection Forms"). Cor. 4.15 gives an independent, purely spectral certificate of that flatness: for a frame-derived transport, $\dim\ker L_\chi=d$. That is a directly usable diagnostic and it is stronger than the manuscript's current holonomy diagnostics, which are noted there to be degenerate in Regime I.

**Where the owner's construction genuinely differs.**
1. **The fibre is not a vector space.** Here $\Gamma_0=\mathbb R^d$ with $\mathrm{SO}(d)$ acting orthogonally, so the Laplacian is *linear* and self-adjoint and the heat kernel is a semigroup of linear operators. The owner's fibres are **statistical manifolds** with the [[Fisher information metric|Fisher-Rao metric]] and a $\mathrm{GL}(K_q)$ congruence action; the fibrewise dynamics is a natural-gradient flow on a curved manifold and the inter-node coupling is a **KL divergence**, not a quadratic form. None of the spectral theory (positive semi-definiteness, kernel dimension, spanning-tree isomorphism) survives that replacement without new work.
2. **The structure group is compact here.** Remark 2.11 restricts to compact connected Lie groups with orthogonal representations. The owner's group is $\mathrm{GL}^+(K_q)$, non-compact — precisely the gap already recorded in the *Scope limits* of [[singer-2012-vector-diffusion-maps]]. Cor. 2.4 sharpens why this matters: full $\mathrm{GL}$-invariance collapses the expressible transformations, so a non-compact-group construction must explain what plays the role of the compact reduction.
3. **There is no variational free energy, no belief/prior pair and no inference semantics.** Processing here *is* diffusion; there is no objective functional being descended, no ELBO reading, and no meta-agent / renormalisation structure.
4. **The base is a graph, not a manifold.** The owner's continuum base $\mathcal C$ of contexts, the connection one-form $A^{(i)}_\mu=U_i^{-1}\partial_\mu U_i$ and the pullback metric construction have no counterpart here; conversely, the graph-homology obstruction theory of §5.4 has no counterpart in the manuscript.

**What can be imported rather than re-derived.** (i) The synchronizability $\leftrightarrow$ $\dim\ker L_\chi=d$ criterion as a flatness test for the implemented transport. (ii) The axiomatic argument that gauge invariance *forces* the connection-Laplacian form, which is a much stronger justification for a covariant Dirichlet term than "by analogy with physics". (iii) The Cor. 2.4 obstruction, as a precise statement of why a local (node-wise) rather than global structure group is necessary. (iv) Appendix A's dictionary between smooth bundles and discrete ones, which is exactly the translation the owner needs between the continuum functional and the implemented lattice.

**Sober note on position.** With [[cassel-2024-sigma-flows]] (Fisher-Rao harmonic maps), [[cassel-2025-bundle-scale-spaces]] (associated-bundle sections with local gauge equivariance and harmonic-section fixed points) and this paper (discrete Yang-Mills energy), the same Heidelberg group has published all three ingredients of the owner's geometric construction within roughly eighteen months, with overlapping authorship and shared DFG funding (SPP 2298; STRUCTURES EXC 2181/1). No one of these papers combines the ingredients, none carries a variational free energy or an inference reading, and the sigma-flow paper explicitly leaves existence and convergence open. The differentiating content of the owner's programme is therefore the *combination* plus the variational/inferential semantics — not the individual geometric ingredients, whose prior-art position should be assumed occupied.
