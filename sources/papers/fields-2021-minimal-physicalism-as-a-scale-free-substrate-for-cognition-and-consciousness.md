## Reading basis

Published open-access 2021 article. Read PDF pages 1–2, 4, and 10–11 (motivation, interaction model, predictions, and conclusion). This is a targeted ingestion reading, not a full proof audit.

## Contribution and scope

The minimal-physicalist framework describes cognition across scales using information exchange constrained by quantum theory and thermodynamics. Its explicit interaction model assumes finite-dimensional systems and a separable pure joint state, then represents boundary communication through binary questions and alternating preparation and measurement. Quantum reference frames organize which environmental differences a system can register. The authors connect this setting to memory, attention, coarse-graining, and self-representation, and formulate predictions intended to apply to basal organisms as well as neural systems. Consciousness as capacity for experience is part of the adopted interpretive framing; the paper does not experimentally establish experience in every system. The finite-resource and separability assumptions must remain visible when using its claims of generality.

## Relevance to this research

This is a direct source for [[Quantum reference frames]], [[Markov Blanket]], and [[Consciousness and Active Inference]]. It offers a detailed version of physics as communication. The project's classical normalized kernels share an operational emphasis but do not inherit a quantum Hilbert space, a Born rule, or a thermodynamic cost for every Bayesian update merely by analogy.
