## Contribution and scope

Part I represents measurement and preparation using quantum reference frames and TQFT morphisms, then builds multi-observer communication diagrams. The code construction introduces an encoding, channel evolution, and decoding, together with familiar error-correction conditions for a specified family of errors. Quantum Darwinism and Bell/EPR scenarios illustrate how shared measurement conventions and environmental correlations can support redundant classical outcomes. The authors argue that useful LOCC protocols require a sufficiently faithful quantum channel and that useful error-correcting codes require a classical means of coordinating bases. Their broad equivalence language therefore concerns practical communication under these assumptions. It should not be read as a formal claim that every LOCC channel is automatically an error-correcting code for arbitrary noise. The text itself notes that practical protection covers only anticipated error operators.

## Relevance to this research

This is a priority source for communication as a physical protocol in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] and for [[Quantum reference frames]]. Encoding, decoding, shared basis information, and noise classes must all be declared before preservation is meaningful. The project's exact statistical recovery kernels provide a useful comparison, but quantum error correction and classical conditional reconstruction have distinct domains and hypotheses. The paper supplies no equality between a TQFT functor and a GL(K) statistical transport.
