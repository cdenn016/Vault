## Contribution and scope

The paper distinguishes sending a finite classical description of a reference frame from sharing the physical operators that identify and use that frame. Alice and Bob must identify the same carrier as well as agree about its pointer state. The authors apply finite black-box identification limits to argue that neither observer can determine the other's implemented identification operators from a finite exchange. They interpret those operators as internal quantum reference frames carrying nonfungible information. Uncertainty about whether the same frame is shared is then associated with decoherence and compared with environmental decoherence in quantum Darwinism. The conclusion concerns unrestricted identification of physical implementations. It does not exclude calibrated approximate agreement, conventions supplied in a model, or useful frame-independent protocols; those are narrower operational tasks.
