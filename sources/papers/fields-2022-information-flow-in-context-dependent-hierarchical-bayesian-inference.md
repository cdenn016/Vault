## Contribution and scope

Fields and Glazebrook use Chu spaces and Channel Theory to describe observations together with their conditions and measurement systems. Classifiers and local logics provide a language for the semantic relations carried by information channels, while cone-cocone diagrams organize hierarchical inference. Their account distinguishes direct contextual influence, which can become explicit by enlarging the description, from intrinsic contextuality, represented by an obstruction to combining locally coherent classifications into an appropriate common cocone. In a Bayesian setting, such incompatibility prevents the straightforward use of a single joint probability distribution for the combined measurement contexts. The paper applies this perspective to perceptual categorization and the frame problem, and leaves the implementation of context switching and prior selection as open questions. The formal claims concern specified observational and categorical structures; ordinary dependence on a changing input is not by itself evidence of the stronger contextual obstruction.

## Relevance to this research

This is relevant to [[Quantum reference frames]] and to receiver-dependent meaning in [[Cue-result generative memory]]. It encourages recording which measurement context a message presupposes and whether local models can be joined consistently.
