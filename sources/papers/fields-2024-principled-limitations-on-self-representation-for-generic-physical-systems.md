## Contribution and scope

The paper studies classical descriptions of a finite physical system's own observational and computational machinery. It models measurement through quantum reference frames acting on sectors of a holographic boundary. Theorem 1 argues that a frame cannot determine its own complete dimension or input/output behavior, or completely characterize other frames, from the accessible boundary data. The proof combines limited interface access with the assumed size relation between a frame and its domain; subsequent discussion connects this to Moore's black-box theorem and Rice's theorem. Adding a metaprocessor is argued to reproduce the same limitation at another boundary. The authors extend the result to self-control, context detection, and cognitive self-explanations. Those broader statements should retain their attribution and complete-identification scope; incomplete knowledge does not by itself exclude useful partial models or experimentally validated control of specified variables.

## Relevance to this research

This source sharpens the difference between an agent's represented model and an external mathematical specification of its implementation.
