## Verified content

Section III.2 introduces causal and information closure and Section III.3 computational closure.
Definition 4 (information closure, Appendix A.4): $I(\mathbf{Z}_{\to t},\mathbf{Z}^{L}_{\to t+1})
= I(\mathbf{X}_{\to t},\mathbf{Z}^{L}_{\to t+1})$ for all $L$, the macro past predicts the macro
future as well as the micro past does. Definition 3 (causal closure, Appendix A.4): the
$\epsilon$-machine and $\upsilon$-machine of the macroscopic process are equivalent. Theorem 1
(Appendix A.4) states that information closure and causal closure are equivalent for
$Z_t=g(\mathbf{X}_{\to t})$. Theorem 2 (Appendix A.6) states that for spatial coarse-grainings
information closure implies computational closure (Definition 5). Theorem 3 (Appendix A.7)
characterises causally/informationally closed levels through strong lumpability of causal
states; the main text (Section III.4) states that a microscopic process has closed levels if and
only if its causal states are strongly lumpable. The framework treats stationary processes;
transients receive no explicit treatment.
