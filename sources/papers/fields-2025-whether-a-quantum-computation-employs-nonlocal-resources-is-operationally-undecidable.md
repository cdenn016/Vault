## Contribution and scope

Fields and colleagues ask what a finite verifier can establish about the physical resources used by an otherwise unknown quantum computer. Their setting separates the verifier's observable classical interface from an environment that contains the alleged provers and possible unobserved quantum channels. They argue that the verifier cannot certify the independence of two putative provers or determine their internal entanglement from its boundary observations. A further argument uses independently chosen spatial reference frames to deny operational certification of a closed timelike resource. The claimed conclusion concerns identifying physical implementations of MIP* and related computation models from observations. It does not refute the mathematical complexity-class identity MIP* = RE. The paper contains no experimental resource-certification result; its broad impossibility statements depend on its generic black-box and boundary assumptions.
