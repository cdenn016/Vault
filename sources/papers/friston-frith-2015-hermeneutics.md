# Active inference, communication and hermeneutics

> [!info] Citation
> Friston, K. J., and Frith, C. D. (2015). *Active inference, communication and hermeneutics*. Cortex, 68, 129–143. [Published full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4502445/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2015.03.025).

[Local published PDF](../pdfs/friston-frith-2015-hermeneutics.pdf), 15 pages; retrieved November 7, 2026.

## TL;DR

The paper models communication as coupled prediction and learning, separating rapid hidden-state inference from slower changes to generative-model parameters.

## Problem & setting

Two simulated birds exchange sounds generated by hierarchical chaotic dynamics. Their model architecture and high-level narrative are supplied; a parameter controlling articulation initially differs. This extends the shared-model communication setting of the authors' companion paper.

## Method

Auditory precision mediates alternating listening and speaking. Prediction errors update hidden-state expectations; accumulated evidence updates parameters between exchanges. Sections 2.4 and 5 distinguish these timescales. The learned parameter maps a higher-level narrative into acoustic dynamics.

## Key results

In the selected simulation, reciprocal hearing supports parameter convergence and maintained song complexity; isolation produces simplification. These are illustrative trajectories under a chosen learnable parameterization, not a general convergence theorem for arbitrary interacting models. See section 5 and Figure 8.

## Relevance to this research

The distinction supports separate state and interface-learning updates in [[Collective active inference]]. The construction does not identify arbitrary cognitive frames, derive recipient selection through [[Expected Free Energy]], or establish a population blanket. [[Generalised synchrony]] remains a dynamical communication analogy with specified coupling assumptions.
