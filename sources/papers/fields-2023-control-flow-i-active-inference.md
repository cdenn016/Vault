## Contribution and scope

Part I identifies control flow as a problem distinct from possessing a repertoire of perceptual and action processes. A resource-limited system must decide which processes to deploy in a particular context and pay the energetic cost of switching between them. The paper develops three related descriptions: transitions among classical dynamical attractors, deployment of quantum reference frames, and sequences of computational processes represented by topological quantum field theories. Its quantum setting assumes a finite bipartite system with a separable joint state over the interval of interest and an explicit interaction-boundary representation. Informative, memory, and thermodynamic sectors distinguish recorded outcomes from the resources supporting their manipulation. The discussion associates internal time with actions and memory writes, and explains why control depends on prior interactions as well as current input. These constructions prepare the tensor-network treatment in Part II; they are not a general empirical demonstration of quantum control in nervous tissue.

## Relevance to this research

The distinction between available computations and their deployment is useful for [[Quantum reference frames]], [[Precision weighting]], and [[Cue-result generative memory]]. A cue may select a process without encoding the process's full result. Applying the formalism to [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] requires keeping thermodynamic expenditure, variational free energy, memory state, and information-geometric update length separate unless a model explicitly relates them.
