# Mind is an abstraction

> [!info] Citation
> Fields, C. (2018) Mind is an abstraction. Constructivist Foundations 13: 350-352.

Local PDF: [PDF](../pdfs/fields-2018-mind-is-an-abstraction.pdf)

## Summary

This commentary questions an epistemic asymmetry used to privilege mind over matter in Bernardo Kastrup's idealism. Fields distinguishes an immediate phenomenal occurrence from a temporally extended mind that holds, compares, and interprets experiences. A mind with memory, capacity, duration, and inferential powers already introduces explanatory abstractions. Experiencing a memory now does not by itself establish the earlier event that the memory purports to represent. Perception likewise includes source-monitoring and confidence judgments, whose felt authority can be misleading.

Fields consequently argues that invoking a mental ground does not avoid the abstraction involved in invoking a material ground. He treats other observers and public records as additional assumptions that make communication and corroboration intelligible. The closing discussion entertains abandoning the demand for a final ontology and allowing pragmatic theories in a possibly dialetheic world. These are philosophical arguments and suggestions; the commentary neither proves idealism false nor derives a physical theory of consciousness.

## Relevance to this research

The distinction between a present record and its represented past is useful for the research program's explicit record laws and information clocks. [[Participatory realism (it from bit)]] can motivate observer-relative descriptions without treating remembered history as self-certifying. The paper supplies no stochastic history kernel, Fisher metric, or calibration identifying information change with physical time; those structures must come from the mathematical construction.
