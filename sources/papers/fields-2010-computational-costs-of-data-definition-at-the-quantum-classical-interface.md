## Contribution and scope

Fields studies the classical work needed to assign meaning to quantum inputs, control parameters, and final measurement outcomes. The paper distinguishes naturally structured encodings from arbitrary associations between classical referents and register states. A table-based telephone-directory example is used to argue that interpretation and oracle setup can cost as much as the nominal quantum search. The conclusion generalizes this to a severe restriction on useful quantum speedups. That broad restriction depends on its data-access and representation assumptions: an arbitrary linear table lookup is not the cost of every decoder, and an efficiently computable predicate need not identify the marked answer in advance. The source is therefore useful as an end-to-end accounting argument, not as an established general refutation of quantum search or quantum algorithmic advantage.

## Relevance to this research

The project can use this as a warning that transmitting a compact code does not make encoding, decoding, or dictionary acquisition free. [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] explicitly separates communicated marginals from receiver-supplied conditional structure. A computational comparison should likewise specify storage, query access, precision, and preprocessing. The paper's particular asymptotic cost claims cannot be transferred to the project's channels without that access model, and no performance experiment is reported here.
