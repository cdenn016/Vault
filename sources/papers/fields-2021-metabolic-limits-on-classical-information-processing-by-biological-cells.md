## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin compare measured cellular energy budgets with an estimated cost of maintaining classical descriptions of molecular position and conformation at very fine spatial and temporal resolution. Their argument treats repeated decoherence as an information-erasing process and combines estimated state-space sizes, decoherence rates, and thermodynamic costs. They conclude that the resulting demand exceeds available metabolic resources and propose that classical information processing is concentrated near cell and compartment boundaries, while bulk cellular biochemistry retains useful quantum coherence. A suggested test looks for correlations violating a Bell inequality between recently separated sister cells. The numerical discrepancy is conditional on the physical accounting used to associate classicality with repeated erasure and on the resolution demanded of the molecular description. It is therefore evidence for the authors' proposed challenge to that description, not direct experimental detection of bulk cellular quantum computation or a measurement of the predicted intercellular entanglement.

## Relevance to this research

This paper supplies an explicit resource argument used in later work on [[Quantum reference frames]] and biological compartmentalization. It is relevant when separating physically recorded information from an analyst's fine-grained model. A classical implementation in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] is not contradicted solely by this estimate: its state variables, effective resolution, thermodynamic interpretation, and relation to microscopic degrees of freedom must first be specified.
