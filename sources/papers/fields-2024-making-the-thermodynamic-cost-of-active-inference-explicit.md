## Contribution and scope

The authors distinguish thermodynamic free energy needed to operate an embodied agent from variational free energy used to score its predictions. Their quantum formulation represents a system boundary by finite information-bearing sectors and describes each measurement/preparation process through a quantum reference frame. An atomic system is defined relative to a representation with one effective reference frame. Within this representation, its energy acquisition and computation share the same boundary; selective control of energy flows requires a composite organization with distinguishable components. The paper then discusses metaprocessors that allocate energy among components and a tradeoff among sensing, memory, fuel acquisition, and boundary exposure. These statements depend on the representational definition of atomicity and on the assumed boundary model. They are not measurements of metabolic costs or a universal numerical conversion between statistical and physical energy.

## Relevance to this research

This source is directly useful for keeping resource accounting explicit in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. It gives a vocabulary for coupling inference to power and memory budgets without identifying VFE with joules. The project's communication law would still need specified energy-transfer variables, units, and dynamics before this becomes a physical implementation. Its compartmentalization discussion motivates a question about resource allocation, not a proof of endogenous parent formation under the project's criteria.
