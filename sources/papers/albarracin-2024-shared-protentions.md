# Shared Protentions in Multi-Agent Active Inference

> [!info] Citation
> Mahault Albarracin, Riddhi J. Pitliya, Toby St. Clere Smithe, Daniel Ari Friedman, Karl Friston, and Maxwell J. D. Ramstead (2024). "Shared Protentions in Multi-Agent Active Inference." *Entropy* **26**(4), 303. https://doi.org/10.3390/e26040303

## TL;DR

The paper combines Husserlian protention, active inference, and category-theoretic polynomial dynamics to formalize how agents may represent shared goals. It proposes compositional interfaces for agents that model one another and a shared environment, but does not prove convergence to a common posterior or construct a general group-level Bayesian mechanics.

## Problem & setting

Shared intentionality requires more than agents having simultaneous individual predictions. The authors distinguish protention as a structured orientation toward future possibilities from an explicit online prediction, and ask how shared protentional content can be represented across a group.

## Method

Polynomial representations encode agent interfaces and their composition. Internal homs and tensor products allow an agent's generative model to include the actions and observations of other agents. Sheaf- and topos-theoretic language is used to discuss consistency among local viewpoints and the possibility of a shared worldview.

## Key results

The paper supplies a formal type language for nested multi-agent models and shows how agents can represent a group as the context needed to close their generative models. The results are conceptual and categorical; they do not establish that stochastic learning dynamics attain consensus or that summed individual free energies equal a group free energy.

## Relevance to this research

This is a useful interface-level comparator for [[Agents as fibre-bundle sections]] and [[Collective active inference]]. Its compositional typing sharpens the distinction between a compatible family of local models and a single normalized joint law. A gauge-aware extension would additionally need transport maps and coherence conditions between local frames; those are not supplied by the polynomial interface alone.
