## Verified content

Individuals are aggregates that preserve a measure of temporal integrity, propagating information
from their past into their future. Formalising this with information theory and graphical models
yields three forms of individuality, organismal, colonial and driven, which differ in
environmental dependence and inherited information. The preprint's first section is titled "The
architecture of individuality"; its abstract says the approach could expand the scope of adaptive
phenomena "particularly in the microscopic and macroscopic regimes of molecular and social
phenomena" and that replication-based definitions "tend to fail in the case of cultural change".
Measurements are coarse-grained into (possibly nested) partitions, and legitimate partitions
propagate information forward so that uncertainty is reduced over time; the closing discussion
describes slow variables as coarse-grained encodings that reduce environmental uncertainty.

## Relevance to this research

On this view a culture counts as an individual to the degree that its coarse variables propagate
their own past, which is graded rather than an event, matching the soft-formation view in
[[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]. "Inherited information" is the founding imprint of
[[multiagentelbo-2026-09-28-early-parent]], and the choice between discarding it after the transient
and keeping it as a parent memory variable is a choice between forms of individuality. The paper
has no timescale-separation analysis or validity window. Interpretive mapping.
