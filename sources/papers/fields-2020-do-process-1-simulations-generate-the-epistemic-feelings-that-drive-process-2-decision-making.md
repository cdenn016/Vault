## Reading basis

Author manuscript dated June 14, 2020; 55 PDF pages. Read PDF pages 1, 18, and 38–39 (setting, attention representation, limits, and conclusion). This is a targeted ingestion reading, not a full proof audit.

## Contribution and scope

Fields and Glazebrook propose that deliberative Process-2 reasoning can be built from repeated Process-1 simulations, with qualitative confidence or epistemic feelings controlling further processing. Chu spaces and cone-cocone diagrams represent distributed information flow, while global-workspace ideas motivate the cognitive interpretation. Downward constraints represent attentional selection; graded weights require additional probability structure. The conclusion describes quantitative confidence as a further aggregation layer over repeated simulations. Diagram commutativity provides a formal consistency condition, but handling genuinely contextual decision environments is explicitly deferred. The proposal is a theoretical cognitive architecture and does not report a new experiment establishing the hypothesized mechanism.

## Relevance to this research

This connects [[Attention and consciousness]] with [[Quantum reference frames]] and the distinction between a stored probability and a process that generates judgments. For cue/result inference, it motivates separating retrieval, iterative simulation, and confidence readout. A cone-cocone diagram does not by itself define a normalized joint distribution or prove that local message updates minimize one global objective. Contextual extensions should retain the authors' stated open status.
