## Contribution and scope

This study attempted to identify observables suitable for a CHSH entanglement test between a human participant and cultured PANC-1 cells. The participant measurements included EEG, heart-rate variability, and skin response; cellular measures included calcium activity and cytoskeletal markers. The authors report classical correlations and changes in cellular invasiveness, but no CHSH violation and no demonstrated entanglement. They emphasize that the measured variables did not satisfy their requirements for an informative test, and discuss the mismatch between millisecond neural recordings and minute-scale cellular measurements. Their discussion of possible quantum explanations remains speculative, and they explicitly leave the interaction mechanism unresolved. The reported treatment-associated outcomes cannot be used as evidence that a quantum communication channel exists, nor does this unsuccessful search establish a general impossibility of biological entanglement.

## Relevance to this research

For [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]], the relevance is methodological: correlations, directed statistical dependencies, and quantum entanglement are different evidential claims. A proposed physical interpretation of communication requires observables and a discriminating protocol. This paper is a negative experimental result for the intended entanglement search, not empirical support for physics-from-inference, nonlocal agency, or the project's gauge geometry. It belongs beside the theoretical papers as a limit on what this research line has experimentally established.
