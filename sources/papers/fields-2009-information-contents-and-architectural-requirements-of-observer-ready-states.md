## Contribution and scope

The paper analyzes the task of observing multiple apparatus over time and later reporting accumulated results. It identifies stored parsers for source and pointer recognition, source-tagged ordered records, conditional branching, and return to a ready state after each cycle. A fixed finite task can be implemented by a finite-state machine; the stronger Turing-equivalent architecture claim applies to the generalized task with arbitrarily extensible observation periods and recognizers. The later quantum interpretation treats the implemented parsers as POVMs. Its Born-rule argument assumes unbiased environmental sampling and chooses ancillary multiplicities proportional to squared amplitudes. Those choices should remain explicit, rather than treating the counting step as a standalone derivation of the quantum probability law. The architectural analysis is the clearest reusable contribution.
