## Contribution and scope

Fields and colleagues ask how a system can experience internally generated imagery while a boundary-based account associates experience with sensory input. Their proposed architecture contains internal informational boundaries, called inner screens, between components that communicate classically. These boundaries are defined in state space and need not coincide with an organism's visible surface. Quantum reference frames supply stable measurement and action standards, while executive components allocate thermodynamic free-energy resources among available processes. The paper distinguishes information carried by a screen from the regulation that determines how that information is read, relating the latter to precision in classical active inference. Internal components can therefore present information to one another without the whole organism having direct access to every internal computation. This is a theoretical account of attention and imaginative experience, not a demonstration of consciousness in arbitrary physical systems. The authors expressly state that the free-energy principle alone is not a theory of consciousness.

## Relevance to this research

The architecture informs distinctions among internal computation, externally available records, and receiver access in [[Cue-result generative memory]] and [[Quantum reference frames]]. It also motivates explicit interfaces between components of [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]]. A classical normalized record kernel can be compared with an inner screen operationally; identifying it with a quantum boundary or a phenomenal experience requires additional assumptions absent from that kernel alone.
