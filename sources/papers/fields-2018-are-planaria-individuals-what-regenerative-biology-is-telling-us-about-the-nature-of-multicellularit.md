## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin use planarian regeneration to question whether an intact organism is always the most informative unit of biological individuality. They review evidence concerning neoblasts, the proliferative stem cells that maintain and regenerate planarian bodies, emphasizing their mobility, genetic heterogeneity, and lineage persistence. On the authors' interpretation, these cells both cooperate to maintain a shared somatic environment and compete for that environment's resources and suitability for their own descendants. The familiar animal body is consequently treated as an obligate environment maintained by a population of potential individuals. The article proposes that some planaria exemplify an incomplete transition to multicellular individuality. It explicitly leaves unresolved how cooperation among neoblasts is regulated strongly enough to preserve coherent morphology and behavior. This is a biological interpretation of reviewed evidence, not a demonstration that individuality has one universally applicable definition or that every planarian lineage has identical organization.
