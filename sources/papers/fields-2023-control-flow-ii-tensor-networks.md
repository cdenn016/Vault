## Contribution and scope

Part II represents active-inference control flow through networks of tensors. A system switches among quantum reference frames or corresponding computational processes; a transition matrix encodes the probabilities of these switches, and contracted tensors provide a representation of ordered operations. The authors extend this construction to hierarchies and discuss implementations using topological quantum neural networks. Their theorem is stated as an equivalence between nontrivial control flow and tensor-network representability. Further corollaries identify factorization with decoherent sectors and describe a free-energy-compliant control network as a decomposition of the identity at a sufficiently large scale. These are stronger assertions than the basic availability of a tensor representation and are recorded here as the paper's claims. The ingestion reading does not independently validate the converse implication or infer an exact identity cycle merely from return toward a steady-state region. The paper also offers biological examples of context-dependent resource deployment.

## Relevance to this research

This supplies representational tools for [[Collective active inference]] and [[Quantum reference frames]], especially when control switches among incompatible tasks. For [[VFE Transformer Program]], it motivates distinguishing a tensor description of a transition from a dynamical account of why that transition occurs. Representability alone does not demonstrate a normalized generative interpretation, exact periodicity, or an autonomous higher-level agent in [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
