# Discrete hierarchical organization of social group sizes

> [!info] Citation
> W.-X. Zhou, D. Sornette, R.A. Hill, R.I.M. Dunbar (2005). "Discrete hierarchical organization
> of social group sizes." *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* **272**(1561), 439–444.
> DOI: [10.1098/rspb.2004.2970](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2004.2970).

## TL;DR

Human social groups do not have a continuum of sizes: fractal analysis across independent data
sets finds a discrete hierarchy of preferred sizes in geometric progression — roughly 3–5, 9–15,
30–45, and onward — with a **preferred scaling ratio close to three**. The layering extends the
social-brain-hypothesis picture from one cognitive limit to a whole nested tower of them.

## What it establishes

An empirical regularity: social organization is hierarchically layered with a small, roughly
constant branching ratio, rather than flat or continuously graded. The ratio-three value is a
measured preference, not a theoretical derivation.

## Relevance to this research

The measured branching ratio near three is the empirical cousin of the laboratory's blocking
ratio $b$: the nested-cycle tower's inner triangles and the ratio-typed fixed structures of the
rescaling map (ratio-2 and ratio-3 endpoints differing by 0.81 relative sup) make the ratio a
physical parameter of hierarchy formation, and this paper says human hierarchies empirically
prefer one. See [[Staged hierarchy formation and RG composability]] and
[[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
