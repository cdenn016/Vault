## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin ask whether major evolutionary transitions can be understood as large-scale attractors analogous to target morphologies in development and regeneration. A developmental system can reach a characteristic anatomy from different initial states and after perturbations; the authors propose extending this pattern to evolutionary transitions such as endosymbiosis, multicellularity, and social organization. Evolution and development are then compared as searches constrained from both smaller and larger scales. Reducing environmental uncertainty supplies the proposed common pressure, while newly formed collectives create both resources and constraints for their components. The paper presents this as a hypothesis that could motivate controlled synthetic and computational studies, not as a demonstration that evolution has a predetermined final form. In particular, the suggested repeatability of major transitions remains distinct from an established universal attractor theorem or from evidence that historical contingency has been eliminated.
