## Contribution and scope

Fields and colleagues argue that finite systems with morphological degrees of freedom and locally limited free-energy resources tend toward architectures supporting hierarchical computation. In the proposed organization, each level coarse-grains incoming information and refines outgoing actions. The review relates this structure to quantum reference frames, cone-cocone diagrams, and topological quantum neural networks, drawing examples from molecular pathways through brain organization. It also distinguishes software simulation of a neural architecture from physically realizing its parameters: repeated memory access can carry costs that are absent from an idealized description of inference alone. The claimed developmental tendency therefore depends on resource constraints, available morphological changes, and the adopted free-energy formulation. The paper explicitly defers detailed quantum-brain models. Its use of a general quantum representation does not provide experimental evidence that a particular biological structure performs a useful coherent quantum computation.

## Relevance to this research

This is prior theory for efficient communication across scales in [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and for the physical cost of inference in [[VFE Transformer Program]]. It motivates asking which messages a hierarchy preserves and where parameters are physically stored. It does not establish that every coarse-graining inherits the same normalized law, preserves Fisher geometry, or produces exact autonomous parent dynamics in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]].
