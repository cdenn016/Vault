# Holographic screens are classical information channels

## Reading basis

Published article, Quantum Reports 2 (2020), DOI 10.3390/quantum2020022, published June 19, 2020. Read PDF pages 1, 5–6, and 9, bipartite assumptions, Section 4/Theorem 1, and Section 6 conclusion. PDF page numbers include cover material.

## Contribution and scope

Fields and Marcianò restrict attention to a finite bipartite quantum system whose two components interact exclusively through one shared classical channel. Theorem 1 asserts an equivalence among a product state, an ideal finite-capacity ancillary classical channel, and an ancillary holographic screen encoding interaction eigenvalues. The screen is ancillary because its coordinates do not alter the interaction being represented. A geometric reading assigns an area through a Planck-scale bit density, while the generalized interface description is intended to be independent of a spacetime embedding. The assumptions of a pure separable joint state, ideal channel, and bipartite closure are central. The theorem should not be paraphrased as saying that every separable mixed state or every noisy classical communication architecture satisfies the same equivalence. The authors defer a complete black-hole information analysis requiring more parties.

## Relevance to this research

This is a compact entry point to [[Emergent spacetime and holography]] as finite information access. It offers the project's agent channels a conceptual comparison, but an ancillary qubit encoding is not automatically a normalized classical observation kernel, and an assigned screen area is not a derived Fisher metric. Any use in [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] should preserve the distinction between a representation of exchanged information and a construction of physical geometry and dynamics.
