## Contribution and scope

Fields and Glazebrook challenge the inference from diverse kinds of experienced or described thought to distinct computational architectures. Their response to Gilead, Trope, and Liberman separates the functional roles of representations from the physical or algorithmic organization implementing them. Beliefs, intentions, episodes, categories, and linguistic structures can differ in use without requiring unrelated architectural principles. The authors argue that introspective access is an interface useful for action and does not transparently reveal the implementation of cognition. They propose that a scale-independent architectural description can support representational diversity across subcellular and organismal levels. This is a short critical commentary, not a new neural experiment or a proof that every cognitive operation shares an identical implementation. Its useful claim is a warning against treating a taxonomy of conscious content as sufficient evidence for a taxonomy of underlying mechanisms.

## Relevance to this research

This distinction is useful for [[VFE Transformer Program]] and [[Cue-result generative memory]]: assigning different semantic labels to internal quantities does not show that distinct mechanisms exist, while sharing an update form does not make every quantity functionally interchangeable. It also limits architectural conclusions drawn from [[Consciousness and the hard problem]]. The appropriate comparison is between explicit state, update, and readout structures, with behavioral or neural evidence needed for claims about their realization.
