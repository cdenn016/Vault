# Using AI methods to evaluate a minimal model for perception

> [!info] Citation
> Prentner, R. and Fields, C. (2019) Using AI methods to evaluate a minimal model for perception. Open Philosophy 2: 503-524 (doi: 10.1515/opphil-2019-0034).

[Local full text](../pdfs/prentner-2019-using-ai-methods-to-evaluate-a-minimal-model-for-perception.pdf). [Primary source](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/prentner-fields-OP-2019.pdf).

## Reading basis

Published open-access 2019 article. Read PDF pages 1, 4, 9, 12, and 17–18 (model motivation, experiment design, and limitations). This is a targeted ingestion reading, not a full proof audit.

## Contribution and scope

Prentner and Fields study networks of elementary binary agents as a minimal model of perception. Local update rules produce collective attractors, and a genetic algorithm selects rules using a supplied payoff function. The reported preliminary experiment uses fixed network topologies and prescribed inputs; it illustrates how stable collective responses can arise from sparse local capacities. The discussion is explicit that stable response patterns are not sufficient for agency. Sensory and motor interfaces, autonomous action selection, and a source of fitness feedback still need to be supplied or derived. The authors identify endogenous embodiment as an open question rather than treating a manually initialized network as an already autonomous organism.

## Relevance to this research

This is a useful predecessor for [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]]: it separates a collective attractor from a newly constituted agent. Its proposed subnetwork interactions resemble the project's formation questions, but the paper leaves the required body, interface, and objective construction unresolved. The third-party simulation is evidence for the stated toy model; it does not verify a gauge-VFE dynamics or establish that a parent probability law closes under aggregation.
