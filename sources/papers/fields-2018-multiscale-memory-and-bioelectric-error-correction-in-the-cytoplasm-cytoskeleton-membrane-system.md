## Contribution and scope

Fields and Levin review inherited biological organization beyond genomic sequence, focusing on the coupled cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, and membrane system. They argue that persistent cellular structure and bioelectric patterns can encode constraints relevant to development and regeneration across generations. Endogenous bioelectricity is proposed to help correct errors in these distributed memories, allowing an anatomical target to be restored despite changes in molecular details or initial conditions. The review uses continuity of cellular structures and experimental manipulations of morphology to motivate a multiscale view of biological information. Its claim concerns interacting material memory resources, rather than an isolated storage site holding a complete blueprint. The role assigned to bioelectricity as an error-correcting core is a theoretical synthesis of the cited evidence; the paper does not derive a universal coding theorem or show that the genome is irrelevant to anatomical organization.
