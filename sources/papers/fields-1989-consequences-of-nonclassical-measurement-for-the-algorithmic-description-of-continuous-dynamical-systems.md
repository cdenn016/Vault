## Contribution and scope

Fields examines claims that continuous dynamical systems possess computational power beyond discrete machines. The argument focuses on measurement: unlimited nonperturbative precision permits excessively rich computational interpretations of continuous trajectories, while finite-resolution physical observations limit the number of distinguishable states. At resolutions where measurement changes the dynamics, the paper uses stochastic transition descriptions instead. It concludes that observable behavior can be represented algorithmically and simulated by a universal Turing machine. That conclusion requires the relevant transition probabilities and observation map to be computable; naming a probability distribution alone does not establish an effective simulation algorithm or its cost. The paper is best treated as an early operational argument about computational attribution and measurement resolution, not a settled general theorem ruling out every model of physical hypercomputation.

## Relevance to this research

This historical precursor is relevant to [[Gauge-Theoretic Multi-Agent VFE Model]] when distinguishing an underlying continuous law from behavior available through finite records.
