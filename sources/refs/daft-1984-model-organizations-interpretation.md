# Toward a Model of Organizations as Interpretation Systems

> [!info] Citation
> Daft, R. L., Weick, K. E. (1984). *Toward a Model of Organizations as Interpretation Systems*. The Academy of Management Review, 9(2), 284-295. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1984.4277657)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/daft-1984-model-organizations-interpretation.pdf), 14 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A comparative model of organizations as interpretation systems with four interpretation modes (enacting, discovering, undirected viewing, conditioned viewing).

## Problem & setting

The opening pages use the game of twenty questions as an analogy: organizations have limited questions and time and try to interpret their environment, and building interpretations of the environment is described as a basic need of individuals and organizations. The local file is a Portuguese-language reprint (RAE-Clássicos, 2005) that carries the English abstract.

## Method

Each mode is determined by two factors: management's beliefs about the environment and organizational intrusiveness. The paper proposes the model and states hypotheses rather than reporting a test.

## Key results

The modes are hypothesized to be associated with organizational differences in environmental scanning, equivocality reduction, strategy and decision making. No empirical results are claimed in the portions read.

## Relevance to this research

The Collective Agents manuscript cites the paper in its related-work section for the view that organizations scan their environment as interpretation systems, next to economic treatments of the firm as an information processor.
