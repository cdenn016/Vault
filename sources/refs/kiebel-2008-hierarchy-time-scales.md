# A Hierarchy of Time-Scales and the Brain

> [!info] Citation
> Kiebel, S. J., Daunizeau, J., Friston, K. J. (2008). *A Hierarchy of Time-Scales and the Brain*. PLoS Computational Biology, 4(11), e1000209. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000209)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kiebel-2008-hierarchy-time-scales.pdf), 12 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes that cortical anatomy recapitulates a temporal hierarchy in the dynamics of environmental states, with fast sensory processing at the lowest level and slow contextual changes at the highest, and supports this with a perceptual-inference model and a review of empirical evidence.

## Problem & setting

The authors ask how brain function can be understood through a hierarchy of temporal scales at which representations of the environment evolve, with faster representations unfolding under slower contextual ones.

## Method

A mathematical model exploits the temporal structure of fast sensory input to track the slower trajectories of its causes, as a proof of concept that slowly changing neuronal states can encode paths of faster sensory states. The paper then reviews empirical evidence for a rostro-caudal gradient of time-scales in cortex.

## Key results

The abstract presents the model as a proof of concept and the framework as yielding predictions testable by manipulating the time-scales of sensory input.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper twice. In the hierarchy section it notes that the paper imposes a separation of timescales between levels, whereas in the manuscript the separation follows from read and write weights. In related work it reports, from targeted extraction of the full text, that a slow level acts as a control parameter of a fast one and that the paper says the separation is not a property of coupled neuronal systems as such.
