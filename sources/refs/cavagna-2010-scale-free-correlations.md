# Scale-free correlations in starling flocks

> [!info] Citation
> Cavagna, A., Cimarelli, A., Giardina, I., Parisi, G., Santagati, R., Stefanini, F., Viale, M. (2010). *Scale-free correlations in starling flocks*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 107(26), 11865-11870. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1005766107)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/cavagna-2010-scale-free-correlations.pdf), 24 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages. The local file is the arXiv preprint, whose title reads "bird flocks" rather than "starling flocks".

## TL;DR

The paper reports measurements on large flocks of starlings showing that the range of velocity correlations between birds scales with the linear size of the flock, so that correlations are scale free. The authors suggest flocks behave as critical systems.

## Problem & setting

Studies of collective animal behavior have mostly asked how global order arises from simple rules. The paper asks instead how the group as a whole responds to perturbations, and proposes that scale-free behavioral correlations achieve this.

## Method

The authors reconstruct the three-dimensional position and velocity of individual birds in large starling flocks and measure how far the velocity fluctuations of different birds are correlated with one another.

## Key results

The spatial range of the correlation is not constant but grows with flock size. The authors read this as meaning that a change in one animal's behavioral state affects, and is affected by, that of all others however large the group, which gives each animal an effective perception range far beyond the direct interaction range. They conclude that flocks behave as critical systems poised to respond to perturbations.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper as an empirical case of scale-free correlations, which it places at or beyond the boundary of its linear, massive theory where the correlation length is comparable to system size. It describes the mechanism behind such correlations, a broken continuous symmetry, as outside that model, and reads the flocks as the massless regime where its size bound is vacuous. See [[Collective motion and flocking]], [[Critical Phenomena]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
