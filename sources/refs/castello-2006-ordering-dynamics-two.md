# Ordering Dynamics with Two Non-Excluding Options: Bilingualism in Language Competition

> [!info] Citation
> Castelló, X., Eguíluz, V. M., San Miguel, M. (2006). *Ordering Dynamics with Two Non-Excluding Options: Bilingualism in Language Competition*. New Journal of Physics, 8(12), 308. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/8/12/308)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/castello-2006-ordering-dynamics-two.pdf), 16 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

The paper extends the voter model with a third, mixed state alongside the two equivalent states, motivated by language competition where the mixed state stands for bilingualism. It studies ordering and coarsening on regular lattices and small-world networks.

## Problem & setting

Interacting agents can each be in state A, state B or a mixed state AB. The paper asks how the system orders over time, how interfaces between domains evolve, and how this changes with the interaction network.

## Method

The authors define the three-state dynamics, then study the ordering process and the associated interface and coarsening dynamics, first on regular lattices and then on small-world networks. The contents list shows sections on the model, coarsening on a lattice, coarsening on a small-world network, and a summary.

## Key results

According to the abstract, agents in the mixed state occupy the interfaces. They change the coarsening mechanism from interfacial-noise driven, as in the voter model, to curvature driven. The authors report that this change also arises for a class of perturbations of the voter model. On a small-world network the mixed agents restore coarsening and remove the metastable states of the voter model, and the time to reach the absorbing state grows like $\ln N$ with system size $N$, against growth like $N$ for the voter model.
