# Toward a unified theory of efficient, predictive, and sparse coding

> [!info] Citation
> Chalk, M., Marre, O., Tkačik, G. (2018). *Toward a unified theory of efficient, predictive, and sparse coding*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 115(1), 186-191. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711114115)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/chalk-2018-unified-efficient-predictive.pdf), 6 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages. The local file is the 2017 bioRxiv preprint, titled "Towards" rather than "Toward".

## TL;DR

The paper builds a single framework that contains efficient coding, sparse coding and predictive coding as special cases and extends to new regimes such as sparse predictive coding. It compares what each objective predicts for neural responses.

## Problem & setting

Theoretical neuroscience offers several first-principles accounts of sensory neurons: efficient coding (maximal information about inputs under constraints), sparse coding (selective responses to rare features) and predictive coding (encoding what is predictive of the future). The paper starts from the statement that, apart from special cases, the relations between them are unclear.

## Method

The authors formulate a unified objective that encompasses the earlier theories, and examine cases where the objectives conflict or act together. They also compare predictive and sparse coding on natural movies.

## Key results

The abstract states that predictive coding can lead neurons either to correlate or to decorrelate their inputs, depending on the stimuli, while efficient coding at low noise always predicts decorrelation. For natural movies, predictive and sparse coding predict qualitatively different responses to visual motion. Only the abstract and the opening of the introduction were read here.
