# The Puzzling Persistence of the Distance Effect on Bilateral Trade

> [!info] Citation
> Disdier, A.-C., Head, K. (2008). *The Puzzling Persistence of the Distance Effect on Bilateral Trade*. Review of Economics and Statistics, 90(1), 37-48. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.1.37)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A meta-analysis of estimated distance effects on bilateral trade, which asks whether the effect has fallen over time.

## Problem & setting

International economics has established that bilateral trade decreases with distance. The paper examines whether improvements over time have weakened this effect.

## Method

The authors examine 1052 distance effects estimated in 78 papers, using information collected on each estimate to test hypotheses about causes of variation.

## Key results

According to the abstract, the negative impact of distance on trade has not shrunk and has instead increased slightly over the last century, and this holds after controlling for many differences in samples and methods.
