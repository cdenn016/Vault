# The Origins of Scaling in Cities

> [!info] Citation
> Bettencourt, L. M. A. (2013). *The Origins of Scaling in Cities*. Science, 340(6139), 1438-1441. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1235823)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A theoretical framework in which a small set of locally operating principles yields scaling relations for the social, spatial and infrastructural properties of cities, compared against data from thousands of cities.

## Problem & setting

The abstract describes cities as interdependent social, economic, infrastructural and spatial systems spanning a wide range of scales, which has made a scientific treatment difficult.

## Method

The author develops a framework that predicts average properties of cities as scaling relations applying to all urban systems, starting from principles that operate locally.

## Key results

The abstract reports that the predictions were confirmed for thousands of cities in urban systems at different levels of development, and that measures of urban efficiency, which balance socioeconomic outputs against infrastructural costs, are independent of city size.
