# The Gravity Equation in International Trade: An Explanation

> [!info] Citation
> Chaney, T. (2018). *The Gravity Equation in International Trade: An Explanation*. Journal of Political Economy, 126(1), 150-177. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/694292)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper offers an explanation for the role of distance in the gravity equation of trade, under assumptions about the firm size distribution and how the distance of a firm's exports depends on its size.

## Problem & setting

The gravity equation says bilateral exports are proportional to economic size and inversely proportional to geographic distance. The abstract says the role of size is well understood and that of distance is not.

## Method

The explanation is conditional: if firm sizes are Pareto distributed, if the average squared distance of a firm's exports is an increasing power function of its size, and if a parameter restriction holds, then the distance elasticity of trade is constant at long distances. Only the abstract was read.

## Key results

Under those assumptions the long-distance elasticity is constant, and when firm sizes follow Zipf's law, trade is inversely proportional to distance.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its abstract-collectives section, in a passage on how the effect of distance on trade has changed over time, as the source of the statement that a constant long-distance elasticity follows from Pareto-distributed firm sizes and a power-law growth of exported distance with size. The connection to the vault is the treatment of economies as collectives whose coupling decays with distance; see [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Network structure — small-world and scale-free]].
