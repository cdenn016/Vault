# The Organization of Decentralized Information Processing

> [!info] Citation
> Radner, R. (1993). *The Organization of Decentralized Information Processing*. Econometrica, 61(5), 1109--1146. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2951495)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

A decision-theoretic model of a firm whose managers are information processors of limited capacity, with efficiency measured by the number of processors and the delay between receiving information and implementing a decision.

## Problem & setting

The abstract describes a firm as a network of limited-capacity processors handling items of information, in one-shot and repeated regimes.

## Key results

According to the abstract, the author characterizes efficient networks in both regimes and derives a production function relating the number of items processed to the number of processors and the delay. He sketches applications to common decision paradigms and implications for decentralization and returns to scale.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for decentralized processing under delay, within its summary of how economics treats the firm as an information processor. The topic parallels the vault's work on communication-limited networks of agents; see [[Communication-constrained inference]], [[Bounded Rationality]] and [[Decentralized Bayesian inference]].
