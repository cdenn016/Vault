# Geographic Routing in Social Networks

> [!info] Citation
> Liben-Nowell, D., Novak, J., Kumar, R., Raghavan, P., Tomkins, A. (2005). *Geographic Routing in Social Networks*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 102(33), 11623-11628. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0503018102)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes a model relating geography to friendship in a social network, in which the chance of befriending a person is inversely proportional to the number of closer people, and shows that short chains can be found in every network that obeys the relationship.

## Problem & setting

Message forwarding along short chains of acquaintances had been verified in real social networks, but the abstract states that earlier theoretical models had not been shown to capture behavior in real networks. The authors introduce a richer model that ties geography to friendship.

## Key results

In a large social network, one third of friendships are reported to be independent of geography, and the remainder follow the proposed relationship. The authors also prove analytically that short chains can be discovered in every network exhibiting it.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the finding that about one third of friendships in a large online network are independent of geography, next to a result on distance decay of mobile-phone links, to show that geography limits connection even where telecommunication links are cheap.
