# Measuring the Diffusion of Linguistic Change

> [!info] Citation
> Nerbonne, J. (2010). *Measuring the Diffusion of Linguistic Change*. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 365(1559), 3821-3828. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2010.0048)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The article examines how linguistic variation depends on geographical distance as a probe of diffusion through ordinary social contact, and asks what feature-level diffusion dynamics are compatible with the sublinear aggregate curve reported since Séguy.

## Problem & setting

The abstract restricts attention to changes probably propagated by contact rather than conquest, recent settlement or large-scale migration. It adopts two simplifying assumptions, acknowledged as imperfect: that variation arises from either diffusion or independent innovation, and that social contact can be operationalized as geographical distance. Quantitative studies since Séguy report sublinear growth of aggregate variation with distance, while dialectology and historical linguistics have mostly traced individual features.

## Method

The author examines simulations of diffusion to ask which dynamics of single features are compatible with the aggregate curve.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this article, from its abstract, for the empirical statement that dialect variation grows sublinearly with distance, in its discussion of abstract collectives and in related work where dialect boundaries are also compared to interfaces produced by a conformity nonlinearity. The connection is to [[Cultural evolution and social learning]], [[Conformist transmission]] and [[Opinion dynamics]] on spatial networks. Only the abstract was read, so the simulation results are not described here.
