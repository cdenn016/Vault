# An Active Inference Model of Collective Intelligence

> [!info] Citation
> Kaufmann, R., Gupta, P., Taylor, J. (2021). *An Active Inference Model of Collective Intelligence*. Entropy, 23(7), 830. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23070830)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kaufmann-2021-active-inference-model.pdf), 33 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

The paper builds a minimal agent-based model of active-inference agents and measures collective performance as agents gain Theory of Mind and goal alignment. Its stated claim is that alignment between agents arises from their own dynamics and is not imposed from outside.

## Problem & setting

The authors argue that formal models of collective intelligence lack a mathematical link between local interactions among individuals and behavior of the collective. They contrast accounts built on individual cognitive mechanisms with system-level accounts that treat collectives as dynamical systems, and propose the free energy principle and its active-inference process theory as a common framework across scales, with agents nested in larger agents.

## Method

Four model variants are compared: baseline active-inference agents, agents with Theory of Mind, agents with goal alignment, and agents with both. Performance of the composite system is the measure of collective intelligence.

## Key results

The authors report illustrative simulation results in which each added cognitive capability raises system performance, and they describe alignment as emerging endogenously rather than from incentives or top-down priors on collective behavior. The paper calls the results illustrative.
