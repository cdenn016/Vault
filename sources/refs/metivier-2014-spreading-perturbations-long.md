# Spreading of Perturbations in Long-Range Interacting Classical Lattice Models

> [!info] Citation
> Métivier, D., Bachelard, R., Kastner, M. (2014). *Spreading of Perturbations in Long-Range Interacting Classical Lattice Models*. Physical Review Letters, 112(21), 210601. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.210601); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.7556)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/metivier-2014-spreading-perturbations-long.pdf), 10 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

Lieb-Robinson-type bounds for a large class of classical Hamiltonian lattice models, including interactions of arbitrarily long range.

## Problem & setting

Lieb-Robinson bounds limit the spread of effects of a local perturbation to an effective causal region. The original proof needs finite-range interactions, and extensions to power-law decay give a region that is no longer cone shaped. The introduction says that for strong long-range interactions, with the decay exponent below the lattice dimension, no Lieb-Robinson-type results were known. The authors note that little was known for classical mechanics apart from specific nearest-neighbor models.

## Method

The bounds are constructed by a suitable rescaling of energy or time. A refined bound, sharper than conventional ones, is used to capture the shape of the causal region, and numerical results for classical long-range XY chains are used as a check.

## Key results

Per the abstract, the effect of a perturbation of the initial state is effectively confined to the interior of a causal region of logarithmic shape, with only small algebraically decaying effects outside. The authors say the refined bound is needed to get this shape right, and they discuss the consequence for relaxation to equilibrium in long-range lattice models.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for the logarithmic causal region reported for classical long-range lattices, as the match for one of the spread regimes it finds for linear message passing with long-range coupling.
