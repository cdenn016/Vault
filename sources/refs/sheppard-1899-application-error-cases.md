# On the Application of the Theory of Error to Cases of Normal Distribution and Normal Correlation

> [!info] Citation
> Sheppard, W. F. (1899). *On the Application of the Theory of Error to Cases of Normal Distribution and Normal Correlation*. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London. Series A, 192, 101-167. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1899.0003)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A memoir applying the theory of error to statistical data whose frequency curves are approximately normal, including the joint frequencies of correlated attributes.

## Problem & setting

The abstract recalls Quetelet's finding that anthropometric frequency curves resemble the normal curve, its extension by Galton to correlation of two or more attributes, and the fact that only finitely many individuals can be observed. It then states that, for a large random sample from a community of practically unbounded size, the theory of error supplies a test of whether a proposed law of distribution or correlation may hold for the community.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper by name for the orthant probability of a bivariate normal, which it re-derives itself: the probability that two correlated Gaussian variables differ in sign equals the angle $\arccos\Gamma$ divided by $\pi$, a result the manuscript calls Sheppard's formula. The paper was not read; the claim attributed to it rests on the manuscript's own derivation and on its attribution. The tie to the vault is through [[Gaussian Beliefs]] and [[Mutual information]].
