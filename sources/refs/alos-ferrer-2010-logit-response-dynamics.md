# The logit-response dynamics

> [!info] Citation
> Alós-Ferrer, C., Netzer, N. (2010). *The logit-response dynamics*. Games and Economic Behavior, 68(2), 413-427. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2009.08.004)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/alos-ferrer-2010-logit-response-dynamics.pdf), 34 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages. The local file is the June 2008 working-paper version, not the journal version.

## TL;DR

The paper characterizes the stochastically stable states of logit-response learning dynamics in games, with an arbitrary specification of which players may revise at each step.

## Problem & setting

The introduction places the dynamics among noisy learning models, contrasting it with the mistakes model. In logit response, players choose actions with full-support logit probabilities that favor higher myopic payoffs, so noise is built in and the size of suboptimal payoffs matters.

## Key results

The abstract of the working paper states convergence to the set of Nash equilibria in best-response potential games, and failure of the dynamics to select potential maximizers beyond exact potential games. It also studies how robust equilibrium selection is to the specification of revision opportunities. The text I read is a 2008 version, so these statements may differ from the published article.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the published paper for the statement that convergence of asynchronous logit revision to a Gibbs law depends on the asynchrony of revisions. The reading record says the manuscript read the abstract only. I have read the first pages of the working-paper version, which treat arbitrary revision opportunities. It connects to the vault's pages on opinion dynamics and game-theoretic dynamics through logit updates.
