# Active inference and epistemic value

> [!info] Citation
> Friston, K., Rigoli, F., Ognibene, D., Mathys, C., Fitzgerald, T., Pezzulo, G. (2015). *Active inference and epistemic value*. Cognitive Neuroscience 6(4), 187-214. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17588928.2015.1020053)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A formal treatment of choice in which agents minimize the expected free energy of future outcomes, with the quality of a policy decomposed into extrinsic and epistemic value.

## Method

By the abstract, minimizing expected free energy is equivalent to maximizing extrinsic value (expected utility under prior preferences) together with epistemic value (information gain). Softmax parameters of earlier discrete-state formulations become the Bayes-optimal expected precision of beliefs about policies. The paper illustrates the theory with simulations.

## Key results

The abstract states that the scheme resolves the exploration-exploitation dilemma: epistemic value is maximized until no further information is gained, after which extrinsic value drives exploitation. It states consistency with the Infomax principle and links precision updates to dopaminergic discharges in conditioning paradigms.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for the epistemic term of the expected free energy of attention, where the believed value of a communication is the expected information of an experiment.
