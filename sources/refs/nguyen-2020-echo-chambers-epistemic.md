# Echo chambers and epistemic bubbles

> [!info] Citation
> Nguyen, C. T. (2020). *Echo chambers and epistemic bubbles*. Episteme, 17(2), 141-161. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2018.32)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The article distinguishes two social epistemic structures that discussions of post-truth and fake news tend to run together: epistemic bubbles, in which relevant voices are left out, and echo chambers, in which they are actively excluded and discredited.

## Problem & setting

Members of an epistemic bubble lack exposure to relevant information and arguments, possibly by accident. Members of an echo chamber have been led to distrust outside sources systematically. The abstract argues that the two need different interventions, since exposure to evidence can break a bubble but may reinforce an echo chamber, and that echo chambers are harder to escape and can pervert the effect of epistemically virtuous action.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript uses this distinction to give a formal reading: omission of voices corresponds to closure of attention, and discrediting corresponds to miscalibrated precision, in its section on misreading, miscalibration and double counting. The work is philosophical and the manuscript's record says only the abstract was read.
