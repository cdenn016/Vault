# Rounding of first-order phase transitions in systems with quenched disorder

> [!info] Citation
> Aizenman, M., Wehr, J. (1989). *Rounding of first-order phase transitions in systems with quenched disorder*. Physical Review Letters, 62(21), 2503-2506. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.62.2503)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The letter argues generally that in two dimensions quenched randomness eliminates discontinuities in the density of the variable conjugate to the fluctuating parameter, with analogous results for continuous symmetry breaking up to $d\le 4$.

## Key results

The abstract states rigorous results for random-field models: uniqueness of the Gibbs state for two-dimensional Ising systems and absence of continuous symmetry breaking in the Heisenberg model for $d\le 4$, as predicted by Imry and Ma. It adds that in two-dimensional random-bond Potts models a transition persists but ceases to be first order.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this letter and its longer companion for the statement that quenched randomness suppresses continuous symmetry breaking up to four dimensions and leaves the two-dimensional random-field Ising model with a unique Gibbs state. It uses this as the disorder background for its coherence analysis, where heterogeneous agent parameters play the role of a random field. The note rests on the abstract and record.
