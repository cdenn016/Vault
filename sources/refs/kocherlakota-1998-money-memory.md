# Money Is Memory

> [!info] Citation
> Kocherlakota, N. R. (1998). *Money Is Memory*. Journal of Economic Theory, 81(2), 232-251. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jeth.1997.2357)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

The paper studies feasible allocations in economic environments without commitment and shows that any allocation feasible with money is also feasible with memory, so that money is technologically equivalent to a primitive form of memory.

## Problem & setting

Following Myerson, feasibility is adapted to environments in which commitment is impossible. The environments have either memory, meaning an agent knows the full histories of all agents with whom he has had direct or indirect contact, or money, an object outside utility and production functions in fixed supply.

## Key results

The main proposition is that allocations feasible with money are feasible with memory. The abstract says the converse may or may not hold, depending on the environment.

## Relevance to this research
