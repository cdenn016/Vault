# Hierarchical Active Inference: A Theory of Motivated Control

> [!info] Citation
> Pezzulo, G., Rigoli, F., Friston, K. J. (2018). *Hierarchical Active Inference: A Theory of Motivated Control*. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 22(4), 294--306. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.01.009)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/pezzulo-2018-hierarchical-active-inference.pdf), 14 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

An opinion article proposing that control and motivation in the brain are complementary aspects, goal propagation and prioritization, of active inference under deep generative models.

## Problem & setting

Motivated control is the coordination of behavior toward affectively valenced goals. The article starts from the usual split between control processes (dorsolateral brain systems) and motivational processes (ventromedial systems) and notes that their respective roles and interactions remain controversial.

## Method

The authors cast the problem in hierarchical active inference: the control hierarchy propagates prior preferences or goals, and the precision of those goals is set by the motivational context, inferred at different levels of a motivational hierarchy. The extract reviewed covers the abstract, the opening discussion and the highlights; it is a conceptual proposal.

## Key results

The proposal is that integrating the two hierarchies underwrites action and policy selection by letting deep inference prioritize goals in a context-sensitive way. The pages read present this as a theoretical perspective, not as a tested result.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the article, known to it from records, as an extension of deep temporal active-inference architectures to planning and control, and reads such hierarchies as not double counting.
