# Optimal Sticky Prices under Rational Inattention

> [!info] Citation
> Maćkowiak, B., Wiederholt, M. (2009). *Optimal Sticky Prices under Rational Inattention*. American Economic Review, 99(3), 769-803. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.3.769)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/mackowiak-2009-optimal-sticky-prices.pdf), 87 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper models price-setting firms that decide what to attend to under a constraint on information flow, and studies how that attention shapes price responses to idiosyncratic and aggregate shocks.

## Problem & setting

Firms face a limit on the information they can process. The local file is the European Central Bank working-paper version of the same title (No. 1009, February 2009), and its extract shows only the cover pages, so the details below come from the abstract.

## Key results

The abstract states that when idiosyncratic conditions are more variable or more important than aggregate conditions, firms attend more to the idiosyncratic ones. When calibrated to match the large average absolute size of price changes in micro data, prices respond quickly and strongly to idiosyncratic shocks and slowly and weakly to nominal shocks, which then have strong and persistent real effects.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for the statement that price-setting firms attend more to firm-specific than to aggregate conditions when the former are more volatile or consequential, next to the original rational inattention model with a finite-capacity channel from observations to actions. It says the result is known from the abstract only, so the model and calibration were not examined. It illustrates a capacity-limited channel in an aggregate setting, see [[Rational inattention]], [[Channel Capacity]], [[Bounded Rationality]] and [[Communication-constrained inference]].
