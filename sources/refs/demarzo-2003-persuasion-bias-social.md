# Persuasion Bias, Social Influence, and Unidimensional Opinions

> [!info] Citation
> DeMarzo, P. M., Vayanos, D., Zwiebel, J. (2003). *Persuasion Bias, Social Influence, and Unidimensional Opinions*. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 118(3), 909-968. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/00335530360698469)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/demarzo-2003-persuasion-bias-social.pdf), 47 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and abstract.

## TL;DR

A boundedly rational model of opinion formation on a social network in which agents fail to adjust for repetition in the information they receive (persuasion bias).

## Problem & setting

Agents communicate repeatedly through a network and treat repeated information as new. The authors argue this explains phenomena such as propaganda, censorship, marketing and the importance of air-time.

## Method

The model is an opinion-updating process on a communication network with agents who do not correct for repetition. The settings explored include bilateral-communication neighborhoods, hierarchies and political science.

## Key results

Under general conditions beliefs converge to a common belief that is a weighted average of initial beliefs. The weights depend on network position and measure social influence, so influence depends on how well connected an agent is and not only on accuracy. Multidimensional beliefs converge to a single line before consensus, which gives unidimensional opinions.

## Relevance to this research

The Collective Agents manuscript cites the paper for persuasion bias as a failure to account for repetition, including the bias toward central members when consensus is reached on literal opinions, and as one of several social-learning accounts of the overvaluation case of double counting. It connects to [[Opinion dynamics]], [[Non-Bayesian social learning]], [[Social influence and conformity]] and [[Information rigidity]].
