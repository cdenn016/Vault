# Cultural Affordances: Scaffolding Local Worlds Through Shared Intentionality and Regimes of Attention

> [!info] Citation
> Ramstead, M. J. D., Veissière, S. P. L., Kirmayer, L. J. (2016). *Cultural Affordances: Scaffolding Local Worlds Through Shared Intentionality and Regimes of Attention*. Frontiers in Psychology, 7, 1090. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01090)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/ramstead-2016-cultural-affordances-scaffolding.pdf), 21 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A conceptual framework for how human agents engage with cultural affordances, extending the notion of affordance to sociocultural forms of life and giving a multilevel account of how affordances are learned and transmitted.

## Problem & setting

The aim is to understand how culture and context interact with human biology to shape behavior, cognition and experience. The authors integrate approaches to the embodied, cognitive and affective substrates of sociality and the sociocultural scaffolding of experience.

## Method

The paper is a theoretical synthesis bridging cognitive and social sciences. It proposes (i) an expanded concept of affordance and (ii) a multilevel account of socioculturally scaffolded affordance learning, with transmission in patterned practices and regimes of shared attention.

## Key results

The stated outcome is an account in which cultural content and normative practices rest on contentless basic mental processes that gain content through immersive participation in social practices regulating joint attention and shared intentionality. The pages read present this as a framework, not an empirical test.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for conventional affordances that rest on shared expectations and not on correlations in the environment, as one route by which cultural priors reach individuals.
