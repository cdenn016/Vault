# Social Value of Public Information

> [!info] Citation
> Morris, S., Shin, H. S. (2002). *Social Value of Public Information*. American Economic Review, 92(5), 1521-1534. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/000282802762024610)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/morris-2002-social-value-public.pdf), 13 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and abstract.

## TL;DR

A study of the welfare effect of public information when agents want their actions to match an underlying state and also to coordinate with one another through a strategic complementarity.

## Problem & setting

The question is how welfare responds to wider dissemination of public information through the media and public disclosures. Agents choose actions suited to the fundamentals and have a coordination motive.

## Key results

Per the abstract, when agents have no socially valuable private information, more public information always raises welfare. When agents also have independent information sources, the welfare effect of more public disclosure is ambiguous. The local file is the paper's web appendix, not the article: its first pages give a two-state example with a binary public signal and private signals of given accuracy, where a player's action mixes its expectation of the state with its expectation of the other player's action. The main results were not available from this file.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper, known from its abstract, for the effect of public signals under coordination motives, which it relates to the balance of precision between the shared and private components in its own results.
