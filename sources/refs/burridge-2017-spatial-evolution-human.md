# Spatial Evolution of Human Dialects

> [!info] Citation
> Burridge, J. (2017). *Spatial Evolution of Human Dialects*. Physical Review X, 7(3), 031008. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.7.031008); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00533)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/burridge-2017-spatial-evolution-human.pdf), 27 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A spatial model of language change in which dialect boundaries behave like interfaces with surface tension, reproducing several observations of dialectologists and yielding an analytical form for Séguy's curve.

## Problem & setting

The geographical pattern of dialects results from history, and the paper asks whether the final pattern is to some extent predictable. The introduction frames culture as formed by individuals having ideas that others copy, with copying historically requiring face-to-face contact, which localizes cultural forms.

## Method

The model is a simple spatial model of language change. According to the abstract, boundaries are governed by a length-minimizing effect analogous to surface tension, mediated by variations in population density that can induce curvature, and by the shape of coastlines or similar borders. A modification describes variation of language use by age in an area undergoing change.

## Key results

The abstract states that the model reproduces dialect continua, isogloss bundling and fanning, the wave-like spread of features from cities, and the effect of human movement on the number of dialects an area supports. It gives an analytical form for Séguy's curve relating geographical and linguistic distance, with a generalization for a population center. The reading record adds that the section on Séguy's curve was read in the arXiv version.

## Relevance to this research

The collective-agents manuscript cites this paper in three places: for the growth of dialect boundaries as the square root of time as an instance of curvature-driven coarsening, for a table row on conformity without leak or pulls, and in related work as a model of dialect boundaries with surface tension produced by a conformity nonlinearity. The manuscript contrasts this with its own derivation of conformity from expected free energy.
