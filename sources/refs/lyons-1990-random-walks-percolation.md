# Random Walks and Percolation on Trees

> [!info] Citation
> Lyons, R. (1990). *Random Walks and Percolation on Trees*. The Annals of Probability, 18(3), 931-958. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176990730)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

The paper defines an average number of branches per vertex, the branching number, for an arbitrary infinite locally finite tree, and shows it governs random walk and percolation on the tree, where it marks phase transitions.

## Problem & setting

For infinite locally finite trees the abstract states that the branching number equals the exponential of the Hausdorff dimension of the tree's boundary in a suitable metric.

## Key results

The abstract reports that the branching number gives the points of phase transition for random walk and percolation. The manuscript's reading record, taken from a review and a later restatement, gives the content: the branching number separates transience from recurrence for the walk that steps toward the root a given factor more often than along each outward edge, and the critical percolation probability is its reciprocal.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript uses the transience criterion for the biased walk to decide a tree-broadcasting condition for Gaussian signals with edge correlation, where the branching number decides the general case away from the critical value.
