## TL;DR

A book chapter reviewing what the free-energy principle implies for the self-organization of living systems across temporal and spatial scales, from cells to organisms and societies, with evolution cast as Bayesian model selection.

## Problem & setting

The chapter asks how biological systems keep their integrity as bounded systems, which requires limiting the long-run entropy of their states. It takes variational free energy minimization as the account of how this is done, with each organism embodying an implicit model of its niche and the niche shaped in turn by the organism's behavior.

## Method

The chapter is a review. From the first pages, it develops active inference and variational free energy as a bound on surprise and on negative model evidence, and it lists Markov blankets and niche construction among its themes.

## Key results

The chapter claims that free-energy minimization is equivalent to maximizing Bayesian model evidence and that natural selection can therefore be described as Bayesian model selection. These are the authors' framing in the abstract; only the first pages were read here.
