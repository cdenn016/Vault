# Language Evolution by Iterated Learning With Bayesian Agents

> [!info] Citation
> Griffiths, T. L., Kalish, M. L. (2007). *Language Evolution by Iterated Learning With Bayesian Agents*. Cognitive Science, 31(3), 441-480. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15326900701326576)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper analyzes iterated learning, in which each learner acquires a language from the previous one, for Bayesian learners that combine a prior with linguistic data. It shows that the long-run outcome depends on whether learners sample from the posterior or choose its maximum.

## Method

The abstract states that for learners who sample from the posterior, iterated learning is a form of Gibbs sampling, and for learners who choose the maximum posterior language it corresponds to a variant of the expectation-maximization algorithm.

## Key results

For posterior-sampling learners, the abstract states that iterated learning converges to a distribution determined entirely by the prior. For maximum-posterior learners the outcome depends on both the prior and the amount of information passed between generations.
