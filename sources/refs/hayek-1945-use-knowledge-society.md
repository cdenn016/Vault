# The Use of Knowledge in Society

> [!info] Citation
> Hayek, F. A. (1945). *The Use of Knowledge in Society*. American Economic Review, 35(4), 519-530.

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A 1945 article in the American Economic Review that the manuscript cites for the claim that prices communicate dispersed knowledge.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript reads the article as arguing that knowledge of particular circumstances is dispersed among individuals and held by no one in full, and that prices communicate the part of it that decisions need, so that users of a scarcer material need learn only that they must economize on it. Its reading record says the full text was read through a reprint and summarized, and that the volume, issue and pages were checked against secondary records and a scan of the masthead. The citation appears in the introduction, in the section on abstract collectives, and in the related-work discussion of rational-expectations aggregation. See [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]], [[Collective active inference]] and [[Common knowledge and Bayesian agreement]].
