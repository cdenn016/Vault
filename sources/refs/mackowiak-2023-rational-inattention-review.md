# Rational Inattention: A Review

> [!info] Citation
> Maćkowiak, B., Matějka, F., Wiederholt, M. (2023). *Rational Inattention: A Review*. Journal of Economic Literature, 61(1), 226-273. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20211524)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/mackowiak-2023-rational-inattention-review.pdf), 73 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and abstract.

## TL;DR

A review of the literature on rational inattention, the theory in which agents cannot process all available information but choose which pieces to attend to. It identifies the main theoretical mechanisms and surveys applications across fields of economics.

## Problem & setting

The review starts from the observation that information consumes the attention of its recipients, so that the information an agent acts on is determined largely by what the agent chooses to attend to. It presents rational inattention as a way to combine this limitation with the optimizing approach of standard economics.

## Method

The first pages give the organization: the theory itself, applications by field, a review of empirical evidence, and a discussion of policy implications, with suggestions for future research interspersed. The extract read here covers only the abstract and the opening motivation.

## Key results

As a review it reports no new theorem in the part read. Its stated conclusion in the abstract is that attention choices imply behavior with several empirically supported departures from standard models.

## Relevance to this research
