# Very particular: Comment on "How particular is the physics of the free energy principle?"

> [!info] Citation
> Friston, K. (2022). *Very particular: Comment on "How particular is the physics of the free energy principle?"*. Physics of Life Reviews, 41, 58-60. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2022.05.002)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/friston-2022-very-particular-comment.pdf), 4 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A short reply to a critique by Aguilera, Millidge, Tschantz and Buckley of the physics of the free energy principle. The author argues that the critique rests on two mistakes and that a canonical Jacobian decomposition with sparse coupling allows solenoidal coupling between some blocks of states.

## Problem & setting

The commentary answers two assertions attributed to the critique: that the principle relies on an equivalence between the dynamics of average states and the average of the dynamics, and that it relies on an absence of perception-action asymmetries. The author calls both assertions wrong.

## Method

The reply is an argument, not a derivation. It states that the principle describes the most likely flow of internal, not external, states as a gradient flow on variational free energy, so the critique's treatment of external states does not concern the principle. It then writes the Jacobian in terms of flow operators and Hessians that satisfy sparse coupling and conditional independence, and argues that solenoidal coupling between external and sensory states, and between active and internal states, is compatible with that form. It also contends that random dynamical systems are Markovian, and it distinguishes loss of detailed balance from entropy production.

## Key results

The note claims that the canonical form contains the linear cases considered in the critique and that the critique's conclusions about detailed balance do not follow. These are the author's assertions in a comment; no new theorem is proved in the pages read.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this comment as a reply that strengthens the definition of sparse coupling, together with a later paper by the same author, while it cites a different paper for the concession that sparse coupling does not imply a blanket in general. The manuscript knows these positions secondarily. The comment is one side of the dispute recorded in [[Markov blanket interpretation debate]]; see also [[Markov Blanket]] and [[Free-energy principle active inference]].
