# Least Squares Estimates in Stochastic Regression Models with Applications to Identification and Control of Dynamic Systems

> [!info] Citation
> Lai, T. L., Wei, C. Z. (1982). *Least Squares Estimates in Stochastic Regression Models with Applications to Identification and Control of Dynamic Systems*. The Annals of Statistics, 10(1), 154-166. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176345697)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper establishes strong consistency and asymptotic normality of least squares estimates when the regressors are stochastic, under weak assumptions on regressors and errors, and applies the results to interval estimation and to recursive identification and control of linear dynamic systems.

## Problem & setting

Least squares is studied where the regressors may themselves be random and depend on past data, as in the identification and control of linear dynamic systems.

## Key results

According to the abstract, consistency and asymptotic normality hold under weak conditions on the regressors and errors. The abstract names two uses: interval estimates for the regression parameters and recursive on-line identification and control schemes.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for one statement: least squares with dependent regressors is strongly consistent under eigenvalue conditions on the regressors. The manuscript's reading record shows the work was known from its abstract, so the eigenvalue conditions are attributed to it on that basis and were not checked in the primary text. The citation supports the estimation step in the framework for communicating agents that learn by regression; see [[Active inference communication and collective boundaries]] and [[Collective active inference]]. Asymptotic normality connects to [[Central Limit Theorem]].
