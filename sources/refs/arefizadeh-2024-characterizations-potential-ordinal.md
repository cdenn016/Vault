# On Characterizations of Potential and Ordinal Potential Games

> [!info] Citation
> Arefizadeh, S., Nedich, A., Dasarathy, G. (2024). *On Characterizations of Potential and Ordinal Potential Games*. arXiv preprint. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06253)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/arefizadeh-2024-characterizations-potential-ordinal.pdf), 18 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A preprint on necessary and sufficient conditions for a game to admit a potential function, extended from one-dimensional to multi-dimensional action spaces, with a construction of the potential from players' cost differences and sufficient conditions for ordinal and generalized ordinal potential games.

## Problem & setting

The introduction motivates the work by the role of potential games in guaranteeing Nash equilibria, and by the difficulty of checking in applications whether a given game is a potential game. Aggregative games, in which each player's cost depends on a function of all players' decisions, are named as a class for which such a test is wanted. The authors state that earlier integral tests require integration over the action space and that characterizations of ordinal potential games are scarce.

## Method

The first part extends earlier one-dimensional characterizations to multi-dimensional action spaces, under differentiable costs. The second part characterizes the existence of a potential function through differences of the players' costs rather than differentials, which gives a construction of the potential and does not require continuity. The abstract reports an application to network congestion games. The last part gives sufficient conditions for ordinal and generalized ordinal potentials.

## Key results

Within the first pages read, the results are stated as characterizations and sufficient conditions; the extract does not reach the proofs or the restatement of the four-cycle criterion in the body, which the manuscript's reading record locates in Section 4.2 and a remark.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this preprint as the place where it read the four-cycle criterion for exact potential games, whose original statement is a theorem of Monderer and Shapley that the manuscript did not read in its source. The criterion is used to decide when a game among communicating agents has a potential, which connects to the Gibbs form of stationary laws under logit revision and to [[Collective active inference]] and [[Multi-agent variational free energy]]. The manuscript treats the primary theorem as known only through this restatement. Related pages: [[Evolutionary game theory and cooperation]], [[Replicator dynamics]].
