## TL;DR

A 1999 article in the Journal of International Economics comparing networks and markets in international trade, as the title and venue establish. The manuscript's record says the abstract was read, but its content is not part of the vault's evidence.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for the finding that proximity, common language and colonial ties matter more for differentiated products than for goods traded on organized exchanges. It states that its own account, in which prices economize on communication when decision-relevant functionals are shared, agrees in sign with this finding and does not derive it. The connection is to the vault's work on communication costs and abstract collectives; see [[Communication-constrained inference]] and [[Network structure — small-world and scale-free]].
