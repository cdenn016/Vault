# Multiscale integration: beyond internalism and externalism

> [!info] Citation
> Ramstead, M. J. D., Kirchhoff, M. D., Constant, A., Friston, K. J. (2021). *Multiscale integration: beyond internalism and externalism*. Synthese, 198(Suppl. 1), 41--70. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02115-x)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/ramstead-2021-multiscale-integration-beyond.pdf), 30 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A philosophy-of-cognition paper that uses the Markov blanket formalism of the free energy principle to argue that cognitive boundaries are nested and multiple, a compromise between internalist and externalist readings.

## Problem & setting

The debate concerns where the boundaries of cognitive systems lie, in the context of enactive, embodied and extended views of cognition. The introduction states the central idea that these boundaries are nested and multiple.

## Method

The authors survey radical views of cognition, describe an internalist reading premised on the Markov blanket formalism, and then develop a multiscale account built on a multiscale ontology of cognitive systems.

## Key results

The argument is that the statistical seclusion of internal from external states, entailed by a Markov boundary, can coexist with multiscale integration of the system through its dynamics. No boundary is privileged, and the relevant boundaries depend on the level described and the explanatory interests. These are philosophical arguments, not derived theorems.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript lists this paper, with another multiscale paper and an appendix of a software-and-mind paper, among works on inferential closure of coarse-grained collectives that it has not read in primary text. It is relevant to the question of which Markov blankets define a collective at which scale; see [[Markov Blanket]], [[Markov blanket interpretation debate]], [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Coarse Graining]].
