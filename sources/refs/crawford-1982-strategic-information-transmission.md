# Strategic Information Transmission

> [!info] Citation
> Crawford, V. P., Sobel, J. (1982). *Strategic Information Transmission*. Econometrica, 50(6), 1431-1451. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913390)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper models strategic communication between a better-informed sender, who sends a possibly noisy signal, and a receiver, who then acts in a way that determines the welfare of both.

## Problem & setting

A sender has private information about a scalar variable and the receiver chooses an action on the basis of the signal. The two parties' preferences need not coincide.

## Method

The authors characterize the Bayesian Nash equilibria under standard assumptions.

## Key results

According to the abstract, equilibrium signaling always has a simple form in which the sender partitions the support of the private variable and reports only which cell the observation lies in. Under further assumptions, the equilibrium with the most partition cells is Pareto-superior to the others, and if agents coordinate on it, the receiver's expected utility rises as preferences become more similar. Equilibrium signaling is therefore more informative when preferences are closer.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper to mark the boundary of its scope: strategic content, where stakes diverge and communication is coarsened, is outside what it treats, and it is listed as partly open in its table of minimal models. The coarsening of messages at divergent preferences is the economic counterpart of the information loss studied in [[Communication-constrained inference]] and [[Active inference communication and collective boundaries]]; see also [[Common knowledge and Bayesian agreement]].
