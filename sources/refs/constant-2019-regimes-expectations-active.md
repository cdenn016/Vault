# Regimes of Expectations: An Active Inference Model of Social Conformity and Human Decision Making

> [!info] Citation
> Constant, A., Ramstead, M. J. D., Veissière, S. P. L., Friston, K. (2019). *Regimes of Expectations: An Active Inference Model of Social Conformity and Human Decision Making*. Frontiers in Psychology, 10, 679. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00679)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/constant-2019-regimes-expectations-active.pdf), 15 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

The paper introduces "deontic value", a construct built on active inference and Markov decision processes, to model how people acquire shared expectations about how to behave in normalized social settings, and so to formalize social conformity.

## Problem & setting

The question is how humans come to hold shared expectations about proper behavior in distinct social settings. The paper frames this through the theory of regimes of expectations, sets of expectations characteristic of a cultural group, and places conformity within social psychology (deference to norms) and cultural evolution (social learning strategies).

## Method

The authors define deontic value as an attribute of choices or action sequences inherited from deontic cues in the environment, such as red traffic lights, which denote an obligatory rule. They describe a circular causality in which cues exist because repeated actions modified the environment, and actions depend on the value of the cues.

## Key results

The paper argues, as a conceptual and normative construction, that this lets epistemic and pragmatic value be cached in the environment, which in effect learns about the behavior of its inhabitants, and describes how this supports learning of habits over neurodevelopmental and transgenerational timescales. The first pages read show no empirical test.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper on two points. Its deontic cues are treated as records in the manuscript's record model, one mode of a transfer kernel that also covers direct and performative channels, and its concentration counts are read as records written by actions and erased by decay. The manuscript also reports finding no treatment, in this work or in related work on cultural priors, of whether a top-down prior built from the receivers' own contributions counts them twice.
