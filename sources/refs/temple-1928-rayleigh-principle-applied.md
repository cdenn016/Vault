# The Theory of Rayleigh's Principle as Applied to Continuous Systems

> [!info] Citation
> Temple, G. (1928). *The Theory of Rayleigh's Principle as Applied to Continuous Systems*. Proceedings of the Royal Society of London. Series A, 119(782), 276-293. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1928.0098)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

Temple examines and extends Rayleigh's method of approximating the frequency of the gravest vibration mode of a continuous system from a trial function, and devises an upper bound on the error of the approximation.

## Problem & setting

Rayleigh's method equates mean kinetic and potential energies for a trial function that satisfies the boundary conditions. It yields a frequency at or above the true fundamental value. The abstract says this had not been investigated for continuous systems, as opposed to systems with finitely many degrees of freedom.

## Method

The paper studies a series of successive approximations to the exact solutions and constructs an error bound for the approximate frequency. It extends the method to the first overtone and to analogous computations of characteristic numbers.

## Key results

According to the abstract: the Rayleigh frequency exceeds the natural frequency, an upper bound on the approximation error is obtained, and the method extends to the first overtone.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for the attribution of the Kato-Temple inequality, which it proves in full in an appendix on collectives; the inequality is used there with the graph Laplacian of a population. The manuscript knew the paper only from the bibliographic record. See [[Graph Laplacian]] and [[Spectral graph theory]].
