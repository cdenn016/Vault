# Mathematical Models to Explain the Origin of Urban Scaling Laws

> [!info] Citation
> Ribeiro, F. L., Rybski, D. (2023). *Mathematical Models to Explain the Origin of Urban Scaling Laws*. Physics Reports, 1012, 1-39. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2023.02.002)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/ribeiro-2023-mathematical-models-explain.pdf), 22 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A synthetic review of the mathematical models proposed to explain urban scaling, the power-law growth of urban quantities with city population. The local file is the arXiv version, whose title ends "a synthetic review".

## Problem & setting

Urban scaling posits that a quantity $Y$ grows with population $N$ as $Y = Y_0 N^\beta$. The introduction reports three empirical regimes: socio-economic quantities scale superlinearly, with an exponent near 1.15; individual-service quantities such as housing scale linearly; infrastructure quantities scale sublinearly.

## Method

The review sets out models built on different premises, namely gravity ideas, densification and city geometry, and hierarchical organization with socio-network properties. It looks for similarities and connections among them and for cases where different models give the same output. For the gravity models it proposes a general framework that contains each analyzed model as a special case.

## Key results

The first pages state the aims and the framework for gravity models; the results of the individual model comparisons were not read for this note.
