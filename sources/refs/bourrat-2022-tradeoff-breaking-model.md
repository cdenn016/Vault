# Tradeoff breaking as a model of evolutionary transitions in individuality and limits of the fitness-decoupling metaphor

> [!info] Citation
> Bourrat, P., Doulcier, G., Rose, C. J., Rainey, P. B., Hammerschmidt, K. (2022). *Tradeoff breaking as a model of evolutionary transitions in individuality and limits of the fitness-decoupling metaphor*. eLife, 11, e73715. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73715)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/bourrat-2022-tradeoff-breaking-model.pdf), 39 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A mathematical and conceptual analysis arguing that particle and collective fitness cannot decouple in evolutionary transitions in individuality, and proposing trait tradeoffs and their breaking as the description of such a transition.

## Problem & setting

Evolutionary transitions in individuality form Darwinian collectives from Darwinian particles, with cells to multicellular life as the standard example. One observation used to identify a completed transition is an increase in collective-level performance with a decrease in particle-level performance, called fitness decoupling, which has been used to interpret both models and experimental data.

## Method

The authors use a mathematical argument comparing fitness calculated for particles and for collectives over equivalent time intervals, and then draw on experimental and theoretical studies to develop a trait-based account built on tradeoffs among traits.

## Key results

The abstract states that fitness of particles and collectives cannot decouple when calculated over appropriate, equivalent intervals, provided the population reaches a stable collective-size distribution. It states that early stages of a transition may involve tradeoffs among particle traits, whereas later stages are likely to involve rupture of such tradeoffs, so that tradeoff-breaking events can serve as a marker of a transition.
