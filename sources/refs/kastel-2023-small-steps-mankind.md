# Small steps for mankind: Modeling the emergence of cumulative culture from joint active inference communication

> [!info] Citation
> Kastel, N., Hesp, C., Ridderinkhof, K. R., Friston, K. J. (2023). *Small steps for mankind: Modeling the emergence of cumulative culture from joint active inference communication*. Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 16, 944986. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2022.944986)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kastel-2023-small-steps-mankind.pdf), 19 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

The paper gives a deep active inference model of cultural transmission as bidirectional communication that induces generalized synchrony between interlocutors' beliefs, with agents also choosing whom to talk to, and simulates cumulative culture.

## Problem & setting

Dynamical models of cultural evolution have become more mathematically sophisticated but have not been tested or validated; the paper aims at a testable formulation of social behavior.

## Method

Cultural transmission is cast as communication that drives belief states toward a particular convergence, and social exchange as active inference in which agents select communication partners. This creates a trade-off between confirming current beliefs and exploring the social environment.

## Key results

In simulations, cumulative culture emerges from joint minimization of uncertainty. The cultural equilibria segregate into groups whose belief systems are sustained by selective, uncertainty-minimizing dyadic exchanges, and the nature of the equilibria depends sensitively on the precision of the probabilistic mappings in each agent's generative model.
