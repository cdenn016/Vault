# Strictly Linear Light Cones in Long-Range Interacting Systems of Arbitrary Dimensions

> [!info] Citation
> Kuwahara, T., Saito, K. (2020). *Strictly Linear Light Cones in Long-Range Interacting Systems of Arbitrary Dimensions*. Physical Review X, 10(3), 031010. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.031010)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kuwahara-2020-strictly-linear-light.pdf), 64 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

For quantum systems in $D$ spatial dimensions with interactions decaying as $R^{-\alpha}$, the paper proves a linear light cone for $\alpha>2D+1$ and gives a state-transfer protocol that violates it for $\alpha<2D+1$.

## Key results

The abstract states a Lieb-Robinson bound for operators separated by distance $R$, with a velocity of order one, and a protocol achieving the bound up to a constant coefficient. In that regime the authors describe the result as the best general constraint on information spreading.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its scale section. It compares the threshold of its linear message-passing cone with the free-particle light cone of long-range quantum systems and says the many-body thresholds of that hierarchy have no analog in its setting, because linear message passing propagates a single tangent vector.
