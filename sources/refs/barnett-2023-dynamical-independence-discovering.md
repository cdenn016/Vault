# Dynamical independence: Discovering emergent macroscopic processes in complex dynamical systems

> [!info] Citation
> Barnett, L., Seth, A. K. (2023). *Dynamical independence: Discovering emergent macroscopic processes in complex dynamical systems*. Physical Review E, 108(1), 014304. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.108.014304)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/barnett-2023-dynamical-independence-discovering.pdf), 40 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages. The local file is the arXiv version (2106.06511v2, August 2021), not the journal version.

## TL;DR

The paper defines emergence of a coarse-grained macroscopic variable as dynamical independence: the macro process follows dynamical laws of its own, quantified by a transformation-invariant information-theoretic measure of dynamical dependence.

## Problem & setting

The introduction poses two questions: which perspectives on a microscopic system reveal emergent dynamical structure, and how to find them from the microscopic dynamics. It takes starling murmurations, cellular-automaton gliders and neural population activity as motivating cases, and builds on Bedau's weak emergence, so that the macro process is reducible to its micro causes and downward causation is excluded.

## Method

Dynamical independence is defined for a macroscopic process in the context of a coupled dynamical environment. Dynamical dependence is computed explicitly for linear systems through state-space modeling, in time and frequency domains, and the paper proposes a multiscale emergence portrait for a system. It also treats discrete- and continuous-time deterministic dynamics.

## Key results

The abstract reports explicit linear-systems computation, a data-driven route to dynamically independent macro variables, and a discussion of neural applications. Deterministic dynamics, including Hamiltonian systems, flocking and cellular automata, are examined as potential applications.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper, whose definition it read in the primary text, in its account of meta-agents and informational closure. The quantity in the manuscript's meta-agent analysis is a conditional transfer entropy whose vanishing is what the paper calls dynamical independence, with the blanket path as the environment. For stationary linear-Gaussian collectives the manuscript reports that the notion coincides with one-step informational closure of Rosas and coauthors.
