## TL;DR

A 1936 Biometrika article of 57 pages on relations between two sets of variates, cited for the name and origin of canonical correlations.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites it where it writes the singular values of a whitened cross-covariance between two sets of variables and notes that the resulting canonical variates are uncorrelated with unit variance, in its discussion of environments and sensing. The paper was not read; the manuscript used the record only, for attribution. See [[Canonical Correlation Analysis]], [[Gaussian Beliefs]] and [[Mutual information]].
