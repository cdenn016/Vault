# Probabilistic Attention for Interactive Segmentation

> [!info] Citation
> Prasad Gabbur, Manjot Bilkhu, Javier R. Movellan (2021). "Probabilistic Attention for Interactive Segmentation." *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* **34** (NeurIPS 2021). Preprint: [arXiv:2106.15338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15338) (v2 read).

## TL;DR

The conference version of the probabilistic reading of attention in [[movellan-2020-probabilistic-transformers]]. Queries and values are generated by a mixture model whose components are indexed by keys, and dot-product attention is a special case of MAP inference in that model. The same model supports EM adaptation of keys and propagation of values at inference time, which the paper applies to interactive semantic segmentation.

## What it establishes

The abstract states that "standard dot-product attention in transformers is a special case of Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference." Section 3.1 defines the mixture over query–value pairs, Section 3.2 derives value inference by EM, and Section 3.3 shows that a constrained isotropic Gaussian mixture, with queries and values conditionally independent given the component, reproduces the standard attention update. Sections 3.5 and 3.6 use the generative model for online unsupervised adaptation of the keys and for propagating values that annotators fix at inference time, and Section 3.8 places position embeddings in the component priors. In the experiments (Section 4), key adaptation raises performance by about 10% mIoU in the low-feedback regime and value propagation improves responsiveness in the high-feedback regime. The authors note that a doubly normalized attention scheme from earlier work emerges as a special case of their key adaptation.

## Relevance to this research

The September 2026 v2 revision of the GL(K) attention manuscript cites this paper alongside [[movellan-2020-probabilistic-transformers]] for the statement that dot-product attention is a special case of MAP inference in a Gaussian mixture. It is a point-query precedent for the manuscript's reading of routing as source-label inference, which the manuscript carries out with Gaussian query and key states.
