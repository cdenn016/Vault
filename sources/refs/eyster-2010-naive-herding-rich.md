# Naïve Herding in Rich-Information Settings

> [!info] Citation
> Eyster, E., Rabin, M. (2010). *Naïve Herding in Rich-Information Settings*. American Economic Journal: Microeconomics 2(4), 221-243. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/mic.2.4.221)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A social-learning model in which people naively believe that each earlier action reflects only that person's private information, so that early signals are overweighted.

## Problem & setting

In sequential social learning, later actors observe earlier actions. The authors assume that people ignore that interim actions also embed earlier signals.

## Key results

The abstract states that this social confirmation bias leads naive herders to herd with positive probability on incorrect actions even in settings where rational players never do. Because they become fully confident even when wrong, they can be harmed on average by observing others.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its treatment of misreading, for agents who neglect that others' actions embed earlier signals and so become confident when wrong. It is listed among social-learning accounts of the overvaluation case, with persuasion bias and correlation neglect.
