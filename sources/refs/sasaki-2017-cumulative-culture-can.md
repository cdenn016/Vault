# Cumulative culture can emerge from collective intelligence in animal groups

> [!info] Citation
> Sasaki, T., Biro, D. (2017). *Cumulative culture can emerge from collective intelligence in animal groups*. Nature Communications, 8(1), 15049. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15049)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/sasaki-2017-cumulative-culture-can.pdf), 6 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

An experiment with homing pigeons shows that groups whose members are repeatedly replaced improve their homing performance over generations, which the authors read as cumulative culture arising from collective intelligence.

## Problem & setting

Studies of collective intelligence in animal groups usually examine one-off performance and assume decisions are not shaped by past experience, whereas cumulative cultural evolution concerns knowledge that ratchets across generations. The paper asks whether the two interact.

## Method

Experimental groups of homing pigeons undergo repeated removal and replacement of individuals, the standard method for testing cumulative cultural evolution, and their solution efficiency is tracked over successive generations.

## Key results

Homing performance improves continuously across generations, and later-generation groups outperform both solo individuals and fixed-membership groups. Routes are more similar in consecutive generations within a chain than between chains, which the authors take as evidence of cross-generational knowledge transfer. They conclude that the results meet the main criteria for cumulative culture without requiring complex cognition.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript lists this study among open problems, as an empirical report of improved collective navigation when group members are replaced successively, next to models of the reciprocal development of social priors and environment structure.
