# On Maximal Correlation, Hypercontractivity, and the Data Processing Inequality studied by Erkip and Cover

> [!info] Citation
> Anantharam, V., Gohari, A., Kamath, S., Nair, C. (2013). *On Maximal Correlation, Hypercontractivity, and the Data Processing Inequality studied by Erkip and Cover*. Preprint. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6133)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/anantharam-2013-maximal-correlation-hypercontractivity.pdf), 11 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages and abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper gives a geometric characterization of the Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi maximal correlation of a pair of random variables and of the chordal slope at infinity of the boundary of their hypercontractivity ribbon. It also gives a counterexample to a data processing inequality claimed by Erkip and Cover and finds the correct tight constant.

## Problem & setting

The introduction defines maximal correlation $\rho_m(X;Y)$ as the maximum of $E[f(X)g(Y)]$ over zero-mean, unit-variance functions of each variable, and restricts attention to discrete variables with finite alphabets and positive marginals. It recalls the connection to hypercontractivity of Markov operators studied by Ahlswede and Gács.

## Key results

The new characterizations are said to give simple proofs of some known facts about these quantities. The counterexample shows that a data processing inequality claimed in earlier work fails, and the corrected constant is stated as tight.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for the remark that, for non-Gaussian pairs, the mutual-information contraction constant can exceed the squared maximal correlation, whereas for a Gaussian source and additive Gaussian channel the coefficient is known in substance. The reading record says the manuscript read the abstract; I read the first pages and did not check the counterexample.
