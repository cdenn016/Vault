# When science mirrors life: on the origins of the Price equation

> [!info] Citation
> Harman, O. (2020). *When science mirrors life: on the origins of the Price equation*. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences, 375(1797), 20190352. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0352)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

A historical article on the origins of the Price equation, arguing that George Price's turn to mathematical population genetics was connected to his personal life.

## Problem & setting

The abstract poses the question of how an abstract piece of mathematics could relate to the biography of its author. It argues that the impetus for Price's work came from a preoccupation with the origins of family after a divorce. It describes the equation as an association between two populations, a mother and a daughter population, that need not mean descent in the narrow genetic sense and that lets selection be seen at several levels at once. The article belongs to a theme issue on fifty years of the Price equation.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the article for the history of the Price decomposition, which it uses for collectives. The reading record says the open copy was consulted through an automated reading of the page: the article states the equation in a covariance form and in a form with an added expectation term, describes the full equation as a partition of trait change into selection and transmission, and attributes it to Price's 1970 paper. The equations' fraction bars were lost in that reading, so no formula was copied. The link is to the manuscript's section on evolution and selection of collectives; see [[Replicator dynamics]], [[Cultural evolution and social learning]], [[Cultural group selection]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
