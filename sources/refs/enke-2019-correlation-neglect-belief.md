# Correlation Neglect in Belief Formation

> [!info] Citation
> Enke, B., Zimmermann, F. (2019). *Correlation Neglect in Belief Formation*. The Review of Economic Studies 86(1), 313-332. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdx081)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/enke-2019-correlation-neglect-belief.pdf), 37 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. The local file is the April 2013 IZA discussion paper version (No. 7372), not the journal version. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

An experimental paper showing that many people treat correlated signals as if they were independent when updating beliefs, which makes beliefs overly sensitive to well-connected information sources.

## Problem & setting

The introduction notes that information structures often generate correlated signals, with news media sharing common sources and opinions in social networks resting on a shared third party. The question is whether decision-makers account for such correlation when they update.

## Key results

The abstract reports, from experiments, that correlation neglect produces overshooting beliefs. In an experimental asset market it is reported to drive individual overoptimism and overpessimism and to give rise to predictable price bubbles and crashes. The authors state that the results support models of boundedly rational social learning.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its discussion of misreading, as an experimental finding that many people neglect correlation between signals, next to naive herding and persuasion bias. It is listed among social-learning accounts of the overvaluation case, where agents count a shared source more than once.
