# Morphogenesis as Bayesian inference: A variational approach to pattern formation and control in complex biological systems

> [!info] Citation
> Kuchling, F., Friston, K., Georgiev, G., Levin, M. (2020). *Morphogenesis as Bayesian inference: A variational approach to pattern formation and control in complex biological systems*. Physics of Life Reviews, 33, 88-108. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2019.06.001)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kuchling-2020-morphogenesis-bayesian-inference.pdf), 28 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

The paper applies the variational free energy principle to morphogenesis, treating cells as information-processing agents whose pattern formation is driven by maximizing model evidence, and uses simulations to reproduce experimental manipulations of development and regeneration.

## Problem & setting

The authors say molecular biology lacks an overarching concept that predicts the emergence of form and the maintenance of complex anatomy, and that classical least-action approaches are hard to use for open systems far from equilibrium.

## Method

The paper derives the mathematics of Bayesian inference as understood in this framework and simulates it, including altered anterior-posterior polarity as in planarian regeneration and aberrant single-cell behavior within an ensemble, described as false beliefs about what a cell should sense and do.

## Key results

The authors report that simple modifications of the inference process can cause and rescue mis-patterning without changing the cell's implicit generative model.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its introduction as an active-inference model of collectives in biology, within the description of a collective as a blanket of blankets, next to collective behavior derived from surprise minimization.
