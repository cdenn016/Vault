# Growth, Innovation, Scaling, and the Pace of Life in Cities

> [!info] Citation
> Bettencourt, L. M. A., Lobo, J., Helbing, D., Kühnert, C., West, G. B. (2007). *Growth, Innovation, Scaling, and the Pace of Life in Cities*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 104(17), 7301-7306. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0610172104)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

An empirical and theoretical study of urban systems showing that many city quantities are power laws in population size, with exponents that fall into distinct classes, and deriving growth equations from these scaling relations.

## Problem & setting

The abstract motivates a predictive, quantitative theory of urban organization and sustainable development. The setting is the set of cities within one urban system, across nations and times.

## Method

The authors fit power laws of population size to quantities ranging from patent production and personal income to electrical cable length, compare the pace of social life with population size, and derive growth equations that separate growth driven by innovation from growth driven by economies of scale.

## Key results

According to the abstract, quantities tied to wealth creation and innovation have exponents near 1.2 (increasing returns), whereas infrastructure quantities have exponents near 0.8 (economies of scale). The authors state that sustained growth then requires innovation cycles to arrive at an accelerating rate to avoid stagnation or collapse. They also compare cities with biological organisms, for which the exponent is below one.
