# Language and the Theory of the Firm

> [!info] Citation
> Crémer, J., Garicano, L., Prat, A. (2007). *Language and the Theory of the Firm*. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 122(1), 373-407. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.122.1.373)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

An economic theory of efficient technical languages and how they interact with the scope and structure of organizations. Efficient languages use precise words for frequent events and vague words for unusual ones.

## Problem & setting

The paper asks which technical language an organization should use and how that choice relates to how broad the organization is. A broader scope captures more synergies but lowers efficiency within each unit, because the shared language must be more generic.

## Method

The abstract describes a characterization of efficient languages inside a model of organizations. It also considers a manager acting as a specialized translator, which allows coordination between units that keep separate languages.

## Key results

According to the abstract, the theory reconciles two documented organizational trends: more centralized information and less centralized hierarchy. The manuscript's reading record states that the content was taken from the abstract level only.
