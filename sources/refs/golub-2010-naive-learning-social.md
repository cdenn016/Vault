# Naïve Learning in Social Networks and the Wisdom of Crowds

> [!info] Citation
> Golub, B., Jackson, M. O. (2010). *Naïve Learning in Social Networks and the Wisdom of Crowds*. American Economic Journal: Microeconomics, 2(1), 112-149. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/mic.2.1.112)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper studies agents who receive independent noisy signals about a variable and then communicate in a network, updating by repeatedly averaging their neighbors' opinions. It gives conditions under which a large society converges to the truth.

## Problem & setting

Agents update naively, taking weighted averages of neighbors' opinions. The abstract states that opinions in a large society converge to the truth if and only if the influence of the most influential agent vanishes as the society grows.

## Key results

The abstract also reports obstructions to this convergence, including prominent groups, structural conditions on the network that ensure efficient learning, and the finding that convergence to the truth is unrelated to how quickly consensus is approached.
