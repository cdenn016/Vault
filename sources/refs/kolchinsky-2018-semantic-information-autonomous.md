# Semantic information, autonomous agency and non-equilibrium statistical physics

> [!info] Citation
> Kolchinsky, A., Wolpert, D. H. (2018). *Semantic information, autonomous agency and non-equilibrium statistical physics*. Interface Focus, 8(6), 20180041. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2018.0041), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kolchinsky-2018-semantic-information-autonomous.pdf), 19 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract and manuscript reading record.

## TL;DR

The paper defines semantic information as the syntactic information a physical system has about its environment that is causally necessary for the system to maintain its own existence, and analyzes it with non-equilibrium statistical physics.

## Problem & setting

The authors contrast Shannon measures of statistical correlation with semantic information, the correlations that carry significance for a given system, and seek a formal theory applicable to any physical system, living or otherwise.

## Method

Causal necessity is defined through counterfactual interventions that scramble system-environment correlations, and maintaining existence through the system's ability to stay in a low-entropy state. The framework is grounded in the system's intrinsic dynamics and leads to formal definitions of value of information, semantic content and agency.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for that definition only, and its record covers the abstract level. In the ordinary-resource section it follows the idea that information about the environment has value through its causal contribution to the system's persistence in a low-entropy state, and states that the linear form and exchange ratio are its own. In the open-problems section it notes that the paper's notion is relative to a chosen viability function, set of interventions, system-environment split and time horizon.
