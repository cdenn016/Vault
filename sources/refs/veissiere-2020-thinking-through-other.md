# Thinking through other minds: A variational approach to cognition and culture

> [!info] Citation
> Veissière, S. P. L., Constant, A., Ramstead, M. J. D., Friston, K. J., Kirmayer, L. J. (2020). *Thinking through other minds: A variational approach to cognition and culture*. Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 43, e90. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X19001213)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/veissiere-2020-thinking-through-other.pdf), 97 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages. The extract covers the abstracts and the first part of the introduction, up to the theory-of-mind debates.

## TL;DR

A target article that integrates shared expectations, patterned attention, cultural evolution and implicit learning in the variational free-energy framework, and proposes that humans acquire culture by inferring other agents' expectations, a process the authors call thinking through other minds.

## Problem & setting

The article takes up a puzzle of implicit cultural learning: norms, habits and expectations are learned and kept stable across large populations, mostly without explicit instruction, and the authors state that a formal account of the mechanisms is lacking. It situates the proposal within debates on theory of mind, between theory-theory, simulation and embodied accounts.

## Method

The authors describe the construction of social niches that afford cultural affordances, and argue that agents learn shared expectations through immersive participation in patterned practices that pattern attention and behavior. The extract does not reach the formal development.

## Key results

The abstract claims that information about other people's expectations is the main domain of statistical regularities humans use to predict and organize behavior, and that the variational treatment bears on the internalist and externalist divide in theory of mind and on the dynamical and representational divide in enactivism. These are the authors' arguments, not derived theorems.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this article as work on cultural priors: priors reach individuals through inference of others' expectations in shared practices. It reads the attention-patterning practices as learning of partner and population models from co-occurrence, and it reports finding no treatment, in this work or in related work on cultural priors, of whether a top-down prior built from receivers' own contributions double counts them. See [[Free-energy principle active inference]], [[Niche Construction]], [[Theory of Mind]] and [[Cultural evolution and social learning]].
