# On the Number and Size of Nations

> [!info] Citation
> Alesina, A., Spolaore, E. (1997). *On the Number and Size of Nations*. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 112(4), 1027-1056. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355300555411)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper models the equilibrium number of countries under different political regimes and degrees of economic integration, as a trade-off between the benefits of large jurisdictions and the costs of heterogeneity in large populations.

## Key results

The abstract lists three implications of the model: democratization leads to secessions, the equilibrium number of countries is generally inefficiently large, and the number of countries rises with economic integration.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for the trade-off between the gains of size and the costs of heterogeneity in determining how many polities exist, and compares it with its own criterion, in which a polity's fixed cost is set against a heterogeneity weight times the correlation volume of preferences. It serves as a reference point for treating nations as abstract collectives. See [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]] and [[Collective active inference]].
