# Optimal payoff functions for members of collectives

> [!info] Citation
> Wolpert, D. H., Tumer, K. (2001). *Optimal payoff functions for members of collectives*. Advances in Complex Systems, 4(2-3), 265-279. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219525901000188)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

A treatment of how to set the payoff utilities of the individual processes in a distributed collective so that self-interested optimization yields high value of a given world utility, within the Collective Intelligence framework.

## Problem & setting

Each process is cast as maximizing its own payoff. The central design question is how to initialize and update those payoffs so the collective achieves a good world utility, as opposed to the team-game choice of giving each process the world utility.

## Method

Under a new mathematical framework the paper reviews the derivation of a class of payoff utilities that are easy for individual processes to maximize and that guarantee high world utility when they are high. These include the Aristocrat Utility and a new variant of the Wonderful Life Utility.

## Key results

The abstract reports experimental demonstrations that these utilities improve on earlier Collective Intelligence payoffs and on the team game, and on endogenizing externalities. The abstract gives no quantitative detail.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript contrasts this work with its own agency result: where Wolpert and Tumer design members' payoffs to serve a world utility, the manuscript finds, under its protocol with shared belief, an exact potential game. The manuscript read the abstract. See [[Collective active inference]], [[Multi-agent variational free energy]] and [[Evolutionary game theory and cooperation]].
