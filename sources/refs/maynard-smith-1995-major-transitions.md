# The Major Transitions in Evolution

> [!info] Citation
> J. Maynard Smith, E. Szathmáry (1995). *The Major Transitions in Evolution.* W.H. Freeman /
> Oxford University Press, Oxford.

## TL;DR

The synthesis of evolution's level-forming events — replicating molecules to chromosomes, cells
to eukaryotes, organisms to colonies and societies — identifying their common structure: smaller
entities that could replicate independently combine into a larger entity, differentiate within
it, and lose independent replication. Higher-level units always form from previously stabilized
lower-level units, never de novo.

## Relevance to this research

The evolutionary-biology form of staged hierarchy assembly, complementing
[[simon-1962-architecture-complexity]]: every observed level of biological organization is a
meta-agent built from stabilized sub-agents, with the loss of independent replication as the
biological analog of the program's parent formation absorbing constituents' degrees of freedom.
The staged-versus-direct free-energy question the laboratory can now pose
([[Staged hierarchy formation and RG composability]]) is the model's version of why transitions
happen stepwise. See [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
