# Strange products of projections

> [!info] Citation
> Kopecká, E., Paszkiewicz, A. (2017). *Strange products of projections*. Israel Journal of Mathematics, 219(1), 271-286. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11856-017-1480-4), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05029)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/kopecka-2017-strange-products-projections.pdf), 15 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

In every infinite-dimensional Hilbert space there exist three orthogonal projections onto closed subspaces such that, from any nonzero starting point, some sequence of applications of the three projections fails to converge in norm.

## Problem & setting

For projections onto $K$ closed subspaces applied in an arbitrary order, iterates converge weakly, and for $K=2$ they converge in norm. The introduction reviews norm convergence in finite dimension and for periodic or quasiperiodic orderings, and the question for $K\ge3$ in infinite dimension with arbitrary orderings, which had stayed open for a long time.

## Method

The paper simplifies an earlier construction for three subspaces and strengthens it from one badly behaved starting point to every nonzero point. It also treats a variant with five projections in which only two index sequences are needed.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper in its scale section to state that in infinite dimension norm convergence can fail for three subspaces, alongside Badea et al. (2012) on when cyclic convergence is geometric. The record notes the reading was of the abstract. See [[Coarse Graining]], [[Fixed-point iteration]] and [[Meta-agents and hierarchical emergence]].
