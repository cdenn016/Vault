# On a Measure of the Information Provided by an Experiment

> [!info] Citation
> Lindley, D. V. (1956). *On a Measure of the Information Provided by an Experiment*. The Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 27(4), 986-1005. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177728069)

No local copy: no open-access file passed validation on November 10, 2026. Reading basis: abstract.

## TL;DR

The paper introduces a measure of the information an experiment provides, derived from Shannon's work and defined relative to a prior distribution over the parameter space, and applies it to compare experiments and to design them.

## Problem & setting

The measure depends on the knowledge held before the experiment, expressed as a prior over parameters. The abstract says it is used to compare some pairs of experiments without reference to priors, a method contrasted with the one discussed by Blackwell, and to solve experimental design problems whose aim is to gain knowledge about the world, not to reach decisions.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites this paper for the expected information of an experiment. In its framework, one form of an agent's objective needs the information term to use the exact posterior of the agent's own model, and the believed value of a message is identified with this expected information and with the epistemic term of the expected free energy of attention.
