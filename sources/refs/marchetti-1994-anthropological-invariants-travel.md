# Anthropological Invariants in Travel Behavior

> [!info] Citation
> Marchetti, C. (1994). *Anthropological Invariants in Travel Behavior*. Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 47(1), 75-88. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-1625(94)90041-8)

[Local PDF](../pdfs/marchetti-1994-anthropological-invariants-travel.pdf), 20 pages; retrieved November 10, 2026. Reading basis: local PDF, first pages.

## TL;DR

A paper arguing that personal travel is governed more by basic instincts than by economic drives, which the author offers as an explanation for the mismatch between cost-benefit analysis and observed traveler behavior.

## Problem & setting

The paper treats humans as territorial animals who want a larger territory but pay for it in exertion and exposure to threat. For this balance the author proposes mean travel time per day as the single adjustable parameter, since time multiplied by mean speed fixes a range.

## Method

The extract shows an argumentative synthesis of anthropological, geographic and transport information, with field work by Zahavi used as the empirical anchor. The part read does not show a formal model.

## Key results

The extract reports Zahavi's empirical conclusion that mean daily exposure time is about one hour across populations, as a mean over the year and the population with narrow tails. It also presents village territories in Greece, with a mean area of a little above 20 square kilometers. The remainder of the paper was not in the pages read.

## Relevance to this research

The manuscript cites the paper for a daily travel budget of about one hour, a typical pedestrian-city size and the linear expansion of cities with faster transport, as empirical anchors for a bound on the diameter of the sets reachable in one round of communication.
