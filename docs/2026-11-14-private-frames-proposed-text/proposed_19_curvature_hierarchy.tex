The wall terms that a Gaussian loop prior leaves in $\pi_b$ can sum dictionary generators that different receivers own, which happens when the loop contains bridges of more than one receiver. They belong to the declared joint density, which is describer-level in the sense of \cref{def:access}. A wall term whose bridges all enter one receiver is a function of that receiver's own dictionaries. Any other wall term enters an owner's own objective through dictionaries owned elsewhere, through their laws in the law-level model of \cref{it2:per-agent-model} and through their means alone in the plug-in model. An owner evaluates such a term under the declared exchange of \cref{it2:dictionary-exchange}, applied to the bridge dictionaries of the loop; on a closed walk that passes through an owner more than once, the exchange comprises every bridge dictionary that this owner holds on the walk, as stated in \cref{it1:owned}. The protocol of the exchange is the open problem \cref{it2:open-messages}, and without the exchange the term remains describer-level. At loop weight $\lambda=0$, the case of \cref{it3:energy}, the wall terms are absent and no exchange is needed.

A wall term of this kind can read bridge dictionaries of several owners, so a realization in which the owners themselves evaluate it uses the declared exchange of \cref{it2:dictionary-exchange}, applied to those bridge dictionaries and comprising every one that an owner holds on a closed walk passing through it more than once (\cref{it1:owned}). The protocol of that exchange is the open problem \cref{it2:open-messages}.
