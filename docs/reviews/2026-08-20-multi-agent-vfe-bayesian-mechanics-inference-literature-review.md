## Verdict

The search found no established synthesis that unifies all of the requested literature. The inspected corpus is best organized into three neighboring, partly overlapping research programs with distinct mathematical objects. Multi-agent or collective active inference studies coupled agents that minimize individual, local, or specially constructed joint variational free energies and can sometimes exhibit a collective Markov blanket or an emergent group-level generative model. Bayesian mechanics gives a stochastic-process account of one blanketed system through a particular partition, synchronization map, and stationary flow; the search found no established general theory called *multi-agent Bayesian mechanics*. Distributed, decentralized, and federated Bayesian inference constitute a mature separate literature on approximating or decomposing a shared posterior across agents or data holders.

This distinction matters for novelty claims. The existing literature already covers social belief sharing, collective motion, epistemic communities, distributed variational message passing, posterior servers, partitioned variational inference, and several narrow exact collective constructions. It does not, on the evidence located here, supply a general equivalence between a reciprocal moving-peer objective with gauge-transported pairwise divergences and coordinate ascent on one fixed joint mean-field evidence lower bound (ELBO). That narrower gap is defensible. A claim that multi-agent variational free energy or collective Bayesian inference is wholly new is not.

## Multi-agent variational free energy and collective active inference

The strongest established body of directly relevant work concerns interacting active-inference agents. Friston et al., [“Federated inference and belief sharing”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105500), model communicated beliefs as observations under sufficiently aligned world models. Their Equation 7 also defines a specialized joint free energy over agents, which the paper explicitly distinguishes from Bayesian belief updating. Heins et al., [“Collective behavior from surprise minimization”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2320239121), show that local active-inference particles can generate flocking, milling, and schooling. Albarracin et al., [“Epistemic Communities under Active Inference”](https://doi.org/10.3390/e24040476), obtain social-network phenomena including epistemic clustering from attention and learning dynamics. These works establish nontrivial collective behavior and, in Friston et al., a particular joint functional, but they do not prove that arbitrary coupled agents perform coordinate ascent on one exact fixed-joint ELBO.

Waade et al., [“As One and Many: Relating Individual and Emergent Group-Level Generative Models in Active Inference”](https://doi.org/10.3390/e27020143), directly address the relationship between individual and group descriptions. Their construction makes a group-level Markov blanket a condition for treating a collective as an agent and estimates a group model in a toy multi-armed-bandit setting. The paper leaves the precise relationship between individual and group free energies open. This is a close conceptual neighbor to a multi-agent ELBO program, but it is not a generic aggregation theorem.

Heins et al., [“Spin glass systems as collective active inference”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06970), provide a stronger but deliberately narrow equivalence. With particular factorized generative models, symmetric mutual precisions, and asynchronous updating, an ensemble of active-inference agents samples the Glauber or Boltzmann dynamics of a spin glass. The authors explicitly describe the equivalence as fragile. It is evidence that exact collective reductions are possible under load-bearing structural assumptions, not that arbitrary coupled free-energy systems possess such a reduction.

Additional adjacent constructions widen the comparison set. Maisto et al., [“Interactive Inference: A Multi-Agent Model of Cooperative Joint Actions”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13113), develop reciprocal prediction and joint action. Albarracin et al., [“Shared Protentions in Multi-Agent Active Inference”](https://doi.org/10.3390/e26040303), formalize shared goals using compositional structures. Taniguchi et al., [“Collective Predictive Coding as Model-Based Science”](https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2024.1353870), and Hoang et al., [a Metropolis-Hastings naming-game account](https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2023.1290604), connect inter-agent communication to distributed inference over shared representations. Maisto, Nuzzi, and Pezzulo’s recent preprint, [“What the flock knows that the birds do not”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10835), reports a higher-order blanket and synergistic collective information in a simulated flock. These are relevant mechanisms and empirical comparators, but none supplies the general fixed-joint ELBO equivalence described above.

## Bayesian mechanics and the multi-agent gap

Bayesian mechanics is a recognizable field, but its load-bearing results are formulated for one blanketed stochastic system. Da Costa et al., [“Bayesian mechanics for stationary processes”](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2021.0518), derive the relationship among stationary diffusion, a Markov-blanket or particular-state partition, a synchronization map, and a free-energy interpretation. Ramstead et al., [“On Bayesian mechanics: a physics of and by beliefs”](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2022.0029), organize the resulting field around partitions, maps, flows, and path- or mode-tracking interpretations. Sakthivadivel, [“Towards a Geometry and Analysis for Bayesian Mechanics”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11900), develops an axiomatic and geometric formalization. Friston’s [“A free energy principle for a particular physics”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10184) supplies the broader recursive-particle framing.

No inspected source establishes a general multi-agent Bayesian-mechanics theorem with multiple individual Markov blankets, reciprocal synchronization maps, and a consistency or coarse-graining law relating them to a group-level generative process. Da Costa et al. explicitly suggest that the external space could be partitioned into several systems that are themselves Markov blankets. The wording identifies interacting blankets as a possible extension rather than presenting the extension as a completed result.

The nearest constructive literature is multiscale or nested-blanket work. Palacios et al., [“On Markov blankets and hierarchical self-organisation”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2019.110089), demonstrate in simulation that suitably equipped microscopic blankets can self-organize into a macroscopic blanket, while rejecting the claim that every coupled random system must do so. The safe conclusion is therefore conditional: a collective can be treated by Bayesian mechanics if a group-level blanket, appropriate stationary or nonequilibrium steady-state conditions, conditional independences, ergodicity assumptions, and a valid synchronization map are demonstrated. Coupling, communication, or coordinated behavior alone does not provide those conditions.

This leaves a genuine research opening. A multi-agent Bayesian-mechanics construction would need to state whether the primitive objects are several blankets embedded in one external space, a hierarchy of nested blankets, or several synchronization maps related by a transport or cocycle condition. It would also need to prove when the individual partitions induce a group partition and when the resulting group dynamics retain the stationary-flow assumptions used by existing Bayesian mechanics. Calling an interacting active-inference simulation “multi-agent Bayesian mechanics” without those obligations would overstate the result.

## Distributed, decentralized, and federated Bayesian inference

Distributed Bayesian inference is the mature comparison field for any claim about a shared posterior or global ELBO. Campbell and How, [“Approximate Decentralized Bayesian Inference”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7471), combine local posterior approximations with a shared-prior correction and address mixture-component permutation alignment. Bandyopadhyay and Chung’s Bayesian consensus-filtering line and Battistelli and Chisci’s Kullback-Leibler average develop graph-based probability-density fusion, including geometric or logarithmic pooling. These methods make topology, reused information, and consensus assumptions explicit; consensus of densities is not automatically identical to centralized Bayes when evidence cycles through a graph.

Hua and Li’s [distributed variational Bayesian algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSP.2015.2493979) and Masegosa et al.’s [“Distributed Variational Message Passing”](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v52/masegosa16.html) distribute variational updates over sensor or computing networks. Hasenclever et al., [“Distributed Bayesian Learning with Stochastic Natural-Gradient Expectation Propagation and the Posterior Server”](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v18/16-478.html), organize asynchronous distributed learning through expectation-propagation sites and a posterior server. Vehtari et al., [“Expectation Propagation as a Way of Life”](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v21/18-817.html), provide a broad framework for partitioned data and local approximations.

Partitioned variational inference makes the factor-decomposition view especially clear. Ashman et al., [“Partitioned Variational Inference”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12275), express a global approximation through site factors that can be updated under federated schedules. Heikkilä et al., [“Differentially Private Partitioned Variational Inference”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11595), add privacy-preserving updates. Mildner et al., [“Federated Generalised Variational Inference”](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/mildner25a.html), broaden the local loss and divergence choices. Wu and Uribe, [“Frequentist Guarantees of Distributed Bayesian Inference”](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-1504.html), analyze asymptotic behavior under networked belief updates. These works clarify the conditions under which local computations approximate a declared global posterior, but they do not claim that agents are physical Markov-blanketed systems.

Recent application-specific variational methods show that the field remains active. Paritosh et al., [“Distributed Variational Inference: An Online Approach”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02606), derive a distributed evidence lower bound and online consensus method. Cao et al., [“Distributed Variational Inference for Online Multi-Robot Cooperative Localization”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18331), apply distributed variational inference to cooperative simultaneous localization and mapping. Rao et al., [“Federated Variational Inference for Bayesian Mixture Models”](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v297/rao26a.html), address mixture models in a federated setting. Their relevance to a gauge-aware program is twofold: they show how a true shared target is declared and decomposed, and mixture models make discrete label or permutation alignment an explicit problem distinct from continuous gauge transport.

## Recent watchlist: broad collective variational principles

Two 2026 arXiv preprints use terminology unusually close to the requested topic: Bouchaffra et al., [“A Collective Variational Principle Unifying Bayesian Inference, Game Theory, and Thermodynamics”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27942), and [“Coalition Free Energy and Adaptive Precision in Multi-Agent Cooperation”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26278). A related preprint develops [a game-theoretic free-energy analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09515). They warrant a dedicated mathematical review because they may change how a novelty statement should be worded. They are recent, unpeer-reviewed, and make broad unification claims, so this review does not treat their terminology as evidence that a general theory has been established.

## Relation to the Gauge-VFE theory

### Overall relation

The inspected literature does not already contain the Gauge-VFE construction, but it does constrain how that construction should be described. The live theory combines one fixed normalized population law, a generally correlated recognition law, exact conditional local and meta-agent variational free energies, gauge-typed belief and model fibers, and exact law-level coarse-graining that retains induced hyperedges, marked attention events, holonomy data, and path memory. The neighboring literatures provide exact special cases, candidate mechanisms for group formation, and decentralized algorithms around parts of this structure. They do not jointly establish it.

The relation is therefore one of conditional embedding and comparison, not wholesale subsumption. A finite collective model can be represented inside the interaction-record construction when its observations, messages, actions, and interaction labels are supplied as normalized kernels of one recognition-independent generative law. That statement is an admissible encoding template. It is not a theorem that every active-inference simulation, stochastic game, or decentralized protocol is already an instance of Gauge-VFE, because their random objects, conditioning records, update schedules, and physical interpretations can differ.

### Exact collective law and the individual-to-group question

The core finite theory answers a question that Waade et al. leave open, but only under declared conditions. With one normalized interaction-record law and one correlated recognition law, the collective variational free energy is the negative log evidence plus a posterior KL divergence. Disintegration gives an exact conditional variational free energy for one agent or any declared block. If the outside marginal is fixed, a unilateral conditional update changes the same collective scalar by exactly the local conditional change. This is a coordinate-potential theorem, not a claim that independently defined local objectives add to the global functional.

The distinction resolves the individual-versus-group sum question conditionally. Incident local objectives generally overcount an interaction once per endpoint or hyperedge member. An exact additive ledger instead contains the joint entropy or total-correlation correction, singleton prior terms, and every interaction record once. At the next scale, the group law and recognition law are obtained by pushing both fine laws through the same declared coarse channel. Their group-level free energy therefore follows from the coarse evidence identity; it is not generically the sum of independently specified individual free energies. This is a conditional answer because the coarse channel, partition variable, support conditions, and hierarchical factorization must first be supplied.

Friston et al.'s joint free energy over agents is not a counterexample to this positioning. It is an important specialized construction and must be acknowledged. Their belief-sharing update assimilates other agents' posterior messages and is explicitly described as formally distinct from Bayesian updating on conditionally independent observations. Gauge-VFE can place communicated beliefs inside a normalized source-message model, but only after the generative role, source independence, and conditioning record are declared. Live posterior exchange cannot silently be relabeled as evidence.

### Gauge structure and frustration

None of the inspected collective or distributed papers supplies the same continuous local-frame structure as Gauge-VFE: separate belief and model representations, their induced probability-law actions, inter-agent transports, and the retention of non-flat holonomy under coarse-graining. Discrete mixture-label or symbol alignment is a permutation-identification problem. Gauge alignment is a continuous equivariance and cocycle problem. A formal construction can relate the two in a particular model, but one does not solve the other.

The spin-glass reduction is the cleanest positive control for the interaction sector. Its symmetric precisions and asynchronous updates provide the detailed-balance conditions under which a collective Boltzmann law appears. Gauge-transported divergences can supply invariant bond energies in a generalization, but spin-glass frustration and gauge holonomy must remain distinct: the former comes from incompatible interaction preferences or signs, whereas the latter comes from path-dependent frame transport. A useful experiment would add nontrivial transport while preserving a declared invariant edge energy, then test exactly which detailed-balance and stationary-law statements survive.

### Meta-agents, partitions, and the coalition overlap

Palacios et al., Waade et al., and Maisto et al. give the full graph/meta-agent chapter empirical and modeling comparators, not its missing theorem. Palacios et al. show that prior-equipped microscopic blankets can assemble a macroscopic blanket and explicitly reject automatic emergence under arbitrary coupling. Waade et al. begin from an identifiable group blanket and infer a group model. Maisto et al. detect a flock-level statistical boundary and synergistic information in simulation. These works can inform partition priors, conditional-independence tests, persistence tests, and external perturbation assays. They do not select a unique partition from the fine law, prove persistence across scales, or establish gauge-compatible closure.

The recent Bouchaffra construction has a more direct mathematical overlap with `grand_canonical_meta_agent_formation.tex` than with the core exact ELBO. On a finite agent set, identify a coalition \(\mathcal C\) with its binary occupation vector \(n\in\{0,1\}^N\), identify inverse temperature \(\beta^{-1}\) with the membership temperature, and absorb a chemical-potential term \(-\mu\sum_i n_i\) into the coalition energy. Then the two functionals belong to the same entropy-regularized Gibbs class. The Harsanyi or Möbius expansion over subsets corresponds to a full hyperedge expansion of the occupation energy, while gauge-transported divergences provide a special invariant choice of pair or hyperedge coefficient.

This overlap narrows any broad priority claim. The project should not claim the first collective variational principle, the first Gibbs law over coalitions, or the first multi-agent free-energy/game connection. The remaining structural distinction is that the core Gauge-VFE theory begins from one normalized agent-and-record probability model with correlated recognition and then types its geometry and coarse maps. The Bouchaffra preprint begins from individual free energies and a separately declared coalition energy. It adds a game-theoretic and Harsanyi layer that Gauge-VFE presently lacks; Gauge-VFE adds normalized-joint, gauge, holonomy, and RG obligations that the preprint does not address. The preprint remains unreviewed, and its Nash and universality claims require a separate theorem audit before use as a foundation.

The standalone grand-canonical note is itself a proposed extension, not part of the main exact theorem chain. Its Gibbs minimizers, gauge covariance, and fixed-control Lyapunov identities follow from its declared functionals, but the edge and membership ensembles are added model structure. Autonomous agency, unique hierarchy, physical thermodynamics, and sustained nonequilibrium remain open there as well.

### Bayesian mechanics is not yet inherited

Gauge-VFE is adjacent to Bayesian mechanics, but the current theory is not yet a formal multi-agent generalization of it. A fixed normalized joint, an ELBO, gauge covariance, and natural-gradient descent do not supply a stationary stochastic process with a Markov blanket and synchronization map. Da Costa et al.'s construction requires a particular-state partition, conditional independence, a steady-state density, a valid synchronization map, and a stochastic flow. The live Gauge-VFE chapters deliberately do not identify their optimization parameter or Fisher duration with physical time.

The missing multi-agent Bayesian-mechanics theorem must establish individual and group Markov blankets, appropriate stationary or nonequilibrium steady-state and ergodicity conditions, individual synchronization maps, and a group synchronization map compatible with the declared coarse channel or posterior bridge. Gauge covariance adds another obligation: the maps and coarse channels must be equivariant and compositionally consistent, including any retained holonomy. PIFB2 already states the correct boundary: its dense, irreversibly coupled attention regime does not license the synchronization-map reading, so its belief fibers are an engineered consensus objective rather than a derived steady-state mechanics.

### Distributed inference is an algorithmic layer around the oracle

The exact finite Gauge-VFE law is best used as a centralized oracle for a later decentralized implementation. PVI supplies the cleanest factor-ownership template because its local sites modify a declared global approximation instead of passing posterior objects without provenance. Paritosh et al.'s distributed ELBO separates modeling error from consensus error; a gauge-aware implementation should add frame-alignment error, path or holonomy sensitivity, and evidence-lineage error. Each transmitted factor should retain identity, owner, prior exponent, version, and ancestry so a communication cycle cannot silently count one record twice.

The strongest experimental ladder is consequently staged. First reproduce the Heins spin-glass special case and its known failure under altered symmetry or update scheduling. Then test exact centralized correlated inference against declared mean-field restrictions. Next distribute owned factors and measure posterior, consensus, gauge-alignment, and lineage errors separately. Only after those controls should the program test learned partitions, persistent meta-agents, lossy communication, privacy, or physical Bayesian-mechanics interpretations.

### Bounded novelty statement

The defensible claim from the inspected corpus is narrower than “multi-agent VFE is new.” The project constructs, in one typed finite and multiscale framework, a posterior-independent normalized population law on associated statistical fibers, generally correlated recognition with exact local and block conditionals, gauge-covariant belief and model channels, and common-channel coarse-graining that retains interaction and holonomy data. No inspected paper combines all of those objects. That is a corpus-bounded positioning statement, not proof of historical priority. The Bouchaffra preprints remove any safe claim to broad collective-variational terminology, and the Bayesian-mechanics literature prevents calling the current theory a completed multi-agent mechanics until the partition-map-flow obligations are proved.

## Consequences for a MultiAgentELBO or gauge-aware research program

The most defensible theoretical center remains an explicitly declared finite joint density with an exact centralized ELBO oracle. Distributed and federated methods then become algorithmic decompositions or approximations to that target, while collective active-inference models become mechanistic comparators. This prevents the same pairwise divergence from being described simultaneously as a physical interaction, a Bayesian likelihood contribution, and a consensus penalty without a derivation relating those roles.

Evidence lineage should be part of the formal state. Factor identity, ownership, prior exponents, version, and ancestry are needed to show that cycles in a communication graph do not count the same observation more than once. A consensus limit can still be useful when exact lineage is unavailable, but it should be labeled as pooling or approximate inference rather than centralized Bayes.

Alignment also separates into at least two problems. Mixture-component or categorical label alignment is a discrete permutation-identification problem already visible in decentralized mixture inference. Gauge transport is a continuous frame-identification problem whose correctness requires equivariance, path consistency, and possibly holonomy or cocycle conditions. Solving one does not solve the other.

For Bayesian mechanics, the central proof obligation is not merely to couple several agents. It is to show that the individual particular-state partitions, reciprocal maps, and environmental coupling induce a legitimate group-level partition and synchronization map under stated dynamical assumptions. The empirical ladder should therefore distinguish: an exact tree-structured case with controlled evidence ownership; a centralized correlated variational family; a decentralized approximation with measured communication and double-counting error; and only then asynchronous, lossy, or privacy-preserving variants.

## Bounded conclusion

The literature supports three conclusions. First, multi-agent variational free energy is already a populated topic under collective active inference, distributed variational inference, predictive coding, and related names. Second, “multi-agent Bayesian mechanics” is best treated as an open extension of existing single-system Bayesian mechanics and nested-blanket work, not as an established subfield and not as an empty intellectual neighborhood. Third, multi-agent Bayesian inference is mature under distributed, decentralized, federated, partitioned, and social-learning traditions, but those traditions usually solve a different problem from embodied collective active inference.

The remaining novelty claim must therefore be narrow and structural: a derivation that connects explicitly coupled agent-wise variational objectives, evidence provenance, and any gauge-transport law to a declared global generative model, together with conditions under which the construction is exact, approximate, or fails. Search failure alone cannot establish novelty; the mathematical obligations and counterexamples must carry that claim.
