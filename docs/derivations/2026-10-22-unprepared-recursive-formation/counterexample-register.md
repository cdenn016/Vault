# Scoped failures and falsification tests

These failures constrain stronger claims. None is a nonexistence proof for the frozen existential target.

## R1. Strong native preference does not imply continuous response redundancy

For two distinct binary sources through an injective binary channel, the Gibbs block normalizer is a two-term exponential sum after removing its receiver entropy factor. Four derivatives at one interior receiver value determine its two source logits: the Hankel determinant is \(c_1c_2(v_1-v_2)^2>0\). Thus an open class of exterior receiver probes identifies the continuous source-law coordinates even when the within-block divergence is small. `routes/native-response-recovery.md` gives the inverse. The final construction uses selective angular channels and a restricted aggregate-angle interface; it does not contradict this inverse.

## R2. Initial Fisher sufficiency can fail immediately under incoming forces

In the two-agent identity-channel example of `routes/geometry.md`, retain only the bit marginal while initially varying that marginal with fixed conditional profiles. The initial Fisher loss is zero. The complete incoming force generates loss \(t^2L+o(t^2)\), with \(L=32/3\) for the displayed positive binary conditionals. Suppressing the incoming derivative falsely removes this obstruction. The final construction uses an isometric section retention and its actual derivative instead of this fixed marginal retention.

## R3. Pointwise phase centering loses contextual geometry

Two positive location laws with phases \(c,-c\) and inertias \(I_1,I_2\) have minimized contextual length \(4I_1I_2/(I_1+I_2)>0\). Centering separately at each context makes the retained law constant and gives zero. The anchored construction retains the relative phase profiles and rotates each whole section by a constant. See geometry-symmetry-interface (S16).

## R4. The canonical Fisher metric need not generate the quotient dynamics

For two location families and retained total phase \(F\), the quotient metric is \(dF^2/(I_1^{-1}+I_2^{-1})\), while the equal-phase representative has metric \((I_1+I_2)dF^2/4\). They differ unless \(I_1=I_2\). An invariant potential with nonzero derivative gives different gradient speeds. The final certificate uses the derived moving-representative vector field, not an uncorrected canonical Fisher gradient.

## R5. Arbitrary microscopic angle responses do not descend

A discarded relative rotation changes \(\mathbb E e^{i\Theta_i}\) for a nonzero first moment while leaving the parent state unchanged. Such a constituent absolute-angle probe therefore fails exact response preservation. The declared protocol permits the whole-block angle sum, not arbitrary future subdivision of a retained block.

## R6. A global root representative has extra finite isotropy

The total anchor phase of a block of size \(n\) has no globally continuous \(n\)-th root. A common rotation by \(2\pi/n\) lies in the discarded group but need not fix the fine laws. Global stabilizer equality is false. Compatible local charts preserve the Lie-algebra inertia, connection, and curvature used in the theorem. A global statistical realization needs additional specification.

## R7. Event-synchronized contextual derivatives contain another term

If contexts use different handoff times \(\tau(c)\), differentiating their states gives \(d_cp_t+Vd\tau\). Omitting the second term confuses a birth-time pullback with a common-time comparison. Moreover, retaining both the event time and the pre-retention event state makes the flow-to-event map locally injective. The final construction selects one partition at its anchor and compares whole sections on the same physical-time slice; actual orientation loss survives its invariant event times.

## R8. A first-stage polarized attractor does not prove later fusion

The separate `routes/native-cliques.md` theorem has attracting opposite-polarization pairs. Its proof supplies permanent first-stage preference, but no later join. Substituting that result for the all-source contracting construction would leave the recursive target open. The final witness uses the different fixed \(J=0\) architecture of staged-cliques throughout both stages.

## R9. A four-agent second join has an empty exterior

Once two pairs exhaust four agents, a statement about their union's contrast against its complement is vacuous. The six-agent extension retains a third parent with strictly smaller cross masses throughout the second event. It makes the final claim nonvacuous without changing the threshold or source priors after formation.

## R10. Not every initial law forms the claimed pairs

Identical uniform binary laws remain uniform under the final architecture. With six singletons, each peer row entry is \(1/6\), while its mass to the other four peers is \(4/6\), so the positive-contrast condition fails. The theorem is an existence result on a full open initial neighborhood, not a universal or random-initialization statement.

## R11. Nonzero finite-time geometry does not imply a uniform eternal bound

In the explicit angular solution, concentrations decay as \(e^{-2t}\) and tend toward Haar measure. Rotation inertia tends to zero. Strict positive geometry on each compact finite horizon does not establish an infinite-time lower bound or indefinite recursive depth.

## R12. An arbitrary contextual template can defeat the advertised loss

The final construction uses the explicitly rotation-equivariant tilt lift in geometry-symmetry-interface (S23), so the whole retained section has the same continuous physical-symmetry fibers.
