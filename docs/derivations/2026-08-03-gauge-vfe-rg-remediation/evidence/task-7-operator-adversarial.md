# Task 7 operator-theory adversarial audit

## Scope

This read-only pass attacked the \(L^p\), Dobrushin, essential-spectrum, and

Agreement is supplemental; the decisive evidence is the typed proof or

## Essential-spectrum reconstruction

The manuscript defines
\[
r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)=r\bigl(U+\mathcal K(X)\bigr)
\]
as the spectral radius of the coset in
\(\mathcal L(X)/\mathcal K(X)\), where \(\mathcal K(X)\) is the ideal of all
compact operators.  It does not substitute the finite-rank quotient.

Under the exact hypotheses that \(X\) is a complex Banach lattice,
\(\mathbf 1\) is quasi-interior, \(U\) is bounded, positive, and unital, and a
strict essential gap supplies a nonzero positive dual eigenfunctional
\(\lambda\) at \(r(U)\), suppose
\(r(U)>r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)\).  Quasi-interiority implies
\(\lambda(\mathbf 1)>0\): otherwise positivity makes \(\lambda\) vanish on
the dense principal ideal generated by \(\mathbf1\).  But
\[
r(U)\lambda(\mathbf1)
=(U^*\lambda)(\mathbf1)
=\lambda(U\mathbf1)
=\lambda(\mathbf1).
\]
Thus \(r(U)=1\), contradicting \(r(U)>1\).  Consequently
\[
r(U)>1\Longrightarrow r(U)=r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)
\]
under the displayed gap, pole, and positive-eigenfunctional hypotheses.
Nothing in this proof licenses quasi-compactness or a weaker Perron theorem.

## Circle witness reconstruction

On \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), let \(D(z)=2z\bmod1\) and define the
forward kernel invariantly by
\[
K(y,A)=\frac12\sum_{z\in D^{-1}\{y\}}\mathbf1_A(z).
\]
For Haar probability, the reverse conditional is
\(\Pi(z,dy)=\delta_{D(z)}(dy)\), hence \(Uf=f\circ D\).  Haar preservation
gives \(\|U^n\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}=1\), so
\[
r_{L^\infty}(U)=1.
\]
For complex periodic \(C^\alpha\), \(0<\alpha\le1\), with the sup-plus-Hölder
norm,
\[
[f\circ D^n]_\alpha\le2^{\alpha n}[f]_\alpha.
\]
The fixed mode \(f(t)=e^{2\pi it}\), evaluated at \(0\) and
\(2^{-(n+1)}\), has a Hölder quotient at least
\(2^{1+\alpha(n+1)}\).  The matching upper and lower root bounds yield
\[
r_{C^\alpha}(U)=2^\alpha.
\]
At \(\alpha=1\), \(C^\alpha\) means the Lipschitz space \(C^{0,1}\), not
\(C^1\).

The standard Hölder norm does not make \(C^\alpha\) a Banach lattice.  Indeed,
\(|\sin(2\pi Nt)|\le1\), but its Hölder norm grows with \(N\), whereas the
constant \(1\) has fixed norm.  Therefore the circle example is a separate
Banach-space topology witness, not an instance of the preceding
Banach-lattice theorem.

## Dobrushin and domain attacks

The essential Dobrushin coefficient is correctly defined modulo the
almost-everywhere ambiguity of the reverse kernel, and the total-variation
oscillation estimate gives
\[
\|\overline U_{n\leftarrow\ell}\|
\le\prod_{k=\ell}^{n-1}\delta_k.
\]
The two finite counterexamples were recomputed:

1. The displayed \(R_0,R_1\) each have coefficient one, while
   \(R_1R_0\) has identical rows and coefficient zero.
2. For \(R_k=(1-a_k)I+a_kJ\) with \(a_k=2^{-k-2}\),
   every \(\delta_k<1\) but their infinite product is positive.

These respectively falsify necessity of the coefficient certificate and the
claim that one-step strictness alone forces nonautonomous forgetting.

The downstream fixed-point text was also checked after repair.  It restricts
the exact Fréchet variations to bounded \(L^\infty\) perturbations and scopes
ordinary spectra to an autonomous bounded endomorphism on one declared
complex Banach space.  General scale-changing derivatives are described only
as a typed cocycle with comparison-dependent growth.

## Adversarial conclusion

PASS for the assigned operator scope.  The result would be falsified by any
remaining use of a finite-rank rather than all-compact Calkin quotient, by
swapping the circle Koopman operator for the averaging transfer operator, by
applying the Banach-lattice theorem to standard Hölder space, or by claiming
that a one-step Dobrushin coefficient is a necessary and sufficient
nonautonomous classifier.  None survives in the reconciled source.
