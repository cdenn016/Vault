## Memory of the coarse internal state of level-0 members

The user asked for the next item of the open mathematics of the manuscript in `manuscripts/Collective_Agents/`, the second half of `open:met-approximate`: a bound on the memory of the coarse internal state when the members fuse literally with fixed gains, beyond the exact recursion of `prop:met-level0`. The result is the new `thm:apx-level0` in a new subsection of `sections/ext1_apx.tex`, with its proof at the end of `sections/appext1_apx.tex` and the check record `chk:apx-level0` at the end of `sections/chkext1_apx.tex`.

The theorem is stated for scalar level-0 members with exact code models, in terms of the block $W_{CC}$ of the update of the members' means: the root-mean-square deviations $\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$ of its row and column sums from their common mean $\bar\lambda$, and the mixing constant $\mathcal S_\perp=\sum_l\Vert W_\perp^l\Vert$ of its restriction to the members' deviations. Part (a) writes the average as a closed recursion plus a defect $\delta_t=n^{-1}\mathsf f_{\mathrm{col}}^\top\mathrm{dev}(t)$, gives the recursion of the deviations in error coordinates, from which the world innovation drops out, and bounds the memory kernels by $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\Vert W_\perp^{l-1}\Vert$. Part (b) bounds the upper limit of the root-mean-square defect by $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}\mathcal S_\perp(\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg}}+\mathsf v_{\mathrm{het}})$, with $\mathsf e_{\mathrm{avg}}$ the error of the average and $\mathsf v_{\mathrm{het}}$ the heterogeneity of the members' inputs and noise. Part (c) bounds the closure defect of the average from both sides; the lower bound, $\frac12\ln(1+\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}^2)$ for isotropic internal noise, contains no mixing constant, so the information-form closure defect of a level-0 average does not fall with a ratio of timescales at fixed column dispersion. Part (d) bounds the distance between the average and the closed coarse recursion fed the realized coarse sensory state. Part (e) locates the slowest mode of $W_{CC}$ under a power bound on $W_\perp$, without reversibility, by an argument with the theorem of Rouché.

The check used exact second moments on 900 scalar networks in nine families and found no violation; the bound of part (b) exceeded the exact stationary value by a median factor between 3.7 and 5.9. On a family with a fixed directed pattern and growing mixing, the closure defect fell from 14.3 to 1.08 times its lower bound and never below it.

An independent proof check reconstructed parts (a) to (e) and found no mathematical error and no mismatch between the check record and the script output. It found one false sentence in the discussion, which said that both bounds of part (b) vanish with $\sigma_{\mathrm{col}}$ although the bound on the deviations does not contain it, and several sentences that claimed too much or too little; all were corrected. The corrections state that the bounds take two second moments of the process as data, that the closure defect registers mixing only down to its floor, that the impossibility of a bound through a timescale ratio is a statement uniform over the class, that part (e) corresponds to part (b)(ii) of `thm:agy-closure-average` and the kernel bound to (b)(i), and that the first line of (a), the kernels, part (c), the identity of (d) and part (e) hold for scalar members with any code models. The hypothesis of part (c) now names the independence of the noises, and the proof of (e) states that the closed disc lies in the region of analyticity. The check's own scripts added a recomputation of the 900 instances, a path-space test that shows the restriction $t\ge1$ in (c) is needed, a test of (e) on 6000 matrices with entries of both signs, and a two-member instance on which the location in (e) is attained; these are recorded in `chk:apx-level0`.

The theorem does not give a counterpart of property (iv) of `thm:met-h5a` for level-0 members, a bound on the Markov error of a non-reversible recursion, the vector-valued case, bounds closed in the network parameters, or the lower bound of (c) under broadcast noise; `open:apx-internal` was rewritten to list these. The passages that said no bound existed were updated in `sections/s01_introduction.tex`, `sections/10a_meta_agents.tex`, `sections/s10a_meta_agents.tex`, `sections/13_discussion.tex`, `sections/05_agency.tex` and `sections/s05_agency.tex`, where `open:agy-discrete-closure` now records that the kernel bound and the location of the slowest mode are available for non-reversible recursions and that the Markov error is not. Both documents were rebuilt, at 215 and 525 pages with no undefined reference.

## Five results on the open mathematics of the manuscript

The user asked for all the items of the ranked list: the approximate meta-agent theorem under violated blanket conditions, the belief of a collective of level-0 members, the Markov error of a non-reversible discrete recursion, and the open problem on renormalization beyond the exact cases. Each lane derived its result in scratch files, was read by an independent proof checker, was revised, and was read again until the check found no error or the round limit was reached. A fifth lane supplied the general statement on which the first relies. All five are now in the supplement.

The general statement is `thm:apx-obs` in a new subsection `sec:apx-obs` of `sections/ext1_apx.tex`. A fine record $z_{t+1}=H_{\mathrm x}x_t+v_t$ is read by the policy, the meta-agent reads a linear summary $s=\mathsf Tz$ through a declared observation model that may be wrong, and it runs the stationary filter of its declared model. The theorem gives the error recursion, the covariance equation, a bound on the upper limit of the excess relative entropy with no smallness condition that reduces to the earlier bound when the observation model is right and the summary is sufficient, and a bound at every round from general starts for a detectable fine process. The observation defect $\mathsf G=\mathsf TH_{\mathrm x}-\bar HC$ enters as a drive and as an addition to the summary noise that is correlated with the prediction error. The insufficiency of the summary enters through a covariance $\mathsf W_{\mathrm{ins}}$ that no declared model removes when the policy reads the summary alone, so no bound in the model defects alone can hold. `prop:apx-obs-information` splits the excess into the divergence from the law given a coarse record and an information term, and identifies the excess with that of the summary filter plus $\frac12\ln\det(P_{\mathrm e}^{-1}P_{\mathsf T})$ for policies that read the summary; the same identity is refuted for policies that read the fine record and for a filter start that is not a function of the randomization. `prop:apx-obs-fixed` covers a fixed gain and a perturbed filter. The check record is `chk:apx-obs`: on 3000 random instances no inequality failed, and the bound exceeded the exact stationary excess by a median factor between 1.0 and 6.8 by family, with a factor above 100 on 102 instances.

The approximate meta-agent theorem without the hypothesis (H-suff) is `thm:apx-h5a-blanket` in `sec:apx-h5a-blanket`. Five kinds of violation are admitted: sensors that read exterior coordinates outside the coarse exterior, sensory weights other than the information form, sensors with memory, sensors that read acts or internal states, and an exterior that reads the members one by one. The record becomes the members' channels, which separate the exterior from the collective in the canonical partition with per-channel noise, and the belief property is stated relative to the law of the exterior given those channels. The theorem gives the five properties with error terms in the closure defect, the observation defect and the insufficiency of the coarse sensory state, for stable linear-Gaussian collectives whose members run the consensus filter; its bound for the belief concerns the stationary limit. `prop:apx-blanket-reference` compares the law given the members' channels with the law given a coarse record, bounds the information term between them uniformly in the round, and caps it when only the first two kinds are present. `prop:apx-blanket-input` shows that the excess is a prediction error and converges, that the insufficiency term is not a floor when the coarse act is correlated with the error of the belief, and that it is a floor when the collective reads its sensors through the coarse sensory state alone. `rem:apx-blanket-partial` covers sensors that sense a part of the coarse exterior. A bound on the leak of the coarse blanket, or on the information term, through the defects and uniform in the window is not obtained and is now the content of `open:apx-beyond`. The check record is `chk:apx-h5a-blanket`.

The belief of a collective of level-0 members is `prop:apx-level0-belief` in `sec:apx-level0-belief`. For scalar members with exact code models it gives the exact value of the excess relative entropy of the belief $\Ncal(\bar m(t),P_{\mathrm c})$ over the law of the world state given the members' individual inputs, bounds on its limit that take second moments of the outside senders' errors as data, an a priori bound that is a function of the parameters alone when no agent is outside the collective, and a statement for the reference law given a coarse record. A bound that is uniform in the round is not obtained. The check record is `chk:apx-level0-belief`, on 1500 networks with unit codes and 300 with other codes, with no violation.

The Markov error is `thm:apx-markov-discrete` in `sec:apx-markov`. For one coordinate of a stable linear recursion, under the power bound on the deviation block and the gap condition of `thm:apx-level0`(e), the error $\sup_u\lvert\Corr(y_t,y_{t+u})-\mu_1^u\rvert$ is bounded for every positive definite noise covariance, to first order in the column dispersion, and for isotropic noise to second order with explicit constants. `prop:apx-markov-anisotropic` shows that the second-order form is false for anisotropic noise, `prop:apx-markov-nonnormal` that the powers of the constant of the power bound are attained, and `cor:apx-markov-level0` gives the form for level-0 members with the exterior held fixed. The joint form for several blocks, members whose exterior responds and a statement in the selecting contrast remain open. The check record is `chk:apx-markov`, on 3020 instances with no violation. A by-product recorded in the lane's output and claimed in no file of the manuscript suggests that the same argument in continuous time would lower the constant of `eq:agy-markov-gen`; it was not written out.

For `open:ren-beyond` four statements went into `sections/s10c_renormalization.tex`. `prop:ren-adiabatic-rate` proves the first-order rate of the error of the memoryless reduction with its coefficient, when the noise factors are invertible, the intensity of the internal noise is scaled with the speed of the internal states and two stability hypotheses hold; the instances of `obs:ren-adiabatic-rate` were not retested against those hypotheses, and the text says so wherever the observation is cited beside the proposition. `prop:ren-consensus-levels` treats finitely many consensus rounds at every level with one representative per unit, for symmetric doubly stochastic matrices and noiseless rounds, and gives the count with which each agent's contribution reaches the top, with an attained bound. `prop:ren-decay-rate` determines the exponential rate of the couplings of the decimated nearest-neighbor precision on $\Z^D$ and, for the block factor two on $\Z^2$, the prefactor along the axes; the prefactor in other cases is numerical evidence. `ex:ren-rank-nonmonotone` is a relay of three fixed codes in a common metric with no common direction whose effective rank rises from one hop to the next, which refutes the monotonicity clause of `conj:sca-rank-down` as it was stated; with a common direction monotonicity holds for such codes. Items (h) and (f) of the open problem are settled under the hypotheses named, items (c) and (e) are narrowed, and the problem now has items (a) to (f). Items (a), (d), the former (g) and `conj:ren-lrg` are untouched.
