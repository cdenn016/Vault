# Archive additions: physics reading synthesis

Read on 2026-11-01. Nine distinct archive additions have source notes and page-specific records in `archive-physics-reading.json`. The existing selected publications remain separate; same-work precursor versions excluded by the root manifest have not received duplicate source notes. These are targeted readings, not independent theorem verification.

The archive adds a clear historical progression. The 1989 report makes observational resolution part of computational attribution. The 2009 observer papers make source parsers, ready states, memory, ordered records, and control flow explicit. The 2010 interface paper asks what classical encoding and decoding cost outside a nominal quantum algorithm. The 2013–2014 papers reinterpret system decomposition as observer-relative semantics and as a resource requiring maintained physical reference frames. These contributions are directly useful for the live project's distinction among represented content, record carriers, evaluators, communication dictionaries, and the complete retained response kernel.

The 2009 ready-state paper explicitly allows finite-state machinery for a fixed finite task; its universal architecture requirement belongs to the extended task with unbounded observation periods and new recognizers. Its Born-rule argument assumes unbiased sampling and amplitude-weighted ancillary multiplicities. The 2011 five-assumptions paper similarly builds reliable classical memory and shared observables into the setup, then states a frequency-based Born-rule argument. Neither should be presented as an assumption-free derivation of quantum mechanics from communication.

The 2010 data-definition preprint gives an appropriate question about complete interface costs, but its strong denial of broad quantum speedups relies on particular table and oracle assumptions. A linear search cost for an arbitrary table is not the cost of every decoding scheme; a predicate can be efficiently computed without supplying its satisfying input. The 1989 simulation conclusion also needs computable transition probabilities, beyond the existence of a stochastic description. These limits should accompany any reuse of their computational claims.

The 2025 classicality preprint extends the authors' argument that a protocol cannot unconditionally certify the physical classical resources it assumes. This is different from model-relative separability tests with supplied dimensionality, measurements, and preparation assumptions. The text sometimes uses product-state equations for separability; general separable mixed states must be distinguished from products. The live MAgent construction's normalized classical kernels are declared premises, so its conditional identities do not purport to answer this unrestricted self-certification problem.

The 2026 amplituhedra preprint is the strongest new geometric extension. It combines TQNN computation, Reshetikhin–Turaev/Turaev–Viro structures, and a proposed amplituhedron correspondence. Its universal-computation representation and its uniqueness claim for amplituhedra are different assertions. The latter is stated with gravity and anomalies excluded and is qualified by purity, isolation, and coarse-graining. No equivalence to the project's classical Fisher geometry, GL(K) connections, or normalized ELBO is supplied. First author is Chris Fields; the corrected source slug is `fields-2026-amplituhedra-generic-quantum-processes`. The initial arXiv submission was 2025, but the read version is v3 from July 2026.
