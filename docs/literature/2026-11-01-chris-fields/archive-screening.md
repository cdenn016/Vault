# Chris Fields archive coverage screening

Screening date: November 1, 2026. This supplement checks coverage outside the selected publications on the author's homepage. It is a bibliographic and access assessment, not a verification of the papers' mathematical claims. The machine-readable companion, `archive-candidates.json`, contains 14 candidates: ten arXiv records and four earlier publications. Eleven records have an identified open PDF location; three have no confirmed open full copy.

The scope is physics as communication, quantum reference frames, the Free Energy Principle, memory and time, observer boundaries, and hierarchy of agents. Nuclear spectroscopy, genomics, bibliometrics, art, and general AI history are excluded. A title or abstract establishes a reason to read a source, not a correspondence to the local gauge/VFE construction.

## Sources and coverage

The local `author-cv.txt` was screened through its pre-2010 publication entries. Its source is the [author's CV/publication list](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/caf-pubs.pdf). The homepage snapshot and `author-catalog.json` supplied the modern comparison baseline. The [registered arXiv author feed](https://arxiv.org/a/fields_c_1.atom) returned 34 entries, while the [arXiv author-name API query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=au%3A%22Fields_Chris%22&start=0&max_results=100) returned 44 entries. These are different collections: the 44 count refers to the author-name search, not the registered author feed. The broader query includes coauthored papers omitted from the registered feed.

Ten arXiv candidates absent from the homepage comparison were retained. This does not establish that every candidate is a distinct work: an early preprint can be a precursor or alternate version of an already listed journal paper. The two nuclear-physics records, arXiv:0909.1753 and arXiv:1001.3897, remain outside this collection's scope. The searches support a bounded coverage supplement, not a claim that all of Fields's work has been found.

## Earlier publications and access

[Consequences of nonclassical measurement for the algorithmic description of continuous dynamical systems](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19890011269) (1989; extra-11) is the strongest direct historical addition. The [NASA NTRS primary record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890011269) names Fields, marks distribution `PUBLIC`, and exposes an [open PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890011269/downloads/19890011269.pdf). Publisher-deposited [Crossref metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/09528138908953699) identifies the journal DOI as 10.1080/09528138908953699. The repository copy is a report/preprint and should not be assumed to share the journal pagination. It addresses physical measurement constraints on the observable computational behavior of continuous systems.

[Observables, measurements, and virtual machines](https://doi.org/10.1080/09528139508953811) (1995; extra-12) appears in the [publisher's volume 7, issue 3 contents](https://www.tandfonline.com/toc/teta20/7/3), pp. 271-274. Publisher-deposited [Crossref metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/09528139508953811) gives July 1995 and the DOI. The publisher provides a first-page preview/PDF link, but direct access was blocked and an open full copy was not confirmed. The 2010 online date reflects later online availability and should not replace the original year.

[Measurement and Computational Description](https://academic.oup.com/book/50139/chapter-abstract/422029639) (1996; extra-13) is chapter 9 of *Machines and Thought: The Legacy of Alan Turing Volume 1*, edited by P. J. R. Millican and A. Clark, pp. 165-177, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198235934.003.0010. Oxford dates publication to November 28, 1996 and shows “Get access.” Its abstract explicitly places computational description on a physical-measurement basis. Search hits mentioning a Turing 1990 internal report were bibliographic references in other authors' papers; they were not treated as full copies of Fields's chapter.

[Why do we talk to ourselves?](https://doi.org/10.1080/09528130110112303) (2002; extra-14) is retained as an adjacent internal-communication and cognitive-architecture source. Publisher-deposited [Crossref metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/09528130110112303) confirms Chris Fields, October 2002, and DOI 10.1080/09528130110112303. No open full copy was confirmed. This is a secondary reading candidate rather than a direct foundation for the physics-as-communication correspondence.

Other pre-2010 CV entries concern nuclear experiments, genomic databases, general AI architectures, neural hardware, art, or travel. The older ecological psychology, goal-generation, and frame-problem papers may matter for a separate intellectual history, but title-level thematic resemblance was insufficient to put them in the present core collection. In particular, “Computational capabilities of biologically-realistic analog processing elements” (1991) is adjacent hardware history rather than a demonstrated missing source for the current QRF/FEP question. These exclusions are scope decisions, not judgments of scientific value.

## Metadata corrections to preserve

The homepage citation for Hartl, Pio-Lopez, Fields, and Levin's 2026 *Remapping and navigation of an embedding space via error minimization* gives DOI 10.1016/j.plrev.2026.06.011. The [publisher record for that article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2026.06.009) gives **10.1016/j.plrev.2026.06.009**, *Physics of Life Reviews* 58, 336-359. The erroneous DOI belongs to Fields's separate *How did we get so smart?*, as confirmed by the [publisher-deposited metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.plrev.2026.06.011), volume 58, 164-165. An [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14096) also exists for the Hartl paper.

The homepage citation for Fields and Levin's 2026 *From complementarity to non-duality: Seeing objects and processes as pragmatic constructs* gives the older paper's DOI. The [publisher-deposited record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.plrev.2026.02.002) confirms **10.1016/j.plrev.2026.02.002**, *Physics of Life Reviews* 57, 1-3; the [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41795389/) independently agrees. DOI 10.1016/j.plrev.2025.01.008 belongs to *Thoughts and thinkers: On the complementarity between objects and processes*, which is a distinct article already present in the author catalog.

[Distributed Information and Computation in Generic Quantum Systems](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-97263-8), by Fields and Glazebrook, is highly relevant: its contents include generic quantum systems, distributed information flow, hierarchical Bayesian inference, the classical and quantum FEP, multi-agent communication, and self-organization. The Springer page identifies subscription content, with only front matter explicitly downloadable in the displayed contents. It lists the ebook release as October 17, 2025, the hardcover release as October 18, 2025, and copyright as 2026. Preserve both release and copyright dates instead of treating this as a contradiction. No lawful open full-book copy was confirmed in this screening, and no chapter download was mistaken for the full book.
