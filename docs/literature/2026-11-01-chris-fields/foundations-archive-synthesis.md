Each note has a local PDF, a full citation and BibTeX entry, an explicit version description, and a record of the pages read. ## Concrete agents and memory

The closest formal precursor in this batch is [[fields-2018-conscious-agent-networks-formal-analysis-and-application-to-cognition]]. Its conscious-agent definition uses measurable spaces, normalized perception/decision/action Markov kernels, and an update counter. The paper supplies finite circuits for memory, categorization, attention, and planning. Its memory architecture uses specified copying and counting operations; the working homogeneous kernels do not themselves learn. Merely taking products of agent spaces does not produce new behavior. The conclusion leaves a general combination theory, relative component clocks, development, and practical testing open. This source belongs early in a reading route on normalized-kernel agents, but its conscious-realism interpretation is separate from the stochastic mathematics. Its supplied compositions do not establish endogenous parent selection, and its FEP language does not identify distinct global, per-agent, and reciprocal objectives.

## Observation, identification, and records

[[fields-2018-sciences-of-observation]] and [[fields-2016-building-observer-into-system]] make system identification a physical task: reference tests identify the intended system while pointer tests report changing values. Finite input-output histories underdetermine unrestricted hidden mechanisms. This supports distinguishing a useful operational model from a claim that the model uniquely identifies its physical realization. It does not prohibit mathematical models with explicitly stipulated domains, preparation laws, or trusted observational protocols.

[[fields-2018-mind-is-an-abstraction]] and [[fields-2018-first-person-science-is-third-person-and-vice-versa]] emphasize that a remembered event is represented by a present experience, and that confidence is not an independent certificate of that event. Shared standards, public records, and other observers enter as operational commitments. [[dietrich-2015-science-generates-limit-paradoxes]] develops a stronger philosophical argument that generalizing such limits can undermine the premises used to establish them. Its dialetheic conclusion depends on those universalizations and on its treatment of practical commitments as knowledge; it is not an empirical result showing all scientific theories contain true contradictions. These sources motivate explicit record-generation and comparison protocols, while supplying no normalized record kernel or Fisher-clock calibration themselves.

## The trajectory-to-history sequence

The historical cognitive sequence begins with [[fields-2011-trajectory-recognition-as-the-basis-for-object-individuation-a-functional-model-of-object-file-insta]]. It proposes that a visual object file is anchored by a recognized trajectory, with location and surface features subsequently bound to it. A hierarchy of specific trajectory-recognition networks is a proposed mechanism, and the suppression of detailed motion information during longer-term categorization is part of that proposal. This is not an experimentally established general architecture.

[[fields-2012-the-very-same-thing-extending-the-object-token-concept-to-incorporate-causal-constraints-on-individu]] extends the account across separate episodes. Its BIC-FCH model links an object token and episodic context to the current encounter through a constructed fictive causal history. The distinction between recorded observations and inferred intervening events is useful for the present research. The paper explicitly calls for controlled tests and leaves details of category updating open. [[fields-2013-the-principle-of-persistence-leibniz-s-law-and-the-computational-task-of-object-re-identification]] then argues that persistence is an effect needing implementation, rather than an explanation supplied by naming a principle. Its discussion of intractability primarily concerns missing information about unobserved intervals; it is not a formal computational-complexity reduction.

[[fields-2013-how-humans-solve-the-frame-problem]] argues that, if this causal-history model is correct, object re-identification and the frame problem coincide for the human architecture. The scope is conditional and architectural. [[fields-2014-motion-identity-and-the-bias-toward-agency]] proposes that agency attribution persists because agent-based histories are initially available and mechanism-based histories require additional analogical construction. Its experimental designs and developmental extensions are proposed tests and hypotheses. The clinical extrapolations in the 2011, 2012, and 2014 papers are not established diagnostic mechanisms.

[[fields-2017-object-permanence]] and [[fields-2016-visual-re-identification-of-individual-objects-a-core-problem-for-organisms-and-ai]] review the broader problem, distinguishing short-term tracking from long-gap identity judgments and stressing the roles of causal expectations, prior structure, and epistemic feelings. The object-permanence entry explicitly leaves the mechanistic relationship between persistence and temporal experience unresolved. None of these sources supplies a quantitative information clock or proves a universal slowdown of parent clocks. They are useful for specifying the identities and histories a model retains, without requiring a statistical-agent theory to reproduce human perception.

## Semantic interfaces and stronger ontological claims

[[fields-2015-reverse-engineering-the-world-a-commentary-on-hoffman-singh-and-prakash-the-interface-theory-of-perc]] uses virtual machines to explain how concealing physical detail can improve useful functionality. Its proposed hierarchy of organismal abstractions is a hypothesis. [[fields-2013-on-recognizing-an-object-with-a-partition]] distinguishes the practical usefulness of coarse partitions from complete objective reference. Its argument requires an unrestricted space of possible hidden alternatives; it does not rule out identification relative to an explicit model class or tolerance.

[[fields-2013-a-whole-box-of-pandoras-systems-boundaries-and-free-will-in-quantum-theory]] proposes a universe-wide measurement interpretation in which systems and observers are semantic virtual machines. Its Born-rule discussion inherits that rule from the universal quantum description. The physical origin of semantic maps and classical memories remains open. Its semantic-freedom argument concerns limits on prediction from local records and should not be presented as a demonstrated ability to control outcomes, or as a theorem excluding all probabilistic prediction.

A concrete mathematical qualification is needed for [[fields-2014-a-physics-based-metaphysics-is-a-metaphysics-based-metaphysics]]. PDF pp. 13-14 treat entanglement as making subsystem Hilbert spaces undefined. Entangled states on a chosen tensor product still admit reduced density operators on its factors. Failure of a pure product-state description does not eliminate the specified subsystem structure. The useful methodological request to expose metaphysical assumptions can be retained without adopting the stronger claim that entanglement abolishes parts.

[[fields-2014-this-boundary-less-world]] develops a popular philosophical account from decompositional invariance through perceptual identity to panpsychism. Mathematical invariance under regrouping does not alone remove dynamically useful structure, and finite observational ambiguity does not prove that no persistent object exists. [[fields-2016-unitary-quantum-theory-as-a-formal-framework-for-a-theory-of-consciousness]] likewise proposes an interpretation connecting awareness and quantum dynamics, while calling for experimental work. Neither source establishes a physical correspondence from classical gauge/VFE laws to phenomenal awareness or entanglement.

[[fields-2017-eigenforms-interfaces-and-holographic-encoding-toward-an-evolutionary-account-of-objects-and-spaceti]] compares stable action-perception loops, conscious-agent networks, Markov blankets, and quantum interfaces. Its coding illustrations motivate a proposed account of experienced spacetime; its dimensionality suggestion and generalized holographic language do not derive physical spacetime or a gravitational area law. Its conclusion leaves discriminating predictions to future work.

## Version boundaries

The conscious-agent network PDF is a 2017 author manuscript associated with a 2018 journal article. The frame-problem PDF is marked in press in August 2012, and the Pandoras PDF is dated October 2012, while both journal articles appeared in 2013. The 2011 trajectory paper uses the repaired official Frontiers PDF. The 2017 eigenforms PDF contains the main article, separate commentaries, and an author response; its note summarizes the primary article on PDF pp. 1-10, not the whole package. The two 2018 Constructivist Foundations commentaries were read only within the Fields contribution, excluding adjacent authors' text. The individual reading records retain these distinctions.
