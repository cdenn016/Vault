\chapter{A Conditional Determinant Geometry}
\label{app:determinant}

The collective construction of \cref{geo:collective} does not require a Lorentzian target. This appendix gives one additional choice for a complex rank-two representation. Its indefinite form is supplied by a determinant; the Fisher metric used to choose the connection remains positive.

Let $W\to\mathcal U$ be a complex rank-two vector bundle with structure group $\GL(2,\mathbb C)$. In a frame, identify the real rank-four bundle $\operatorname{Herm}(W)$ with Hermitian matrices transforming by $H'=gHg^\dagger$. Suppose the collective target laws $p_i$ on $W$ have finite second moments and that their moment maps are smooth on the admitted regular families. With the same declared positive weights as in the collective metric, set
\begin{equation}
M=\mathcal M(\boldsymbol p)=\sum_iw_i\int zz^\dagger p_i(dz),
\qquad
e(X)=d\mathcal M_{\boldsymbol p}r_X.
\label{geo:det-moment}
\end{equation}
Equivariance of the moment map gives
\begin{equation}
e=dM+A^F M+M(A^F)^\dagger,
\qquad e'=g e g^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The first expression is independent of the stabilizer-valued representative of $A^F$ by \cref{geo:aggregate}.

\propositionheading{Determinant metric}{geo:determinant-metric}
Assume $\mathcal U$ is four dimensional and the actual one-form $e:T\mathcal U\to\operatorname{Herm}(W)$ is a bundle isomorphism. Supply a smooth positive determinant-scale section $\kappa_{\det}$ with local transformation
$\kappa_{\det}'=|\det g|^2\kappa_{\det}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\det}(H,K)
=\tfrac12[\Tr(HK)-\Tr(H)\Tr(K)],
\qquad
\gamma(X,Y)=\kappa_{\det}^{-1}\eta_{\det}(eX,eY).
\label{geo:determinant-form}
\end{equation}
defines a gauge-invariant metric with one negative and three positive directions. The positive-definite Hermitian cone determines a time orientation through $e$.
\begin{proof}
For a Hermitian matrix
$H=\begin{pmatrix}t+z&x-iy\\x+iy&t-z\end{pmatrix}$,
direct evaluation gives
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\det}(H,H)=-\det H=-t^2+x^2+y^2+z^2.
\end{equation}
Multiplicativity of the determinant under congruence gives
$-\det(gHg^\dagger)=|\det g|^2(-\det H)$. Polarizing proves the same scaling law for $\eta_{\det}(H,K)$. The compensator cancels that factor in \cref{geo:determinant-form}, and the isomorphism $e$ preserves its inertia. Positive Hermitian matrices have $t>0$ and $t^2>x^2+y^2+z^2$, so they form one timelike cone, preserved by congruence.
\end{proof}

The positive determinant scale has a canonical choice built from the same data. Where $M$ is positive definite, $\det(gMg^\dagger)=|\det g|^2\det M$, so $\kappa_{\det}=\det M$ obeys the required transformation law. For $2\times2$ matrices $\Tr(B^2)=(\Tr B)^2-2\det B$; applying this to $B=M^{-1}H$ and polarizing gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\eta_{\det}(H,K)}{\det M}
=\tfrac12\bigl[\Tr(M^{-1}HM^{-1}K)-\Tr(M^{-1}H)\Tr(M^{-1}K)\bigr].
\end{equation}
With this scale,
\begin{equation}
\gamma=\tfrac12\bigl(h^{\mathcal{CN}}-\vartheta\otimes\vartheta\bigr),
\qquad
h^{\mathcal{CN}}(X,Y)=\Tr\bigl(M^{-1}e(X)M^{-1}e(Y)\bigr),
\qquad
\vartheta(X)=\Tr\bigl(M^{-1}e(X)\bigr).
\label{geo:det-canonical}
\end{equation}
Here $h^{\mathcal{CN}}$ is the Fisher metric of the proper complex Gaussian law with covariance $M$, pulled back by $e$, and $\vartheta=d\log\det M+2\operatorname{Re}\Tr A^F$ is the covariant derivative of its entropy $\log\det(\pi eM)$. The indefinite signature is a rank-one subtraction from a Fisher-type metric: a direction is timelike exactly when the squared entropy rate exceeds the squared Fisher speed, and the dilation $e=cM$ has $\gamma=-c^2$. The form is invariant under constant rescaling of $M$, so any physical scale remains a calibration. For non-Gaussian targets $h^{\mathcal{CN}}$ is the Fisher metric of the moment projection, not of the actual laws. The scale also changes the geometry. For an exact soldering $e=dM$ with constant $\kappa_{\det}$, $\gamma$ is the pullback of a flat Minkowski form. With $\kappa_{\det}=\det M$ it is the pullback of the $\GL(2,\mathbb C)$-invariant form $-\det(dM)/\det M$ on the positive Hermitian cone, which is a Lorentzian product of a line with hyperbolic three-space and has scalar curvature $-6$.

The rank hypothesis is imposed on the actual contextual first jet. It constrains the number and configuration of the target laws.

\propositionheading{Rank of the aggregate jet for proper complex Gaussian targets}{geo:det-rank}
Let $p_i$ be centered proper complex Gaussian laws with positive-definite covariances $M_i$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, with the collective metric $\sum_iw_i\Tr(M_i^{-1}h_iM_i^{-1}k_i)$ and the action $M\mapsto gMg^\dagger$. Then
\begin{equation}
H=\Bigl\{(h_i)_i:\ \sum_iw_iM_i^{-1}h_i=0\Bigr\},
\qquad
d\mathcal M(h)=\sum_iw_ih_i.
\label{geo:det-horizontal}
\end{equation}
For $n=1$, $H=0$. For $n=3$ at triples whose orbit has the maximal dimension seven, the rank of $d\mathcal M|_H$ is at most three, so $e$ cannot be an isomorphism on a four-dimensional base there. For $n=4$, at $M_1=I$, $M_2=2I$, $M_3=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)$, $M_4=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ with unit weights, the orbit has dimension seven and $d\mathcal M|_H$ is surjective.
\begin{proof}
An orbit direction is $(\xi M_i+M_i\xi^\dagger)_i$ for $\xi\in\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb C)$. By cyclicity of the trace its pairing with $(h_i)_i$ is $2\operatorname{Re}\Tr(B\xi)$ with $B=\sum_iw_ih_iM_i^{-1}$. This vanishes for all $\xi$ exactly when $B=0$, equivalently when $B^\dagger=\sum_iw_iM_i^{-1}h_i=0$; this proves \cref{geo:det-horizontal}. For $n=1$ it forces $h_1=0$. Put $k_i=w_ih_i$. For $n=3$, $\dim H=12-7=5$, and the rank of $d\mathcal M|_H$ is $5$ minus the dimension of $\{k:\sum_ik_i=0,\ \sum_iM_i^{-1}k_i=0\}$. Eliminating $k_3$ leaves $\mathsf Ak_1+\mathsf Bk_2=0$ with $\mathsf A=M_1^{-1}-M_3^{-1}$ and $\mathsf B=M_2^{-1}-M_3^{-1}$. When $\mathsf A,\mathsf B$ are invertible, $(k_1,k_2)=(\mathsf A^{-1},-\mathsf B^{-1})$ and $(\mathsf A^{-1}\mathsf B\mathsf A^{-1},-\mathsf A^{-1})$ are Hermitian solutions, independent unless $\mathsf B$ is a multiple of $\mathsf A$. So the rank is at most three on the complement of a proper real-algebraic subset, which is dense in the open set of triples with seven-dimensional orbits. The rank of a smoothly varying linear map is lower semicontinuous, so the set where it is at least four is open; being disjoint from a dense set, it is empty. For the four-agent tuple, a unitary $g$ fixing $\operatorname{diag}(1,2)$ is diagonal, and a diagonal unitary fixing $M_4$ is scalar, so the stabilizer is the central circle and the orbit has dimension seven. Given a Hermitian $S$, the choice $k_1=-S$, $k_2=2S$, $k_3=k_4=0$ satisfies $\sum_iM_i^{-1}k_i=0$ and $\sum_ik_i=S$.
\end{proof}
For $n\le3$ centered proper complex Gaussian targets, no section over an open four-dimensional base has an aggregate jet $e$ that is a continuous bundle isomorphism. The stabilizer of a tuple is conjugate to the unitaries commuting with every $M_1^{-1/2}M_jM_1^{-1/2}$, so the orbit dimension is seven, six, or four according as these Hermitian matrices fail to commute, commute without all being scalar, or are all scalar. The orbit dimension along a section is lower semicontinuous, hence locally constant on an open dense set, and on each component of that set the section stays in one orbit-type stratum. On the maximal stratum the proposition gives rank $e\le3$. On the jointly diagonalizable stratum, in a frame with $M_1=I$ and every $M_j$ diagonal, the non-orbit tangent directions are diagonal tuples, and their horizontal projections are diagonal because diagonal and off-diagonal Hermitian matrices are orthogonal for the collective metric at diagonal base points; so $e$ takes diagonal values and has rank at most two. On the proportional stratum it takes values in the multiples of $M$ and has rank at most one, and for $n=1$, $H=0$. Thus $\det e=0$ on a dense open set and, by continuity, everywhere. At an isolated tuple of smaller orbit dimension $d\mathcal M|_H$ can be onto, for example for two agents with covariances $M$ and $2M$, whose orbit and horizontal space are four dimensional; but a section realizing a full-rank first jet there leaves the stratum, and its $e$ is discontinuous at that point. Four agents are the minimum in this family: at the four-agent tuple of the proposition a section whose residuals span horizontal preimages of a basis gives an invertible $e$ at that point, and hence on a neighborhood. For improper complex laws the central phase acts nontrivially on the pseudo-covariance $\E[zz^T]$, which transforms as $gPg^T$, so the normal equation can then determine the phase component of the connection; we do not develop that case. Surjectivity of the horizontal differential of $\mathcal M$ establishes the availability of such a jet at a law tuple, but does not select a section or prove persistence under inference. If the base has dimension greater than four and $e$ is surjective, the same formula gives a nondegenerate form on the quotient vector bundle by $\ker e$. A quotient-manifold interpretation still requires a regular leaf quotient and basicness, as in \cref{geo:quotient}.

\propositionheading{Circle completion}{geo:circle-completion}
Let $\mathscr Q_{\det}\to\mathcal U$ be the unit circle bundle of $\det W$ for the line metric whose local coefficient is $\kappa_{\det}^{-1}$. Write $\pi_{\det}$ for its projection. Supply a real principal circle connection $\omega_{\det}$ normalized to one on the vertical generator of period $2\pi$, and a smooth positive radius $r_{\det}:\mathcal U\to(0,\infty)$. Then
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{KK}}
=\pi_{\det}^*\gamma
+(r_{\det}\circ\pi_{\det})^2\omega_{\det}\otimes\omega_{\det}
\label{geo:kk-metric}
\end{equation}
is circle invariant and has one negative and four positive directions.
\begin{proof}
The connection splits each total tangent space into its horizontal subspace and the one-dimensional vertical subspace. On the first, \cref{geo:kk-metric} is the pullback of $\gamma$; on the second, it is positive because $r_{\det}>0$. The two subspaces are orthogonal. The base pullback, radius, and principal connection are invariant under the circle action, proving invariance of the total metric.
\end{proof}

A supplied global $\GL(2,\mathbb C)$ connection $A$ gives the local expression
$\omega_{\det}=d\theta_{\det}+\operatorname{Im}\Tr A$ on this circle bundle. The coordinate transformation $\theta_{\det}'=\theta_{\det}+\arg\det g$ cancels
$\operatorname{Im}\Tr A'=\operatorname{Im}\Tr A-d\arg\det g$.
When the collective minimization determines only a class, its lift to a full connection is further data. For instance, central phase rotations act trivially on centered proper complex Gaussian laws and their covariance matrices, so that family cannot fix the corresponding phase connection from its Fisher normal equations. Neither the positive circle radius nor equations of motion for $\gamma$ follow from the construction. The determinant and circle choices give this conditional geometric example without identifying it with physical spacetime dynamics.
