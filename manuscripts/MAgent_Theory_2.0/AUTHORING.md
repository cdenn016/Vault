# MAgent Theory 2.0 authoring conventions

The main entry point is `MAgent_Theory_2.0.tex`. The separate proof supplement is `MAgent_Theory_2.0_supplement.tex`. The explanatory companion is `MAgent_Theory_2.0_companion.tex`, with chapters and editable vector figures under `companion/`. All three compile within this directory and use its local style and bibliography. The main manuscript must be readable without the supplement. The companion is self-contained and explains the constructions through physical pictures, worked examples, and short derivations.

The subject is a gauge-covariant theory of agents carrying general probability laws, internal records, separate belief and model interactions, and inherited recursive organization. Exact constructions, identities, and counterexamples take priority over approximation estimates. Do not add a renormalization or error-bound program by default. Elementary variational inequalities remain permissible when they are part of an exact identity or optimization result.

Write concise, connected American English prose. Define every space and map before using it. Give the hypotheses inside each result and provide human-readable proofs. Important constructions and their decisive proofs belong in the main text or its appendices. Secondary algebra and supporting proofs belong in the separate supplement. Avoid duplicated proofs, repeated disclaimers, proof outlines presented as proofs, status badges, and end-of-chapter summaries that merely repeat the chapter.

Use semantic numbered headings supplied by `preamble.tex`, and refer to labels through `\cref` or `\Cref`. Punctuate display equations as parts of sentences. Do not use `\;`, `\,`, or `\!`, bullet lists in mathematical prose, decorative horizontal rules, or formulaic promotional language. Tables may collect notation. Statements should distinguish definitions, constitutive choices, proved consequences under stated assumptions, and open problems.

No document may cite or link another unpublished manuscript by the author. All required definitions, constructions, and proofs must appear locally. Cite only external literature. Do not include repository paths, implementation modules, workflow history, review records, or tool names in the scientific text. Build instructions and source-work records remain outside that text.

Notation uses a base `\mathcal C`, a principal bundle `P`, state and model spaces `\mathsf K,\mathsf M`, joint agent law `\zeta_i(dk,dm)=q_i(dk\mid m)s_i(dm)`, and marginals `q_i,s_i`. The product restriction is `\zeta_i=q_i\otimes s_i`. A model evaluator is a normalized kernel, not the law `s_i` alone. Use sector superscripts `q,s` for links, generators, reference laws, frames, and interface maps. Use `\beta` and `\gamma` for their respective source rows. A record readout `\rho_i:\mathsf S_i\to\mathsf O_i` is distinct from its random value and from the kernel that produces it. The complete configuration is `x\in\mathsf S`; reserve sample coordinates `k,m` for represented possibilities.

Default interaction graphs are directed. Opposite attending edges need not be inverses. Geometric reversal of a particular transport is its inverse. Primitive identity self-comparisons and inherited coarse loop information must be distinguished. Finite-dimensional matrices and finite alphabets are examples, not restrictions of the measure-theoretic definitions. A smooth statistical family is an additional hypothesis wherever derivatives are taken.

Inherited parent dynamics and boundary responses are the main formation obligations. Static marginalization, posterior approximation, and dynamical inheritance are different claims. Do not claim endogenous formation, recurrent formation, derived base geometry, or emergent timescale separation from a definition or a supplied example. Keep the outstanding construction stated precisely.

The companion follows the same notation and scientific scope. Explain the mechanism behind an equation, what its variables represent, and the assumptions that change its interpretation. Do not reproduce the older companion's external manuscript links, status badges, audit scorecards, or renormalization program. A supplied hierarchy is an inheritance example. The separate finite-time fusion construction uses a nonsmooth square-root-KL interaction on a restricted lognormal family; do not transfer its conclusions to the original smooth KL dynamics or to internally heterogeneous parents.

No numerical experiments, GPU work, or Lean are part of these authoring tasks. Build PDFs sequentially and inspect their rendering. Preserve unrelated Research checkout changes.

The smooth conditional-spectrum construction uses fixed independent private reference factors and receiver conditional reconstruction channels. It is an exact general-law state completion, not a finite-parameter closure or a theorem for every channel. Its marked version preserves only the declared feedback protocols. The smooth erasure example uses an engineered flat channel gate and a finite product preparation. It selects changing source-preference groups and discards private laws, but its empirical parent keeps all public child states. Do not describe this as discovered sufficient interfaces, compression of those public states, or recursive smooth formation.

The collective-phase projection uses the original Gibbs-reduced KL potential, identity channels, a fixed coupled evaluator, and full bounded log-density charts near a stable common reference. It discards continuous public differences while inheriting a virtual collective trajectory. The fine children are not set equal, but their differences decay. Ordinary fresh child sampling fails to factor through the phase. Do not call the projection an inherited complete interface, a selected hierarchy, persistent heterogeneous organization, or recursive formation. The Gibbs identifiability theorem applies to unrestricted identity-channel probes; it is not a universal obstruction for arbitrary channels.
