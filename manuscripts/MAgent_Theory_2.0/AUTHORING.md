# MAgent Theory 2.0 authoring conventions

The main entry point is `MAgent_Theory_2.0.tex`. The separate proof supplement is `MAgent_Theory_2.0_supplement.tex`. The explanatory companion is `MAgent_Theory_2.0_companion.tex`, with chapters and editable vector figures under `companion/`. All three compile within this directory and use its local style and bibliography. The main manuscript must be readable without the supplement. The companion is self-contained and explains the constructions through physical pictures, worked examples, and short derivations. Chapters are named here by their files under `chapters/`; the printed chapter numbers of `02_geometry.tex` to `19_curvature_hierarchy.tex` are one higher than their file numbers.

The subject is a gauge-covariant theory of agents that hold beliefs and generative models, carry general probability laws and internal records, interact through separate belief and model channels, and may form inherited recursive organization. Exact constructions, identities, and counterexamples take priority over approximation estimates. Do not add a renormalization or error-bound program by default. Elementary variational inequalities remain permissible when they are part of an exact identity or optimization result.

The conditional-prediction section and `appendices/conditional_memory.tex` start from normalized kernels on general standard Borel spaces. Keep correlated recognition distinct from the product-family population dynamics. Component labels are model presentations, not peer-source rows or independent agents. The Gaussian response is a partial derivative at fixed bank and record covariance. Its signed cross-covariance is distinct from the positive posterior Fisher tensor, which bounds it (`cmem:response-bound`). Moment matching minimizes the inclusive divergence, loses joint dependence, and need not preserve later Gaussian record laws. Shared bank parameters do not by themselves match variable record kernels. The carried-label filter is exact only for its declared fresh-emission history; belief reuse requires its actual conditional record law. No full-stack free energy, local learning rule, or new inheritance or formation theorem follows. Keep implementation settings and empirical claims outside this construction.

The interacting-memory appendix and companion use a supplied common preparation, cross-agent coupling, private initial records, known posterior-sampling encoders, and a shared transcript. A fresh posterior sample is conditionally independent of the represented result given the speaker's information; it is not a direct measurement of that result. Keep the unknown peer record in the receiver likelihood. The exact changing state is the joint private-record posterior with a fixed preparation reconstruction, own private value, and schedule. On the reachable family that posterior is a product tilt of the fixed coupling (`imem:product-tilt`): each message multiplies its speaker's factor, the cross-agent dependence is that of the preparation, and with the preparation fixed the two private-record marginals determine it. The product of the two marginals alone does not. Persistent records remain when their queries are admitted. This is sufficient retention on the stated family, not minimality or lossy parent compression. The affine singleton-presentation Gaussian closure is special, and the finite-mixture counterexample excludes only that closure family; with several presentations even the first posterior is not a finite Gaussian mixture. The parity counterexample deletes a registered cross-agent distinction even while retaining complete local beliefs. Historical policy values are fixed when varying the free energy. Communication redistributes the preparation's evidence; new measurements, learning, differing agent models, reciprocal dynamics, and formation require separate constructions.

## Writing and structure

Write concise, connected American English prose. Define every space and map before using it. Give the hypotheses inside each result and provide human-readable proofs. Important constructions and their decisive proofs belong in the main text or its appendices. Secondary algebra and supporting proofs belong in the separate supplement. Avoid duplicated proofs, repeated disclaimers, proof outlines presented as proofs, status badges, and end-of-chapter summaries that merely repeat the chapter.

Use semantic numbered headings supplied by `preamble.tex`, and refer to labels through `\cref` or `\Cref`. Punctuate display equations as parts of sentences. Do not use `\;`, `\,`, or `\!`, bullet lists in mathematical prose, decorative horizontal rules, or formulaic promotional language. Tables may collect notation. Statements should distinguish definitions, constitutive choices, proved consequences under stated assumptions, and open problems.

No document may cite or link another unpublished manuscript by the author. All required definitions, constructions, and proofs must appear locally. Cite only external literature. When a result is an instance of named classical mathematics (lumpability and Markov functions, sufficiency and Blackwell comparison, Cencov monotonicity, Shahshahani and replicator Fisher dynamics, the mechanical connection and O'Neill quotient metrics, symmetric criticality and fixed-point subspaces, asymptotic phase, centroid and linkage clustering, anchored opinion pooling, agreement theorems, the method of slices and freezing), say so and cite the primary source; state what is new relative to it. Do not include repository paths, implementation modules, workflow history, review records, or tool names in the scientific text. Build instructions and source-work records remain outside that text.

## Notation

Notation uses a base `\mathcal C`, a principal bundle `P`, state and model spaces `\mathsf K,\mathsf M`, joint agent law `\zeta_i(dk,dm)=q_i(dk\mid m)s_i(dm)`, and marginals `q_i,s_i`. The product restriction is `\zeta_i=q_i\otimes s_i`. Use sector superscripts `q,s` for links, generators, reference laws, frames, and interface maps. Use `\beta` and `\gamma` for their respective source rows. A record readout `\rho_i:\mathsf S_i\to\mathsf O_i` is distinct from its random value and from the kernel that produces it. The complete configuration is `x\in\mathsf S`; reserve sample coordinates `k,m` for represented possibilities. Call `r_i` the presentation prior; reserve "reference law" for the laws `\bar q_i,\bar s_i` in reference coordinates. A passive change is a tuple `\mathbf g=(g_i)_i`.

One structure group `G` acts on both sectors through two representations, `\varrho_q` on the belief space `V_q` (dimension `K`) and `\varrho_s` on the model space `V_s` (dimension `\tilde K`). There is one principal bundle `P` and two associated content bundles `\mathcal E_q,\mathcal E_s`; the statistical bundle carries laws over their fiber product with the diagonal action. A gauge transformation of an agent is one element `g_i\in G` acting on both sectors; the product group `G_q\times G_s` is not used. Bundle morphisms `\Phi,\tilde\Phi` of the two content bundles are induced by one principal bundle map through intertwining maps. Group-valued links are `\theta_{ij},\tilde\theta_{ij}\in G` with generators `\omega_{ij},\tilde\omega_{ij}\in\mathfrak g`, acting as `\Theta^q_{ij}=\varrho_q(\theta_{ij})` and `\Theta^s_{ij}=\varrho_s(\tilde\theta_{ij})`. The general theory keeps both link fields; the main focus is the tied case `\theta_{ij}=\tilde\theta_{ij}`, which results must state as a hypothesis. The sector mismatch `\Xi_{ij}=\tilde\theta_{ij}\theta_{ij}^{-1}` is covariant under conjugation at the receiver. Tied reciprocal parts are computed in `G` and then represented; a per-sector principal root need not respect the representations.

Default interaction graphs are directed. Opposite attending edges need not be inverses. Geometric reversal of a particular transport is its inverse. A self channel is constitutive data: identity by default, nonidentity where a construction declares it (as in the Wishart and open-law constructions). Primitive self-comparisons and inherited coarse loop information must be distinguished. Finite-dimensional matrices and finite alphabets are examples, not restrictions of the measure-theoretic definitions. A smooth statistical family is an additional hypothesis wherever derivatives are taken.

## General laws and Gaussian simplifications

The theory is stated for general probability laws on standard Borel spaces, and its definitions, objectives, and constructions must not depend on Gaussian structure. Gaussian laws, linear-Gaussian records and codes, and linearizations at a tangent point are computational simplifications: they make examples explicit, give closed forms, and keep numerical checks cheap. A result proved only in such a family states the family in its hypotheses, says which conclusions are expected to hold for general laws and which are features of the family, and poses the general-law version as an open problem when it is not proved. Features of the Gaussian family include posterior covariances that do not depend on the data, mean-field means that equal exact posterior means, closed-form Schur complements and log-normalizers, finite-dimensional sufficient statistics, and the transitive action of affine groups on Gaussian laws; none of them may be presented as a property of the theory. Where a general-law statement is available, such as a chain-rule, data-processing, or disintegration identity, state it first and give the Gaussian case as its instance. The same rule applies to the working documents in this directory.

## Beliefs, generative models, and the three objectives

An agent holds beliefs and generative models. A model presentation `m` indexes a generative model: the evaluator `G_i(m,dk)` is its prior over represented contents and a record kernel `\ell_i(do\mid k,m)`, with a declared density and reference measure, is its likelihood. The presentation prior `r_i` is the agent's prior over presentations. The recognition law `\zeta_i`, with marginals `q_i` (belief, perception) and `s_i` (belief over generative models), approximates the posterior of that model. A model law alone predicts nothing; prediction requires the evaluator and record kernel. Codes and languages are not components of the generative model; a shared language is a separately declared hypothesis. Agents of one kind (a species) share a generative model up to frame: their reference models are related by one element of `G` acting on both sectors; a relation by an arbitrary bijection is an isomorphism of generative models. Two agents give the same predictions of every number of records from the same model law exactly when their composite record kernels agree (`prop:predictive-equivalence`); matched evaluators are not required and do not suffice by themselves.

Three objectives are studied and must never be conflated. Their standing is fixed (author decision, 2026-10-30): the global free energy is primary for formation, and formation is posed in it (`05_global_formation`); the per-agent free energies describe agent dynamics, what each agent computes with its peers' laws as data; the reciprocal potential is a configuration-level device whose results are read as global statements only through an exact identification (`thm:dyn-registered`, `thm:dyn-three-limits`) and otherwise as results about the potential itself.

1. The global picture: one normalized joint over the contents and records of all agents, with product recognition. Each agent's local free energy is the global free energy as a function of its own block, so local updates are coordinate descent of one functional. Peers enter only through expectations of their contents, linearly in their laws.
2. The per-agent picture: each agent has its own normalized generative model in which its own evaluator and the channelled laws of its peers are mixture sources for its contents (and its own reference and peers' channelled model laws are mixture sources for its presentations), with latent source labels and record likelihoods. Peers' laws are data for the agent, not variables it controls. The resulting dynamics is non-reciprocal and in general not a gradient flow; with fixed rows and channels it can still contract (`prop:dyn-per-agent-contraction`). Its Gibbs-reduced and chart versions are defined in `def:dyn-per-agent-flow`. The record-likelihood per-agent link model of `18_link_dynamics` is a different per-agent construction; name which one is used.
3. The reciprocal law potential `def:dyn-potential` (the cooperative consensus potential). Its Fisher gradient flow moves each source to reduce the costs of the receivers that attend to it. State the obstruction `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe` only at its proved scope: with fixed positive rows on finite alphabets, the potential is not an affine function of the free energy of any finite normalized model under any recognition rule when an attended edge is an identity channel, and with fixed input-dependent channels it is not, on any open set, an affine function of the free energy of a model whose recognition is the product of the agent laws. Constant-output channels, countable auxiliary latents and coupled recognition on atomless spaces give exact identifications (`rem:dyn-no-global-scope`), so never write that it is "not the free energy of any fixed normalized model" without these hypotheses. `06_fusion` to `12_open_formation` study this flow; their results are results about this device, not formation results of the primary objective. Do not call it inference without qualification, and do not transfer its conclusions to the other two pictures without proof.

Records enter both free-energy pictures as likelihood terms and persist in the objective. In the reciprocal potential they act only as declared assimilation events; state that composition explicitly where it is used. Under Gaussian references, affine evaluators, linear-Gaussian channels and log-quadratic records, the global and per-agent flows are tangent to the Gaussian product family, while the reciprocal flow is not; say which applies. On the linear-Gaussian mean slice the three objectives are limits of one normalized model (`thm:dyn-three-limits`): the reciprocal stationary means are the posterior means of a global model with comparison records of precision `\beta_{ij}+\beta_{ji}`, whose back-action on the compared agent is the source-role force; the per-agent stationary means are posterior means of a global model exactly when the rows are reversible. Cite it at that scope only; off the slice (free covariances, non-Gaussian laws, law-dependent channels) relating the pictures is open (`op:dyn-three-pictures`). A construction that treats one objective must not be summarized as a result about another.

## Formation standard

`inh:recursive-formation` is the formation standard. A construction is a recursive formation only if it satisfies every clause, measured with the objects that `05_inheritance` fixes before any flow is analyzed. (F1) the admitted class is open in the chart topology of the declared dynamics (full grade) or relatively open in a declared invariant family not contained in finitely many orbits (family grade, codimension reported). (F2) the selector comes from a declared family `S_c`, `c\in\mathsf C`, fixed in advance, with every real constant ranging over a set with nonempty interior, the readout convention of `inh:null-flow`, and a stated persistence criterion. (F3) inheritance holds for the parent protocol and child-level probe classes of `inh:protocol-classes`, which are functions of the block and the constitutive data alone. (F4) some probe of `B_I` separates two configurations of the reachable post-handoff set with the same retained state; report whether the separation persists and whether the fibers are symmetry orbits. (F5) interaction necessity holds pointwise on a nonempty open set, against both the label-preserving null (peer costs deleted, labels and priors kept, so a self source keeps the weight `inh:null-self-row`) and the renormalized null (deleted labels removed), for every constant in `\mathsf C`, with the horizon convention of the standard and partition sequences compared up to the order of merges of disjoint blocks; a split verdict fails, and the frozen-environment control is reported beside it. (F6a) the parents select a union under the inherited dynamics with the same selector family and data, and (F6b) the second stage does not form from a set with nonempty interior of the declared counterfactual class `inh:counterfactual-class`. The strong grade is (F5) at every admitted point, for every constant, against both nulls; the amplifying grade uses a declared metric and is reported as degenerate where within-block diameters vanish. A construction meeting (F1), (F2), (F3) and (F6a) and shown to fail (F4), (F5) or (F6b) is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance; one meeting (F1) to (F3) with one stage is a single-stage selection with exact inheritance; a clause neither established nor refuted leaves the classification undetermined, and the construction is then "not shown to be a recursive formation". Report every construction of `06_fusion` to `14_collective_local_parent` by the recipe at the end of the standard, naming its objective and every failing or undetermined clause. No construction in the manuscript is proved to satisfy every clause.

Inherited parent dynamics and boundary responses are the main formation obligations. Static marginalization, posterior approximation, and dynamical inheritance are different claims. Do not claim endogenous formation, recurrent formation, derived base geometry, or emergent timescale separation from a definition or a supplied example.

## Regularity and gauge

The Fisher flow is proved on finite positive tables. Any use on densities, circles, or general standard Borel spaces requires a declared chart (for example bounded log-density charts in a Banach space), stated Lipschitz hypotheses on the first variation, well-posedness, and uniqueness of every coarse flow whose autonomy is claimed. State these hypotheses in the result. Cite `prop:dyn-chart-flow` for local chart well-posedness, `prop:dyn-chart-global` for global existence with fixed identity or two-sided bounded channels, and `def:dyn-per-agent-flow` for the Gibbs-reduced and chart per-agent dynamics. Every null and control of `inh:null-flow` needs the same hypotheses as the flow it controls.

Per-agent frame covariance of the constitutive data is automatic for dressed quantities and constrains nothing by itself. Distinguish naturality of a rule (it commutes with passive re-expression) from active equivariance (it commutes with active transformations of the reference laws); the substantive symmetry requirement is reference equivariance at a context, which must be imposed explicitly where used. The bundle connection does dynamical work only where a vector field, row, channel, or record depends on it; otherwise it is a diagnostic of the evolved sections, and the text says so. Within the formation constructions the gauge structure supplies the orbits that a parent forgets and does no dynamical work. Words such as "gauge", "physical", and "public" require a declared observable algebra (`def:observable-algebra`): say which agents, channels, records, or external probes can resolve the distinction in question. A distinction that only a child-level law diagnostic resolves is gauge relative to every declared algebra; never call it physical or nongauge.

Covariant pullback geometry is a main construction on an explicitly regular statistical tier. Use the Fisher metric of the actual joint law; a marginal sum is its metric only under the appropriate product restriction. Passive gauge invariance does not remove dependence on the chosen connection. The collective normal equation determines a connection class modulo infinitesimal isotropy, with comparison maps and weights declared; it is the shape-space pullback and the mechanical connection of the declared comparison data, and it is singular where isotropy jumps. Fixed Markov channels contract Fisher information, whereas moving channels include their own derivative. Vanishing horizontal defect is sufficient, not necessary, for pullback contraction. Dynamical, response, and collective-connection inheritance are separate conditions. The determinant/Lorentz and circle construction is an optional example with extra data; positive Fisher geometry does not create indefinite signature. Do not identify sample reference coordinates with the contextual base or infer selection and persistence from an available full-rank first jet.

## Scope of the existing constructions

All constructions in `06_fusion` to `12_open_formation` concern the reciprocal law potential (or, in `06_fusion`, a nonsmooth modification of it) and must say so; they are surrogate results unless an identification with the global picture is proved for them. New formation work is posed under the global free energy first, as in `05_global_formation`, and a result is re-proved there before the reciprocal track is extended.

The finite-time fusion construction uses a nonsmooth modification of the reciprocal potential made by four constitutive changes (square-root divergences, a restored receiver, multiplicity-weighted coefficients, fixed rows) on a restricted lognormal family; its links are pure gauge and every datum synchronizes completely in finite time. On the common-offset family its coalescence order is the normalized-centroid linkage of the initial offsets for every coupling (`fus:ncl`), so the interaction sets event times and the exactness of coincidence, not which blocks form. It meets the interaction-necessity clause only through its exact-equality selector, compression fails, and the second stage forms from every counterfactual handoff: it is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance. Do not transfer its conclusions to the original smooth KL dynamics or to internally heterogeneous parents.

The smooth conditional-spectrum construction uses fixed independent private reference factors and receiver conditional reconstruction channels. It is an exact general-law state completion in which the private sector acts on the public flow as open-loop forcing, not a finite-parameter closure or a theorem for every channel; its compression is the equimeasurability quotient of each private log density, and its general-space form needs the stated regularity hypotheses. Its marked version preserves only the declared feedback protocols. It selects nothing. The smooth erasure example uses an engineered flat channel gate and a finite product preparation, and its selector is the connected components of the preference graph. Its heterogeneity is transient, its empirical parent keeps all public child states, its compression holds only through a transient label quotient of symmetry type, and its interaction necessity is undetermined (first and last partitions agree with the nulls; the intermediate ones are not determined). It is a single-stage selection. Do not describe this as discovered sufficient interfaces, compression of those public states, persistent heterogeneous organization, or recursive smooth formation.

The collective-phase projection uses the original Gibbs-reduced KL potential, identity channels, a fixed coupled evaluator, and full bounded log-density charts near a stable common reference; it is a Banach-chart instance of asymptotic phase. Ordinary fresh child sampling fails to factor through the phase. Do not call the projection an inherited complete interface, a selected hierarchy, persistent heterogeneous organization, or recursive formation. The Gibbs identifiability theorem applies to unrestricted identity-channel probes; for a fixed channel it identifies only the channelled laws. A probe class computed from the phase law is not a declared class.

The recursive boundary-closure theorem uses a supplied binary tree, public sign partitions, and arbitrary bounded positive profiles over a general base. It is an equivariant symmetry reduction: the discarded public partition data are invisible to the fine energy, metric, and admitted records. Their contrast decays near the partition-independent laws, is never lost in finite time, and decays globally on a finite base (`cor:boundary-contrast-global`). Its source sets exclude self, so the two nulls coincide; the null preserves the same family, descriptors and interface, and the exact hierarchy is a property of the prepared family and its symmetry, not of the interaction. Measured against the standard, (F1) holds in the family grade and (F3) and (F4) hold for the supplied hierarchy, while (F2), (F5) and (F6) are not addressed. Retain the nested unordered profile tree, not a flat multiset. Its class cost is a forward/reversed pair-law KL and generally differs from KL to the averaged source. Fresh uniform-descendant samples and common base-record conditioning descend; shared-child reuse and unrestricted external probes do not. Phase losses are continuous in total variation but have no Fisher tangent. Do not claim endogenous membership selection, arbitrary regrouping, a fixed-size parent, or smooth dimensional compression on an open law chart.

The Wishart construction uses common-precision matrix Wishart laws, context-constant individual shapes, a constant model-independent evaluator, product recognition, global moment-dependent normalized channels, exact Gibbs rows, and a fixed threshold membership rule with constants in a declared interval. Its membership schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the initial belief shapes and is independent of the coupling; the renormalized null and the frozen-environment control reproduce it, and the coupling-deformation control is a separate statement. Its self costs are nonzero, so the label-preserving null has agent-dependent clocks already at zero coupling (`form:lp-null`), and integrations indicate a different partition sequence on an open set; the (F5) verdict is split and fails, and the union forms from every counterfactual handoff, so (F6b) fails whatever the null. It is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance, not a recursive formation. Preserve the complete channel derivative and the primitive population count at every join. The lost model-shape coordinate is dynamically inert, and the inherited contextual geometry decays.
