\chapter*{Status and summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Status and summary}
This working document is not part of the main manuscript and is not cited by it. It asks whether parents that are created by a state-determined handoff when a wall forms, and that then act back on their members, give an inherited recursive formation in the sense of the formation standard of the main manuscript, stated in its chapter \emph{Inherited Parents and Recursive Formation}, which we call the inheritance chapter. The clauses (F1) to (F6) of that standard, its two null flows and its frozen-environment control are restated in \cref{it7:standard-restated}, so that the verdicts below can be read without the main manuscript. The constructions are stated for general probability laws. The Gaussian location family is the computational instance, and every statement that depends on it says so.

Feedback of the obvious kinds does not change what forms. A parent added as one more source in its members' rows multiplies the row part of each member's force by a common factor when the member agrees with it, leaves record forces unchanged, and alters no exponent of the rows (\cref{it7:capture}); in an example without records the same blocks form and only the second stage is delayed. In an objective with a gradient flow, such as the global joint of Items~2 and~3 or the reciprocal potential with fixed rows, a top-down cost invariant under the collective motion of a block exerts no force on the block's momentum whenever that motion has a momentum function, as it does in the Gaussian location family (\cref{it7:momentum}). An exactly inherited pooled parent therefore does not see it; the sharpening of walls reported in Item~3 passes through the distribution of that momentum among the members, which sparse bridges read. A post-handoff flow with a globally attracting equilibrium cannot meet the counterfactual clause (F6b) when persistence means that the candidate relation holds at all later times, the equilibrium lies off the boundary of the candidate set, and no competing block is selected first (\cref{it7:attractor}). The selector used here selects after a finite dwell, and for such selectors a unique attractor excludes nothing: a strictly convex Gaussian hierarchy in a constant metric can meet (F6b), because the length of a transient dwell in the candidate set depends on the handoff (\cref{it7:dwell}). In the Gaussian tangent global joint of Items~2 and~3, run in its own Fisher metric, such instances were found numerically only with declared parent anchors that pull the parents together.

What changes outcomes is the pooling of evidence. When a block forms, its members share one source label: they infer jointly which source to trust, with the prior over sources applied once and their costs added. For general laws the shared row depends on the block only through the linear pool of the members' laws, and it equals the Gibbs row of a single receiver holding that pool at temperature $T/n$ (\cref{it7:pool}); for sources in an exponential family it depends only on the pooled mean parameter. The parent presents the pool to its members and to every receiver outside the block. With parents that present the pool, a tempered label, which averages the members' costs instead of adding them, gives the same vector field as the dynamics without feedback at every internally synchronized configuration (\cref{it7:tempered}), so the factor $n$ in the exponent is the operative part of the feedback.

In the Gaussian location family the shared-label parent is exactly inherited between handoffs and compresses. The pooled mean of every block evolves autonomously, by the per-agent law of a single agent at temperature $T/n$ with one additional source at its own law, while the members' deviations from the pool relax at a fixed rate to offsets determined by their records, whatever the spread within the block (\cref{it7:exact}). For linear consensus on a fixed graph, exact block-mean dynamics over external equitable partitions were analyzed by \citet{MartiniEgerstedtBicchi2010} and \citet{EgerstedtEtAl2012}, and \citet{OClery2013} relate them to observability and coarse graining; here the rows are state-dependent Gibbs rows. Universal pooled closure does not extend beyond Gaussian families of the sufficient statistic: if the pooled parent of a two-member block closes for every configuration and every target, the covariance of the sufficient statistic is constant and its laws are Gaussian with one covariance (\cref{it7:characterization}). In the coordinates of the sufficient statistic such a family is a Gaussian location family; the common-variance lognormal family on $(0,\infty)$ is an example whose laws are not Gaussian. For Bernoulli laws pooled closure fails.

In a twelve-agent instance with four groups of three conflicting records, the label-preserving null flow never selects a block, and the renormalized null selects only the block of all twelve agents (\cref{it7:null,it7:null-rn}). The shared-label flow selects the four groups and then, on each side, the union of two first-stage parents. The second stage reduces to a one-dimensional equation for the gap between two parents, with a stable merged state, an unstable middle root, and a stable separated state held by the records; interval arithmetic certifies that these are its only zeros (\cref{it7:reduced}). The union forms from every post-handoff state in the stated ranges whose gap lies below the middle root by more than $2\times10^{-8}$, and never from one whose gap lies above it by more than that (\cref{it7:counterfactual}). Post-handoff states shifted outside the middle root therefore never unite, while the control without feedback unites from every tested shift; these shifted states are not, however, the counterfactual class of the standard. The bistability exists only for record gaps above a saddle-node of the gap equation, certified to lie in $(1.0722,1.0723)$. At slightly larger record gaps than the instance's the hierarchy stops at depth one in the separated state, and the distance from which two blocks still unite falls with block size, from $0.963$ for blocks of three to $0.593$ for blocks of six.

Measured against the standard, the instance is not shown to be a recursive formation, and its classification is undetermined (\cref{it7:ch-standard}). The admitted class is open in the full grade, so (F1) holds. The selector is declared with single values of its constants; it meets (F2) in form, but the standard asks for a declared set of constants with nonempty interior, over which (F5) is not established. Inheritance (F3) is proved for the dynamics and for read-only parent queries at a fixed partition; the process with candidate clocks, blocks of unequal size and merges at handoffs is not shown to descend from the retained state, so (F3) is open. Compression (F4) has numerical witnesses on the reachable set at the first stage; at the second stage it depends on whether the parent type records the time of its formation, which is not declared, so it is open there. Interaction necessity (F5) at the declared constants holds against both nulls on an open neighborhood of a tested state: both null histories are proved on the whole admitted class, while the history of the shared-label flow rests on fixed-step integration of the first stage with explicit margins, everything after the first-stage handoff following from certified inequalities. The frozen-environment control selects the four groups and no union. (F6a) holds on the tested states under the same proviso. (F6b) holds for the shifted post-handoff states by certified inequalities, but against the counterfactual class of the standard it is open: no such class is declared, and widened initial states along which the first stage has not occurred by the actual handoff times give handoff gaps below the middle root, from which the union forms. Exactness holds only in Gaussian families of the sufficient statistic, the compressed coordinates are transient, the selector thresholds and the label-sharing handoff are declared, and the parameter window is narrow. A comparison with the literature found exact quotients of linear consensus, Bayesian inference of latent social groups, representative agents for coherent groups, and Bayesian structure learning of hierarchies, but no construction combining shared-label pooling, exact block-mean closure away from synchrony, and merging that depends on the state at formation (\cref{it7:ch-prior}). This is not a priority claim.

A derived selector replaces the declared thresholds by a comparison of free energies (\cref{it7:ch-derived}). A block is selected when the shared-label models that the handoff would create, together with the models of all other units, have a lower free energy than the current ones, and the comparison has held for the declared window. For general laws this structure free energy reads only the spread of each block, $n$ times a generalized Jensen--Shannon divergence, and $n$ times the free energy of its pool at temperature $T/n$, which includes the block's own parent at cost zero (\cref{it7:structure-fe}). Two agents whose mutual attentions have a product above the peer prior gain from a shared label, through a saving of labels and a double use of their laws in the pool, and the gain grows with their divergence while they attend to each other (\cref{it7:mutual}). The criterion detects attention, not agreement, and only blocks, not single agents, have a merge radius. The derived selector therefore groups whatever attention the initial state contains, and in the instance the label-preserving null selects the four groups at once. The criterion for uniting two parents is a radius, $d_*=0.7685$ for blocks of three, below the middle root of the gap equation (\cref{it7:radius}), so the dependence on the post-handoff gap survives (\cref{it7:derived-counterfactual}). With $\tau_p=1.25$ the label-preserving null and the frozen-environment control select the four groups and nothing more, the renormalized null selects the two sides and then all agents, and the shared-label flow selects the four groups and then their unions. The window decides the regime: with an earlier handoff the hierarchy stops at depth one, as the label-preserving null does, a handoff in a short later interval gives a mixed regime, and with the window $\tau_p=2$ of the declared instance the flow selects the two unions directly as a single stage.

Every identity was checked numerically at small sizes in the accompanying checks, numbered (1) to (28) in the text; the checks support the instances tested and are not proofs. Check~(27) is the exception: it certifies the zeros and sign margins of the reduced gap equations and the union radius by outward-rounded interval arithmetic, so those inequalities are proved. Statements that rest on numerical integration are labeled as such.

\chapter{Setting}
\label{it7:ch-setting}

The agents $i\in\mathcal V$, $|\mathcal V|=N$, hold belief laws $q_i$ on a standard Borel space $\mathsf Y$. The model sector is trivial in this document; it enters only through a common evaluator $G$, a law on $\mathsf Y$ shared by agents of one species. In the per-agent mixture-source model of the dynamics chapter of the main manuscript (\emph{Free Energies and Coupled Dynamics of Agent Laws}), agent $i$ has a finite source set $\mathcal N_i$ containing its own label, with positive prior row $\pi_i$. Its self cost is $D_{ii}=\KL(q_i\Vert G)$, its cost for a peer $j$ is $D_{ij}=\KL(q_i\Vert\nu_{ij})$ with $\nu_{ij}$ the law of $j$ as compared by $i$, and its records $o_i$ enter through a likelihood $\ell_i(o_i\mid k)$ that persists in the objective. At temperature $T$ the per-agent free energy is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_i=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal N_i}\beta_{i\nu}D_{i\nu}+T\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i)+\int-\log\ell_i(o_i\mid k)q_i(dk),
\label{it7:fe}
\end{equation}
and its minimizing row is the Gibbs row $\beta_{i\nu}=\pi_{i\nu}e^{-D_{i\nu}/T}/Z_i$. The main manuscript's per-agent model has $T=1$; another temperature corresponds to tempered factors. Each agent follows the Fisher gradient flow of its own $\mathcal F_i$, with the laws of the other agents held as data. By the envelope identity, the differential of $\mathcal F_i$ at its Gibbs row is $\sum_\nu\beta_{i\nu}dD_{i\nu}$ plus the differential of the record term, so the force on $q_i$ is a row-weighted sum of divergence forces plus a record force that no row weights.

\definitionheading{Units, presented laws and the wall selector}{it7:selector}
At each time the agents are partitioned into units: the blocks selected so far that are contained in no later block, and the agents that belong to no block. Each unit $U$ presents a law $P_U$, which is the agent's own law for an agent and is declared by the handoff of \cref{it7:handoff} for a block. Fix thresholds $0<\delta_c<\delta_w$ and a window $\tau_p>0$. Units $U\ne V$ are coherent if $\KL(P_U\Vert P_V)<\delta_c$ and $\KL(P_V\Vert P_U)<\delta_c$. A set $C$ of at least two units is a candidate if it is a connected component of the coherence graph and if $\KL(P_U\Vert P_V)>\delta_w$ and $\KL(P_V\Vert P_U)>\delta_w$ for every $U\in C$ and every unit $V\notin C$. A handoff selects the union of the agents of $C$ as a block once $C$ has been a candidate without interruption for a time $\tau_p$. A selected block is never dissolved.

The selector reads only the current presented laws and their recent history, so each handoff is a measurable function of accessible history, as (F2) requires. It is a declared threshold selector of the kind used in the Wishart, open-law and per-agent chapters of the main manuscript. Its block relation, internal coherence together with a wall, is the contrast structure that Item~3 found at stationary states with parents.

\definitionheading{Controls}{it7:controls}
The controls are those of the formation standard (\cref{it7:standard-restated}). A cost is a peer cost when its compared law depends on the law of another agent; here these are the costs $D_{ij}$ with $j\ne i$ and, after a handoff, the costs to the block's parent and to the other units. The label-preserving null $\Phi^0_{\mathrm{lp}}$ deletes every peer cost while labels, priors and all other data are kept, so the null self row is
\begin{equation}
\beta^0_{ii}=\frac{\pi_{ii}e^{-D_{ii}/T}}{\pi_{ii}e^{-D_{ii}/T}+1-\pi_{ii}},
\label{it7:null-row}
\end{equation}
the convention of the per-agent chapter of the main manuscript (\emph{Per-Agent Grouping with Persistent Records}). The renormalized null $\Phi^0_{\mathrm{rn}}$ also removes the labels of the deleted costs, so the evaluator receives weight one and, in the Gaussian instance below, each agent follows $\dot y_i=(m_G-y_i)+\rho(o_i-y_i)$. The frozen-environment control $\Phi^{\rm fr}$ deletes nothing and holds the law of every other agent at its initial value in every cost and row that enters the force on an agent. Along each control the selector reads the presented laws through the formulas of \cref{it7:selector}, and after a handoff along a control the control remains the fine flow, the selector reading the pools of the selected blocks computed from the fine states. The control without feedback $\Phi^{\rm off}$ uses the same selector and records the same handoffs, but every agent keeps its individual row, sources and channels after a handoff, so the parents are passive.

The control without feedback is not part of the standard. It answers a different question: whether the action of a parent on its members changes what forms.

\remarkheading{Regularity and event conventions}{it7:conventions}
In the Gaussian instance every flow used here, before, between and after handoffs and along every control, is the flow of a smooth vector field on $\R^{12}$ whose rows are smooth and bounded and whose growth is at most linear, so its solutions exist for all time and are unique; the same holds for the parent flow (iii) of \cref{it7:exact} on the unit means. For general laws no chart for the per-agent flow is declared; the general-law results below use only first variations and make no claim about flows. A moment projection onto a regular exponential family requires the pooled mean parameter to lie in the interior of the mean domain, which fails, for example, for a block of Bernoulli point masses. Candidates of \cref{it7:selector} are connected components, hence disjoint, and all candidates whose windows end at one time are selected together. Where the derived selector of \cref{it7:ch-derived} has several minimizers it takes the one whose blocks, listed by their smallest agents, come first lexicographically. The checks evaluate the selectors after every integration step of length $0.05$ unless stated otherwise, so the event times they report lie on that grid.

\chapter{What top-down feedback cannot do}
\label{it7:ch-negative}

\propositionheading{A parent as one more source}{it7:capture}
Let receiver $i$ have the Gibbs row $\beta_i$ over a finite source set $\mathcal N_i$ at temperature $T$, and add a source $A$ with prior share $a\in(0,1)$, multiplying the other priors by $1-a$. The new row satisfies
\begin{equation}
\beta'_{i\nu}=(1-\beta'_{iA})\beta_{i\nu},\qquad\nu\in\mathcal N_i.
\label{it7:capture-rows}
\end{equation}
If the first variation of $D_{iA}$ vanishes at the current law of $i$, as it does when the compared law of $A$ equals $q_i$, then the force on $q_i$ is $1-\beta'_{iA}$ times its row part before the addition, plus the unchanged record force.
\begin{proof}
With $Z'_i=(1-a)Z_i+ae^{-D_{iA}/T}$ we have $\beta'_{i\nu}=(1-a)\pi_{i\nu}e^{-D_{i\nu}/T}/Z'_i=\beta_{i\nu}(1-a)Z_i/Z'_i$ and $1-\beta'_{iA}=(1-a)Z_i/Z'_i$, which is \cref{it7:capture-rows}. By the envelope identity the row part of $d\mathcal F'_i$ is $\sum_\nu\beta'_{i\nu}dD_{i\nu}+\beta'_{iA}dD_{iA}$, which equals $(1-\beta'_{iA})\sum_\nu\beta_{i\nu}dD_{i\nu}$ where $dD_{iA}=0$. The record term is weighted by no row, and the Fisher field is linear in the differential.
\end{proof}

The exponents of the row are unchanged, so the parent does not change which of the old sources the member trusts. Without records, the parent only rescales each member's velocity by a positive factor at agreement. With records, it raises the weight of the records relative to the row part by the factor $(1-\beta'_{iA})^{-1}$, a form of stubbornness. Away from agreement it also pulls the member toward its compared law, which reduces the spread inside the block. Check~(2) confirms \cref{it7:capture-rows} and the force decomposition. Check~(15) runs twelve agents in four groups without records, each agent's evaluator target in its own row and each member adding its pooled parent with share $a$; for $a=0$, $0.3$ and $0.6$ the same four blocks and the same two unions are selected, and the unions occur at $t\approx40$, $100$ and $245$. Both nulls select nothing there, because the initial state is an equilibrium of each null flow, at which adjacent divergences are $0.27>\delta_c$.

\propositionheading{Top-down costs are internal forces}{it7:momentum}
Let $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0+\tau\mathcal E_{\rm td}$ be continuously differentiable on a Riemannian manifold $(\mathsf S,g)$, and let $x(t)$ solve $\dot x=-\operatorname{grad}_g\mathcal E$. Let $J$ be continuously differentiable on $\mathsf S$ with $\xi=\operatorname{grad}_gJ$. If $\mathcal E_{\rm td}$ is invariant along $\xi$, that is $d\mathcal E_{\rm td}(\xi)=0$, then
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}J(x(t))=-d\mathcal E_0(\xi)\bigl(x(t)\bigr),
\label{it7:momentum-rate}
\end{equation}
which does not involve $\tau$.
\begin{proof}
$\frac{d}{dt}J(x)=dJ(\dot x)=g(\xi,\dot x)=-g(\xi,\operatorname{grad}_g\mathcal E)=-d\mathcal E(\xi)=-d\mathcal E_0(\xi)$.
\end{proof}

In the Gaussian location family with laws $\mathcal N(y_i,P_i^{-1})$, the Fisher metric on the means is $\bigoplus_iP_i$. The common translation of the members of a block $I$ and of a parent $A$ by $u\in\R^K$ is $\xi_u=\operatorname{grad}J_u$ with $J_u=u^T(\sum_{i\in I}P_iy_i+P_Ay_A)$. The top-down factor of Item~3 depends on $y_i-y_A$ and on the cross-scale dictionary, so its cost is invariant under $\xi_u$, and the block momentum $\sum_{i\in I}P_iy_i+P_Ay_A$ has a rate independent of $\tau$ at every configuration (check~(3a)). Suppose in addition that the parent is the pool, with top-down cost $\frac\tau2\sum_{i\in I}\Vert y_i-\bar y_I\Vert^2$, that the members have equal precisions, and that every member of a block attends to every member of every other block with one weight. Then the block sums form a closed linear system, the external equitable partition of \citet{OClery2013}, and their trajectories do not depend on $\tau$ (check~(3b), residual $6\times10^{-16}$). With sparse bridges, or with a parent that has its own state and prior, the block sums do depend on $\tau$ (checks~(3b) and~(3c)). The top-down cost moves momentum between the parent and the members and among the members, and the bridges read where it sits. The sharpening of walls under top-down coupling found in Item~3 is of this kind, and it is not available to a parent that is exactly inherited through the block momentum.

For general laws the proposition needs a momentum function for the collective motion. In natural coordinates of an exponential family the common shift of natural parameters has momentum $\sum_i\mu_i$, the sum of mean parameters. A Kullback--Leibler cost between members of the family is a Bregman divergence of the log-partition $\psi$, and differentiating it along a common shift shows that it is invariant for every pair exactly when $\nabla\psi$ is affine, that is, when the sufficient statistic is Gaussian with one covariance. Such families are Gaussian location families in the coordinates of the sufficient statistic; the common-variance lognormal family, with sufficient statistic $\log y$, is one. Outside them the proposition applies to costs constructed to be invariant, not to the divergences of the theory.

\propositionheading{A globally attracting equilibrium excludes counterfactual recursion under all-time persistence}{it7:attractor}
Fix the first-stage blocks of a construction and let $\mathcal C$ be a set of post-handoff states. Suppose the post-handoff flow is deterministic and every trajectory starting in $\mathcal C$ converges to one equilibrium $z^*$. Let the relation that some second-stage block $B$ is a candidate be given by strict inequalities between continuous functions of the state, defining an open set $S$, and let persistence mean that the relation holds at all later times. If $z^*\in S$ and no other block is selected along these trajectories before $B$, the block $B$ is selected from every state in $\mathcal C$ and (F6b) fails for $B$. If $z^*\notin\bar S$, no state in $\mathcal C$ leads to a persistent selection of $B$.
\begin{proof}
If $z^*\in S$, some neighborhood of $z^*$ lies in $S$, and every trajectory from $\mathcal C$ remains in it after a finite time, so the candidate persists beyond any window $\tau_p$; since no other selection intervenes, the flow is unchanged until then. If the relation holds at all times after some $t_0$ along a trajectory converging to $z^*$, then $z^*$ is a limit of points of $S$.
\end{proof}

The proposition says nothing when $z^*\in\partial S$: for $\dot x=-x$ on $\R$ and $S=\{x>0\}$, trajectories from $x_0>0$ stay in $S$ and those from $x_0<0$ never enter it. Without the hypothesis on competing selections the first alternative fails when another block is selected during a transient and changes the flow. The selector of \cref{it7:selector} persists for a finite window, not at all later times, and for it the proposition gives no conclusion about (F6b).

\remarkheading{A convex hierarchy with a finite dwell meets (F6b) (numerical)}{it7:dwell}
Take two first-stage blocks $I=\{a_1,a_2\}$ and $J=\{b_1,b_2\}$ with records $o=(-0.775,-0.725,0.725,0.775)$, and add at the handoff declared parents with means $m_I,m_J$, initialized at the pools of their members, with the strictly convex energy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E=\sum_{B\in\{I,J\}}\Bigl[\sum_{i\in B}\bigl(\tfrac12(y_i-o_i)^2+\tfrac12(y_i-m_B)^2\bigr)+4m_B^2\Bigr],
\label{it7:dwell-energy}
\end{equation}
whose last terms anchor both parents at zero. Let the flow be its gradient flow in the constant metric $\operatorname{diag}(20,20,20,20,6,6)$, and let the selector of \cref{it7:selector} read the parents' presented laws of precision $6$, with $\delta_c=0.05$, $\delta_w=0.5$ and $\tau_p=2$. The Hessian has eigenvalues $1.757$, $1.757$, $2$, $2$, $10.24$ and $10.24$, and the unique minimizer has parent gap $1/6$, with divergence $0.083>\delta_c$, outside the closure of the coherence set. From a handoff with block gap $0.5$, at which the members of each block are coherent and the blocks are walled, the parent gap falls to $0.1175$, stays below the coherence radius for $4.81$ time units, and the union is selected at $t=3.785$. From a handoff with block gap $0.7$, also walled, the parent gap stays above $0.150$ and no union forms. Both trajectories converge to the minimizer (check~(29)). The length of a transient dwell in the candidate set depends on the handoff, and that suffices for (F6b) relative to these two handoffs. In the Gaussian tangent global joint of Items~2 and~3, whose own flow uses the Fisher metric of its factors, this energy comes with the metric $\operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,2,10,10)$, and then neither handoff unites. A random search over that joint, with first stages produced by its own flow and the selector of \cref{it7:selector}, found instances meeting (F6b), every one of them with declared parent anchors that pull the parents together, and none with each parent anchored at the record mean of its own cluster; this search is not among the checks. Whether declared parent anchors count as new constitutive data under (F6a), and whether such instances exist when parents interact only through inherited bridges, are open (\cref{it7:open-convex}).

\chapter{Shared labels}
\label{it7:ch-shared}

\definitionheading{Shared-label handoff}{it7:handoff}
Let a handoff select a block $I$ of $n=|I|\ge2$ agents. Suppose that every member compares each source outside $I$ through the same channel, so that a source $\nu$ presents one compared law $\nu_\nu$ to all members, and that the members share the evaluator $G$. From the handoff on:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The members carry one source label $J_I$. Its sources are the evaluator, a parent $A_I$, and the units outside $I$. Its prior $\pi_I$ is the average of the members' priors, with the mass that the members gave to one another assigned to $A_I$ and the mass that they gave to the agents of an outside unit assigned to that unit. Each member descends, with respect to its own law and with all other laws as data, the block free energy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_I=\sum_\nu\beta_{I\nu}\sum_{i\in I}D_{i\nu}+T\KL(\beta_I\Vert\pi_I)+\sum_{i\in I}\int-\log\ell_i(o_i\mid k)q_i(dk),
\label{it7:block-fe}
\end{equation}
whose minimizing row is $\beta_{I\nu}=\pi_{I\nu}\exp(-\sum_{i\in I}D_{i\nu}/T)/Z_I$.
\item[(b)] The parent presents the linear pool $\bar q_I=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}q_i$ or, when the compared laws lie in a declared family $\mathsf E$, its moment projection $P_I$ onto $\mathsf E$. The members compare themselves to it, $D_{iA}=\KL(q_i\Vert P_I)$, and every receiver outside $I$ replaces the agents of $I$ in its source set by $A_I$, with their total prior.
\item[(c)] A later handoff that selects a set of units replaces them in the same way by one block whose members are all their agents. The parents of the replaced blocks are then no longer sources.
\end{enumerate}
The tempered label replaces $T$ by $nT$ in the row of (a).

Part (a) is top-down attention: a member no longer decides alone which source to trust; the block decides, with every member's evidence. Part (b) is top-down prediction and routing: members are pulled toward what the block presents, and the block speaks to the rest of the population through its parent. The rule is declared once, before any selection, and applies at every level, so a second stage uses it without a new parameter.

\propositionheading{The shared row reads the pool}{it7:pool}
Under \cref{it7:handoff}, for every source $\nu$ whose costs are finite,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in I}\KL(q_i\Vert\nu_\nu)=\sum_{i\in I}\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_I)+n\KL(\bar q_I\Vert\nu_\nu).
\label{it7:compensation}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\beta_{I\nu}=\frac{\pi_{I\nu}\exp\bigl(-n\KL(\bar q_I\Vert\nu_\nu)/T\bigr)}{\sum_\mu\pi_{I\mu}\exp\bigl(-n\KL(\bar q_I\Vert\nu_\mu)/T\bigr)},
\label{it7:pooled-row}
\end{equation}
the Gibbs row at temperature $T/n$ of a single receiver with law $\bar q_I$, and the parent presenting $\bar q_I$ has relative cost zero. If every compared law lies in a regular exponential family $\mathsf E$ with sufficient statistic $t$, and the parent presents the moment projection $P_I$, defined by $\E_{P_I}t=\bar\mu_I=n^{-1}\sum_i\E_{q_i}t$, then for every $p\in\mathsf E$
\begin{equation}
\KL(\bar q_I\Vert p)=\KL(\bar q_I\Vert P_I)+\KL(P_I\Vert p),
\label{it7:pythagoras}
\end{equation}
and the shared row is the Gibbs row at temperature $T/n$ of the costs $\KL(P_I\Vert\nu_\nu)$. It depends on the block only through $\bar\mu_I$, and $A_I$ again has relative cost zero.
\begin{proof}
Write $\log(q_i/\nu)=\log(q_i/\bar q_I)+\log(\bar q_I/\nu)$, integrate against $q_i$, and sum over $i$, using $\sum_iq_i=n\bar q_I$; this is the receiver-pool identity of the dynamics chapter with equal weights. The term $\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_I)$ is the same for every source and cancels from the normalized row, and with the parent presenting $\bar q_I$ it is the whole cost of $A_I$. For \cref{it7:pythagoras}, write $p=p_\theta$ with log density $\theta\cdot t-\psi(\theta)$ relative to a base measure; then $\KL(\bar q_I\Vert p_\theta)=-H(\bar q_I)-\theta\cdot\bar\mu_I+\psi(\theta)$ with $H$ the entropy relative to that measure. Subtracting the same expression at $\theta^*$ with $\nabla\psi(\theta^*)=\bar\mu_I$ gives $\psi(\theta)-\psi(\theta^*)-(\theta-\theta^*)\cdot\nabla\psi(\theta^*)=\KL(p_{\theta^*}\Vert p_\theta)$, the Pythagorean identity of \citet{Csiszar1975}. The cost of $A_I$ is then the common term plus $n\KL(\bar q_I\Vert P_I)$, which is also common to all sources.
\end{proof}

Check~(1) confirms \cref{it7:compensation,it7:pooled-row,it7:pythagoras} on a six-point alphabet, including that two blocks with the same pool and different members have the same row.

\remarkheading{When the shared row is a posterior}{it7:posterior}
In the registered-agreement model of the dynamics chapter, an agent's Gibbs row is the posterior of the source it heard, given that it registered agreement with that source, at temperature equal to the reciprocal message count. If the members of $I$ heard one common source $J_I$ and their registrations are conditionally independent given $J_I$, the posterior of $J_I$ is $\pi_I$ times the product of the members' registration probabilities, and the source-independent factors cancel; it is the row of \cref{it7:handoff}(a). The shared row is then the exact posterior of a common cause. Members that have influenced one another do not in general supply conditionally independent evidence, and the row is then a Gibbs row of summed losses, a generalized-Bayes update in the sense of \citet{BissiriHolmesWalker2016}, not a posterior. It adds the members' costs, unlike a logarithmic opinion pool with weights summing to one \citep{GenestZidek1986}; the tempered label is the pool that averages them.

\propositionheading{A tempered label is neutral at synchrony}{it7:tempered}
Suppose the priors are homogeneous, $\pi_{ii}=\pi_s$ and $\pi_{ij}=\pi_p$ for $j\ne i$, all channels are identity channels, and every parent presents the linear pool of its members, or every member law lies in the family $\mathsf E$ onto which the parents project. At a configuration where every block is internally synchronized, $q_i=\bar q_I$ for $i\in I$, the force on every agent under the shared-label handoff with the tempered label equals its force under the control without feedback. With the untempered label, every cost in the row of a block of $n$ members is $n$ times the corresponding cost in the control.
\begin{proof}
At synchrony, in the control, member $i$ of $I$ has the evaluator cost $\KL(\bar q_I\Vert G)$, cost zero to each of the other $n-1$ members with total prior $(n-1)\pi_p$, and cost $\KL(\bar q_I\Vert\bar q_V)$ to each of the $n_V$ agents of an outside unit $V$, with total prior $n_V\pi_p$; the sources at cost zero exert no force. The tempered shared row has costs $n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}D_{i\nu}$, which at synchrony are $\KL(\bar q_I\Vert G)$, zero for $A_I$ with prior $(n-1)\pi_p$, and $\KL(\bar q_I\Vert P_V)=\KL(\bar q_I\Vert\bar q_V)$ for $V$ with prior $n_V\pi_p$, because under either hypothesis $P_V=\bar q_V$ at synchrony. The rows, the compared laws and the record forces coincide. Without tempering each cost is the sum of $n$ equal member costs.
\end{proof}

Check~(6) gives a difference of $2\times10^{-16}$ between the tempered label and the control at a synchronized configuration, and $0.06$ for the untempered label. At synchrony the whole effect of the shared label is evidence accumulation: a block of $n$ members is $n$ times as selective as one of its members. The synchronized set is not invariant when the records differ inside blocks, so the proposition compares vector fields there, not trajectories; the tempered label differs from the control only through the spread inside blocks.

\remarkheading{Dictionaries}{it7:dictionaries}
With receiver-owned dictionaries, as in Items~1 to~3, member $i$ reads source $\nu$ through its own dictionary $\bar\theta_{i\nu}$, acting by push-forward, and the compared laws differ across members. Relative entropy is invariant under one measurable bijection applied to both arguments, so if there are measurable bijections $g_i$, applied to the members' laws, with $g_i\bar\theta_{i\nu}=\Theta_\nu$ for every source $\nu$ outside $I$, then $\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert\bar\theta_{i\nu}\nu)=\sum_i\KL(g_iq_i\Vert\Theta_\nu\nu)$, and \cref{it7:pool} applies to the laws $g_iq_i$. Such maps exist exactly when $\bar\theta_{j\nu}\bar\theta_{i\nu}^{-1}$ does not depend on $\nu$ for all members $i,j$: then $g_j=\bar\theta_{1\nu}\bar\theta_{j\nu}^{-1}$ works for every $\nu$, and conversely $g_j^{-1}g_i=\bar\theta_{j\nu}\bar\theta_{i\nu}^{-1}$. The maps $g_i$ act on the members' laws at fixed sources; they are not passive frame changes. Equivalently, every closed walk that goes from member $i$ to an outside source $\nu$, from $\nu$ to member $j$, from $j$ to a second source $\mu$ and from $\mu$ back to $i$, with each source edge traversed against its direction by the inverse of its dictionary, has trivial signed-walk holonomy, and in this sense the block is flat toward its sources, with $\Theta_\nu$ as a common meta-level dictionary. This is not the condition of the flat-wall proposition of Item~3, which concerns directed walks and needs internal directed paths and bridges in both directions. For a block that is curved toward its sources the shared row is not in general a function of the pool of any such images of the members' laws; it can be when the member laws are invariant under the discrepancies, as isotropic Gaussian laws are under rotation dictionaries.

\chapter{Exact parents in the Gaussian location family}
\label{it7:ch-exact}

\theoremheading{Exact inheritance of shared-label parents at a fixed partition}{it7:exact}
Let $\mathsf Y=\R^K$, let every agent law, compared law, presented law and the evaluator be Gaussian with one fixed precision matrix $\Lambda$, recognition being restricted to the location family $\mathcal N(y,\Lambda^{-1})$ whose Fisher metric on the means is $\Lambda$, let all channels be identity channels, and let each record have the likelihood $\mathcal N(o_i;k,R^{-1})$; write $\rho=\Lambda^{-1}R$. The parent of a block presents $\mathcal N(\bar y_U,\Lambda^{-1})$, the moment projection of its pool onto the location family. After any sequence of shared-label handoffs, let $\mathcal U$ be the set of units, $n_U$ their sizes, $\bar y_U$ and $\bar o_U$ the averages of the members' means and records, and $m_\nu$ the mean of source $\nu$: $m_G$ for the evaluator, $\bar y_U$ for the unit's own parent, and $\bar y_V$ for a unit $V\ne U$. Then for every $U\in\mathcal U$ and $i\in U$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\beta_{U\nu}=\pi_{U\nu}\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{n_U}{2T}\Vert\bar y_U-m_\nu\Vert_\Lambda^2\bigr)/Z_U$;
\item[(ii)] $\dot y_i=\sum_\nu\beta_{U\nu}(m_\nu-y_i)+\rho(o_i-y_i)$;
\item[(iii)] $\frac{d}{dt}\bar y_U=\sum_\nu\beta_{U\nu}(m_\nu-\bar y_U)+\rho(\bar o_U-\bar y_U)$;
\item[(iv)] $\frac{d}{dt}(y_i-\bar y_U)=-(I+\rho)(y_i-\bar y_U)+\rho(o_i-\bar o_U)$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, on every set of configurations with one fixed set of units that is invariant under the flow (ii), that is, between handoffs, the retention $R(x)=(\bar y_U)_{U\in\mathcal U}$ satisfies the law condition (L), the dynamical condition (D) with parent flow (iii), the channel condition (C), and the response condition (B) for the read-only parent protocols of \cref{it7:classes} answered within the same interval, as these conditions are defined for an inherited parent in the inheritance chapter. The retention is not injective when some unit has $n_U\ge2$, and two configurations in one fiber remain distinct and in one fiber at all later times of the interval.
\begin{proof}
(i) For a source with mean $m$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in U}\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(y_i,\Lambda^{-1})\Vert\mathcal N(m,\Lambda^{-1})\bigr)=\frac{n_U}2\Vert\bar y_U-m\Vert_\Lambda^2+\frac12\sum_{i\in U}\Vert y_i-\bar y_U\Vert_\Lambda^2,
\end{equation}
and the second term, which is the whole cost of the own parent, does not depend on the source; it cancels from the normalized row. (ii) The derivative of \cref{it7:block-fe} with respect to $y_i$ is $\sum_\nu\beta_{U\nu}\Lambda(y_i-m_\nu)+R(y_i-o_i)$, since the record term is $\frac12\Vert o_i-y_i\Vert_R^2$ plus a constant. The Fisher flow multiplies by $-\Lambda^{-1}$, and the rows sum to one. (iii) Average (ii) over $i\in U$; the rows and the source means do not depend on $i$. (iv) Subtract (iii) from (ii) and use $\sum_\nu\beta_{U\nu}=1$.

By (i) the rows of every unit are functions of the unit means. By the routing of \cref{it7:handoff}(b) every compared law is the evaluator or a presented law $\mathcal N(\bar y_V,\Lambda^{-1})$, so (iii) is a closed system in $(\bar y_U)_U$ and $R\circ\Phi_t=\Phi^c_t\circ R$ for the flow $\Phi^c$ of (iii), which is well posed by \cref{it7:conventions}. The presented laws, and the rows, channels and evaluator read by (iii), are functions of $R$, which gives (L) and (C). The deviations $(y_i-\bar y_U)_{i\in U}$ with zero sum span a subspace of dimension $(n_U-1)K$ on which $R$ is constant. By (iv) they evolve by an invertible affine flow that does not depend on the unit means, so distinct deviations stay distinct while the unit means move together by (iii).

For (B), a read-only query of $A_U$ at time $s$ returns a draw from $\mathcal N(\bar y_U(s),\Lambda^{-1})$, a function of $R(\Phi_sx)=\Phi^c_s(R(x))$, and it changes no state, record or row. The joint law of the response and of the retained state at any later time $s'$ of the interval is therefore $K\bigl(\Phi^c_sR(x),\cdot\bigr)\otimes\delta_{\Phi^c_{s'}R(x)}$, with $K(z,\cdot)$ the presented law at retained value $z$, a function of $R(x)$. For a finite adaptive schedule, in which the time and the target of each query are measurable functions of the earlier responses, the joint law of the responses and the retained state factors through $R(x)$ by induction on the number of queries, because the responses do not enter the dynamics.
\end{proof}

Check~(7) fixes the four first-stage units of the twelve-agent instance of \cref{it7:ch-instance} after their handoffs and integrates the fixed-partition flow (ii) for twenty time units; it does not run the selector, and the instance itself unites inside that window. The block means agree with the solution of (iii) to $8\times10^{-15}$, and the deviations agree with the closed form of (iv) to $1.4\times10^{-15}$. Exact block-mean dynamics of linear consensus over external equitable partitions on a fixed graph were analyzed by \citet{MartiniEgerstedtBicchi2010} and \citet{EgerstedtEtAl2012} and related to observability and coarse graining by \citet{OClery2013}, and for nonlinear networks the quotient over such partitions describes the synchronized states \citep{SchaubEtAl2016}. Here the rows depend on the state, the closure holds for every spread inside the blocks, and it is created by the handoff rule.

\remarkheading{What is not proved: the hybrid process}{it7:hybrid}
\Cref{it7:exact} is a statement at a fixed partition. The process to which (F3) applies is hybrid. The selector runs candidate clocks, and the first-stage handoffs are not simultaneous: on the evaluation grid of the checks they occur at four times between $t=2.13$ and $t=2.35$. At the first handoff the clocks of the other groups are running. They started under the dynamics with individual rows, which depend on the members' individual laws, so they are not functions of the retained means of a block already handed off, and they fix the later handoff times. The mean-only retention also omits the partition, the block sizes $n_U$, which enter the union's temperature and its prior masses, the record means $\bar o_U$ and the reassigned prior rows $\pi_U$, and neither the merge of retained states at the union nor the descent of the selection events is proved. Condition (B) is proved only for read-only queries. A protocol that assimilates a member-specific record fails it: a record $o'$ with Gaussian likelihood of precision $R'$ sent to member $k$ moves the block mean by $n^{-1}(\Lambda+R')^{-1}R'(o'-y_k)$, which is not a function of $R$. Clause (F3) for the hybrid process is therefore open; a route is stated in \cref{it7:mass-parent}.

\corollaryheading{Composition and type at a fixed partition}{it7:type}
The parent flow (iii) is the per-agent flow of a single Gaussian location agent with mean $\bar y_U$ and record $\bar o_U$, at temperature $T/n_U$, whose source set contains, besides the evaluator and the other units, a source at its own law with prior $\pi_{UA}$. A shared-label handoff applied by the primitive rule of \cref{it7:handoff}(c) to units formed by earlier handoffs returns a unit of the same form whose size is the sum of their sizes, and at a fixed partition successive retentions compose by the composition proposition of the inheritance chapter. A block formed from blocks $I$ and $J$ has temperature $T/(n_I+n_J)$, and the map from the two parents to their union, $(\bar y_I,\bar y_J)\mapsto(n_I\bar y_I+n_J\bar y_J)/(n_I+n_J)$, is again not injective.
\begin{proof}
The first statement is (iii) read as the flow (ii) of a single agent with the stated rows; a single agent is the case $n_U=1$, with prior zero on the source at its own law. A handoff by the primitive rule adds the members' costs, which by (i) are the units' costs weighted by their sizes, and returns a unit of the stated form with the summed size. At a fixed partition, composition is the composition of the two exact retentions.
\end{proof}

The corollary does not define a handoff on agents of this class: the rule of \cref{it7:handoff} is stated for primitive agents with one temperature. Treating each parent as one agent whose cost is counted once, with priors averaged without weights, does not in general reproduce the primitive rows for blocks of unequal size; the rule that does adds the exponents $\KL(P_U\Vert\nu_\nu)/T_U$ with $T_U=T/n_U$ and averages priors and record means with weights $n_U$ (\cref{it7:mass-parent}). Composition across a union also needs the merge of retained states to descend, which is not proved.

\remarkheading{A mass-carrying parent}{it7:mass-parent}
The following is a route to (F3) for the hybrid process, not a result of this document. Let the parent of a unit carry $p_U=(n_U,\bar y_U,\bar o_U,\pi_U)$, with Gibbs exponents $n_U\KL(P_U\Vert\nu_\nu)/T$, temperature $T/n_U$ and Fisher metric $\Lambda$. Merge units $U_1,\dots,U_m$ by $n=\sum_kn_k$, $\bar y=\sum_kn_k\bar y_k/n$, $\bar o=\sum_kn_k\bar o_k/n$ and $\pi=\sum_kn_k\pi_k^{\rm re}/n$, where $\pi_k^{\rm re}$ moves the mass that $U_k$ gave to the other merged units onto the new parent. Associativity is that of weighted means, and agreement with the primitive rule would follow from the blockwise identity
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in W}\KL(q_i\Vert\nu)=\sum_{k}\Bigl[\sum_{i\in U_k}\KL(q_i\Vert P_{U_k})+n_k\KL(P_{U_k}\Vert\nu)\Bigr],\qquad W=\textstyle\bigcup_kU_k,
\label{it7:blockwise}
\end{equation}
which holds for moment-projected location laws by the decomposition of a sum of squares. Add the running candidate clocks to the retained state. Both selectors read only presented laws, so the selection events, the clocks and the merges would then be functions of the retained state, and with the read-only class of \cref{it7:classes} the joint kernels would factor as in the proof of \cref{it7:exact}. Whether the retained state also records formation times decides the second-stage verdict of (F4) (\cref{it7:ch-standard,it7:open-hybrid}).

\theoremheading{Exact pooled parents force a Gaussian sufficient statistic}{it7:characterization}
Let the members' laws and all compared laws lie in a regular exponential family with sufficient statistic $t:\mathsf Y\to\R^d$, natural parameter $\theta$ in an open set $\Theta$, log-partition $\psi$, and mean parameter $\mu=\nabla\psi(\theta)$. Let the dynamics be the per-agent Fisher flow of the block free energy \cref{it7:block-fe} without records, with the parent presenting the moment projection of the pool. Suppose that for blocks of two members the velocity of the pooled mean parameter $\bar\mu=(\mu_1+\mu_2)/2$ is, at every configuration, a function of $\bar\mu$ and of the row-weighted source parameter $c=\sum_\nu\beta_\nu\theta_\nu$, for every value of $c$ in $\Theta$. Then $\nabla^2\psi$ is constant on $\Theta$, and the laws of $t$ form a Gaussian family with one covariance and varying mean; when $t$ is the identity on $\mathsf Y=\R^d$, the family is the Gaussian location family.
\begin{proof}
In natural coordinates the Fisher flow is $\dot\theta_i=-\partial_{\mu_i}\mathcal F_I$, and $\partial_\mu\KL(p_\theta\Vert p_{\theta'})=\theta-\theta'$, so $\dot\theta_i=c-\theta_i$, the rows summing to one. With $H(\mu)=\nabla^2\psi(\theta(\mu))$, the covariance of $t$, the pooled velocity is $\frac12\sum_iH(\mu_i)(c-\theta(\mu_i))$. It is affine in $c$. Requiring it to be constant on each fiber $\{(\mu_1,2\bar\mu-\mu_1)\}$ for every $c$ in the open set $\Theta$ makes both $H(\mu_1)+H(2\bar\mu-\mu_1)$ and $K(\mu_1)+K(2\bar\mu-\mu_1)$ independent of $\mu_1$, where $K(\mu)=H(\mu)\theta(\mu)$. The mean-parameter domain is open and convex. The first condition gives $DH(\mu_1)=DH(\mu_2)$ for all $\mu_1,\mu_2$, so $H$ is affine. Since $D\theta=H^{-1}$, $DK(\mu)[v]=DH[v]\theta(\mu)+v$, and the second condition makes $DH[v]\theta(\mu)$ independent of $\mu$; as $\theta(\mu)$ ranges over the open set $\Theta$, $DH[v]=0$ for every $v$. Hence $\nabla^2\psi$ is constant and $\psi$ is quadratic. The Laplace transform of the law of $t$ under the base measure is $e^{\psi}$, so by its uniqueness the laws of $t$ are Gaussian with covariance $\nabla^2\psi$; for $d=1$ this is the constant-variance case of the classification of \citet{Morris1982}.
\end{proof}

For Bernoulli laws $H(p)=p(1-p)$. Two members with $p=(0.5,0.5)$ and $p=(0.2,0.8)$ have the same pool and the same rows, and with a single source of natural parameter $1$ their pooled velocities are $0.25$ and $0.16$ (check~(4)); no update of the pool reproduces both. The theorem concerns the pooled parent only. Another retention, such as the canonical parent of the inheritance chapter for the routed interface, may still be exact for general laws; whether it compresses is open (\cref{it7:open-general}).

\remarkheading{What the parent forgets}{it7:forgets}
The fibers of $R$ are transient. By (iv) each member's deviation converges at the rates of $I+\rho$ to $(I+\rho)^{-1}\rho(o_i-\bar o_U)$, so on the attracting set each member's law is determined by its parent's mean and its own record. The parent forgets the members' records except their mean, and the transient part of their deviations; it forgets nothing that the members' own records do not restore. The fibers are inert in the sense used in the Wishart chapter of the main manuscript (\emph{Two-Stage Selection with Exact Inheritance on a Wishart Family}): no presented law, row or record depends on them. They are not orbits of a symmetry of the fine field, since a shift of the deviations does not preserve the affine equation (iv). Relative to the child-level probes of \cref{it7:classes} they are not gauge, because a receiver attending to one member distinguishes two configurations of a fiber; relative to the parent protocols they are invisible. The interface on which $R$ is exact does not exist before the handoff. While the members attend individually, their Gibbs rows, and the rows of every receiver attending to them, depend on each member's own mean, and the block mean is not autonomous. The handoff creates this operational equivalence; it is not present for every partition beforehand, which distinguishes the construction from the quotients supplied before membership in the open-law and per-agent chapters of the main manuscript. It is created by a declared rule, however, not by dynamics that are unchanged at the handoff.

\chapter{An instance}
\label{it7:ch-instance}

Take $N=12$, $K=1$, $\Lambda=\lambda=6$, $T=1$, homogeneous priors $\pi_s=0.05$ and $\pi_p=0.95/11$, evaluator mean $m_G=0$, and $\rho=0.005$. The records form four groups of three, $L_1=(-3.2,-3.05,-2.9)$, $L_2=(-2.1,-1.95,-1.8)$, $R_1=-L_2$ and $R_2=-L_1$, held by agents $0$ to $2$, $3$ to $5$, $6$ to $8$, and $9$ to $11$. The thresholds are $\delta_c=0.05$, $\delta_w=0.5$ and $\tau_p=2$. Every cost is $\frac\lambda2$ times a squared difference of means, so two units are coherent when their means differ by less than $0.129$ and walled when they differ by more than $0.408$. The admitted class is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A=\{y(0)\in\R^{12}:|y_i(0)-o_i|<0.005\text{ for all }i\},
\label{it7:admitted}
\end{equation}
which is open. No pair is coherent at $t=0$, since adjacent records differ by $0.15$ and $\frac\lambda2(0.14)^2=0.0588>\delta_c$. The members of a group disagree in their records, so a group becomes coherent only if the interaction contracts it; this is the device of the per-agent chapter of the main manuscript. The flows are those of \cref{it7:exact}(ii) after the handoffs and of the per-agent Gibbs flow with individual rows before them.

\propositionheading{The null flow never selects}{it7:null}
From every state in $\mathcal A$ the null flow never selects a block, so its partition sequence is the initial partition alone.
\begin{proof}
Under $\Phi^0$ each agent follows $\dot y_i=\beta^0_{ii}(y_i)(m_G-y_i)+\rho(o_i-y_i)$ with $\beta^0_{ii}$ of \cref{it7:null-row} and $D_{ii}=\frac\lambda2y_i^2$, so $\beta^0_{ii}(y)\le\frac{\pi_s}{1-\pi_s}e^{-3y^2}$. On $|y|\ge1.795$ this is at most $3.3\times10^{-6}$, and the evaluator term is at most $1.1\times10^{-5}$ in absolute value for $|y|\le3.205$. At $y=o_i\pm0.005$ the record term has magnitude $2.5\times10^{-5}$ and points toward $o_i$, so the interval $[o_i-0.005,o_i+0.005]$ is forward invariant. Every pair of agents then stays at distance at least $0.14$, with divergence at least $0.0588>\delta_c$, so no pair is ever coherent and no candidate exists.
\end{proof}

Check~(8) integrates the null flow to $t=1000$: the agents end within $0.0012$ of their records, and the smallest divergence between adjacent agents is $0.0675$.

\propositionheading{The renormalized null selects only the whole population}{it7:null-rn}
From every state in $\mathcal A$ the renormalized null flow selects no block before the block $\mathcal V$ of all twelve agents, which it selects once; its partition sequence is the initial partition followed by $\{\mathcal V\}$.
\begin{proof}
Under $\Phi^0_{\mathrm{rn}}$ the agents follow $\dot y=-\kappa y+\rho o$ with $\kappa=1+\rho$, since $m_G=0$. For agents $i\ne j$ write $o_{ij}=o_i-o_j$, $y_{ij}=y_i-y_j$, $r=\rho/\kappa$ and $e(t)=e^{-\kappa t}$. Then
\begin{equation}
y_{ij}(t)=o_{ij}c(t)+\varepsilon_{ij}e(t),\qquad c(t)=r+(1-r)e(t),\qquad\varepsilon_{ij}=y_{ij}(0)-o_{ij},
\end{equation}
with $|\varepsilon_{ij}|<0.01$ on $\mathcal A$. Since $c\ge(1-r)e$ and $|o_{ij}|\ge0.15$, $y_{ij}=o_{ij}c(1+\theta_{ij})$ with $|\theta_{ij}|\le0.01/(0.15(1-r))<0.0671$. The agents therefore keep the order of their records, a coherent pair, at distance below $0.12910$, has $c|o_{ij}|<0.1384$, and a walled pair, at distance above $0.40824$, has $c|o_{ij}|>0.3826$. Before any handoff the units are the agents, and a candidate other than $\mathcal V$ is a set of consecutive agents whose adjacent record spacings $s$ satisfy $cs<0.1384$ and whose adjacent outside spacings $g$ satisfy $cg>0.3826$, so that $g>2.76s$ for the largest inner spacing $s$. The record spacings are $0.15$ within groups, $0.8$ between the two groups of a side and $3.6$ between the sides, so the only such sets are the four groups, which require $c\in(0.3826/0.8,0.1384/0.15)$, and the two sides, which require $c\in(0.3826/3.6,0.1384/0.8)$. Because $c-r=(1-r)e^{-\kappa t}$ decreases, the time spent with $c$ in an interval $(c_1,c_2)$ is $\kappa^{-1}\log((c_2-r)/(c_1-r))$, at most $0.659$ for the groups and $0.504$ for the sides, both below $\tau_p=2$, so neither is selected. Every adjacent pair is coherent once $3.6c<0.1384$, which happens in finite time because $3.6r<0.018$, and it stays coherent because $c$ decreases. With no unit outside it, $\mathcal V$ is then a candidate at all later times and is selected after $\tau_p$; after that handoff no candidate remains.
\end{proof}

Check~(28) confirms the history $\{\mathcal V\}$ at $t=5.55$ on the evaluation grid from the fixed draw and twelve further states of $\mathcal A$.

\remarkheading{Selections of the shared-label flow (numerical)}{it7:events}
From every tested state in $\mathcal A$, fifteen in all (the fixed draw of checks~(7) to~(10), twelve uniform draws in check~(12), and one draw each in checks~(11) and~(14)), the shared-label flow selects $L_1$, $L_2$, $R_1$ and $R_2$, and then $L_1\cup L_2$ and $R_1\cup R_2$; on the evaluation grid the first-stage handoffs fall between $t=2.13$ and $t=2.35$ and the unions between $t=6.95$ and $t=8.05$. The selected blocks are the same for every state. Along the fixed-draw trajectory each group enters the candidate condition transversally, the slope of its coherence margin being at least $0.092$ at entry, and over the rest of its persistence window the coherence margin, $\delta_c$ minus the largest edge of the group's minimum spanning tree, stays at least $0.0045$ while the wall margin stays at least $0.43$ (check~(17)). The recorded gaps between the left parents after the first stage, from two states, lie between $0.595$ and $0.622$, at least $0.33$ below $d_u-0.005$ (checks~(9), (10) and~(14)), so by \cref{it7:counterfactual} the unions follow from certified inequalities, and only the first stage rests on integration. By continuous dependence on initial data, which holds across the switching times of the handoffs because each entry is transversal, the same blocks are selected on an open neighborhood of the fixed draw, provided the integration error on $[0,2.6]$ stays below these margins. The integration is fourth-order Runge--Kutta with fixed steps between $0.005$ and $0.05$; it was not validated by interval arithmetic.

\propositionheading{The second stage reduces to a gap equation}{it7:reduced}
After the four first-stage handoffs write $m_1=\bar y_{L_1}$, $m_2=\bar y_{L_2}$, $d=m_2-m_1$, and $s=\bar o_{L_2}-\bar o_{L_1}$, which is $1.1$ in the instance; the same statement holds for the variants of \cref{it7:depth} with record gap $s\in[1.0,1.3]$, whose left record means lie in $[-3.15,-1.85]$. While the left parent means lie in $[-3.3,-1.7]$ and the right ones in $[1.7,3.3]$,
\begin{equation}
\dot d=F(d)+r(t),\qquad F(d)=\rho(s-d)-2w_3(d)d,\qquad w_n(d)=\frac{ne^{-ad^2}}{n-1+ne^{-ad^2}},
\label{it7:gap}
\end{equation}
with $a=n\lambda/(2T)=9$ for $n=3$ and $|r(t)|\le10^{-10}$.
\begin{proof}
By \cref{it7:exact}(i) the row of $L_1$ has the evaluator with prior $\pi_s$ and cost $9m_1^2$, its own parent with prior $2\pi_p$ and cost zero, $L_2$ with prior $3\pi_p$ and cost $9d^2$, and $R_1,R_2$ with prior $3\pi_p$ each and cost $9(m_1-\bar y_R)^2$. On the stated ranges the evaluator weight relative to $2\pi_p$ is at most $\frac{\pi_s}{2\pi_p}e^{-26.0}<1.5\times10^{-12}$, and the right-side weights are below $e^{-100}$. Dropping these weights from (iii) leaves $\dot m_1=w_3(d)(m_2-m_1)+\rho(\bar o_{L_1}-m_1)$, the prior $\pi_p$ cancelling from $w_3$, and the same for $m_2$ with the roles exchanged; the dropped terms contribute less than $10^{-10}$ to $\dot d$. Subtracting the two equations gives \cref{it7:gap}. The stated ranges are forward invariant up to these terms, since each parent is pulled toward the other left parent and toward its own record mean, both inside the range.
\end{proof}

For $s=1.1$ the function $F$ has exactly three zeros in $(0,\infty)$, $d_m=0.0045647$, $d_u=0.9630362$ and $d_s=1.0831195$, each enclosed in an interval of width below $10^{-9}$, with $F'(d_m)=-1.205$, $F'(d_u)=0.0062$ and $F'(d_s)=-0.0034$ (check~(9)). Check~(27) certifies the zero count and the enclosures by interval arithmetic, using that both terms of $F$ are negative for $d\ge s$, together with the bounds $F'>0.005$ within $0.005$ of $d_u$, $F'<-0.002$ within $0.005$ of $d_s$, $F<-3.2\times10^{-5}$ on $[0.12909,d_u-0.005]$, $F>1.6\times10^{-5}$ on $[d_u+0.005,d_s-0.005]$ and $F<-1.7\times10^{-5}$ on $[d_s+0.005,1.6]$. The merged state $d_m$ and the separated state $d_s$ are stable, and the middle root $d_u$ separates their basins. The separated state is an equilibrium held by the records: without them, $\rho=0$, the only zero is $d=0$. For $s=1.0722$ the equation has one zero and for $s=1.0723$ three (check~(27)), so the middle root and the separated state appear in a saddle-node between these record gaps.

\corollaryheading{Shifted post-handoff states}{it7:counterfactual}
Let $s=1.1$ and consider post-handoff states in which $L_1,L_2,R_1,R_2$ are the selected blocks, the left parent means lie in $[-3.3,-1.7]$ with gap $d_0$, and the right ones in $[1.7,3.3]$. If $d_0<d_u-2\times10^{-8}$, the union $L_1\cup L_2$ is selected. If $d_0>d_u+2\times10^{-8}$, it is never selected, $d(t)$ stays above $\min(d_0,d_u+0.005)$, and for every $\varepsilon>5\times10^{-8}$ it eventually remains within $\varepsilon$ of $d_s$.
\begin{proof}
All inequalities used are those certified in check~(27), together with $|r|\le10^{-10}$. Since $F(d_u)=0$ and $F'>0.005$ within $0.005$ of $d_u$, $F(d)+r$ has the sign of $d-d_u$ whenever $2\times10^{-8}<|d-d_u|\le0.005$. In the first case $d$ therefore decreases to $d_u-0.005$, then through $[0.12909,d_u-0.005]$, where $F<-3.2\times10^{-5}$, below the coherence radius $0.1291$ in finite time, and it stays below it, because $F<0$ at $0.12909$ and the only zero of $F$ below that point is $d_m$. The units $L_1$ and $L_2$ are then coherent and walled from the right side, so the union is selected after the window $\tau_p$. In the second case $d$ increases to $d_u+0.005$ or starts above it, the sign bands keep it in $[d_u+0.005,1.6]$, and on $[d_s-0.005,d_s+0.005]$ the slope bound $F'<-0.002$ confines it eventually to within $10^{-10}/0.002$ of $d_s$; so $L_1$ and $L_2$ are never coherent.
\end{proof}

The handoffs whose gap was recorded, from two states, have gaps between $0.59$ and $0.63$ at $t=2.6$, after all four first-stage handoffs (checks~(9), (10) and~(14)), and so lie in the first case; all fifteen tested states unite. The shifted states of check~(10) move $L_1$ and $L_2$ apart after the first stage. Gaps of $0.595$, $0.795$ and $0.895$ unite, at $t=7.6$, $18.4$ and $61.6$; gaps of $1.095$ and $1.395$ remain near the separated state $1.0831$. Every one of these states is walled, since its gap exceeds $0.408$, so all of them lie in the domain of the selector. They are produced by shifting post-handoff states, not by applying the handoff to trajectories along which the first stage has not occurred, so they are not the counterfactual class of the standard (\cref{it7:ch-standard}).

\remarkheading{Controls and depth (numerical)}{it7:depth}

\chapter{The formation standard}
\label{it7:ch-standard}

\definitionheading{The formation standard, restated}{it7:standard-restated}
The definitions are those of the inheritance chapter of the main manuscript, specialized to deterministic constructions. A construction declares its objective and its flow $\Phi$, an admitted class of initial configurations, a selector family $S_c$ with constants $c$ in a set $\mathsf C$, and two rules: a parent protocol rule, which assigns to each block $I$ a class $A_I$ of protocols that address the block only through channels and records that read it as a whole, and a child-level probe rule, which assigns to $I$ a class $B_I$ of probes that agents, channels, records or declared external queriers can apply to individual members before a handoff of $I$, and which contains $A_J$ for every block $J$ merged into $I$. Both rules depend only on the block and on the constitutive data. A cost is a peer cost when its compared law depends on the law of another agent. The label-preserving null deletes the peer costs and keeps all labels and priors; the renormalized null also removes the labels of the deleted costs; the frozen-environment control holds every other agent's law at its initial value in each agent's force. The selector is evaluated along every control through the original formulas, and after a handoff a control remains the fine flow. The partition sequence $P(x)$ of an initial configuration $x$ is the sequence of selected partitions, two sequences being identified when they differ by the exchange of consecutive merges of disjoint sets of agents. The clauses are these.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(F1)] The admitted class is a nonempty set of initially unpartitioned configurations, open in the chart topology of the declared dynamics (full grade), or relatively open in a declared invariant family that is not contained in finitely many orbits (family grade).
\item[(F2)] The selector belongs to a family $S_c$, $c\in\mathsf C$, fixed before the flows are analyzed, with every real constant ranging over a set with nonempty interior and a stated persistence criterion, and each handoff is a measurable function of the state or of accessible history.
\item[(F3)] Each selected block $I$ carries an inherited parent: a Borel retention $R_I$ on a Borel invariant set $\Omega_I$ that contains the reachable post-handoff set $\Omega_I^{\rm reach}$, through which the parent laws (L), the dynamics (D), the joint kernels of the responses of protocols in $A_I$ and the next retained state (B), and every channel and evaluator read (C) factor.
\item[(F4)] For each handoff some $b\in B_I$ has different response laws at two configurations of $\Omega_I^{\rm reach}$ with the same retained value.
\item[(F5)] Against each null and for each $c\in\mathsf C$, there is a nonempty open subset of the admitted class on which $P(x)$ differs from the null sequence $P^0(x)$, both taken on $[0,\infty)$; the clause holds when this is true against both nulls and for every $c\in\mathsf C$.
\item[(F6)] (F6a) The parents select a block containing at least two first-stage parents, by the same selector family, constants and constitutive data, without resetting their source data. (F6b) A counterfactual class $\mathcal C$ is declared beforehand. It contains the actual first-stage handoff states and the states obtained by applying the same retentions, at the same handoff times, to trajectories from a declared class $\mathcal U$ containing the admitted class along which the first stage has not occurred, and it excludes states outside the domain of the selector. The second-stage event occurs after the actual handoffs and does not occur from a subset of $\mathcal C$ with nonempty interior.
\end{enumerate}
A construction that meets (F1), (F2), (F3) and (F6a) and is shown to fail one of (F4), (F5) and (F6b) is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance. A clause that is neither established nor shown to fail leaves the classification undetermined. The strong grade of (F5) holds when (F5) holds at every admitted point, for every $c\in\mathsf C$, against both nulls. Two of these requirements are read here as the inheritance chapter currently states them, and the verdicts below depend on them: a verdict of (F5) that holds against one null and fails against the other fails, and (F5) must hold over a declared constant set with nonempty interior.

\definitionheading{Declared classes of the instance}{it7:classes}
The parent protocol rule gives each unit $U$ the read-only class $A_U$. At a time fixed in advance, or chosen as a measurable function of earlier responses, a receiver outside $U$ queries the parent $A_U$ and receives a fresh draw from its presented law $P_U$; no record is assimilated by any member, and no member is named. The child-level probe rule gives $B_U$: a receiver attending to one member $i$ of $U$ through an identity channel receives a fresh draw from $q_i$, and a receiver querying the parent of a unit merged into $U$ receives a fresh draw from its presented law. The selector family is that of \cref{it7:selector} with the single constants $\delta_c=0.05$, $\delta_w=0.5$ and $\tau_p=2$, and persistence on the finite window $\tau_p$. The rules and the selector are fixed before the retention and the flows. No counterfactual class of the type of (F6b) is declared; \cref{it7:counterfactual} concerns shifted post-handoff states.

Each clause is now checked for the instance of \cref{it7:ch-instance}, with its status stated as proved, numerical or open.

(F2) Proved in form, with an open requirement. \chapter{A derived selector}
\label{it7:ch-derived}

The selector of \cref{it7:selector} declares two thresholds and a window. This chapter replaces the thresholds by a comparison of free energies: a block is selected when the models that the handoff would create have a lower free energy than the current ones. The handoff rule \cref{it7:handoff}, the flows and the instance are unchanged, and the window $\tau_p$ remains declared.

\definitionheading{Structure free energy and the derived selector}{it7:derived}
Let $\mathcal U$ be the current units. A coarsening of $\mathcal U$ is a partition $\mathcal Q$ of $\mathcal V$ each of whose blocks is a union of units of $\mathcal U$. For a block $U\in\mathcal Q$ with $n_U$ agents, let $\mathcal F_U(\mathcal Q)$ be the minimum over rows of the block free energy \cref{it7:block-fe} of $U$ under one shared label, whose sources are the evaluator, the parent $A_U$ when $n_U\ge2$, and the other blocks of $\mathcal Q$, each presenting its law as in \cref{it7:handoff}(b), with the priors of \cref{it7:handoff}(a); for a single agent it is the minimum of \cref{it7:fe} over the row. The structure free energy of $\mathcal Q$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F(\mathcal Q)=\sum_{U\in\mathcal Q}\mathcal F_U(\mathcal Q),
\label{it7:structure}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal F(\mathcal U)$ is the sum of the current free energies of the units. At each time let $\mathcal Q^*(t)$ be a minimizer of $\mathcal F$ over the coarsenings of $\mathcal U(t)$ at the current laws. A block of $\mathcal Q^*(t)$ that is not a unit is a candidate, and a handoff selects it once it has been a candidate without interruption for a time $\tau_p$. Several candidates may be selected at one time, and a selected block is never dissolved.

The record terms of \cref{it7:structure} are the same for every coarsening at fixed laws, so the comparison reads only the laws and the source priors. The sum runs over all blocks of $\mathcal Q$, because merging some units also changes the sources of the others. The selector is a measurable function of the history of the laws, through the candidate clocks, as (F2) requires; the clocks belong to the state of the process, as those of \cref{it7:selector} do. By the readout convention of the standard (\cref{it7:standard-restated}), the same selector is applied along every control: it evaluates \cref{it7:structure} with the actual costs at the laws of the control, and after a handoff along a control it evaluates the blocks' pools computed from the fine states. At $T=1$ each $\mathcal F_U$ is the variational free energy of a normalized model of the members' contents and records with the laws of the other blocks as data, so minimizing \cref{it7:structure} compares evidence bounds under a uniform prior over coarsenings. This is structure learning in the sense of \citet{CzegelEtAl2019}, and greedy merging by such comparisons is the scheme of Bayesian hierarchical clustering \citep{HellerGhahramani2005}.

\propositionheading{The structure free energy reads spreads and pools}{it7:structure-fe}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{it7:handoff}, let every parent present the linear pool of its members. For every coarsening $\mathcal Q$ and every block $U\in\mathcal Q$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_U(\mathcal Q)=\sum_{i\in U}\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_U)-T\log\Bigl(\pi_{UA}+\sum_\nu\pi_{U\nu}e^{-n_U\KL(\bar q_U\Vert\nu_\nu)/T}\Bigr)+\sum_{i\in U}\int-\log\ell_i(o_i\mid k)q_i(dk),
\label{it7:structure-split}
\end{equation}
where $\nu$ runs over the evaluator and the other blocks of $\mathcal Q$, and $\pi_{UA}=0$ when $n_U=1$. If the compared laws lie in a regular exponential family $\mathsf E$ and every parent presents the moment projection $P_U$ of its pool, the same holds with $\sum_{i\in U}\KL(q_i\Vert P_U)$ as the first term and $\KL(P_U\Vert\nu_\nu)$ in the exponent. In the Gaussian location family of \cref{it7:exact}, with homogeneous priors $\pi_s$ and $\pi_p$ and evaluator mean $m_G$, the structure free energy minus the record terms is
\begin{equation}
\frac12\sum_{U\in\mathcal Q}\sum_{i\in U}\Vert y_i-\bar y_U\Vert_\Lambda^2-T\sum_{U\in\mathcal Q}\log\Bigl((n_U-1)\pi_p+\pi_se^{-\frac{n_U}{2T}\Vert\bar y_U-m_G\Vert_\Lambda^2}+\sum_{V\ne U}n_V\pi_pe^{-\frac{n_U}{2T}\Vert\bar y_U-\bar y_V\Vert_\Lambda^2}\Bigr).
\label{it7:structure-gauss}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{it7:compensation} the summed cost of a source $\nu$ for the members of $U$ is $\sum_{i\in U}\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_U)+n_U\KL(\bar q_U\Vert\nu_\nu)$, and that of the parent is the first term alone. The minimum over rows of $\sum_\nu\beta_\nu c_\nu+T\KL(\beta\Vert\pi)$ is $-T\log\sum_\nu\pi_\nu e^{-c_\nu/T}$, the Gibbs variational principle. Factoring $e^{-\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_U)/T}$ out of the sum gives \cref{it7:structure-split}. For moment projections, \cref{it7:pythagoras} gives $\KL(\bar q_U\Vert\nu_\nu)=\KL(\bar q_U\Vert P_U)+\KL(P_U\Vert\nu_\nu)$ for every $\nu_\nu\in\mathsf E$, and \cref{it7:compensation} with $P_U$ in place of $\nu$ gives $\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_U)+n_U\KL(\bar q_U\Vert P_U)=\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert P_U)$, which is also the parent's cost. In the Gaussian location family $\KL(\mathcal N(y,\Lambda^{-1})\Vert\mathcal N(m,\Lambda^{-1}))=\frac12\Vert y-m\Vert_\Lambda^2$, and by \cref{it7:handoff}(a) the parent of $U$ receives the mass $(n_U-1)\pi_p$ that the members gave to one another, a block $V$ the mass $n_V\pi_p$, and the evaluator $\pi_s$.
\end{proof}

Check~(18) confirms both general forms on a six-point alphabet, and check~(20) confirms \cref{it7:structure-gauss} against explicit Gibbs rows to $6\times10^{-14}$. The first term of \cref{it7:structure-split} is $n_U$ times the generalized Jensen--Shannon divergence of the members with equal weights \citep{Lin1991}; in the Gaussian location family it is the within-block sum of squares in the Fisher metric, the objective of centroid clustering \citep{Lloyd1982}. The second term is $n_U$ times the free energy, at temperature $T/n_U$, of a single receiver that holds the pool and has one additional source at cost zero, the block's own parent, carrying the mass that the members gave to one another. For a block far from every other source the second term is close to $-T\log((n_U-1)\pi_p)$, and \cref{it7:structure-gauss} becomes a centroid criterion with a penalty for each block, of the kind that Dirichlet-process mixtures give in the limit of small variance \citep{KulisJordan2012}; here the penalty depends on the block size and is fixed by the source prior instead of being declared.

\propositionheading{Mutual attention favors a merge}{it7:mutual}
Let $i\ne j$ be single-agent units that compare each other through identity channels, with Gibbs rows $\beta_i,\beta_j$, mutual priors $\pi_{ij},\pi_{ji}$ and costs $D_{ij},D_{ji}$, and let $\mathcal Q$ merge them, so that their parent has prior $\pi_A=(\pi_{ij}+\pi_{ji})/2$ by \cref{it7:handoff}(a). Let the parent present the linear pool, or the moment projection onto a family that contains $q_i$ and $q_j$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_i+\mathcal F_j-\mathcal F_{\{i,j\}}(\mathcal Q)\ge T\log\frac{\beta_{ij}\beta_{ji}\pi_A}{\pi_{ij}\pi_{ji}}+\max(D_{ij},D_{ji}).
\label{it7:mutual-bound}
\end{equation}
Let an agent $v$ outside the pair compare $i$ and $j$ through one Markov channel, with costs $D_{vi},D_{vj}$ and current attention $\beta_{v\{ij\}}=\beta_{vi}+\beta_{vj}$ to the pair, and let it attend to the parent through the same channel with prior $\pi_{vi}+\pi_{vj}$. If the parent is the linear pool, or in the Gaussian location family its moment projection, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_v(\mathcal Q)-\mathcal F_v\le-T\log\Bigl(1-\beta_{v\{ij\}}\bigl(1-e^{-|D_{vi}-D_{vj}|/(2T)}\bigr)\Bigr).
\label{it7:outsider-bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Write $Z_i$ for the normalizer of the row of $i$. Since $\beta_{ij}=\pi_{ij}e^{-D_{ij}/T}/Z_i$, we have $\mathcal F_i=-T\log Z_i=-T\log\pi_{ij}+D_{ij}+T\log\beta_{ij}$, and likewise for $j$. The normalizer of the pair's shared row contains the term of its parent, so $\mathcal F_{\{i,j\}}(\mathcal Q)\le-T\log\pi_A+D_{iA}+D_{jA}$, where $D_{lA}$ is the cost of member $l$ for the parent. In both cases the parent's law minimizes the summed cost $\KL(q_i\Vert p)+\KL(q_j\Vert p)$ over the laws $p$ it may present, by \cref{it7:compensation} and, for the projection, \cref{it7:pythagoras}. The laws $q_j$ and $q_i$ are among them, so $D_{iA}+D_{jA}\le\min(D_{ij},D_{ji})$. Adding the three relations gives \cref{it7:mutual-bound}, because $D_{ij}+D_{ji}-\min(D_{ij},D_{ji})=\max(D_{ij},D_{ji})$.

For $v$, only the terms of $i$ and $j$ in its normalizer $Z_v$ change. Before the merge they sum to $b\le(\pi_{vi}+\pi_{vj})e^{-\min(D_{vi},D_{vj})/T}$. After it the parent's term is $(\pi_{vi}+\pi_{vj})e^{-D_{vA}/T}$, and $D_{vA}\le\frac12(D_{vi}+D_{vj})$: for the pool by convexity of $\KL$ in its second argument, after the channel, which maps the pool to the pool of the channelled laws; in the Gaussian location family by convexity of the squared norm. Since $\frac12(D_{vi}+D_{vj})-\min(D_{vi},D_{vj})=\frac12|D_{vi}-D_{vj}|$, the new term is at least $be^{-|D_{vi}-D_{vj}|/(2T)}$, so the new normalizer is at least $Z_v-b\bigl(1-e^{-|D_{vi}-D_{vj}|/(2T)}\bigr)$, and $b=\beta_{v\{ij\}}Z_v$.
\end{proof}

The first term of \cref{it7:mutual-bound} is a saving of labels, the Occam factor of Bayesian model comparison \citep{MacKay1992}: two agents that attend to each other pay for two independent choices of source in their own models and for one in the shared model, whose parent collects the mass they gave to each other. The second term is a double use of the members' laws. The parent presents the law closest to both members in summed divergence, so each member is compared with a law that contains itself, and the bound grows with the divergence between the members as long as they attend to each other. A cavity parent, which presents to each member the pool of the other members, removes the double use: for a pair its cost is $D_{ij}+D_{ji}$, and the bound keeps only its first term. It also makes the cost of the own parent differ from the cost common to all sources by a multiple of the spread, so the shared row depends on the spread and \cref{it7:exact} fails. The saving of labels remains with the cavity parent. Check~(19) confirms both bounds on $2000$ random draws on a five-point alphabet, with smallest slacks $0.088$ and $0.0011$.

\corollaryheading{The derived selector reads initial attention}{it7:derived-null}
Let the peer priors be homogeneous, $\pi_{ij}=\pi_p$. At a state where two agents satisfy
\begin{equation}
T\log\frac{\beta_{ij}\beta_{ji}}{\pi_p}+\max(D_{ij},D_{ji})>-T\sum_{v\ne i,j}\log\Bigl(1-\beta_{v\{ij\}}\bigl(1-e^{-|D_{vi}-D_{vj}|/(2T)}\bigr)\Bigr),
\label{it7:derived-null-cond}
\end{equation}
the partition into single agents does not minimize \cref{it7:structure}, so the derived selector has a candidate there. In particular, if such a pair exists at a state of the admitted class, the selector has a candidate at time zero along both $\Phi$ and $\Phi^0$.
\begin{proof}
By \cref{it7:mutual}, merging the pair lowers $\mathcal F$ by at least the left side minus the right side of \cref{it7:derived-null-cond}. The two flows start from the same state, and the selector is the same function of the state.
\end{proof}

With the declared selector an admitted class could be chosen on which the label-preserving null flow never selects (\cref{it7:null}), because the coherence radius $0.129$ lies below the spacing of the records. The derived selector has no radius to choose. It groups agents that attend to one another, and the records alone make agents attend to one another when nothing else lies closer. The criterion detects attention, not agreement: by \cref{it7:mutual-bound} the members' gain grows with $\max(D_{ij},D_{ji})$ as long as they attend to each other, so the selector merges two single agents that attend mainly to each other at any divergence, and a merge radius exists only between blocks, whose own-parent mass caps the attention term (\cref{it7:radius}). The comparison is therefore derived relative to a declared model family, a declared prior over coarsenings and a declared window. A first stage is interaction-necessary only if the interaction changes the minimizer of \cref{it7:structure} within the window. At the fixed draw of the instance, $\beta_{01}\beta_{10}=0.254$ and $\beta_{12}\beta_{21}=0.229$ exceed $\pi_p=0.086$; for the pair $\{0,1\}$ the members gain $1.737$ against the bound $1.145$, and the outsiders' free energies change by $-0.0035$. Over all $4{,}213{,}597$ partitions of the twelve agents, the minimizer of \cref{it7:structure} at the fixed draw is the four groups, with margin $1.715$ to the next (check~(21)). The enumeration can be reduced exactly. Every attention term has an argument at most $\pi_s+(N-1)\pi_p=1$, so the structure free energy is at least $\frac\lambda2$ times the within-block sum of squares. A block that holds a left and a right agent, at distance at least $3.59$ at the fixed draw, contributes at least $\frac\lambda2\cdot\frac12(3.59)^2>19$, more than the value $7.563$ of the four groups, so the minimizer respects the sides, and up to cross-side weights below $e^{-38}$ it is the union of the minimizers over the $203$ partitions of each side.

\propositionheading{The derived union criterion is a radius}{it7:radius}
After the four first-stage handoffs, let the parent means lie in the ranges of \cref{it7:reduced} and write $d=m_2-m_1$. The minimizer of \cref{it7:structure} over the coarsenings of the four units contains $L_1\cup L_2$ when $\Gamma_3(d)>10^{-10}$ and does not when $\Gamma_3(d)<-10^{-10}$, where, for two parents of $n$ members each,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_n(d)=\log\frac{2n-1}{\pi_p}-2\log\bigl(n-1+ne^{-n\lambda d^2/(2T)}\bigr)-\frac{n\lambda}{4T}d^2.
\label{it7:radius-fn}
\end{equation}
If $(2n-1)\pi_p<1$, then $\Gamma_n$ has exactly one zero $d_*(n)$ in $(0,\infty)$, is positive below it and negative above it, and is decreasing above it. In the instance, with $T=1$ and $\lambda=6$, $d_*(3)=0.7685$.
\begin{proof}
For the union, $\sum_{i\in L_1\cup L_2}(y_i-\bar y)^2=W_{L_1}+W_{L_2}+\frac{n_1n_2}{n_1+n_2}d^2$, the decomposition of a sum of squares about the pooled mean, so the spreads of $L_1$ and $L_2$ cancel between the two coarsenings, whatever their values, and the union adds $\frac\lambda2\cdot\frac32d^2$. By \cref{it7:structure-gauss} each separate parent contributes $-\log(2\pi_p+3\pi_pe^{-9d^2})$ and the union $-\log(5\pi_p)$, once the evaluator and the right units are dropped from the normalizers. As in the proof of \cref{it7:reduced}, the evaluator's weight relative to $2\pi_p$ is below $1.5\times10^{-12}$ for a left parent and smaller still for the union, and every weight between the two sides is below $e^{-100}$, which also bounds the change in the right units' free energies. Hence $\mathcal F(\text{separate})-\mathcal F(\text{union})=\Gamma_3(d)+e$ with $|e|<10^{-10}$. Every other coarsening joins a left and a right unit, whose means differ by at least $3.4$, so its spread term is at least $\frac\lambda2\cdot\frac32(3.4)^2>50$; each attention term of a block of at least three members lies in $[0,-\log(2\pi_p)]$, so the attention terms of two coarsenings of the four units differ by less than $8$, and such a coarsening has a larger structure free energy than the four separate units. The choices on the two sides are independent up to the same weights.

For the zeros put $u=d^2$ and $a=n\lambda/(2T)$. The function $h(u)=\log(n-1+ne^{-au})$ has $h''(u)=a^2n(n-1)e^{-au}/(n-1+ne^{-au})^2>0$, so $\Gamma_n$ is strictly concave in $u$. It has the value $-\log((2n-1)\pi_p)>0$ at $u=0$ and tends to $-\infty$. A strictly concave function with these properties has exactly one zero, is positive before it, negative after it, and decreasing after it, because its maximum lies before the zero.
\end{proof}

The radius lies between the merged root and the middle root of the gap equation, $d_m=0.00456<d_*=0.7685<d_u-0.005=0.958$. Check~(27) certifies $d_*(3)\in(0.76849,0.76851)$, $\Gamma_3<-1.5$ on $[0.96804,1.6]$ and $\Gamma_3<-2.5$ on $[1.09,1.11]$. For other block sizes $d_*(2)=1.087$, $d_*(4)=0.600$ and $d_*(6)=0.410$; the value $\Gamma_n(0)=-\log((2n-1)\pi_p)$, the saving of labels when two synchronized parents merge, shrinks as the parents' share of the prior grows, down to $-\log(1-\pi_s)=0.051$ when the union holds all agents. Check~(25) confirms the reduction to within $2\times10^{-12}$ over $72$ configurations in the stated ranges.

\corollaryheading{Shifted post-handoff states under the derived selector}{it7:derived-counterfactual}
Consider post-handoff states as in \cref{it7:counterfactual}, with gap $d_0$ and no running candidate clock. If $d_0<d_u-2\times10^{-8}$, the union $L_1\cup L_2$ is selected. If $d_0>d_u+2\times10^{-8}$, the gap stays above $\min(d_0,d_u+0.005)$ for all time and the union is never selected.
\begin{proof}
By the proof of \cref{it7:counterfactual}, with the certified inequalities of check~(27), in the first case $d$ falls below $d_*$, which lies in $[0.12909,d_u-0.005]$, in finite time and stays below it, so by \cref{it7:radius} the union is a block of the minimizer from then on and is selected after $\tau_p$. \end{proof}

The derived selector removes the two thresholds but not the window, which takes over their role. \chapter{Prior work and open problems}
\label{it7:ch-prior}

The ingredients have precedents. Inferred group membership that feeds back on individual updating appears in the meta-contrast model of \citet{Salzarulo2006}, where categories are recomputed from the opinion context and agents move toward prototypes. It also appears in the latent-group model of \citet{GershmanPouncyGweon2017}, where an observer infers group structure with shared choice parameters and the posterior determines social influence. Replacing locally coherent groups by representatives that act at the next stage is the hierarchical consensus scheme of \citet{PeronyEtAl2012}, with prescribed stages and no feedback to the members. \citet{Martins2009} derived bounded confidence from Bayesian updating, with stubbornness from shrinking individual uncertainty. Normalized state-dependent weights are the setting of \citet{MotschTadmor2014}, and anchoring of opinions to fixed data is the model of \citet{FriedkinJohnsen1990}. Exact block-mean dynamics of linear consensus over an external equitable partition were analyzed by \citet{MartiniEgerstedtBicchi2010} and \citet{EgerstedtEtAl2012}; \citet{OClery2013} related them to observability and coarse graining, and \citet{SchaubEtAl2016} used these partitions for cluster synchronization in nonlinear networks. Multilevel selection has been written as Bayesian inference, with the formation of new levels as structure learning \citep{CzegelEtAl2019}. We found no work that combines a shared source label for dynamically formed groups, exact block-mean closure for state-dependent rows away from synchrony, and merging that depends on the state at formation. The comparison covered the works cited here, each checked against its publisher record, and does not establish priority.

\openproblemheading{General laws}{it7:open-general}
Find an exactly inherited compressive parent for shared-label blocks outside Gaussian families of the sufficient statistic. Candidates are a retention other than the pool, such as the canonical parent of the inheritance chapter for the routed interface, a pooled parent on a declared invariant submanifold of an exponential family, or an approximate inheritance whose defect is controlled by the spread inside the block. Alternatively, show that no exact compressive parent exists for Bernoulli blocks with Gibbs rows.

\openproblemheading{Hybrid inheritance}{it7:open-hybrid}
Carry out the route of \cref{it7:mass-parent}: define the parent type with its mass, record mean, prior row and running clocks, prove that the hybrid process descends between events, at events and after composition, with the read-only class of \cref{it7:classes}, and decide whether the type records formation times, which settles the second stage of (F4). With a hard selector each handoff triggered by a transversal entry places the handoff states on a hypersurface, so a parent that records its formation time recovers one forgotten coordinate per event; a general statement of this constraint is open.

\openproblemheading{Counterfactual class}{it7:open-counterfactual}
Declare a class $\mathcal U$ of initial configurations and the resulting counterfactual class of the standard for the instance, and decide (F6b) against it. The widened initial states of check~(28) give handoff gaps below the middle root, from which the union forms, so a class on which the union fails on a set with nonempty interior must produce larger post-handoff gaps without a first stage.

\openproblemheading{A corrected convex statement}{it7:open-convex}
In the Gaussian tangent global joint of Items~2 and~3 with its own flow, decide whether parents anchored at the record means of their own clusters exclude (F6b): whether the parent gap from every handoff of a declared class approaches its limit without entering the coherence set unless the limit lies in it. Decide also whether instances meeting (F6b) exist when the parents interact only through inherited bridges, and whether declared parent anchors count as new constitutive data under (F6a).

\openproblemheading{A derived persistence criterion}{it7:open-persistence}
The derived selector of \cref{it7:ch-derived} keeps the window $\tau_p$, and the window decides whether the hierarchy stops at depth one, forms two stages, or forms one coarser stage (\cref{it7:derived-standard}). Replace it by a criterion read from the agents' models, for example accumulated evidence for the shared label in the sense of a sequential test \citep{Wald1945}, or the message counts of the registered-agreement model of the dynamics chapter, and decide whether the two-stage regime survives.

\openproblemheading{Interaction necessity at both stages}{it7:open-both}
Under the derived selector the first stage reads the attention present in the initial state (\cref{it7:mutual,it7:derived-null}). Decide whether a prior over coarsenings that offsets the saving of labels, or a cavity parent together with an exact replacement for \cref{it7:exact}, gives an admitted class on which the label-preserving null does not select the first-stage blocks while the second stage still depends on the first, and whether some instance gives interaction necessity at both stages without either change.

\openproblemheading{Persistent compression}{it7:open-persistent}
The coordinates discarded here are transient. Combine the shared label with the code-invisible sectors of Items~1 to~3, or with the flatness condition of \cref{it7:dictionaries}, so that the parent forgets coordinates that persist and that an admitted child-level probe still reads.

\openproblemheading{The reciprocal potential}{it7:open-reciprocal}
In the per-agent model a source feels no force from the receivers that attend to it. Under the reciprocal potential a parent that presents a pool receives source-role forces from every receiver. Item~2 shows that, with means, rows and the other costs frozen, the stationary variance of a single sender with one outgoing edge increases with its disagreement; that is not a statement about pooled sources along the joint flow. Determine whether source-role widening offsets the $n$-fold sharpening of the shared row, and whether exact inheritance survives the source-role terms. This is the question about certainty raised in Item~3.

\openproblemheading{Depth}{it7:open-depth}
By \cref{it7:capture-distance} the capture distance falls with block size, faster than $n^{-1/2}$ in the computed range. Determine the depth reached from random records, and whether partial pooling, with the members' costs added with weight $n^{p-1}$ for some $0<p<1$, allows any finite depth while keeping counterfactual dependence at every stage.

\openproblemheading{Validated numerics}{it7:open-validated}
Certify the first-stage events on an explicit open subset of $\mathcal A$ by interval integration, on $[0,2.6]$ for the declared selector and on $[0,1.30]$ for the derived selector. The unions then follow from the certified inequalities of check~(27), given handoff gaps below $d_u-0.005$ (and above $d_*$ for the derived selector), and with them the open-set form of (F5) and (F6a) at the declared constants.
