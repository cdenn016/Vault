\chapter{Free Energies and Coupled Dynamics of Agent Laws}
\label{ch:dynamics}

Interacting agents can be given three different objectives, and this chapter keeps them apart. In the global picture all agents are parts of one normalized joint over their contents and records, and each agent's local free energy is that global free energy viewed as a function of its own recognition factor. In the per-agent picture each agent has its own normalized generative model in the sense of \cref{def:generative-model}, in which its own evaluator and the communicated laws of its peers are competing sources of prior information. The third object is a direct interaction potential between laws, specified without an observation likelihood. Its gradient flow moves each source toward the receivers that attend to it. We first develop this reciprocal potential, its source rows, and its Fisher dynamics, because the constructions of \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation} use it. We then give the two free energies and prove two restricted obstructions to identifying the reciprocal potential with a free energy: with the source rows fixed and an identity channel on some attended edge, no normalized model over finitely many finite-valued variables reproduces it on the interior of finite alphabets under any recognition rule, and, for fixed channels on finite alphabets, no normalized model whose recognition is exactly the product of the agent laws reproduces it on any open set unless every attended peer channel is constant in its input. Outside these hypotheses exact identifications exist, and \cref{rem:dyn-no-global-scope} records them. On the linear-Gaussian mean slice the three objectives are limits of one normalized model (\cref{thm:dyn-three-limits}).

The three objectives do not have the same standing. The global free energy is the only one with an evidence, a posterior that the dynamics targets, and one functional that every local update descends, and it is the objective in which formation is posed (\cref{ch:global-formation}). The per-agent free energies describe what each agent computes when its peers' laws are data. The reciprocal potential is a configuration-level device: its results are read as statements about the global picture only through an identification such as \cref{thm:dyn-registered} or \cref{thm:dyn-three-limits}, and otherwise as results about the potential itself.

\section{A potential for the two interacting sectors}
\label{sec:dyn-potential}

Fix a context and a finite set of agents. Agent $i$ has state and model spaces $\mathsf K_i,\mathsf M_i$, both standard Borel. In this chapter its joint law is restricted explicitly to $\zeta_i=q_i\otimes s_i$. Choose a proper model reference $r_i$ and a normalized evaluator $G_i:\mathsf M_i\rightsquigarrow\mathsf K_i$. The reference joint is
\begin{equation}
 R_i(dk,dm)=r_i(dm)G_i(m,dk),\qquad
 \mathcal E_0=\sum_i\KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i).
 \label{eq:dyn-local-joint}
\end{equation}
The evaluator couples the sectors even though their recognition law is a product. Where the terms are finite, disintegration gives
\begin{equation}
 \KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i)
 =\KL(s_i\Vert r_i)+\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm).
 \label{eq:dyn-local-split}
\end{equation}
This is a conditional relative-entropy identity: the model marginal contributes the first term and the state conditional contributes the second. In particular, the expectation of a log evaluator in this objective is different from the log of the predictive mixture $s_iG_i$.

\remarkheading{What the product restriction removes}{rem:dyn-product}
Over unrestricted joint laws the unique minimizer of $\KL(\zeta_i\Vert R_i)$ is $\zeta_i=R_i$, whose belief conditional is the evaluator. Under the product restriction and an evaluator density $g_i$, the belief minimizer at fixed $s_i$ is instead $q_i\propto\exp\int\log g_i(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)$, which differs from the model prediction $s_iG_i$. The restriction therefore carries part of the distinction between belief and model. It is also not invariant under the joint-law dynamics. For binary $k,m\in\{-1,1\}$, let the joint Fisher flow be $\partial_t\log\zeta_i=-(h-\E_{\zeta_i}h)$ with $h(k,m)=\log(\zeta_i/R_i)(k,m)+h^q(k)+h^s(m)$, where the peer terms $h^q,h^s$ depend only on the marginals. The additive terms cancel in the log odds ratio $\operatorname{LOR}(\zeta)=\sum_{k,m}km\log\zeta(k,m)$, so $\partial_t\operatorname{LOR}(\zeta_i)=-(\operatorname{LOR}(\zeta_i)-\operatorname{LOR}(R_i))$. Dependence between the sectors is generated at unit rate whenever $R_i$ is not a product. The product flow used below is the restriction of the dynamics to the product family, not a consequence of it.

Let $\mathbf q=(q_i)_i$ and $\mathbf s=(s_i)_i$. For each allowed directed edge $j\to i$, choose normalized channels $\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q]:\mathsf K_j\rightsquigarrow\mathsf K_i$ and $\mathcal K^s_{ij}[\mathbf s]:\mathsf M_j\rightsquigarrow\mathsf M_i$. Their dependence on the laws is part of the specified interaction. Throughout this chapter a belief channel depends only on the belief laws and a model channel only on the model laws, not on the rows, interfaces, or memory variables that \cref{def:int-channel} also admits; a dependence on the other sector or on the rows adds the corresponding terms to \cref{eq:dyn-channel-differential} and changes the row statements of \cref{sec:dyn-rows,sec:dyn-fisher}. A law-dependent channel is required to be a kernel $\mathcal K^q_{ij}:\mathsf S\times\mathsf K_j\rightsquigarrow\mathsf K_i$, jointly in the configuration and the source value, and likewise in the model sector. Then $x\mapsto\nu^q_{ij}(x)$ is Borel by a monotone-class argument, relative entropy is Borel on pairs of laws through its supremum over finite partitions from a countable generating algebra \citep[Lemma 5.2.2]{Gray1990}, a result Gray attributes to Dobrushin, the Borel structure on laws being the one generated by the evaluations, and the costs, Gibbs rows, and reduced potential below are Borel functions of the configuration, as \cref{prop:record-law} and the record appendix require. Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu^q_{ij}&=q_j\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q],&
 D^q_{ij}&=\KL(q_i\Vert\nu^q_{ij}),\\
 \nu^s_{ij}&=s_j\mathcal K^s_{ij}[\mathbf s],&
 D^s_{ij}&=\KL(s_i\Vert\nu^s_{ij}).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dyn-peer-costs}
\end{equation}
The source sets $\mathcal N_i^q,\mathcal N_i^s$ are finite and nonempty, with positive normalized reference rows $\pi_i^q,\pi_i^s$. Belief routing uses $\beta_i$ and model routing uses $\gamma_i$ on their respective sets.

\definitionheading{Reciprocal law potential}{def:dyn-potential}
For fixed $T_q,T_s>0$, set
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E={}&\mathcal E_0
 +\sum_i\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^q}\beta_{ij}D^q_{ij}
                 +T_q\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i^q)\right]\\
 &+\sum_i\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^s}\gamma_{ij}D^s_{ij}
                 +T_s\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\pi_i^s)\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dyn-potential}
\end{equation}
The reference laws, evaluators, source priors, and temperatures are held fixed in the variations below. The admissible domain consists of laws for which all displayed divergences are finite.

This potential is a constitutive choice at the level of agent laws. No distribution over these law configurations is required to define it. It contains no record; records enter its dynamics only through the assimilation events of \cref{def:dyn-record-interrupted}. Its gradient is reciprocal: a law that serves as a source appears in the cost of every receiver that attends to it, so the flow moves sources as well as receivers (\cref{sec:dyn-variation}). \Cref{sec:dyn-per-agent} gives the per-agent alternative in which peers' laws are data, and \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} shows, under its stated hypotheses, that this potential is not a global variational free energy. General probability laws enter \cref{eq:dyn-potential}; differentiability will require stronger hypotheses. An identity self-channel has $D^q_{ii}=0$ throughout its domain, with the same convention in the model sector. Independent opposite attending edges remain independent data.

The receiver side of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} is a pooling rule. With the other agents, rows, and channels frozen, and with the appearances of $q_i$ as a source ignored, stationarity of $q_i$ gives $\log q_i=(1+\sum_j\beta_{ij})^{-1}\bigl(\int\log g_i(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log\nu^q_{ij}\bigr)+\mathrm{const}$, a logarithmic opinion pool of the evaluator and the compared laws \citep{GenestZidek1986,Abbas2009}. Iterated relative-entropy averaging over a network is the consensus scheme of \citet{BattistelliChisci2014}, with \citet{DeGroot1974} as its linear antecedent. The source side, which \cref{prop:dyn-compensation} identifies, is not part of these pooling rules.

The correlated local inference of \cref{sec:conditional-prediction,cmem:free-energy} makes a different use of the same generative-model definition: it conditions a declared joint exactly before any output projection. Its component evidence weights and finite conditioning step do not identify it with the product-family population flow considered here.

\section{Source selection and reduction}
\label{sec:dyn-rows}

\propositionheading{Gibbs rows and the exact reduced potential}{prop:dyn-gibbs}
Hold the agent laws fixed and suppose every source cost is finite. Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Z_i^q&=\sum_j\pi_{ij}^q e^{-D^q_{ij}/T_q},&
 \beta_{ij}^*&=\frac{\pi_{ij}^q e^{-D^q_{ij}/T_q}}{Z_i^q},\\
 Z_i^s&=\sum_j\pi_{ij}^s e^{-D^s_{ij}/T_s},&
 \gamma_{ij}^*&=\frac{\pi_{ij}^s e^{-D^s_{ij}/T_s}}{Z_i^s}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dyn-gibbs}
\end{equation}
These are the unique minimizing rows. With
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}
 =\mathcal E_0-T_q\sum_i\log Z_i^q-T_s\sum_i\log Z_i^s,
 \label{eq:dyn-reduced}
\end{equation}
the exact identity is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}
 +T_q\sum_i\KL(\beta_i\Vert\beta_i^*)
 +T_s\sum_i\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\gamma_i^*).
 \label{eq:dyn-row-gap}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
For one row, $\log\beta_j^*=\log\pi_j-D_j/T-\log Z$. Substitute this expression into $T\KL(\beta\Vert\beta^*)$ to obtain the row objective plus $T\log Z$. Summing proves \cref{eq:dyn-row-gap}. Relative entropy is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when its two probability laws agree, which proves uniqueness, including competitors on the simplex boundary.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:dyn-gibbs} is the finite case of the Gibbs variational principle, the convex duality between relative entropy and the log moment generating function \citep{DonskerVaradhan1975,DupuisEllis1997}. What is specific here is that the costs $D_{ij}$ are themselves divergences between agent laws, so the rows move with the laws they select.

On a differentiable family, direct differentiation of the finite sums in \cref{eq:dyn-gibbs} gives
\begin{equation}
 d\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}
 =d\mathcal E_0+\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}^*dD^q_{ij}
                    +\sum_{ij}\gamma_{ij}^*dD^s_{ij}.
 \label{eq:dyn-envelope}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $-T_qd\log Z_i^q=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^*dD^q_{ij}$, and the other sector is identical. There is no derivative of an optimized row to add. Every dependence of its cost on the laws still belongs in $dD$. If a source prior were varied, this formula would also contain $-T_q\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}^*d\log\pi_{ij}^q$ and its model counterpart.

\remarkheading{Identity self sources and learned source priors}{rem:dyn-self-sources}
Suppose the source set of $i$ contains $i$ with an identity self-channel, so $D^q_{ii}=0$. Since every cost is nonnegative, $\pi_{ii}^q\leq Z_i^q\leq1$, hence $\beta_{ii}^*\geq\pi_{ii}^q$ and $\beta_{ij}^*\leq(\pi_{ij}^q/\pi_{ii}^q)e^{-D^q_{ij}/T_q}$ for $j\neq i$. A peer whose divergence exceeds $T_q\log(\pi^q_{ij}/(\pi^q_{ii}\varepsilon))$ receives row weight below $\varepsilon$, so at fixed priors the reduced interaction is a smooth form of bounded-confidence opinion dynamics \citep{HegselmannKrause2002}. If instead the prior row $\pi_i^q$ is made a dynamical table of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} with unit Fisher mobility, then $\partial\mathcal E/\partial\pi^q_{ij}=-T_q\beta_{ij}/\pi^q_{ij}$ and its Fisher flow is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \dot\pi_i^q=T_q(\beta_i-\pi_i^q).
 \label{eq:dyn-prior-learning}
\end{equation}
With Gibbs rows this is $\dot\pi^q_{ij}=(T_q/Z_i^q)\pi^q_{ij}(e^{-D^q_{ij}/T_q}-Z_i^q)$, a replicator equation with fitness $e^{-D/T_q}$ \citep{HofbauerSigmund1998}, slow at small $T_q$ without an inserted multiplier. An identity self source then has $\dot\pi^q_{ii}=(T_q/Z_i^q)\pi^q_{ii}(1-Z_i^q)\geq0$, and the infimum of the row terms over priors and rows is approached in the limit of isolation, where each agent attends only to itself. A learned-prior mechanism therefore needs self excluded from the learnable rows or its self prior held fixed. Whether learned rows without self concentrate on a pairing is open.

\section{The derivative includes every interaction role}
\label{sec:dyn-variation}

To display the variation, choose densities against fixed references on a regular dominated family. Write $a$ for the density of a receiver law and $b$ for that of its compared law. For mass-preserving variations and justified differentiation under the integrals,
\begin{equation}
 d\KL(a\Vert b)
 =\int\log(a/b)da-\int(a/b)db.
 \label{eq:dyn-kl-differential}
\end{equation}
Here the notation $\int f da$ denotes integration against the signed variation of the law, rather than differentiation of the sample coordinate. The formula follows by differentiating $a\log(a/b)$; the additional integral of $da$ is zero.

For a belief interaction the compared-law variation is
\begin{equation}
 d\nu^q_{ij}
 =(dq_j)\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q]
  +q_jD\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q](d\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:dyn-channel-differential}
\end{equation}
The second term differentiates the prescribed channel itself. Thus an agent contributes when it is a receiver, when it is a source, and whenever its law changes a channel elsewhere. For fixed discrete kernels, the incoming contribution from $j\to i$ to a variation at source value $v$ is
\begin{equation}
 -\beta_{ij}\sum_u
 \frac{q_i(u)}{\nu^q_{ij}(u)}\mathcal K^q_{ij}(v,u).
 \label{eq:dyn-incoming}
\end{equation}
The model sector has the same expression with $q,\beta$ replaced by $s,\gamma$. If the channels are constructed from law-dependent moments or generators, \cref{eq:dyn-channel-differential} also differentiates those moments and generators. Keeping their numerical values fixed computes a different vector field.

An identity self-channel illustrates why the variable identification matters. Here $a=b$ and $da=db$, so \cref{eq:dyn-kl-differential} is zero throughout the state space. Freezing the source as an old snapshot while moving the receiver replaces this zero functional by a divergence from that snapshot. Its first derivative still vanishes at equality, but its curvature and its force after displacement differ. More generally, directed source selection does not make the gradient of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} one-way: a source appears in the energy of every row that attends to it. Each directed edge $j\to i$ therefore carries influence in both directions. The force on the source is the receiver likelihood ratio $q_i/\nu^q_{ij}$ pulled back through the adjoint of the channel, so it is supported on what the channel reads; for a deterministic readout $T$ it is a function of $T(v)$, and only the message marginal of the source moves. The communication model of \cref{ch:prediction} sends messages from source to receiver only. A population of communicating agents realizes the reciprocal flow only if this reverse message is also modeled; otherwise each source must be able to read the laws of every receiver that attends to it. Every agent descends the same total $\mathcal E$, so the population plays an identical-interest game in the sense of \citet{MondererShapley1996}. \Cref{sec:dyn-per-agent} shows what is lost when each agent descends only its own objective.

\propositionheading{Receiver and source pools}{prop:dyn-compensation}
Let the receivers $i\in I_j$ of a source $j$ use a common fixed channel $K_j$, with weights $\beta_{ij}>0$, $W_j=\sum_{i\in I_j}\beta_{ij}$, and pooled receiver law $\bar q_{\to j}=W_j^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_j}\beta_{ij}q_i$. If all displayed divergences are finite,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i\in I_j}\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i\Vert q_jK_j)
 =\sum_{i\in I_j}\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i\Vert\bar q_{\to j})
 +W_j\KL(\bar q_{\to j}\Vert q_jK_j).
 \label{eq:dyn-compensation}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Expand both sides with $\int\log$ of densities against a common reference. The receiver entropies agree. On the right, $-\sum_i\beta_{ij}\int\log\bar q_{\to j}dq_i+W_j\int\log\bar q_{\to j}d\bar q_{\to j}=0$ because $\sum_i\beta_{ij}q_i=W_j\bar q_{\to j}$, and the remaining cross term equals the left cross term for the same reason.
\end{proof}

\Cref{eq:dyn-compensation} is the compensation identity for the relative entropy of a mixture \citep{Topsoe1979}, applied with the common channel. The first term on the right does not contain $q_j$. At fixed rows the complete source-role force on $q_j$ is therefore the force of $W_j\KL(\bar q_{\to j}\Vert q_jK_j)$, which fits one source law to the arithmetic pool of its receivers in the mass-covering direction, while the receiver-role force is the logarithmic pool above. In a Gaussian covariance sector with identity channel, the source force on the variance $v_j$ from one receiver $\mathcal N(m_i,v_i)$ is $(v_j-v_i-(m_i-m_j)^2)/(2v_j^2)$, which vanishes at $v_j=v_i+(m_i-m_j)^2$: a source more confident than its receivers' spread is widened.

\section{Fisher dynamics with equal mobilities}
\label{sec:dyn-fisher}

The kinetic metric in this chapter uses its declared product recognition family. It is the product specialization of \cref{geo:joint-fisher}, with separate Fisher blocks for the source rows. The contextual tensor in \cref{geo:pullback} instead evaluates changes along the base through a connection; it does not replace the vertical metric used for inference at a fixed context.

\theoremheading{Local flow and exact dissipation on positive tables}{thm:dyn-finite-flow}
Suppose the state and model alphabets are finite, all $r_i$ and evaluator entries $G_i(m,k)$ are strictly positive, and the channel entries are twice continuously differentiable functions of the interior probability tables. Assume every compared-law entry is positive there. Let $u^a$ range over all tables $q_i,s_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i$. Equip their product with the Fisher metric
\begin{equation}
 g_u(v,w)=\sum_a\sum_z\frac{v_z^aw_z^a}{u_z^a},
 \qquad \sum_zv_z^a=\sum_zw_z^a=0.
 \label{eq:dyn-fisher-metric}
\end{equation}
For every interior initial condition there is a unique maximal interior solution of
\begin{equation}
 \dot u_z^a=-u_z^a\left(h_z^a-\sum_wu_w^ah_w^a\right),
 \qquad h_z^a=\frac{\partial\mathcal E}{\partial u_z^a}.
 \label{eq:dyn-fisher-flow}
\end{equation}
Along this solution,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}
 =-\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{u^a}(h^a)\leq0.
 \label{eq:dyn-dissipation}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
The stated positivity and regularity make $\mathcal E$ twice continuously differentiable in interior simplex coordinates. The right side of \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} is therefore locally Lipschitz, giving a unique local solution and its maximal continuation in the interior. Its entries sum to zero within each table. Centering $h^a$ removes any ambiguity from extending $\mathcal E$ off the normalized tables. For every tangent vector $v$, the metric pairing of $u^a(h^a-\E_{u^a}h^a)$ with $v^a$ equals $\sum_zh_z^av_z^a$. This identifies the Fisher gradient. Finally, substitute \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} into $\dot{\mathcal E}=\sum_{a,z}h_z^a\dot u_z^a$ to obtain the sum of negative variances. Interior continuation does not assert that the trajectory stays away from the boundary for all time.
\end{proof}

On a single simplex \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} is the replicator equation with fitness $-h$, the metric \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-metric} is the Shahshahani metric, and its identification as a gradient flow is Shahshahani's theorem \citep{Shahshahani1979,HofbauerSigmund1998}; \cref{eq:dyn-dissipation} is the corresponding form of Fisher's fundamental theorem. \Cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow} is that statement on a product of simplices with law-dependent channels. For fixed channels the local statement extends to all time.

\propositionheading{Global flow for fixed channels}{prop:dyn-global-flow}
In addition to the hypotheses of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow}, suppose every channel is independent of the laws. Positivity of every compared law on the interior then means that no channel has a null column, so $c_{\min}=\min_{i,j,z}\sum_v\mathcal K_{ij}(v,z)>0$ in each sector. Then every interior solution of \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} exists for all $t\geq0$, its entries are bounded below by a positive constant uniformly in time, $\int_0^\infty\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{u^a}(h^a)dt\leq\mathcal E(u(0))$, and it converges to a single critical point of $\mathcal E$.
\begin{proof}
Consider the belief tables. Constants in $h$ cancel on centering, so replace the belief force of agent $i$ by $\tilde h_z=\log q_i(z)-\int\log G_i(m,z)s_i(dm)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log(q_i(z)/\nu_{ij}(z))+\sigma_z$, where $\sigma_z\leq0$ collects the incoming terms \cref{eq:dyn-incoming}. Let $m(t)$ be the minimum of $\log q_i(z)$ over agents and states, attained at $(i,z)$. Every entry of every belief table is at least $e^m$, so $\nu_{ij}(z)\geq c_{\min}e^m$ and $\tilde h_z\leq m+C_G+\log^+(1/c_{\min})$, with $C_G=\max(-\log G)$. On the other hand $\E_{q_i}\tilde h\geq-\log|\mathsf K_i|-N$, where $N$ is the number of agents, which bounds the total row weight $\sum_k\beta_{ki}$ of the receivers attending to $i$: the entropy term is at least $-\log|\mathsf K_i|$, the evaluator and receiver terms are nonnegative, and each incoming term averages to $-\beta_{ki}$ under $q_i$. Hence the lower Dini derivative of $m$ is at least $-m-C$ with $C=C_G+\log^+(1/c_{\min})+\log\max_i|\mathsf K_i|+N$, and $m(t)\geq\min(m(0),-C)$. The model tables are treated in the same way; their force also contains the evaluator term $-\sum_kq_i(k)\log G_i(m,k)\leq C_G$, so the model constant is $C+\max_{i,m}(-\log r_i(m))$, with $\log\max_i|\mathsf M_i|$ in place of $\log\max_i|\mathsf K_i|$. With these floors every $D_{ij}$ is bounded by a constant $\bar D$, and the same argument applied to $h_j=D_{ij}+T(\log(\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij})+1)$ bounds the rows below by $\min(\log\beta_{ij}(0),-\bar D/T-\log(1/\min\pi))$ in logarithm. The trajectory therefore stays in a compact subset of the interior, so it is global. Since $\mathcal E\geq0$, integrating \cref{eq:dyn-dissipation} bounds the dissipated variance. With fixed channels $\mathcal E$ is real analytic on the interior of the product of simplices, and on the compact set the Fisher metric is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one, so the {\L}ojasiewicz gradient inequality \citep{Lojasiewicz1963} gives finite length, and hence convergence to one critical point, of the bounded trajectory; this is {\L}ojasiewicz's convergence theorem for gradient trajectories of real-analytic functions, of which \citet{AbsilMahonyAndrews2005} give the discrete-iterate counterpart.
\end{proof}

For law-dependent channels the channel-derivative term of \cref{eq:dyn-channel-differential} can be unbounded near the boundary, and no statement of this kind follows without further hypotheses.

\propositionheading{Row relaxation}{prop:dyn-row-relaxation}
Hold all costs of one row fixed and write $l_j=\log(\beta_j/\beta_j^*)$ with $\beta^*$ from \cref{eq:dyn-gibbs}. Under \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow}, $\frac{d}{dt}(l_j-l_k)=-T(l_j-l_k)$ for every pair of sources.
\begin{proof}
The row force is $h_j=D_j+T(\log(\beta_j/\pi_j)+1)=Tl_j+T(1-\log Z)$, and \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} gives $\frac{d}{dt}\log\beta_j=-(h_j-\E_\beta h)$.
\end{proof}

All blocks have unit mobility, and \cref{prop:dyn-row-relaxation} shows its price: under unit mobility, log-odds contrasts within a row relax at rate exactly $T$, so rows that select sharply are slow. Exact Gibbs rows are the formal limit of infinite row mobility, in which the strictly convex row objective is slaved to its unique interior minimizer; this is a Tikhonov-type singular limit, which is not proved here, and it is not a property of the unit-mobility flow. One may optimize the source rows at each state and apply the same construction to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$ and the matter tables alone; \cref{eq:dyn-envelope} gives its complete derivative. The two flows are different vector fields with different transients, but they share their critical points, Morse indices, and nullities (\cref{prop:dyn-morse}), hence their linear stability at nondegenerate critical points. Their local minima also correspond: $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}+T_q\sum_i\KL(\beta_i\Vert\beta_i^*)+T_s\sum_i\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\gamma_i^*)\geq\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$ with equality on the graph of the Gibbs rows, which are continuous in the laws. At degenerate critical points the Hessians do not decide Lyapunov stability, and no equivalence of the two flows' stability is claimed there. Equal mobility has a justification beyond convention. Unit mobilities are the Fisher metric of the single product law of all tables, regarded as one law of all represented variables and source labels, because the Fisher metric of a product law restricted to the product family is the sum of the factor metrics. If the kinetic metric is required to be the restriction of a metric on the law space of all these variables jointly that is invariant under Markov congruent embeddings, \v{C}encov's theorem \citep{Cencov1982} forces it, on finite sample spaces, to be a constant multiple of the Fisher metric, so equal mobility is fixed up to one global time scale. The product step holds on general spaces, since cross terms of centered scores of independent factors vanish; the uniqueness step on general spaces needs, in addition to invariance under sufficient statistics, that the metric be a local and weakly continuous tensor field on parametrized measure models, which is the setting of \citet{AyJostLeSchwachhofer2015}. In the registered-agreement model of \cref{thm:dyn-registered} the configuration is a lattice variable with a point recognition, and that model does not itself supply a kinetic metric. Imposing invariance on each table separately allows an independent constant for each table, equivalent to replicating that table. A persistent slow model sector would have to follow from the interactions and their state, rather than from an inserted small multiplier in its equation.

\propositionheading{Full and reduced flows have the same critical structure}{prop:dyn-morse}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow}, $(u,\beta^*(u),\gamma^*(u))$ is a critical point of $\mathcal E$ exactly when $u$ is a critical point of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$, and at such a point the Hessians of $\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$ on the tangent spaces have the same number of negative and zero eigenvalues.
\begin{proof}
At fixed laws the row terms are $\sum_j\beta_jD_j+T\KL(\beta\Vert\pi)$, whose Hessian on the tangent space of the simplex is $T$ times the Fisher metric, hence positive definite; the row optimum is $\beta^*$, and \cref{eq:dyn-envelope} identifies the critical points. The Hessian of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$ is the Schur complement of the positive-definite row block in the Hessian of $\mathcal E$, and Haynsworth's inertia additivity \citep{Haynsworth1968} gives the claim.
\end{proof}

For a regular finite-dimensional family with positive-definite Fisher tensor $g_{ab}(\theta)$, the corresponding equation is $\dot\theta^a=-(g^{-1})^{ab}\partial_b\mathcal E$ \citep{Amari1998}. Whenever the derivatives exist, contraction gives $\dot{\mathcal E}=-(\partial\mathcal E)^Tg^{-1}(\partial\mathcal E)$. Bimeasurable re-expression of samples, carrying all kernels and reference laws with it, preserves relative entropy and Fisher score inner products. On a family preserved by that re-expression, the gradient therefore transforms as a tangent vector; this is passive covariance. Active equivariance would further need equivariant constitutive maps for the law-dependent channels, as in \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance}, and transported references and evaluators. These statements concern fixed coordinate changes; coordinates that themselves evolve require their coordinate-velocity term.

\definitionheading{Restricted-family flow}{def:dyn-restricted-flow}
Let $\theta\mapsto p_\theta$ be an admitted regular finite-dimensional family for some of the laws, with Fisher tensor $g(\theta)$. The restricted-family flow is $\dot\theta=-g(\theta)^{-1}\partial_\theta\mathcal E(p_\theta)$. For a Gaussian law $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$ on $\R^d$, with $\nabla_\mu\mathcal E$ and the symmetric gradient $\nabla_\Sigma\mathcal E$, it reads
\begin{equation}
 \dot\mu=-\Sigma\nabla_\mu\mathcal E,
 \qquad
 \dot\Sigma=-2\Sigma(\nabla_\Sigma\mathcal E)\Sigma.
 \label{eq:dyn-gaussian-flow}
\end{equation}

The restricted velocity is $-g(\theta)^{-1}$ applied to the score pairing of the first variation, $\partial_{\theta^a}\mathcal E=\E_{p_\theta}[\varphi\,\partial_a\log p_\theta]$. When the full velocity $-(\varphi-\E\varphi)$ has finite Fisher norm, this is its Fisher-orthogonal projection onto the tangent space of the family. That norm can be infinite: with an identity channel the source term of a Gaussian source $q_i$ contains $q_k/q_i$ for each attending receiver $q_k$, and $\E_{q_i}[(q_k/q_i)^2]$ is finite exactly when $2\Sigma_k^{-1}-\Sigma_i^{-1}$ is positive definite. The score pairing remains finite, since $\E_{q_i}[\psi q_k/q_i]=\E_{q_k}\psi$ for polynomial $\psi$, so \cref{eq:dyn-gaussian-flow} is defined in that case as well, as a weak projection. The restricted flow is a solution of the full equation only on families to which the full velocity is tangent, and the source role prevents this for Gaussian agents under the reciprocal potential. No result of this chapter establishes well-posedness of the full density-level flow from Gaussian initial data, since Gaussian laws with different parameters do not share a bounded log-density chart.

\propositionheading{Gaussian agents are not an invariant family}{prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance}
Let all belief laws be Gaussian on $\R^d$ with identity channels and fixed rows, let the evaluators be Gaussian with means affine in $m$ and fixed covariance, and let the model laws have finite second moments. Suppose at least one receiver $k\neq i$ attends to $i$ with $\beta_{ki}>0$ and $q_k\neq q_i$. Then the Fisher velocity $-(\varphi-\E_{q_i}\varphi)$ of $q_i$, with $\varphi$ the first variation of $\mathcal E$ in $q_i$, is not the logarithmic derivative of any differentiable curve of Gaussian laws.
\begin{proof}
For a curve of Gaussian laws, $\partial_t\log q$ is a polynomial of degree at most two. The local term contributes $\log q_i-\int\log g_i(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)$, and each receiver term $\beta_{ij}\log(q_i/q_j)$; both are polynomials of degree at most two. By \cref{eq:dyn-incoming} with identity channels, the source terms sum to $-\sum_k\beta_{ki}q_k/q_i=-\sum_k\beta_{ki}e^{Q_k}$ over the attending receivers, with each $Q_k=\log(q_k/q_i)$ a polynomial of degree at most two. Choose a line on which $Q_k$ is nonconstant for the given receiver; it exists because $q_k\neq q_i$. Restricted to it, every term with nonconstant $Q_k$ either grows faster than every polynomial at one end of the line, when the leading coefficient of $Q_k$ there is positive, or tends to zero at both ends, when it is a negative quadratic coefficient. If some term grows, the positive sum grows faster than every polynomial at that end. Otherwise the sum of the nonconstant terms is positive and tends to zero at both ends, which no polynomial does. In either case the source sum is not a polynomial on the line, while all other terms of $\varphi$ are. Hence $\varphi$ is not a polynomial of degree at most two.
\end{proof}

The obstruction belongs to the source role of the reciprocal potential, and the two free-energy pictures do not have it.

\propositionheading{Gaussian closure of the per-agent and global flows}{prop:dyn-gaussian-closure}
Let $\mathsf K_i,\mathsf M_i$ be Euclidean spaces. In the per-agent picture of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent}, let the model references $r_i$ be Gaussian, the evaluators $G_i(m,\cdot)$ Gaussian with mean affine in $m$ and fixed covariance, every channel linear Gaussian, so that each compared law $\nu^q_{ij},\nu^s_{il}$ of a Gaussian source is Gaussian, and every record log likelihood $\log\ell_i(o\mid k,m)$ a polynomial of degree at most two in $(k,m)$. At every configuration of Gaussian laws $q_i,s_i$ the first variations of $\mathcal F_i$ in $q_i$ and in $s_i$ are polynomials of degree at most two, for all row values, so the per-agent Fisher velocity is tangent to the Gaussian product family. In the global picture of \cref{prop:dyn-global-local}, let the prior be Gaussian and every likelihood factor log quadratic in the contents it reads; then at every Gaussian product recognition the mean field $\bar e_i$ is a polynomial of degree at most two in $y_i$, the block minimizer $Q_i^*$ is Gaussian, and the Fisher velocity of $\mathcal F$ in $Q_i$ is tangent to the Gaussian family.
\begin{proof}
In the per-agent picture the peers' laws are data, so no source term appears. The first variation of \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy} in $q_i$ is $\log q_i-\sum_j\beta_{ij}\int\log\kappa_{ij}(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)-\int\log\ell_i(o\mid\cdot,m)s_i(dm)$ up to a constant, using $\sum_j\beta_{ij}=1$. The logarithm of a Gaussian evaluator with affine mean is a quadratic polynomial in $(k,m)$, so its integral against $s_i$ is a quadratic polynomial in $k$ whose coefficients involve the first two moments of $s_i$; the compared laws are Gaussian in $k$; the record term is quadratic by hypothesis. The first variation in $s_i$ is $\log s_i-\sum_l\gamma_{il}\log\rho_{il}+\beta_{ii}\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))+\E_{q_i}[-\log\ell_i(o\mid k,m)]$ up to constants and terms independent of $m$; the relative entropy between Gaussians of equal covariance with mean affine in $m$ is quadratic in $m$, and the other terms are quadratic by hypothesis. In the global picture $\log p$ is a quadratic polynomial of $y$, so $\bar e_i=\int\log p\,dQ_{-i}$ is a quadratic polynomial of $y_i$ whenever the factors $Q_j$ have finite second moments, and the first variation $\log a_i-\bar e_i$ of \cref{eq:dyn-local-free-energy} is quadratic. A centered quadratic polynomial is the logarithmic velocity of a curve of Gaussian laws, which proves tangency.
\end{proof}

Under these hypotheses \cref{eq:dyn-gaussian-flow}, applied to $\mathcal F_i$ or to $\mathcal F$, gives a solution of the full density-level equation, and it is the full flow wherever that equation has unique solutions, for as long as the covariances of the restricted solution stay positive definite. Tangency is a feature of the Gaussian family, not a property of general laws: for non-Gaussian evaluators, channels, or records, neither free-energy picture preserves a finite-dimensional family in general. Noninvariance under the reciprocal potential and its evidence-augmented form is a property of their source role.

The inheritance and formation results of \cref{ch:smooth-closure} to \cref{ch:open-formation} are proved for full density charts or for families declared invariant under the full flow in their own chapters. They do not transfer to the restricted Gaussian flow without a separate proof. Channels that are constant in their input, as in \cref{ch:conditional-formation}, remove the source term and with it this obstruction.

The density-level flow used in those chapters needs its own hypotheses. For a law $q$ on a standard Borel space with probability reference $\lambda$, write $q_l=e^l\lambda/\E_\lambda e^l$ for $l$ in the chart $L^\infty_0(\lambda)=\{l\in L^\infty(\lambda):\E_\lambda l=0\}$, and use log-odds coordinates for rows. Suppose $\mathcal E$ has a first variation $\varphi_q\in L^\infty(\lambda)$ in each law, in the sense that $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal E(q_{l(t)})=\sum\int\varphi_q d\dot q$ along every continuously differentiable chart curve.

\definitionheading{Fisher flow in bounded log-density charts}{def:dyn-chart-flow}
The chart Fisher flow is $\dot l=-(\varphi_{q_l}-\E_\lambda\varphi_{q_l})$ for every law, together with \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} for the rows.

\propositionheading{Local well-posedness in charts}{prop:dyn-chart-flow}
Suppose the map from chart coordinates to the first variations $\varphi_q\in L^\infty(\lambda)$ is Lipschitz on bounded sets. Then for every initial point there is a unique maximal continuously differentiable solution on $[0,t^*)$, and if $t^*<\infty$ the chart coordinates are unbounded as $t\to t^*$. Along it, $\partial_t\log q=-(\varphi_q-\E_q\varphi_q)$ and $d\mathcal E/dt=-\sum\operatorname{Var}_q(\varphi_q)\leq0$, the sum running over all laws and rows. The hypothesis holds for relative-entropy terms when the reference laws and evaluators have bounded log densities against $\lambda$ and every channel is either an identity between agents sharing $\lambda$ or a fixed kernel with densities bounded above and below by positive constants; for law-dependent channels it is an additional hypothesis on the first variation of the channel.
\begin{proof}
The maps $l\mapsto\E_{q_l}f=\E_\lambda[fe^l]/\E_\lambda e^l$ are locally Lipschitz in $(l,f)\in L^\infty\times L^\infty$, so the vector field is Lipschitz on bounded sets, and the Picard--Lindel\"of theorem in a Banach space gives local existence, uniqueness, and the continuation alternative \citep{Dieudonne1960}. Since $\log q_l=l-\log\E_\lambda e^l$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\log\E_\lambda e^l=\E_q\dot l$, the logarithmic velocity is $-(\varphi_q-\E_q\varphi_q)$; integrating it against $\varphi_q dq$ gives the variance. Under the stated bounds, the receiver terms $\log(q_i/\nu_{ij})$ and evaluator terms are bounded, the source terms $-\int(q_i/\nu_{ij})(w)\mathcal K_{ij}(v,dw)$ are bounded, and each is a composition of exponentials, logarithms on bounded ranges, and integrals against fixed bounded kernels, hence Lipschitz on bounded sets.
\end{proof}

For fixed channels the maximum-principle argument of \cref{prop:dyn-global-flow} transfers to the chart, with essential infima and suprema in place of minima and maxima. Because the densities are taken against the probability reference $\lambda$, the constants do not depend on the size of the sample space.

\propositionheading{Global chart flow for fixed channels}{prop:dyn-chart-global}
Assume the sufficient conditions of \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow} with every channel fixed: $|\log g_i|\leq C_g$ and $|\log r_i|\leq C_r$ for the evaluator and reference densities, and every channel either an identity between agents sharing a reference or a fixed kernel whose density lies in $[c,C_K]$ with $0<c\leq C_K<\infty$. Let the rows be fixed or follow \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow}. Put $C=C_g+C_r$, let $N$ be the number of agents, and set
\begin{equation}
 A=2C+N+\max(0,-\log c),\qquad
 B=2\mathcal E(0)+3C+\max(0,\log C_K)+N(1+C_K/c).
 \label{eq:dyn-chart-global-constants}
\end{equation}
Then every chart solution exists for all $t\geq0$, and the essential infimum $m(t)$ and essential supremum $M(t)$, over all agents and both sectors, of the log densities against their references satisfy $m(t)\geq\min(m(0),-A)$ and $M(t)\leq\max(M(0),B)$. In particular $\int_0^\infty\sum\operatorname{Var}_q(\varphi_q)dt\leq\mathcal E(0)$.
\begin{proof}
Write $u=\log(dq_i/d\lambda)$ for a belief density; $t\mapsto u$ is continuously differentiable into $L^\infty$, and $\dot u=\E_{q_i}\varphi_i-\varphi_i$. Since $\lambda$ is a probability law, $m\leq0\leq M$. By \cref{eq:dyn-kl-differential} the first variation is, up to constants, $\varphi_i=u-\int\log g_i(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log(q_i/\nu_{ij})-\sum_k\beta_{ki}\int(q_k/\nu_{ki})(w)\mathcal K_{ki}(\cdot,dw)$. For the lower bound, the evaluator term is at most $C_g$, a kernel receiver term is at most $u-\log c=(u-m)+(m-\log c)\leq(u-m)+\max(0,-\log c)$ because $\nu\geq c$ and $m\leq0$, an identity receiver term $u-\log q_j$ is at most $u-m$, and the source terms are nonpositive. Since $\sum_j\beta_{ij}\leq1$, $\varphi_i\leq2u-m+C_g+\max(0,-\log c)$. On the other side $\E_{q_i}\varphi_i\geq-C_g-N$, because $\KL(q_i\Vert\lambda)$ and the receiver divergences are nonnegative and each source term integrates to $-\beta_{ki}$ under $q_i$. Hence $\dot u\geq m-A-2u$ almost everywhere. For $0<h<1/2$, the expansion $u(t+h)=u+h\dot u+o(h)$ in $L^\infty$ and $u\geq m$ almost everywhere give $u(t+h)\geq m-h(m+A)-o(h)$ almost everywhere, so the lower right Dini derivative of $m$ is at least $-(m+A)$. The model sector is identical: its evaluator term $-\int\log g_i(m,k)q_i(dk)$ varies in $m$ by at most $C_g$ and the reference adds $C_r$. Comparison gives $m(t)\geq\min(m(0),-A)$.

For the upper bound, fix $\varepsilon>0$ and consider points where $u\geq M-\varepsilon$. There a kernel receiver term is at least $u-\log C_K\geq-\varepsilon-\max(0,\log C_K)$, because $M\geq0$, an identity receiver term is at least $u-M\geq-\varepsilon$, a kernel source term is at least $-\beta_{ki}C_K/c$ because $\int(q_k/\nu_{ki})(w)k(z,w)\lambda(dw)\leq C_K/c$, and an identity source term $-\beta_{ki}q_k/q_i$ is at least $-\beta_{ki}e^{\varepsilon}$. Since $\mathcal E$ is nonincreasing by \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow}, $\sum_j\beta_{ij}D_{ij}\leq\mathcal E(0)$ and $\KL(q_i\Vert\lambda)\leq\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)+C_g\leq\mathcal E(0)+C_g$, so $\E_{q_i}\varphi_i\leq2\mathcal E(0)+2C$. Hence at those points $\dot u\leq B-u+\delta(\varepsilon)$ with $\delta(\varepsilon)\to0$, while at the remaining points $u+h\dot u\leq M-\varepsilon+h\Vert\dot u\Vert_\infty\leq M$ for small $h$. The upper Dini derivative of $M$ is therefore at most $B-M$, and $M(t)\leq\max(M(0),B)$; the model sector is the same with $C_r$. With these bounds every divergence $D_{ij}$ is bounded, and the row argument of \cref{prop:dyn-global-flow} bounds the rows below. Since the chart coordinate is $l=\log q-\E_\lambda\log q$, it stays bounded, and the continuation alternative of \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow} gives $t^*=\infty$. Integrating the dissipation identity and using $\mathcal E\geq0$ gives the last claim.
\end{proof}

The fixed-channel hypothesis enters the floor estimate. For law-dependent channels the channel-derivative term of \cref{eq:dyn-channel-differential} can grow as a density tends to zero, and no global statement is made. Convergence on general spaces would need a {\L}ojasiewicz--Simon inequality and is open. Other global statements are stated where they are used.

The Fisher--Rao metric used here is, up to scale, the metric singled out by invariance under sufficient statistics: on finite sample spaces by \citet{Cencov1982}, and on general sample spaces among local and weakly continuous tensor fields on parametrized measure models by \citet{AyJostLeSchwachhofer2015}.

\section{A global free energy and its local free energies}
\label{sec:dyn-likelihood}

Choose a proper prior $P_0(dy,dJ)$ on represented contents and source labels, and a normalized record kernel $\mathscr R(do\mid y,J)$. Then $\mathbb P=P_0\mathscr R$ is normalized by integrating the record first. At a declared record density $\ell_o(y,J)$ with $0<Z_o=\int\ell_o dP_0<\infty$, its posterior is $\Pi_o=\ell_o P_0/Z_o$. Substitution proves
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert P_0)-\E_Q\log\ell_o
 =\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o,
 \label{eq:dyn-fixed-vfe}
\end{equation}
when the split terms are defined. The right side supplies the extended definition otherwise. This is the variational free energy of a fixed model \citep{NealHinton1998,JordanEtAl1999,Friston2010}. A product family for $Q$ is permitted and its block updates remain coupled through the likelihood factors. Each block derivative contains every factor incident on it.

In the global picture the represented contents of all agents are variables of one such model. Write $y=(y_1,\dots,y_N)$ with $y_i\in\mathsf Y_i$, where $\mathsf Y_i$ collects the state and model coordinates of agent $i$, and let $\lambda_i$ be a sigma-finite reference on $\mathsf Y_i$. Source labels, when present, are further coordinates: they may be included in an agent's block, treated as additional blocks, or integrated out, and the proposition below holds for every finite block partition. Peer interactions are likelihood factors: for example, a comparison record \cref{eq:sample-comparison} between agents $i$ and $j$ contributes a factor that depends on $(y_i,y_j)$.

\propositionheading{Local free energies of a global model}{prop:dyn-global-local}
Let $\Pi_o$ have a density $p$ with respect to $\bigotimes_j\lambda_j$, and restrict recognition to products $Q=\bigotimes_jQ_j$ with $Q_j=a_j\lambda_j$. Assume $\int|a_j\log a_j|d\lambda_j<\infty$ for every $j$, $\int|\log p|dQ<\infty$, and, for the fixed $i$ below, $\int|\log p(y_i,\cdot)|dQ_{-i}<\infty$ for $\lambda_i$-almost every $y_i$. Fix $i$ and the factors $Q_{-i}=\bigotimes_{j\neq i}Q_j$, and put
\begin{equation}
 \bar e_i(y_i)=\int\log p(y_i,y_{-i})Q_{-i}(dy_{-i}),
 \qquad
 Z_i=\int e^{\bar e_i}d\lambda_i.
 \label{eq:dyn-mean-field-field}
\end{equation}
If $0<Z_i<\infty$, then with $Q_i^*=e^{\bar e_i}\lambda_i/Z_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q_i\Vert Q_i^*)-\log Z_i
 +\sum_{j\neq i}\int a_j\log a_jd\lambda_j-\log Z_o.
 \label{eq:dyn-local-free-energy}
\end{equation}
Consequently the local free energy of agent $i$, defined as $\mathcal F$ viewed as a function of $Q_i$ alone, has the unique minimizer $Q_i^*$ among competitors $Q_i'=a_i'\lambda_i$ satisfying the same integrability hypotheses, and every update of one factor to its minimizer lowers $\mathcal F$ or leaves it unchanged. Along any schedule of such block updates at whose visited configurations the hypotheses hold, $\mathcal F$ is nonincreasing; on finite alphabets with $p>0$ they hold at every configuration, while on general spaces their propagation must be checked for the model at hand. The peers enter $\bar e_i$ only through expectations, so $\bar e_i$ is affine in each $Q_j$ with $j\neq i$.
\begin{proof}
By the assumed integrability, $\mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o=\sum_j\int a_j\log a_jd\lambda_j-\int\log p dQ-\log Z_o$. Fubini's theorem gives $\int\log p dQ=\int\bar e_idQ_i$. Since $\log(dQ_i/dQ_i^*)=\log a_i-\bar e_i+\log Z_i$, the terms involving $Q_i$ equal $\KL(Q_i\Vert Q_i^*)-\log Z_i$, which proves \cref{eq:dyn-local-free-energy}. The remaining statements follow because relative entropy is nonnegative and vanishes only at equality, and because $\bar e_i$ is an integral of a fixed function against the product $Q_{-i}$.
\end{proof}

This is the coordinate-ascent identity of mean-field variational inference \citep{Bishop2006,BleiEtAl2017}, here read as a statement about agents: each agent minimizing its own local free energy, with its peers' current factors given, performs coordinate descent of one collective functional. The identification requires that every agent's model be a conditional of the same joint and that peers act through expectations of their contents. The latter property is what the two other objectives of this chapter lack. In this chapter ``local free energy'' means the global free energy as a function of one mean-field block. The single-agent correlated objective of \cref{cmem:free-energy} and the marginal objectives of \cref{imem:free-energy}, whose minimizers are exact marginal posteriors, are different objects.

With independent source priors and one likelihood factor reading each label, a factorized source row has cost $C_{ij}=\E_{Q_Y}[-\log\ell_i(o_i\mid Y,j)]$ and optimizer $\beta_{ij}\propto\pi_{ij}e^{-C_{ij}}$. This expected log likelihood differs from both the peer divergence and the likelihood integrated over a prior. For an isolated source record, exact source inference gives $\mathbb P(J=j\mid o)\propto\pi_j\E_{P_Y}\ell_j(o\mid Y)$. Equality of these different rows needs additional hypotheses; \cref{prop:dyn-label-model,thm:dyn-registered} give two exact settings.

\propositionheading{Entropy and the likelihood Jensen gap}{prop:dyn-jensen}
On standard Borel spaces, let $k(w\mid v)$ be a density of a normalized kernel into a receiver space with sigma-finite reference $\lambda$. Let the receiver law be $q=a\lambda$ and the source law be $p$. Put $b(w)=\int k(w\mid v)p(dv)$. Assume $0<b(w)<\infty$ for $q$-almost every $w$, $k>0$ almost everywhere under $q\otimes p$, and finiteness of $\E_q|\log a|$, $\E_q|\log b|$, and $\E_{q\otimes p}|\log k|$. Define $p^w(dv)=k(w\mid v)p(dv)/b(w)$ on this full-measure set. Then
\begin{equation}
 \E_{q\otimes p}[-\log k(W\mid V)]
 =H_\lambda(q)+\KL(q\Vert pK)
   +\int\KL(p\Vert p^w)q(dw).
 \label{eq:dyn-jensen}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
The density ratio gives $\KL(p\Vert p^w)=\log b(w)-\int\log k(w\mid v)p(dv)$. Integrating against $q$ and using $-\int\log b dq=H_\lambda(q)+\KL(q\Vert pK)$ proves the identity. The last term vanishes exactly when $p^w=p$ for $q$-almost every $w$.
\end{proof}

The entropy here is relative to the declared reference. A record likelihood normalized in $o$ need not be normalized in the receiver coordinate $w$, so \cref{prop:dyn-jensen} requires that additional kernel interpretation. Even when it applies, the entropy and conditional divergence can affect the dynamics.

The identity gives the exact relation between the Gibbs rows and a normalized source-label model in one sector.

\propositionheading{Gibbs rows and a normalized label model}{prop:dyn-label-model}
Let all alphabets be finite with counting reference, let the represented contents $Y_i\sim r_i$ be independent, let labels $J_i\sim\pi_i$ range over nonself sources, and let each receiver register one binary record whose success probability given $J_i=j$ is $k_{ij}(Y_i\mid Y_j)^{1/T}$, where $k_{ij}(w\mid v)=\mathcal K_{ij}(v,\{w\})>0$. With all records registered and product recognition $\bigotimes_iq_i\otimes\bigotimes_i\beta_i$, the free energy $\mathcal F$ of this model satisfies
\begin{equation}
 T\mathcal F
 =\mathcal E
 +\sum_i\bigl[H(q_i)+(T-1)\KL(q_i\Vert r_i)\bigr]
 +\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}G_{ij},
 \qquad
 G_{ij}=\int\KL(q_j\Vert q_j^w)q_i(dw),
 \label{eq:dyn-label-model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E$ is \cref{eq:dyn-potential} in this sector with local term $\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert r_i)$, and $q_j^w$ is the source conditional of \cref{prop:dyn-jensen}. The recognition-optimal rows are $\beta_{ij}\propto\pi_{ij}e^{-(D_{ij}+G_{ij})/T}$, and they equal the Gibbs rows \cref{eq:dyn-gibbs} exactly when $G_{ij}$ does not depend on $j$.
\begin{proof}
Since $k_{ij}\leq1$ on a finite alphabet, the record probabilities are normalized. Substitution gives $T\mathcal F=T\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert r_i)+T\sum_i\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i)+\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}\E_{q_i\otimes q_j}[-\log k_{ij}]$. Apply \cref{eq:dyn-jensen} to each expectation and use $\sum_j\beta_{ij}=1$. The row statement is \cref{prop:dyn-gibbs} with costs $D_{ij}+G_{ij}$.
\end{proof}

This generalizes the Gaussian calculation of the supplement. A channel whose kernel vanishes on a set charged by $q_i\otimes q_j$, such as a deterministic bit-transmitting channel, makes the expected log likelihood infinite and has no reading as a label likelihood of this kind.

There is also an exact obstruction to universal equality. Freeze a channel $K$ and receiver $q$. Suppose $p_1K,p_2K$ have positive densities that differ on a set of positive $q$-measure, with both divergences finite. Strict convexity of $-\log$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \KL(q\Vert (tp_1+(1-t)p_2)K)
 <t\KL(q\Vert p_1K)+(1-t)\KL(q\Vert p_2K),\qquad 0<t<1.
 \label{eq:dyn-affine-obstruction}
\end{equation}
This follows pointwise on the differing-density set and then by integration. By contrast, the expectation $\E_{q\otimes p}e(W,V)$ of any fixed integrable sample energy is affine in $p$. It cannot equal the divergence at both endpoints and their strict mixture. The obstruction concerns a fixed sample energy; it does not exclude a direct law potential, a frozen-template episode, or an explicitly different probability model whose variables are law points.

\section{Per-agent free energies}
\label{sec:dyn-per-agent}

In the per-agent picture each agent has its own normalized generative model, and the communicated laws of its peers enter that model as data. Formally the per-agent picture is a class: each agent descends the free energy of a normalized model in which the current laws of its peers are parameters and its own laws are recognition laws. This chapter develops one instance in detail, the mixture-source model below, in which the agent's own evaluator and reference become one source among several and a latent label records which source supplies the prior. Two other instances occur. A sequence of frozen-source episodes in the sense of \cref{cor:dyn-frozen-source} has each agent descend its own terms of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} at frozen sources, with the additive local term $\mathcal E_0$ rather than a competing self source. The tangent models of \cref{ch:link-dynamics} enter peers through a comparison-record likelihood rather than through mixture sources. Results proved for one instance do not transfer to another without a separate argument.

\definitionheading{Per-agent mixture-source model}{def:dyn-per-agent}
Fix agent $i$ and the current laws of its peers. Let the finite source sets $\mathcal N_i^q,\mathcal N_i^s$ contain the self label $i$, with positive normalized priors $\pi_i^q,\pi_i^s$. Define content sources and presentation sources by
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{ii}(m,\cdot)=G_i(m,\cdot),\quad
 \kappa_{ij}(m,\cdot)=\nu^q_{ij}\ (j\neq i),\qquad
 \rho_{ii}=r_i,\quad
 \rho_{il}=\nu^s_{il}\ (l\neq i),
 \label{eq:dyn-mixture-sources}
\end{equation}
with $\nu^q_{ij},\nu^s_{il}$ as in \cref{eq:dyn-peer-costs}. The per-agent model on $(m,k,J,L,o)$ is
\begin{equation}
 P_i(dm,dk,J=j,L=l,do)
 =\pi^s_{il}\rho_{il}(dm)\pi^q_{ij}\kappa_{ij}(m,dk)\ell_i(k,m;do).
 \label{eq:dyn-per-agent-model}
\end{equation}
It is normalized for every value of the peer laws. Every incoming channel $\mathcal K^q_{ij},\mathcal K^s_{il}$ of agent $i$ is required to be independent of agent $i$'s own laws $(q_i,s_i)$. Those peer laws are then parameters of agent $i$'s model, not variables over which it infers. If an incoming channel read $(q_i,s_i)$, the model would depend on the law it is used to score, $\mathcal F_i$ would not be the free energy of a fixed model in agent $i$'s recognition, and \cref{def:dyn-per-agent-flow} would differentiate the model itself.

\propositionheading{Per-agent free energy}{prop:dyn-per-agent}
In \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-model}, fix a record value $o$ with $0<P_i(o)<\infty$, where the record density $l_i(o\mid k,m)$ is jointly measurable as in \cref{def:generative-model}, and define $\mathcal F_i$ by \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} for this model. Write $P_i^0$ for the record-free part of \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-model}, and take recognition $Q=s_i\otimes q_i\otimes\beta_i\otimes\gamma_i$ on $(m,k,J,L)$ with $\KL(Q\Vert P_i^0)<\infty$, equivalently with every source cost of positive row weight finite. Then $\E_Q[(\log l_i)^+]\leq\KL(Q\Vert P_i^0)+\log(1+P_i(o))$, and in $(-\infty,\infty]$
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal F_i={}&\sum_l\gamma_{il}\KL(s_i\Vert\rho_{il})+\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\pi_i^s)
 +\sum_j\beta_{ij}\int\KL(q_i\Vert\kappa_{ij}(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)\\
 &+\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i^q)
 +\iint-\log l_i(o\mid k,m)q_i(dk)s_i(dm).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy}
\end{equation}
For other recognition laws \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} supplies the extended definition. For fixed laws with $\E_{s_i\otimes q_i}(\log l_i)^+<\infty$ and at least one finite cost in each sector, the minimizing rows are the Gibbs rows of \cref{prop:dyn-gibbs} at unit temperature, with $e^{-\infty}=0$ for infinite costs, self costs $\KL(s_i\Vert r_i)$ and $\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)$, and peer costs $D^s_{il},D^q_{ij}$.
\begin{proof}
No density of the sources is needed. Disintegrate $P_i^0$ successively along $L$, $m$, and $J$; the chain rule for relative entropy in $[0,\infty]$ \citep{Gray1990}, with $\sum_l\gamma_{il}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}=1$, gives $\KL(Q\Vert P_i^0)$ as the sum of the four nonnegative terms of \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy} other than the record term. For the record term, apply the variational characterization of relative entropy \citep{DonskerVaradhan1975} to the truncations of $g=(\log l_i)^+$ and pass to the limit by monotone convergence: $\E_Qg\leq\KL(Q\Vert P_i^0)+\log\E_{P_i^0}e^g$, and $e^g\leq l_i+1$ with $\E_{P_i^0}l_i(o\mid K,M)=P_i(o)$. Thus the record term is defined in $(-\infty,\infty]$. Since $dP_i(\cdot\mid o)/dP_i^0=l_i/P_i(o)$, \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} equals $\KL(Q\Vert P_i^0)-\E_Q\log l_i$, which proves \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy}. At fixed laws the row terms are those of \cref{prop:dyn-gibbs} with $T_q=T_s=1$; a source of infinite cost receives weight zero at every finite value, and the proof of \cref{prop:dyn-gibbs} applies on the face of finite-cost sources.
\end{proof}

The extended definition matters. If $\KL(\Pi\Vert P_i^0)=\infty$ at the exact posterior $\Pi$, which can happen when the record likelihood is unbounded, the separated expression \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy} reads $\infty-\infty$ at the optimum, while \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} gives the finite value $-\log P_i(o)$. In the per-agent model the self costs are these evaluator and reference divergences; they are not the identity-channel divergences $D^q_{ii}=0$ of the reciprocal potential.

At unit temperatures the peer terms of \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy} are exactly the peer terms of the reciprocal potential, and the self terms reproduce the split \cref{eq:dyn-local-split} of $\mathcal E_0$, now weighted by the self probabilities $\beta_{ii},\gamma_{ii}$. The agent's own model competes with its peers for the role of prior, and the rows infer which source to trust. Records add a likelihood term that persists in the objective. A temperature $T\neq1$ multiplies the label divergences. Dividing the objective by $T$ also tempers the record factor and the entropies of $q_i$ and $s_i$, so the result is not the free energy of a normalized model. Only the row block at fixed laws is the loss-based update of \citet{BissiriHolmesWalker2016} with weight $1/T$; the whole objective is a block-weighted generalized variational objective.

\definitionheading{Per-agent dynamics}{def:dyn-per-agent-flow}
On finite positive tables satisfying the hypotheses of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow}, with incoming channels as in \cref{def:dyn-per-agent} and positive record likelihoods $l_i(o\mid k,m)>0$, let each table $u^a$ belong to one agent $i(a)$. The per-agent dynamics is \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} with $h^a_z=\partial\mathcal F_{i(a)}/\partial u^a_z$, where the peer laws inside $\mathcal F_{i(a)}$ take their current values. The Gibbs-reduced per-agent dynamics replaces each agent's rows at every state by the unit-temperature Gibbs rows of \cref{prop:dyn-per-agent} and applies \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow} to the belief and model tables alone, with the own-block first variations of the reduced objective $\mathcal F_i^{\mathrm{red}}=\min_{\beta_i,\gamma_i}\mathcal F_i$. On general standard Borel spaces, the chart per-agent dynamics is the field of \cref{def:dyn-chart-flow} with $\varphi_{q_i},\varphi_{s_i}$ the own-law first variations of $\mathcal F_i$ or of $\mathcal F_i^{\mathrm{red}}$ at the current peer laws.

The proof of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow} applies without change to both finite-table versions and gives a unique maximal interior solution. On charts, the proof of existence, uniqueness, and the continuation alternative in \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow} uses no gradient structure, so it applies whenever the map from the chart coordinates of all laws to the own-law first variations is Lipschitz on bounded sets of $L^\infty$. This holds when references, evaluators, and record likelihoods have bounded log densities, each incoming channel is an identity, a fixed kernel with two-sided density bounds, or a channel whose output has a logarithm that is a Lipschitz function of the source chart coordinate on bounded sets, and the Gibbs rows are then smooth functions of bounded costs. A finite-dimensional family to which this field is tangent is then invariant, by uniqueness; tangency alone gives only a solution that stays in the family. The dissipation identity does not carry over: $d\mathcal F_i/dt$ is minus the sum of agent $i$'s own variances plus the pairing of the derivative of $\mathcal F_i$ in the peer laws with the peers' velocities, and agent $i$ controls only the first part. Let $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i\mathcal F_i$, regarded as a function of all tables. The per-agent force on $u^a$ omits from $\partial\mathcal F_{\mathrm{tot}}/\partial u^a$ exactly the derivatives of the other agents' free energies, namely the source-role terms \cref{eq:dyn-incoming} and the channel derivatives \cref{eq:dyn-channel-differential} of edges that $i(a)$ does not receive. The Fisher gradient flow of $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{tot}}$ keeps those terms. It is the Fisher gradient flow of a reciprocal potential of the evidence-augmented form \cref{eq:dyn-evidence-potential} at unit temperatures, with a cross-sector self cost $\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)$ competing as a source in place of the additive term $\mathcal E_0$. Results proved for the reciprocal flow therefore do not transfer to the per-agent dynamics without a separate argument. For the reciprocal potential, \cref{prop:dyn-morse} transfers critical points and linear stability between the evolving-row and Gibbs-reduced conventions; its Schur-complement argument uses a symmetric Hessian, and no corresponding transfer is proved for the non-gradient per-agent field.

\propositionheading{Per-agent dynamics need not be a gradient flow}{prop:dyn-nonreciprocal}
Take three agents with Bernoulli belief laws of log odds $l_1,l_2,l_3$, a trivial model sector, evaluators equal to Bernoulli references of log odds $\varrho_i$, identity channels, and rows held fixed at $\beta_{ii}=1/(1+w)$ and $\beta_{i,i+1}=w/(1+w)$ with $w>0$ and indices modulo three. The per-agent belief dynamics at these frozen rows is
\begin{equation}
 (1+w)\dot l_i=-(l_i-\varrho_i)-w(l_i-l_{i+1}),
 \label{eq:dyn-three-cycle}
\end{equation}
whose linear part has eigenvalues $-1/(1+w)$ and $(-1-3w/2\pm i\sqrt3w/2)/(1+w)$. No Riemannian metric makes \cref{eq:dyn-three-cycle} the gradient flow of a function. By contrast, the linearization of the Fisher gradient flow of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} at any interior critical point is self-adjoint for the Fisher metric and has real spectrum.
\begin{proof}
For a Bernoulli law the Fisher flow in log odds is $\dot l=-\partial F/\partial p$, and $\partial_p\KL(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(p'))=l-l'$, which gives \cref{eq:dyn-three-cycle}. Its linear part is circulant, with eigenvalues $(-1-w+w\omega)/(1+w)$ for the cube roots of unity $\omega$. At an equilibrium of a gradient flow $\dot x=-g^{-1}\nabla F$ the linearization is $-g^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}F$, which is self-adjoint for $g$ and hence has real eigenvalues; the same argument applies to \cref{eq:dyn-potential}.
\end{proof}

The frozen rows are not an instance of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent-flow}, in which rows evolve, but the conclusion survives there when the evaluators are equal, $\varrho_i=\varrho$, and the source priors are $(1/(1+w),w/(1+w))$. Then $l_i=\varrho$ with rows at the prior is an equilibrium, every cost vanishes there with zero gradient, the row block and the belief block decouple in the linearization, and the belief block is the linear part of \cref{eq:dyn-three-cycle}. The ring is not a gradient flow, but it still has a strict global Lyapunov function: its matrix is normal with negative-definite symmetric part, so $|l-l^*|^2$ decreases. The absence of a gradient structure does not imply the absence of a Lyapunov function, and for fixed rows and channels the per-agent belief dynamics is a global contraction.

\propositionheading{Contraction of per-agent beliefs at fixed rows and channels}{prop:dyn-per-agent-contraction}
On finite alphabets, hold the model laws, the rows, and the record values fixed, with $\beta_{ii}\geq b>0$ for every agent, every incoming belief channel fixed with no null column, and $l_i(o\mid k,m)>0$. In coordinates $\theta_i=\log q_i$ modulo additive constants, with the seminorm $\Vert\theta\Vert=\max_i\operatorname{osc}(\theta_i)$, where $\operatorname{osc}(f)=\max f-\min f$, the per-agent belief dynamics has a unique equilibrium $\theta^*$, every solution exists for all $t\geq0$, and $\Vert\theta(t)-\theta^*\Vert\leq e^{-bt}\Vert\theta(0)-\theta^*\Vert$. In particular $\Vert\theta-\theta^*\Vert$ is a strict global Lyapunov function.
\begin{proof}
Up to additive constants in each agent, the per-agent belief force is $\varphi_i=\theta_i-\Phi_i(\theta)$ with $\Phi_i(\theta)=\beta_{ii}\int\log G_i(m,\cdot)s_i(dm)+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\log(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})+\int\log l_i(o\mid\cdot,m)s_i(dm)$, so the flow is $\dot\theta=-\theta+\Phi(\theta)$ modulo constants. If $e^aq'\leq q\leq e^{a'}q'$ entrywise, the same bounds hold for $q\mathcal K$ and $q'\mathcal K$, so $\operatorname{osc}(\log q\mathcal K-\log q'\mathcal K)\leq\operatorname{osc}(\log q-\log q')$; the absence of null columns makes every $q_j\mathcal K_{ij}$ positive. Hence $\operatorname{osc}(\Phi_i(\theta)-\Phi_i(\theta'))\leq(1-\beta_{ii})\Vert\theta-\theta'\Vert$, and $\Phi$ is a contraction with constant $1-b$ on the finite-dimensional quotient by constants. It has a unique fixed point $\theta^*$ by Banach's theorem, and the field is globally Lipschitz, so solutions are global. For two solutions, $\delta=\theta-\theta'$ satisfies $\delta_i(t+h)=(1-h)\delta_i+h[\Phi_i(\theta)-\Phi_i(\theta')]+o(h)$ modulo constants, so the upper Dini derivative of $\Vert\delta\Vert$ is at most $-\Vert\delta\Vert+(1-b)\Vert\delta\Vert$. Taking $\theta'=\theta^*$ proves the estimate.
\end{proof}

The nonexpansiveness of positive kernels in the oscillation of log ratios is the elementary half of Birkhoff's contraction for the Hilbert projective metric. Evolving rows, law-dependent channels, and a coupled model sector are outside this proposition, and whether some instance of the per-agent class has no Lyapunov function at all is not decided here.

The cross-derivative criterion of \citet{MondererShapley1996} says the same as \cref{prop:dyn-nonreciprocal} in game-theoretic terms. On alphabets with at least three points the agent-wise objectives $\KL(q_1\Vert q_2)$ and $\KL(q_2\Vert q_1)$ have unequal mixed partial derivatives unless $q_1=q_2$. On a binary alphabet these derivatives are $-1/(x_2(1-x_2))$ and $-1/(x_1(1-x_1))$ in the success probabilities, which agree only on $x_1=x_2$ and $x_1=1-x_2$. In both cases the objectives admit no exact potential on any open set. Damped oscillation on directed cycles is the characteristic behavior of non-reciprocal interactions \citep{FruchartEtAl2021}. The reciprocal potential excludes it by construction, and an exact inheritance of a Fisher gradient flow cannot create it, since differentiating an exact intertwining by a submersion at an equilibrium shows that the parent spectrum is contained in the real spectrum of the fine linearization.

\section{The reciprocal potential is not a global free energy}
\label{sec:dyn-no-global}

\propositionheading{No global free-energy identification}{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe}
Hold the rows of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} fixed and positive. Let all state and model alphabets be finite, with the positivity hypotheses of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow}, so that $\mathcal E$ is finite on the interior of the configuration space.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Suppose agent $i$ attends to a source $j\neq i$ through an identity channel, with $\mathsf K_i=\mathsf K_j$ of at least two points. There is no normalized joint $\mathbb P$ over finitely many finite-valued variables, record value $o$, rule $x\mapsto Q(x)$ assigning recognition laws, and constants $a>0$, $b$ with $\mathcal E(x)=a\mathcal F(Q(x);o)+b$ on the interior.
\item[(b)] Suppose the channels are fixed and agent $i$ attends to a source $j\neq i$ through a belief channel $\mathcal K_{ij}$ that is not constant in its input, that is, whose rows are not all equal; an identity channel is an instance. There is no normalized joint $\mathbb P$ and record value $o$ such that, with the product recognition $Q=\bigotimes_k\zeta_k$ whose factors are the agent laws themselves, $\mathcal E=a\mathcal F(Q;o)+b$ with $a>0$ on an open set of interior configurations.
\item[(c)] Conversely, suppose every channel is fixed and is either an identity self channel or constant in its input, $\mathcal K^q_{ij}(v,\cdot)=C^q_{ij}$ and $\mathcal K^s_{il}(w,\cdot)=C^s_{il}$, and suppose the coefficients $1+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}$ and $1+\sum_{l\neq i}\gamma_{il}$ take one common value $c$ over all agents and both sectors. Then $\mathcal E=c\mathcal F(\bigotimes_k\zeta_k;o)+b$ on the whole interior, for the normalized product model $\bigotimes_i\Pi_i$ with $\Pi_i\propto\exp(\Phi_i/c)$, $\Phi_i=\log R_i+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}\log C^q_{ij}+\sum_{l\neq i}\gamma_{il}\log C^s_{il}$, and a trivial record.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
For (a), every finite value of $\mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o$ satisfies $\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)=\sum Q\log Q-\sum Q\log\Pi_o\leq\max_{\operatorname{supp}\Pi_o}(-\log\Pi_o)$, a finite bound independent of $Q$. Every term of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} is nonnegative, so $\mathcal E\geq\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i\Vert q_j)$. Let $q_i$ tend to the point mass at $u$ and $q_j$ to the point mass at $v\neq u$ through interior laws. Then $\mathcal E$ is finite along the path and tends to infinity, which no bounded function can match.

For (b), $\mathcal F(Q;o)$ is a sum of single-factor terms $\int\zeta_k\log\zeta_k$ and the integral of a fixed function against $\bigotimes_k\zeta_k$, which is affine in each factor. Hence the third derivative in a mass-preserving direction $w$ of $q_i$ and twice in a mass-preserving direction $v$ of $q_j$ vanishes identically for $i\neq j$. For $\mathcal E$ the only terms containing both $q_i$ and $q_j$ come from the edges between $i$ and $j$; with fixed channels the edge $i\to j$ contributes nothing to this derivative, since its second derivative in $q_j$ does not depend on $q_i$, and the edge $j\to i$ contributes
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{ij}\sum_uw(u)\frac{(v\mathcal K_{ij})(u)^2}{(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(u)^2}.
 \label{eq:dyn-no-global-third}
\end{equation}
Because the rows of $\mathcal K_{ij}$ are not all equal, some mass-preserving $v$ has $\phi=v\mathcal K_{ij}\neq0$, and $\phi$ sums to zero, so it takes both signs. If the ratio $\phi/(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})$ had constant absolute value at every point $q_j+tv$ of an interval of $t$, then $|\phi(u)|=c(t)((q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(u)+t\phi(u))$ for all $u$; comparing a point with $\phi>0$ and a point with $\phi<0$ determines $t$ uniquely, a contradiction. Hence every open set contains configurations at which $u\mapsto(\phi/(q_j\mathcal K_{ij}))(u)^2$ is nonconstant, and $w=e_a-e_b$ for two points with different values makes \cref{eq:dyn-no-global-third} nonzero. An affine identity on an open set would equate the two derivatives there. For an identity channel on a two-point alphabet, with $w=v=(v_1,-v_1)$, \cref{eq:dyn-no-global-third} equals $\beta_{ij}v_1^3[q_j(1)^{-2}-q_j(2)^{-2}]$.

For (c), an identity self channel contributes nothing, and a constant channel gives $\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i\Vert C^q_{ij})=-\beta_{ij}H(q_i)-\beta_{ij}\E_{q_i}\log C^q_{ij}$, and likewise in the model sector. With $\KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i)=-H(q_i)-H(s_i)-\E_{\zeta_i}\log R_i$, the hypothesis on the coefficients gives $\mathcal E=\sum_i[-cH(\zeta_i)-\E_{\zeta_i}\Phi_i]+\mathrm{const}=c\sum_i\KL(\zeta_i\Vert\Pi_i)+\mathrm{const}$, and $\sum_i\KL(\zeta_i\Vert\Pi_i)$ is the free energy of the product recognition in the product model with a trivial record.
\end{proof}

Part (c) is the degenerate exception to (b): such channels transmit no information about the source and exert no source force. The proposition is narrower than the statement that the reciprocal potential is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws, and that broader statement is false.

\remarkheading{Scope of the obstruction}{rem:dyn-no-global-scope}
Each hypothesis of \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} is used. First, the finiteness of the model in (a) is essential. Let $\Theta$ be a countable latent variable with prior $2^{-(n+1)}$, take the model that is uniform on all agent contents and independent of $\Theta$, and let the recognition rule be the product of the agent laws with an auxiliary factor $Q''(x)=(1-t)\pi_\Theta+t\delta_{n^*}$ whose divergence from the prior is tuned to $\mathcal E(x)$ minus the agent terms plus a constant; since $\mathcal E$ is bounded below and $\KL(Q''\Vert\pi_\Theta)$ ranges over $[0,(n^*+1)\log2]$, this reproduces $\mathcal E$ plus a constant on the whole interior, with the agent laws as recognition factors. Second, the product restriction in (b) is essential off finite alphabets. On atomless spaces, let $Q(x)$ be a Gaussian-copula coupling of the agent laws $\zeta_i$ whose total correlation equals $\mathcal E(x)-\mathcal E_0(x)\geq0$, which an equicorrelation parameter in $[0,1)$ attains. The chain rule gives $\KL(Q\Vert\bigotimes_iR_i)=\sum_i\KL(\zeta_i\Vert R_i)+\operatorname{TC}(Q)$, and total correlation is invariant under measure isomorphisms of the marginals, so $\mathcal F(Q(x);o)=\mathcal E(x)$ for the fixed model $\bigotimes_iR_i$ with a trivial record, while the marginals of $Q(x)$ are the agent laws. On finite alphabets the total correlation of a coupling is bounded, which is why (a) survives there. Third, the input dependence in (b) is essential, by (c). Fourth, the Gibbs-reduced potential is outside (a): when every receiver has an identity self source with positive prior, $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}\leq\mathcal E_0-T_q\sum_i\log\pi^q_{ii}-T_s\sum_i\log\pi^s_{ii}$ is bounded on finite alphabets. Whether (b) extends to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}$ with fixed channels, to law-dependent channels, and to non-finite spaces in a declared chart is not established here. Finally, the obstruction has no content in the mean directions. On the slice of unit-variance Gaussian beliefs with identity channels and fixed rows, $\beta_{ij}\KL(\mathcal N(\mu_i,1)\Vert\mathcal N(\mu_j,1))=\beta_{ij}(\mu_i-\mu_j)^2/2$ differs by a constant from the expected negative log likelihood of a Gaussian comparison record of precision $\beta_{ij}$ registered at zero, so $\mathcal E$ equals a mean-field free energy plus a constant there. The obstruction lives in the variance and shape directions.

The obstruction concerns models in which the agent laws are recognition laws. The per-agent picture evades it because each $\mathcal F_i$ is a fixed model only with its peers' laws held as data. Two further identifications place the agent laws elsewhere. In the first, agent $i$ receives $n$ independent messages from a source $J\sim\pi_i^q$ with law $\nu^q_{iJ}$ and registers whether their empirical type equals $q_i$, a type with denominator $n$. The method of types gives the registration probability $|T_{q_i}|\exp\{-n[H(q_i)+\KL(q_i\Vert\nu^q_{ij})]\}$ given $J=j$ \citep[Theorem 11.1.2]{CoverThomas2006}, so the posterior of $J$ given registration is the Gibbs row \cref{eq:dyn-gibbs} with $T_q=1/n$. There the laws are parameters of message laws. In the second, a prior proportional to $e^{-\mathcal E/\tau}$ against the Fisher volume on finite positive tables is normalizable because $\mathcal E\geq0$ and that volume is finite; records then add a likelihood, and the mode follows the Fisher flow of $\mathcal E-\tau\log\ell$. There the laws are latent variables of a hierarchical model. Neither construction makes $q_i$ or $s_i$ a recognition law.

The first identification is exact on a type lattice. Fix $n\geq1$, set $T_q=T_s=T=1/n$, and let $\mathcal X_n$ be a finite set of configurations $x=(q_i,s_i)_i$ on finite alphabets in which every $q_i$, $s_i$, and $q_i\otimes s_i$ is a type with denominator $n$ and every compared law $\nu^q_{ij}(x),\nu^s_{ij}(x)$ and reference joint $R_i$ is positive; law-dependent channels are allowed. In the registered-agreement model, $X$ is uniform on $\mathcal X_n$, the labels $J^q_i\sim\pi^q_i$ and $J^s_i\sim\pi^s_i$ are independent, agent $i$ receives conditionally independent batches $Y^0_i\sim R_i^{\otimes n}$, $Y^q_i\sim\nu^q_{iJ^q_i}(X)^{\otimes n}$, and $Y^s_i\sim\nu^s_{iJ^s_i}(X)^{\otimes n}$, and it registers the binary records that the empirical types of these batches equal $q_i\otimes s_i$, $q_i$, and $s_i$. Write $o$ for the event that every record is registered, $|T_p|$ for the size of the type class of $p$, and $c_n(p)=H(p)-n^{-1}\log|T_p|$. When both sectors are nontrivial, a product $q_i\otimes s_i$ of interior laws is an $n$-type only if $n$ divides every product of the numerators of $q_i$ and $s_i$, which is impossible for prime $n$; interior configurations require composite $n$, for example marginals that are $m$-types with $n=m^2$.

\theoremheading{Registered agreement}{thm:dyn-registered}
In the registered-agreement model the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\mathbb P(o^q_i\mid x,J^q_i=j)=|T_{q_i}|\exp\{-n[H(q_i)+D^q_{ij}(x)]\}$, and likewise in the model sector and for the reference batch with $\KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i)$.
\item[(ii)] The posterior of $J^q_i$ given $x$ and $o$ is the Gibbs row $\beta^*_i(x)$ of \cref{eq:dyn-gibbs}, and likewise for $J^s_i$.
\item[(iii)] For recognition $\delta_x\otimes\bigotimes_i\beta_i\otimes\bigotimes_i\gamma_i$ of $(X,J)$, with the batches integrated out, the free energy satisfies $T\mathcal F=\mathcal E(x,\beta,\gamma)+C_n(x)+T\log|\mathcal X_n|$, where $C_n(x)$ is the sum of $c_n$ over the registered targets and $0\leq c_n(p)\leq|\operatorname{supp}p|\log(n+1)/n$.
\item[(iv)] $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}(x)=-T\log\mathbb P(o\mid x)-C_n(x)$, and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}(x)+C_n(x)+T\log|\mathcal X_n|\geq-T\log\mathbb P(o)$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
A sequence of type $p$ has probability $\exp\{-n[H(p)+\KL(p\Vert\nu)]\}$ under $\nu^{\otimes n}$ \citep[Theorem 11.1.2]{CoverThomas2006}; summing over the type class gives (i). In the posterior of a label the factor $|T_{q_i}|e^{-nH(q_i)}$ is common to all sources and cancels, which gives (ii). For (iii), $\KL(\delta_x\Vert\mathrm{Unif})=\log|\mathcal X_n|$, the label terms are $\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi^q_i)$ and $\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\pi^s_i)$, and by (i) each expected negative log likelihood is $n$ times a divergence of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} plus $nc_n$ of its target; multiplying by $T=1/n$ gives the identity. The bounds on $c_n$ are those on the size of a type class \citep[Theorem 11.1.3]{CoverThomas2006}. For (iv), $-T\log\mathbb P(o\mid x)$ equals $\mathcal E_0(x)+C_n(x)-T\sum_i\log Z^q_i-T\sum_i\log Z^s_i$ by (i), and the free energy at the optimal rows bounds the negative log evidence.
\end{proof}

In this model the row temperature is the reciprocal number of exchanged messages, the Gibbs rows are exact posteriors of which source was heard, and the reduced potential is, up to the receiver-only term $C_n$, a negative log evidence at a point recognition of the configuration. The identification needs equal temperatures and one batch size; unequal temperatures give a weighted composite likelihood in the sense of \citet{BissiriHolmesWalker2016}. A nonuniform configuration prior $\rho$ adds $-T\log\rho(x)$. On general standard Borel spaces, registration of an empirical law in a neighborhood of the target gives the divergence as a large-deviation rate through Sanov's theorem \citep{Csiszar1984}; the double limit that would make (iii) exact there is not established here. Resampling $n$ messages from a table $p$ has covariance $(\operatorname{diag}p-pp^T)/n$, which is $T$ times the inverse of the Fisher metric \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-metric}, so the message count would set the temperature of a stochastic version of the flow, up to a factor fixed by the time unit and the noise convention (one resampling round per unit time in a Langevin equation with this covariance gives $1/(2n)$); that version and its metastability are open.

At frozen sources the same device needs no type lattice.

\corollaryheading{Frozen-source episodes}{cor:dyn-frozen-source}
Hold the compared laws $\nu_{ij}$ of agent $i$ fixed, let $T=1/N$ with $N$ a positive integer, and consider the normalized model $J\sim\pi_i$, $X^{1:N}\mid J\sim\nu_{iJ}^{\otimes N}$. For the tied-copy recognition $\beta_i\otimes q_i^{\otimes N}$,
\begin{equation}
 T\KL\bigl(\beta_i\otimes q_i^{\otimes N}\Vert\pi_i(dJ)\nu_{iJ}^{\otimes N}\bigr)
 =\sum_j\beta_{ij}D_{ij}+T\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i),
 \label{eq:dyn-frozen-source}
\end{equation}
and for a sequence of type $q_i$ the posterior of $J$ is the Gibbs row at temperature $T$. Replicating the reference joint in the same way represents the local term, so at $T_q=T_s=1/N$ the terms of one agent at frozen sources are $T$ times the record-free free energy of a fixed normalized model, and records attached to the copies add a likelihood with an evidence for each frozen episode.
\begin{proof}
The chain rule gives $\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i^{\otimes N}\Vert\nu_{ij}^{\otimes N})$, and relative entropy is additive over independent copies. The posterior statement is (ii) of \cref{thm:dyn-registered} for one row.
\end{proof}

Freezing the sources removes their source-role forces, so a sequence of such episodes belongs to the per-agent class of \cref{sec:dyn-per-agent}, not to the reciprocal flow. It is a different instance from the mixture-source model: each episode minimizes agent $i$'s own terms of \cref{eq:dyn-potential}, with the additive local term and rows at temperature $1/N$, not $\mathcal F_i$, in which the evaluator competes as a source with weight $\beta_{ii}$.

\definitionheading{Record-interrupted reciprocal dynamics}{def:dyn-record-interrupted}
Fix a Borel set $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{adm}}$ of admitted configurations, the reciprocal Fisher flow $(\Phi_t)$ on it, declared record times $0<t_1<t_2<\cdots$ with $t_k\to\infty$, record kernels $R_k(x,do)$ evaluated at the pre-record state, and measurable assimilation maps $A:\mathsf S_{\mathrm{adm}}\times\mathsf O\to\mathsf S_{\mathrm{adm}}$, for example the conditioning updates of \cref{ch:prediction}; experiments may be chosen from the history by a declared policy. Assume $(t,x)\mapsto\Phi_t(x)$ is jointly Borel on its domain and that $A$ maps admitted states into admitted states. The record-interrupted dynamics is the adaptive history of \cref{def:adaptive-history} with stage kernels
\begin{equation}
 J_k(x;dx',do)=R_k(\Phi_{t_k-t_{k-1}}(x),do)\,\delta_{A(\Phi_{t_k-t_{k-1}}(x),o)}(dx'),
 \label{eq:dyn-record-interrupted}
\end{equation}
with $t_0=0$, right-continuous paths that follow the flow between records, and the history stopped at the first record time beyond the maximal existence time of the flow. Its finite history laws exist and its infinite-history law is unique by the Ionescu-Tulcea theorem.

On finite positive tables joint measurability holds because the flow is continuous; on bounded log-density charts it is part of the hypothesis. The invariance of $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{adm}}$ is a real condition: conditioning on a record whose likelihood vanishes on part of an alphabet sends an interior table to the boundary, where the flow of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow} is not defined. With deterministic record times the process is a piecewise-deterministic process in the sense of \citet{Davis1984} for the time-augmented state, the declared times acting as forced jumps. The reciprocal potential decreases along each flow interval but need not decrease at an assimilation, since conditioning on a record can move an agent away from the minimizer of \cref{eq:dyn-potential}. In the free-energy pictures the same record is a likelihood term, and the objective it enters is fixed between records.

Relative to a fixed normalized model, the reciprocal potential therefore gives up three things: an evidence for model comparison, a posterior that the dynamics targets (its stationary points are compromises among references and peers), and a learning criterion for references, evaluators, priors, and temperatures. Monotonicity across records needs a precise statement. In the free-energy pictures the objective itself changes at each record, by $\E_Q[-\log\ell(o_{k+1}\mid\cdot)]$ at the current recognition, which is nonnegative for discrete records. What is monotone is its optimized value $-\log P(o_{1:k})$, nondecreasing in $k$ for discrete records though not in general for densities, together with each free energy along its own descent between records. No functional is monotone along the composite of descent intervals and records in any of the three pictures. The stationary set of the reciprocal potential does not depend on records, but the stationary point that the flow reaches can. When the potential has several stable stationary points, an assimilation can move the state into another basin, and this selection persists after the flow relaxes, without any declared record dependence. Any other persistent record dependence must be declared.

\definitionheading{Evidence-augmented reciprocal potential}{def:dyn-evidence-potential}
For registered records $o=(o_i)_i$ with declared densities of the record kernels $\ell_i$ of \cref{def:generative-model}, set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_o=\mathcal E+\sum_i\iint-\log\ell_i(o_i\mid k,m)q_i(dk)s_i(dm).
 \label{eq:dyn-evidence-potential}
\end{equation}

The added term is the record term of \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy}, so $\mathcal E_o$ is the reciprocal potential with the per-agent record likelihoods kept in the objective. Its stationary points depend on the records. The constructions of \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation} are proved for $\mathcal E$ with records entering only as assimilation events, and they are not claimed for $\mathcal E_o$, except for the base-only broadcast evidence term of \cref{rem:boundary-evidence}.

For fixed channels the first question one would ask about the three pictures has a short negative answer.

\propositionheading{Per-agent dynamics is not mean-field coordinate descent}{prop:dyn-per-agent-not-global}
On finite positive tables, hold the model laws and rows fixed and let every belief channel be fixed. Suppose that on an open set of interior belief configurations the per-agent belief force of each agent $i$ equals, up to constants, the block force $\log q_i-\bar e_i$ of one mean-field global free energy as in \cref{prop:dyn-global-local}. Then every peer channel $\mathcal K_{ij}$ with $\beta_{ij}>0$ is constant in its input.
\begin{proof}
The per-agent force contains $-\beta_{ij}\log(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})$ as its only dependence on $q_j$, while $\bar e_i$ is affine in $q_j$. Equality up to constants on an open set makes $\log(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(k)-\log(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(k')$ affine in $q_j$ there, for all $k,k'$. Along a line $q_j+tv$ with $v$ mass-preserving, the ratio of the two affine functions $(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(k)+t(v\mathcal K_{ij})(k)$ and $(q_j\mathcal K_{ij})(k')+t(v\mathcal K_{ij})(k')$ is then the exponential of an affine function of $t$ on an interval, which forces the ratio to be constant, so $v\mathcal K_{ij}$ is proportional to $q_j\mathcal K_{ij}$. Summing over $k$, $v\mathcal K_{ij}$ sums to zero and $q_j\mathcal K_{ij}$ to one, so $v\mathcal K_{ij}=0$ for every mass-preserving $v$, and all rows of $\mathcal K_{ij}$ coincide.
\end{proof}

The per-agent dynamics is therefore coordinate descent of a global free energy on an open set only when every attended peer transmits no information about its source. Stationary points and restricted flows can still coincide on special families, and on the linear-Gaussian mean slice the three objectives are limits of one normalized model. Fix $N$ agents with belief laws $\mathcal N(\mu_i,1)$ on $\R$, a trivial model sector, evaluators $\mathcal N(a_i,1)$, identity channels, and fixed rows $\beta_{ij}\geq0$ on the peer edges $j\to i$, $j\neq i$. Write $L_\omega$ for the Laplacian of a symmetric nonnegative weight matrix $\omega$, $(L_\omega)_{ii}=\sum_{k\neq i}\omega_{ik}$ and $(L_\omega)_{ij}=-\omega_{ij}$, put $W_{ij}=\beta_{ij}+\beta_{ji}$, and let $L^{\mathrm{out}}$ be the out-degree Laplacian of the rows, $L^{\mathrm{out}}_{ii}=\sum_{k\neq i}\beta_{ik}$ and $L^{\mathrm{out}}_{ij}=-\beta_{ij}$. The rows are reversible when there are $\varpi_i>0$ with $\varpi_i\beta_{ij}=\varpi_j\beta_{ji}$ for all $i\neq j$.

\theoremheading{Three objectives on the linear-Gaussian mean slice}{thm:dyn-three-limits}
On this slice the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The reciprocal potential is $\mathcal E(\mu)=\frac12\sum_i(\mu_i-a_i)^2+\frac12\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_{ij}(\mu_i-\mu_j)^2$, its unique stationary point is $\mu^{\mathrm{rec}}=(I+L_W)^{-1}a$, and its restricted-family flow is $\dot\mu=-\nabla\mathcal E$.
\item[(ii)] Fix $n\geq1$ and $T=1/n$. In the normalized model $M_n$ the latent means $X_i\sim\mathcal N(a_i,1/n)$ are independent, and for every edge with $\beta_{ij}>0$ agent $i$ registers at zero a comparison record $o_{ij}\mid X\sim\mathcal N(X_j-X_i,1/(n\beta_{ij}))$, the deviation of the average of $n$ messages of law $\mathcal N(X_j,1/\beta_{ij})$ from its own mean. For every Gaussian recognition $\mathcal N(\mu,\tau I)$,
\begin{equation}
 T\mathcal F\bigl(\mathcal N(\mu,\tau I);o=0\bigr)=\mathcal E(\mu)+c_n(\tau),
 \label{eq:dyn-three-limits}
\end{equation}
with $c_n(\tau)=\frac\tau2\operatorname{tr}(I+L_W)-\frac{N}{2n}\log(2\pi e\tau)+T\kappa_n$ independent of $\mu$, where $\kappa_n$ collects the logarithms of the normalizing constants of the prior and record densities. The exact posterior is $\mathcal N(\mu^{\mathrm{rec}},T(I+L_W)^{-1})$ at every $n$, the mean-field block minimizers of \cref{prop:dyn-global-local} are Gaussian with means at the same fixed point, and at $n=\tau=1$ the recognition factors are the agent laws themselves.
\item[(iii)] Both per-agent instances of \cref{sec:dyn-per-agent} have the stationary condition $(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})\mu=a$, where $\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}}$ is built from the rows $\tilde\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ij}$ for the frozen-source episodes of \cref{cor:dyn-frozen-source} and $\tilde\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ij}/\beta_{ii}$ for the mixture-source model at fixed rows, whose self row is $\beta_{ii}>0$. The stationary point $\mu^{\mathrm{pa}}=(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})^{-1}a$ is unique and globally exponentially attracting under $\dot\mu=-(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})\mu+a$. It equals $\mu^{\mathrm{rec}}$ for every $a$ exactly when $\tilde\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ij}+\beta_{ji}$ for all $i\neq j$, and per-agent rows $\tilde\beta$ arise in this way from some reciprocal rows exactly when they are symmetric.
\item[(iv)] The per-agent map $a\mapsto\mu^{\mathrm{pa}}$ equals the posterior-mean map of a global model with independent priors $X_i\sim\mathcal N(a_i,1/\varpi_i)$ and comparison records of precisions $\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}\geq0$ registered at zero exactly when the rows $\tilde\beta$ are reversible with weights $\varpi$, and then $\lambda_{ij}=\varpi_i\tilde\beta_{ij}$. In that case the frozen-source per-agent flow is the restricted Fisher flow of this global free energy on the slice of recognition laws $\mathcal N(\mu_i,1/\varpi_i)$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
For (i), $\KL(\mathcal N(\mu_i,1)\Vert\mathcal N(\mu_j,1))=\frac12(\mu_i-\mu_j)^2$, so $\nabla\mathcal E=(I+L_W)\mu-a$, since each unordered pair collects the weights of both directed edges. The matrix $I+L_W$ is positive definite, and the Fisher metric of the unit-variance family in $\mu$ is the identity, which gives the restricted flow of \cref{def:dyn-restricted-flow}. For (ii), $-\log p_n(x,o=0)=n\mathcal E(x)+\mathrm{const}$ by direct substitution, so the posterior is Gaussian with precision $n(I+L_W)$ and mean $\mu^{\mathrm{rec}}$. For a quadratic function $f$, $\E_{\mathcal N(\mu,\tau I)}f=f(\mu)+\frac\tau2\operatorname{tr}\nabla^2f$, and the entropy of $\mathcal N(\mu,\tau I)$ is $\frac N2\log(2\pi e\tau)$; with \cref{eq:dyn-fixed-vfe} this gives \cref{eq:dyn-three-limits}. The block minimizer of $\mathcal F$ in $Q_i$ is Gaussian by \cref{prop:dyn-gaussian-closure}, with mean solving the $i$th row of $n(I+L_W)\mu=na$, so the means of every fixed point of the block updates solve the same system. For (iii), the force on $\mu_i$ in the frozen-source episodes is $(\mu_i-a_i)+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}(\mu_i-\mu_j)$, and in the mixture-source model at fixed rows it is $\beta_{ii}(\mu_i-a_i)+\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{ij}(\mu_i-\mu_j)$, by \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy} with a unit-variance evaluator. The matrix $I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}}$ has positive diagonal and is strictly row diagonally dominant, so by Gershgorin's theorem \citep{Gershgorin1931} every eigenvalue has real part at least one, which gives uniqueness and exponential attraction. Equality of $(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})^{-1}a$ and $(I+L_W)^{-1}a$ for every $a$ is equality of the matrices, whose off-diagonal entries are $-\tilde\beta_{ij}$ and $-W_{ij}$. For (iv), the posterior mean of the global model is $(\Lambda_0+L_\lambda)^{-1}\Lambda_0a$ with $\Lambda_0=\operatorname{diag}(\varpi)$. Equality with $(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})^{-1}a$ for every $a$ is $\Lambda_0+L_\lambda=\Lambda_0(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})$, that is $L_\lambda=\Lambda_0\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}}$, whose off-diagonal entries give $\lambda_{ij}=\varpi_i\tilde\beta_{ij}$; symmetry of $\lambda$ is reversibility, and the diagonal entries then agree because both matrices have zero row sums. On the slice $\mathcal N(\mu_i,1/\varpi_i)$ the Fisher metric is $\Lambda_0$, so the restricted flow is $\dot\mu=-\Lambda_0^{-1}((\Lambda_0+L_\lambda)\mu-\Lambda_0a)=-(I+\tilde L^{\mathrm{out}})\mu+a$.
\end{proof}

The model $M_n$ makes precise where the source-role force of \cref{sec:dyn-variation} comes from. The record $o_{ij}$ belongs to the receiver $i$, but its likelihood depends on $X_j$ as well, so in the joint posterior it pulls the source; this back-action is the term that the per-agent picture removes by holding $X_j$ as data, and it is why the per-agent gap is $(a-b)/3$ and the reciprocal and global gaps are $(a-b)/5$ for two agents with $\beta_{12}=\beta_{21}=1$. The agent laws of the reciprocal potential appear in $M_n$ as message laws, and only at $n=\tau=1$ as recognition laws, in agreement with the last part of \cref{rem:dyn-no-global-scope}. Reversibility in (iv) is Kolmogorov's cycle condition on the rows \citep{Kolmogorov1936,Kelly1979}, so a single directed edge, or a directed cycle without its reverse edges, has no global reading; then the reciprocal flow moves the source while the per-agent source stays at its reference. Part (iv) does not contradict \cref{prop:dyn-per-agent-not-global}, which concerns block forces on open sets of finite tables; the slice here has positive codimension in every chart. The theorem is a statement about the declared slice. With free Gaussian variances the reciprocal stationary point weights each peer cost by the inverse source variance, its variances are widened by the source force rather than shrunk to the mean-field values $(1+\sum_kW_{ik})^{-1}$, and for two agents with $a-b=1$ and unit rows its gap is about $0.2065$ instead of $1/5$; beyond Gaussian laws no identification is claimed.

\openproblemheading{Relating the three objectives}{op:dyn-three-pictures}
For law-dependent channels, characterize the configurations and channels for which the per-agent belief forces equal the block forces of one mean-field global free energy on an open set, so that the per-agent dynamics is coordinate descent of a global free energy as in \cref{prop:dyn-global-local}; for fixed channels \cref{prop:dyn-per-agent-not-global} gives the answer. Beyond the mean slice of \cref{thm:dyn-three-limits}, determine the families on which the reciprocal, per-agent and global objectives have the same stationary points, with the map between their configurations and parameters stated explicitly, starting with Gaussian laws of free covariance and with law-dependent channels, and decide whether a sample-message model of the kind $M_n$ reproduces the reciprocal potential off that slice as $n\to\infty$. Decide whether a species in the sense of \cref{def:shared-model}, with matched evaluators and information, admits such a common joint. For each construction of \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation}, determine whether its conclusions hold under the per-agent dynamics or under a global free energy.
