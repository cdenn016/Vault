\chapter{Law-Dependent Interactions}
\label{ch:interactions}

An interaction compares or transmits information between agents. Its law may depend on the agents' current statistical states. The general object is a normalized Markov channel between represented spaces; exponential links of matrix generators are one constitutive example of such a channel, not the definition of an interaction. Gauge covariance specifies how this dependence is represented when coordinates change; it does not require the interaction to be a frame identification or select a unique mechanism. We first construct these interactions at one context. Transport between different contexts is a separate datum, whose transformation law is recorded in \cref{app:connection}.

\section{Two sectors and their comparison spaces}

Fix a context $c$ and a finite set of agents defined there. Write
\begin{equation}
\nu_i^q=q_i,\qquad \nu_i^s=s_i,
\end{equation}
for the belief and model marginals of agent $i$. The sector index $a$ takes values in $\{q,s\}$. These laws live on standard Borel spaces $E_i^a$. No product assumption between the two sectors, or between different agents, is required to define an interaction between their marginals. Such a product is a separate choice when a joint recognition law is needed.

In a vector realization choose reference spaces $V_q,V_s$ and invertible presentation maps
\begin{equation}
U_i^a:V_a\longrightarrow E_i^a,\qquad
\bar q_i=(U_i^q)^{-1}_\#q_i,\qquad
\bar s_i=(U_i^s)^{-1}_\#s_i.
\label{eq:int-reference-laws}
\end{equation}
We also write $\bar\nu_i^a=(U_i^a)^{-1}_\#\nu_i^a$ and $\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a=(\bar\nu_i^a)_i$ for the tuple of reference laws in sector $a$. Here $E_i^a$ is agent $i$'s displayed coordinate copy of $V_a$. At a common context the agents' statistical sections take values in the fibers over one point of bundles \cref{eq:statistical-bundle} associated to one principal bundle; in the linear realization with common $V_q,V_s$ these fibers coincide. The per-agent presentations are points of the frame torsor at that context, used to display each agent's law, and are not separate fibers. One structure group $G$ acts through the representations $\varrho_q,\varrho_s$ of \cref{eq:content-bundles}, so a passive change at agent $i$ is a single element $g_i$, with $g_i^a=\varrho_a(g_i)$. Independent sector changes would require the product group $G_q\times G_s$, which the theory does not use; the collective connection of \cref{geo:collective} depends on this choice, because the group enters its normal equation. The two presentations do not identify the belief and model interaction maps. Their domains, parameters, and dependence on the laws remain separately specified.

A passive re-expression $y_i'=g_i^a y_i$ sends $\nu_i^a$ to $(g_i^a)_\#\nu_i^a$ and $U_i^a$ to $g_i^aU_i^a$. Therefore the reference laws in \cref{eq:int-reference-laws} are unchanged. A common reference change is one element $h\in G$; it acts on both sectors at once, $U_i^a\mapsto U_i^a h_a$ with $h_a=\varrho_a(h)$, and sends every reference law to $(h_a^{-1})_\#\bar\nu_i^a$. The first operation changes an agent's displayed coordinates; the second changes the common coordinates in which interaction parameters are written.

\definitionheading{Normalized interaction channel}{def:int-channel}
Let $\mathsf S$ be the standard Borel configuration space of \cref{ch:foundations}. A sector channel from source $j$ to receiver $i$ is a Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_{ij}^a:\mathsf S\times E_j^a\rightsquigarrow E_i^a,
\end{equation}
that is, $(x,y)\mapsto\mathcal K_{ij}^a[x](y,A)$ is jointly measurable for every measurable $A$ and is a probability measure in $A$. At configuration $x$ it sends a law to $(\nu_j^a\mathcal K_{ij}^a[x])(A)=\int\mathcal K_{ij}^a[x](y,A)\nu_j^a(dy)$. When the channel does not depend on the configuration we omit $x$. Covariance requires
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_{ij}^{a\prime}(g_j^a y,A)
=\mathcal K_{ij}^a(y,(g_i^a)^{-1}A).
\label{eq:int-kernel-covariance}
\end{equation}
Composition of sending, transmission, and receiving kernels produces another normalized channel by iterated integration. Invertibility is not required. Alternatively, two channels can send the receiver and source laws into a common message space, where their communicated predictions are compared. The primitive self channel $\mathcal K_{ii}^a$ is constitutive data. The identity channel is one choice; later constructions may declare a nonidentity self channel, and they state which.

Joint measurability makes $x\mapsto\nu_j^a\mathcal K_{ij}^a[x]$ a measurable map into $\Prob(E_i^a)$, with the sigma-algebra generated by the evaluations, by a monotone-class argument, so the divergences, rows, and potentials built from these channels in \cref{ch:dynamics} are measurable functions of the configuration.

\propositionheading{Covariant law comparison}{prop:int-kl-covariance}
Under \cref{eq:int-kernel-covariance}, the divergence
\begin{equation}
D_{ij}^a=\KL(\nu_i^a\Vert\nu_j^a\mathcal K_{ij}^a),
\label{eq:int-peer-divergence}
\end{equation}
is unchanged by passive bimeasurable coordinate changes, including when its value is infinite.
\begin{proof}
Changing the integration variable in the kernel gives
$\nu_j^{a\prime}\mathcal K_{ij}^{a\prime}=(g_i^a)_\#(\nu_j^a\mathcal K_{ij}^a)$.
For any bimeasurable bijection $g$, absolute continuity is preserved and the transformed Radon--Nikodym derivative is $(d\mu/d\eta)\circ g^{-1}$. Its logarithmic integral is therefore unchanged. Failure of absolute continuity is also preserved, giving infinity on both sides.
\end{proof}
This is the coordinate invariance of relative entropy \citep{KullbackLeibler1951}. It makes the comparison intrinsic without asserting that either law is a posterior under a fixed population model.

\section{Reference consistency}

Per-agent covariance is automatic for any rule written in reference coordinates. If a channel is specified by a reference kernel $\bar{\mathcal K}_{ij}^a:V_a\rightsquigarrow V_a$ and displayed as $\mathcal K_{ij}^a(y,A)=\bar{\mathcal K}_{ij}^a((U_j^a)^{-1}y,(U_i^a)^{-1}A)$, then \cref{eq:int-kernel-covariance} holds identically, and \cref{prop:kl-coordinate} with $f=(U_i^a)^{-1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\KL(\nu_i^a\Vert\nu_j^a\mathcal K_{ij}^a)
=\KL(\bar\nu_i^a\Vert\bar\nu_j^a\bar{\mathcal K}_{ij}^a).
\label{eq:int-dressed-cost}
\end{equation}
The frames therefore act as a dressing field, as stated after \cref{eq:reference-laws}: per-agent covariance constrains no reference rule. What remains concerns the common reference change, in the two forms distinguished there.

\hypothesisheading{Reference consistency}{hyp:int-reference-consistency}
A reference rule is reference consistent if, for every common reference change $h\in G$, acting through $h_a=\varrho_a(h)$ in both sectors, re-expressing all reference laws and supplied interface data leaves the displayed channel unchanged. A rule whose sector-$a$ kernel reads laws of both sectors is re-expressed in both. For a reference kernel this means
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal K}_{ij}^{a\prime}\bigl[(h_a^{-1})_\#\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a\bigr](h_a^{-1}v,h_a^{-1}B)
=\bar{\mathcal K}_{ij}^a[\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a](v,B),
\label{eq:int-kernel-reference}
\end{equation}
and for the exponential generators below the theory imposes the generator-level strengthening \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance}. For exponential links the hypothesis itself is $e^{L'}=h_a^{-1}e^{L}h_a$, which agrees with \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance} when the generators have spectra in the strip $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi$, by uniqueness of the principal logarithm, and is weaker otherwise; the generator form is the one that differentiation of generators in \cref{eq:int-total-variation} and priors on generators require. Without reference consistency, a displayed interaction depends on the choice of reference point in the frame torsor and is not a function of the bundle data; on a nontrivial principal bundle no global choice of that point exists.

Reference consistency in this sense is naturality: any rule built from its inputs passes once every non-invariant ingredient, such as a fixed metric, code, or direction, is declared as supplied data and re-expressed with the reference laws. It excludes hidden structure and nothing more. Active equivariance holds the rule and every supplied datum fixed and requires the displayed channels to commute with the common action of $G$. The moment family below satisfies it with no supplied data, since the inverse bilinear form is taken from the law itself, as do invariant references and Haar channels under their declared groups; fixed codes, fixed lab directions, and fixed anchor metrics do not, and reduce the actively symmetric group to their stabilizers. A result that needs either property names it.

\section{Exponential links: one constitutive example}

Choose a message space $H_a$ and reference interfaces
\begin{equation}
T_j^a:V_a\longrightarrow H_a,\qquad
O_i^a:H_a\longrightarrow V_a.
\end{equation}
Their direct content map is $M_{ij}^a=U_i^aO_i^aT_j^a(U_j^a)^{-1}$. A deterministic channel can push the entire law through this map even when the image is lower dimensional. A density formula for a nonsingular law cannot then be used on the original ambient space without accounting for that change of support.

\definitionheading{Law-dependent reference generator}{def:int-generator}
Let $\mathcal L_{ij}^a$ be a measurable function of the tuple of reference laws $\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a$, and of any explicitly declared interface data, with values in $\operatorname{End}(V_a)$. Define
\begin{equation}
L_{ij}^a=\mathcal L_{ij}^a(\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a),\qquad
\Theta_{ij}^a=U_i^a\exp(L_{ij}^a)(U_j^a)^{-1}.
\label{eq:int-exponential-link}
\end{equation}
Dependence on the two endpoint laws alone is the special case $\mathcal L_{ij}^a(\bar\nu_i^a,\bar\nu_j^a)$. For a receiving--sending factorization, $L_{ij}^a=O_i^a(\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a)T_j^a(\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a)$, provided both maps refer to the same $H_a$ and $V_a$. The associated deterministic channel is $\mathcal K_{ij}^a(y,\cdot)=\delta_{\Theta_{ij}^ay}$, which is an instance of \cref{def:int-channel}. For exponential links the primitive self link is the identity, $L_{ii}^a=0$.

The exponential is taken on the reference endomorphism, not on a map with different domain and codomain. Its values lie in $\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(V_a))$, which is contained in the identity component $\GL^+(V_a)$ and, for $\dim V_a\ge2$, is a proper subset of it: the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant one and no real logarithm. By \citet{Culver1966}, a real invertible matrix has a real logarithm exactly when, for every negative eigenvalue $\lambda$ and every size $k$, the Jordan blocks $J_k(\lambda)$ occur an even number of times; here $J_2(-1)$ occurs once. Orientation-reversing and discrete interactions are therefore not exponential links, although they are channels in the sense of \cref{def:int-channel}. A smaller group acting on the frames can still re-express exponential links covariantly. Interpreting them as parallel transports of a connection for that smaller group would additionally require generators in its represented Lie algebra.

\definitionheading{Group-valued links}{def:int-group-links}
Let $\mathfrak g$ be the Lie algebra of $G$, and identify frames with elements $U_i\in G$ as in \cref{eq:reference-laws}. Group-valued links assign to each directed edge $j\to i$ a belief reference link $\bar\theta_{ij}\in G$ and a model reference link $\tilde{\bar\theta}_{ij}\in G$, either of which may depend on the laws as in \cref{def:int-generator}, and set
\begin{equation}
\theta_{ij}=U_i\bar\theta_{ij}U_j^{-1},\qquad
\tilde\theta_{ij}=U_i\tilde{\bar\theta}_{ij}U_j^{-1},\qquad
\Theta_{ij}^q=\varrho_q(\theta_{ij}),\qquad
\Theta_{ij}^s=\varrho_s(\tilde\theta_{ij}).
\label{eq:int-group-links}
\end{equation}
Where the reference links lie in the image of the exponential, the exponential chart writes them as $\bar\theta_{ij}=\exp(\omega_{ij})$ and $\tilde{\bar\theta}_{ij}=\exp(\tilde\omega_{ij})$ with generators $\omega_{ij},\tilde\omega_{ij}\in\mathfrak g$, and the reference generators of \cref{eq:int-exponential-link} are $L_{ij}^q=d\varrho_q(\omega_{ij})$ and $L_{ij}^s=d\varrho_s(\tilde\omega_{ij})$. The links are tied when $\theta_{ij}=\tilde\theta_{ij}$, that is, when one $G$-valued link acts on both sectors through the two representations. A law-dependent tied link is then a function of the reference laws of both sectors, and reference consistency is required under the joint action of one $h\in G$.

Opposite edges remain independent: $\theta_{ji}$ is not required to equal $\theta_{ij}^{-1}$, in either sector.

\propositionheading{The sector mismatch is covariant}{prop:int-mismatch}
Under a passive change $U_i\mapsto g_iU_i$ at every agent, $\theta_{ij}\mapsto g_i\theta_{ij}g_j^{-1}$ and $\tilde\theta_{ij}\mapsto g_i\tilde\theta_{ij}g_j^{-1}$. The mismatch
\begin{equation}
\Xi_{ij}=\tilde\theta_{ij}\theta_{ij}^{-1}\in G
\label{eq:int-mismatch}
\end{equation}
transforms by conjugation at the receiver, $\Xi_{ij}\mapsto g_i\Xi_{ij}g_i^{-1}$. Its conjugacy class is therefore independent of the frames, and the tied case $\Xi_{ij}=e$ is a frame-independent condition.
\begin{proof}
The reference links are unchanged by a passive change, so
\begin{equation}
\theta_{ij}'=g_iU_i\bar\theta_{ij}U_j^{-1}g_j^{-1}=g_i\theta_{ij}g_j^{-1},
\end{equation}
and likewise for $\tilde\theta_{ij}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\Xi_{ij}'=g_i\tilde\theta_{ij}g_j^{-1}g_j\theta_{ij}^{-1}g_i^{-1}=g_i\Xi_{ij}g_i^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

The general theory keeps both link fields. Results that use one $G$-valued link for both sectors state the tied hypothesis. The channels see only $\varrho_q(\theta_{ij})$ and $\varrho_s(\tilde\theta_{ij})$, and these are realized by one $G$-valued link $\theta^*$ exactly when $\Xi_{ij}$ lies in the normal subgroup $\ker\varrho_s\ker\varrho_q$: if $\theta^*=k_q\theta_{ij}=k_s\tilde\theta_{ij}$ with $k_a\in\ker\varrho_a$, then $\Xi_{ij}=k_s^{-1}k_q$, and conversely. The frame-independent invariant of the displayed interaction is therefore the conjugacy class of the image of $\Xi_{ij}$ in $G/(\ker\varrho_s\ker\varrho_q)$, and the tied hypothesis that later results need is $\Xi_{ij}\in\ker\varrho_s\ker\varrho_q$, for which $\Xi_{ij}=e$ suffices. Only when the representations are faithful does a nonidentity mismatch mean that no single link realizes both displayed transports; for example, the congruence action $Y\mapsto gYg^T$ of $SO(2)$ has kernel $\{\pm I\}$, and $\tilde\theta_{ij}=-\theta_{ij}$ gives the nonidentity mismatch $-I$ with the same displayed model transport.

\propositionheading{Endpoint covariance and reference consistency}{prop:int-endpoint}
The links in \cref{eq:int-exponential-link} obey
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{ij}^{a\prime}=g_i^a\Theta_{ij}^a(g_j^a)^{-1},
\label{eq:int-endpoint}
\end{equation}
and their associated channels satisfy \cref{eq:int-kernel-covariance}. The links are also independent of a common reference rechoice if
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{ij}^{a\prime}\bigl((h_a^{-1})_\#\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a\bigr)
=h_a^{-1}L_{ij}^a h_a,
\label{eq:int-reference-equivariance}
\end{equation}
where the prime includes the re-expression of any supplied interface data.
\begin{proof}
Passive site changes leave the reference laws and generator unchanged, so multiplication by the transformed endpoint frames proves \cref{eq:int-endpoint}. For the channel, $\delta_{g_i\Theta g_j^{-1}g_jy}(A)=\delta_{\Theta y}(g_i^{-1}A)$. Under a reference rechoice, the exponential series gives $e^{h_a^{-1}Lh_a}=h_a^{-1}e^Lh_a$. Substitution cancels both occurrences of $h_a$ in \cref{eq:int-exponential-link}.
\end{proof}
For factored generators, $O_i^a\mapsto h_a^{-1}O_i^a$ and $T_j^a\mapsto T_j^a h_a$ suffice. Message-coordinate changes $T_j^a\mapsto r_aT_j^a$, $O_i^a\mapsto O_i^ar_a^{-1}$ cancel within the product. By \cref{eq:int-dressed-cost}, \cref{eq:int-endpoint} holds for every generator rule and constrains nothing; \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance} is the form in which \cref{hyp:int-reference-consistency} is imposed on exponential links. It constrains a rule only once the supplied interface data are fixed; with those data re-expressed it is the naturality condition described after \cref{hyp:int-reference-consistency}.

\propositionheading{Low-rank generator and invertible transport}{prop:int-rank}
For finite matrices $O:H\to V$ and $T:V\to H$, the map $OT$ has rank at most $\dim H$, whereas $e^{OT}$ is invertible. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
e^{OT}v=v\quad(v\in\ker T),\qquad
\operatorname{im}(e^{OT}-I)\subseteq\operatorname{im}O.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
The rank statement follows from factorization. The exponential series and $e^{OT}e^{-OT}=I$ prove invertibility. Every positive power of $OT$ vanishes on $\ker T$, and every such power has image in $\operatorname{im}O$. Summing the convergent series proves the remaining assertions.
\end{proof}
Thus the exponential link retains an identity action in unread directions. It is a different operation from sending only the selective content $OTv$. Because the link is invertible, \cref{prop:kl-coordinate} gives $\KL(\nu_i\Vert(\Theta_{ij})_\#\nu_j)=\KL((\Theta_{ij}^{-1})_\#\nu_i\Vert\nu_j)$: an exponential link of a low-rank generator compares complete laws and discards nothing. Information-discarding, or selective, channels are noninvertible kernels of \cref{def:int-channel}. The identity self-link also differs from a coarse internal loop: a parent may retain nonidentity loop information inherited from several child interactions.

\section{An explicit constitutive family for general laws}

The preceding definition does not select the functional $\mathcal L$. The following choice shows that nonconstant, reference-consistent generators can depend directly on laws without restricting them to a Gaussian family.

\constructionheading{Moment-dependent receiving and sending}{def:int-moment-family}
For each sector let $V_a=\R^{d_a}$ and admit laws with finite second moments and positive-definite covariance. In reference coordinates write their means and covariances as $\mu_i^a$ and $\Sigma_i^a$. For a supplied scalar $\lambda_{ij}^a$ on each nonself directed edge, put
\begin{equation}
L_{ij}^a=\lambda_{ij}^aO_i^aT_j^a,\qquad
O_i^a(t)=\mu_i^a t,\qquad
T_j^a(v)=(\mu_j^a)^T(\Sigma_j^a)^{-1}v.
\label{eq:int-moment-generator}
\end{equation}
Here $H_a=\R$, and $L_{ij}^a=\lambda_{ij}^a\mu_i^a(\mu_j^a)^T(\Sigma_j^a)^{-1}$ has rank at most one. Set $L_{ii}^a=0$ separately. The edge coefficient is constitutive data, not a consequence of covariance.

\propositionheading{Equivariance of the moment family}{prop:int-moment-equivariance}
The family \cref{eq:int-moment-generator} satisfies \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance} under every invertible linear reference change.
\begin{proof}
Under $U_i^a\mapsto U_i^a h_a$, the moments become
$\mu_i^{a\prime}=h_a^{-1}\mu_i^a$ and
$\Sigma_j^{a\prime}=h_a^{-1}\Sigma_j^a h_a^{-T}$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
(\mu_j^{a\prime})^T(\Sigma_j^{a\prime})^{-1}
=(\mu_j^a)^T(\Sigma_j^a)^{-1}h_a.
\end{equation}
Multiplication with the transformed receiving column gives $L_{ij}^{a\prime}=h_a^{-1}L_{ij}^a h_a$.
\end{proof}
No fixed Euclidean metric was inserted into this comparison: the source covariance supplies the inverse bilinear form and transforms with the law. Finite moments and invertible covariance are assumptions of this example alone. Centered laws give zero generators in this family, so it does not encode all distinctions between laws. The family is never reciprocal on independent means: if $\mu_i^a$ and $\mu_j^a$ are linearly independent and $\lambda_{ij}^a,\lambda_{ji}^a\neq0$, then $\Theta_{ij}^a\Theta_{ji}^a\neq I$. Indeed, in reference coordinates $e^{L_{ij}}=I+\varphi_1(t_{ij})L_{ij}$ with $t_{ij}=\Tr L_{ij}$ and $\varphi_1(t)=(e^t-1)/t>0$, extended by $\varphi_1(0)=1$, because $L_{ij}^2=t_{ij}L_{ij}$ for a rank-one matrix; hence the inverse of $e^{L_{ji}}$ is $I-\varphi_1(-t_{ji})L_{ji}$ and $e^{L_{ij}}e^{L_{ji}}=I$ would force $\varphi_1(t_{ij})L_{ij}=-\varphi_1(-t_{ji})L_{ji}$, an equality of two nonzero rank-one matrices with images $\operatorname{span}\mu_i$ and $\operatorname{span}\mu_j$.

For a non-Gaussian example in two dimensions, take independent centered Laplace coordinates of variance one and translate their joint law to means $e_1$ and $e_2$ for two agents. Both covariances equal $I$. With identity frames and unit edge coefficients,
\begin{equation}
L_{12}=E_{12},\qquad L_{21}=E_{21},\qquad
\Theta_{12}\Theta_{21}
=(I+E_{12})(I+E_{21})
=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:int-nongaussian-loop}
\end{equation}
The loop is nontrivial even though both interfaces have rank one. This is a communication loop at one context, not the curvature of a base connection. \Cref{ch:link-base-transport} constructs base transport from smooth contextual fields of law and link data with a declared comparison rule.

\section{Loops, reciprocity, and what a law-level cost can see}

Opposite attending edges are independent constitutive choices. In particular, $\Theta_{ji}$ need not equal $\Theta_{ij}^{-1}$, as \cref{eq:int-nongaussian-loop} demonstrates. Geometric reversal of the \emph{same} transport is instead its inverse. Endpoint covariance and conjugation of loop products are the familiar transformation laws of lattice link variables \citep{Wilson1974}, here holding in display coordinates only; neither imposes an inverse relation between two independently modeled interactions. Directed non-inverse links at one context form a representation of the interaction quiver. Under independent changes in $\GL(V_a)$ at each vertex, its polynomial invariants are generated by traces of products along oriented cycles \citep{LeBruynProcesi1990}. The passive changes of this theory act through $\varrho_a(G)$, and for a proper subgroup the invariant ring is larger: for orthogonal $G$, $\Tr(\Theta_{ij}^T\Theta_{ij})$ and the other singular-value functions of a single open edge are already invariant. The common reference change is one element of $G$ acting by simultaneous conjugation on all reference links through $\varrho_q$ and $\varrho_s$ at once. When $\varrho_a(G)=\GL(V_a)$, the invariants of that action include traces of arbitrary words in the links \citep{Procesi1976}, for example $\Tr(e^{L_{12}}e^{L_{34}})$, which is not a path product; for every subgroup such traces remain invariant.

\propositionheading{Reciprocal and backtrack parts of a directed pair}{prop:int-reciprocal}
Let $i\neq j$, let $\Theta_{ij}$ and $\Theta_{ji}$ be invertible links of one sector with values in $\GL(V_a)$, and suppose the backtrack loop $N_{ij,j}=\Theta_{ji}\Theta_{ij}$ at $j$ has no eigenvalue in $(-\infty,0]$. Write $N_j$ for $N_{ij,j}$ when the pair is fixed. With the principal square root, put
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{ij}=\Theta_{ij}N_j^{-1/2},
\qquad
\Theta_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}N_j^{1/2}.
\label{eq:int-reciprocal}
\end{equation}
Then $\Omega_{ji}=\Omega_{ij}^{-1}$, and under passive endpoint changes $\Omega_{ij}'=g_i\Omega_{ij}g_j^{-1}$ and $N_j'=g_jN_jg_j^{-1}$. This is the unique factorization $\Theta_{ij}=\Omega B_j$, $\Theta_{ji}=\Omega^{-1}B_i$ with the balance condition $B_i=\Omega B_j\Omega^{-1}$ and with the spectrum of $B_j$ in the open right half-plane.
\begin{proof}
The loop $N_i=\Theta_{ij}\Theta_{ji}=\Theta_{ij}N_j\Theta_{ij}^{-1}$ is similar to $N_j$, so it also has no eigenvalue in $(-\infty,0]$. For a primary matrix function $f$ defined on the common spectrum of $AB$ and $BA$, $Bf(AB)=f(BA)B$ \citep[Chapter~1]{Higham2008}. With $A=\Theta_{ij}$, $B=\Theta_{ji}$ and $f(z)=z^{-1/2}$,
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{ji}\Omega_{ij}
=\Theta_{ji}N_i^{-1/2}\Theta_{ij}N_j^{-1/2}
=N_j^{-1/2}\Theta_{ji}\Theta_{ij}N_j^{-1/2}=I.
\end{equation}
For the transformation law, $N_j'=g_jN_jg_j^{-1}$ by \cref{eq:int-endpoint}, and the principal square root commutes with similarity, so $(N_j')^{-1/2}=g_jN_j^{-1/2}g_j^{-1}$. For uniqueness, balance gives $N_j=\Omega^{-1}B_i\Omega B_j=B_j^2$, and the square root with spectrum in the open right half-plane is the principal one, so $B_j=N_j^{1/2}$ and $\Omega=\Omega_{ij}$.
\end{proof}
The principal square root and the identity $Bf(AB)=f(BA)B$ for primary matrix functions are in \citet[Sections~1.7 and~1.8]{Higham2008}. The reciprocal parts $\Omega$ form a representation in which reversal is inversion, as for the transport of the extended general linear frame bundle in \cref{app:connection}, while $N_j$ records nonreciprocity. A real square root, and hence a balanced real split, can fail to exist: on the moment family below with identity frames, unit covariances, $\mu_1=e_1$, $\mu_2=e_2$, and coefficients with $\lambda_{12}\lambda_{21}\le-4$, the backtrack has determinant one and trace $2+\lambda_{12}\lambda_{21}\le-2$, so it is similar to $J_2(-1)$ or has two distinct negative eigenvalues, and it has no real square root. The case $i=j$ is excluded; for a nonidentity self channel the formula would return the matrix sign of $\Theta_{ii}$.

The split is computed in each sector separately and does not respect group-valued or tied links: the principal root does not commute with a representation when the representation moves an eigenvalue across the branch cut. For $G=SO(2)$ and a tied pair with backtrack rotation angle $0.8\pi$, the fundamental sector has reciprocal part $\varrho_q$ of a rotation, while a charge-two sector has backtrack angle $1.6\pi\equiv-0.4\pi$ and principal root angle $-0.2\pi$, not the image $0.8\pi$ of the fundamental root. A group-level split avoids this. If $\theta_{ji}\theta_{ij}=\exp(X)$ with $X\in\mathfrak g$ and every eigenvalue of $d\varrho_a(X)$ has imaginary part in $(-\pi,\pi)$ in each sector used, put $\Omega^G_{ij}=\theta_{ij}\exp(-X/2)$. Then $\varrho_a(\theta_{ji}\theta_{ij})^{1/2}=\varrho_a(\exp(X/2))$ by uniqueness of the square root with spectrum in the open right half-plane, so $\varrho_a(\Omega^G_{ij})$ is the sector split \cref{eq:int-reciprocal} in every such sector, it lies in $\varrho_a(G)$, tied links have tied reciprocal parts, and $\Omega^G_{ji}=(\Omega^G_{ij})^{-1}$ because $\theta_{ij}\theta_{ji}=\exp(\operatorname{Ad}_{\theta_{ij}}X)$.

When $\Theta_{ji}=\Theta_{ij}^T$, \cref{eq:int-reciprocal} is the polar decomposition. That condition depends on the frame, since the transpose is not covariant under non-orthogonal changes. A covariant form uses the law metrics $M_i=\Sigma_i^{-1}$ of laws with positive-definite covariance: call the pair law-adjoint when $\Theta_{ji}=M_j^{-1}\Theta_{ij}^TM_i$, a condition preserved by passive changes because $M_i\mapsto g_i^{-T}M_ig_i^{-1}$. For a law-adjoint pair, $N_j=M_j^{-1}(\Theta_{ij}^TM_i\Theta_{ij})$ is a product of two symmetric positive-definite matrices, so its spectrum is positive and the hypothesis of \cref{prop:int-reciprocal} holds. Every real power of $N_j$ is self-adjoint for $M_j$, and therefore $\Omega_{ij}^TM_i\Omega_{ij}=M_j$, that is, $\Omega_{ij}\Sigma_j\Omega_{ij}^T=\Sigma_i$: the reciprocal part carries the sender covariance onto the receiver covariance. In \cref{eq:int-nongaussian-loop}, $N_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ is positive definite and $\Omega_{12}=5^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$ is a rotation. The nontrivial two-cycle of that example is therefore pure nonreciprocity, $\Omega_{12}\Omega_{21}=I$; cycle products of the reciprocal parts on cycles of length at least three can carry reciprocal holonomy.

The peer-law comparison terms introduced above and in \cref{ch:dynamics} have the form $\KL(\nu_i\Vert\nu_j\mathcal K_{ij})$. By \cref{prop:loop-blindness}, a loop map $H$ entering a comparison only through $H_\#\nu$ cannot be distinguished, at one configuration, from another map with the same transported law, while the shared-sample law $(\id,H)_\#\nu$ determines $H$ up to a $\nu$-null set. A rotation $R$ is invisible in this sense against a centered Gaussian $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$ exactly when $R\Sigma R^T=\Sigma$; for every $\Sigma$ in dimension at least two this stabilizer contains nonidentity rotations, the half-turns in planes of eigenvectors, and for isotropic $\Sigma$ it is the whole rotation group. The reciprocal dynamics can still separate such loop maps along trajectories through its source forces, as described after \cref{prop:loop-blindness}. Under a frame change $g$ at the base vertex the shared-sample pair transforms diagonally, $(Y,HY)\mapsto(gY,(gHg^{-1})gY)$. A direct shared-sample loop experiment therefore requires a joint law coupling the routes. This is one way to identify the loop map. Grounded edge messages can also identify dictionaries, and hence their products, to the extent allowed by their codes and referents; \cref{ch:communication-links} states that experiment and its identifiability conditions. These observations use more information than the two marginal laws in the original comparison.

\propositionheading{Covariance-dressed loop defect}{prop:int-loop-defect}
Let $W$ be the product of the links of a closed walk based at $i$ in a linear sector, for example the two-cycle $W=\Theta_{ij}\Theta_{ji}$, with links produced by a reference-consistent rule, and let $\Sigma_i$ be the covariance of $\nu_i$ in the same displayed coordinates, assumed positive definite. Then
\begin{equation}
Q_{ij}=\tfrac12\Tr\bigl(\Sigma_i(I-W)^T\Sigma_i^{-1}(I-W)\bigr)
=\tfrac12\bigl\Vert\Sigma_i^{-1/2}(I-W)\Sigma_i^{1/2}\bigr\Vert_F^2,
\label{eq:int-loop-defect}
\end{equation}
is unchanged by passive changes and by every common invertible reference change, is nonnegative, and vanishes exactly when $W=I$.
\begin{proof}
Under $g\in\GL(E_i)$, $W\mapsto gWg^{-1}$ and $\Sigma_i\mapsto g\Sigma_ig^T$. Substitution gives $\tfrac12\Tr(g\Sigma_i(I-W)^T\Sigma_i^{-1}(I-W)g^{-1})$, which equals the original trace by cyclicity. Because the rule is reference consistent, a common reference change acts on the reference loop and reference covariance in the same way; for a rule that is not, the displayed links themselves change and $Q_{ij}$ changes with them. The second expression is a squared Frobenius norm of a similarity transform of $I-W$, which proves the last two assertions.
\end{proof}
Continuous conjugation-invariant functions of $W$ alone cannot detect a unipotent loop such as $I+E_{12}$, whose conjugacy class contains $I$ in its closure, whereas $Q=\tfrac12$ for that loop with $\Sigma_i=I$ and $Q=2$ with $\Sigma_i=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)$. The defect is invariant under joint re-expression of the loop and the base covariance; it is not a function of the conjugacy class of $W$ alone. For \cref{eq:int-nongaussian-loop}, $Q_{12}=\tfrac32$. This link defect remains positive for a nonidentity map that fixes the law, so it differs from a transported-law divergence. It is not a term of the original potential of \cref{ch:dynamics}. \Cref{ch:communication-links,ch:link-dynamics} separately introduce latent generators with an anchored loop prior in a declared tangent model. Their recognition dynamics does not alter the fixed-link formation constructions.

\section{Dependence, direction, and inherited responses}

Covariance constrains which law-only generators are possible. If a reference transformation fixes all input laws and all supplied data, \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance} requires its matrix to commute with the resulting generator. Identical centered laws invariant under every orthogonal transformation therefore cannot select a distinguished off-diagonal direction through a fully orthogonally equivariant law-only rule. Directional interfaces can carry additional information, but that information must be declared.

When links depend on the current laws, differentiation must include that dependence. At fixed frames a variation of an objective has the form
\begin{equation}
d\mathcal E=d_{\nu}\mathcal E+
\sum_{a,i,j}\langle d_{L_{ij}^a}\mathcal E,
D\mathcal L_{ij}^a[d\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a]\rangle.
\label{eq:int-total-variation}
\end{equation}
This statement uses a differentiable statistical family and differentiable constitutive maps. It includes both the sender and receiver occurrences, and every other law on which the generator depends. Freezing the link during an update defines another declared dynamics; it is not generally the derivative of the substituted objective. Nor does substituting live recognition laws into a likelihood make that likelihood part of a fixed generative model during recognition variation.

An inherited parent's state must determine its future boundary response, including the law dependence of its generators. If two child configurations with identical retained data give different future responses, those data do not support autonomous closure.
