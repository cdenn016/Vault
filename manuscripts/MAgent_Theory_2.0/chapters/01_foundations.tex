\chapter{Agents, Possibilities, and Records}
\label{ch:foundations}

The program behind this theory asks whether interacting agents can produce further agents. This text supplies the objects needed to pose that question exactly, the conditions under which a parent description inherits the dynamics of its children, and constructions measured against a formation standard (\cref{inh:recursive-formation}). No construction in this manuscript is proved to satisfy every clause of that standard; each construction states which clauses it meets, which it fails, and which remain undetermined, and the construction of a recursive formation is posed as \cref{inh:formation-open}. The first distinction is between an agent's probability law and the possibilities represented by that law. The second is between comparing two descriptions and specifying an interaction that changes them. A parent construction must preserve enough of both to continue the dynamics.

\section{Statistical sections}

Let $\mathcal C$ be a second-countable smooth base of contexts and let $P\to\mathcal C$ be a principal $G$-bundle, with $G$ a second-countable Lie group. A context labels where a description applies; it is not identified with physical position or time. For each agent $i$ let $\mathsf K_i$ and $\mathsf M_i$ be standard Borel spaces with jointly Borel actions $G\times\mathsf K_i\to\mathsf K_i$ and $G\times\mathsf M_i\to\mathsf M_i$. Their elements $k$ and $m$ represent state contents and model presentations. The actions may be different representations of the same group. They act on probability measures by pushforward. When no agent index is needed we write $\mathsf K,\mathsf M$.

Throughout, one structure group acts on both sectors. A gauge transformation of an agent is a single element of $G$, acting on state contents and on model presentations through the two actions; there is no separate belief group and model group. The represented contents form two associated bundles over the same principal bundle,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_q=P\times_G\mathsf K,\qquad
\mathcal E_s=P\times_G\mathsf M,
\label{eq:content-bundles}
\end{equation}
which in a linear realization are vector bundles $P\times_{\varrho_q}V_q$ and $P\times_{\varrho_s}V_s$ for representations $\varrho_q:G\to\GL(V_q)$ and $\varrho_s:G\to\GL(V_s)$, of ranks $K=\dim V_q$ and $\tilde K=\dim V_s$. \Cref{eq:content-bundles} is written for one agent; agent $i$ has the content bundles $P\times_G\mathsf K_i$ and $P\times_G\mathsf M_i$, all associated to the one principal bundle $P$. The statistical bundle defined next carries laws over the fiber product of $\mathcal E_q$ and $\mathcal E_s$, on which $G$ acts diagonally.

\definitionheading{Statistical agent section}{def:agent-section}
Set $\mathsf Y_i=\mathsf K_i\times\mathsf M_i$, with the product action, and give $\Prob(\mathsf Y_i)$ the sigma-algebra generated by evaluation on measurable sets. The associated statistical bundle of agent $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B_i=P\times_G\Prob(\mathsf Y_i),
\qquad (u g,\zeta)\sim(u,g_\#\zeta).
\label{eq:statistical-bundle}
\end{equation}
The statistical component of agent $i$ is a Borel section $\zeta_i$ of $\mathcal B_i$ over an open domain $\mathcal U_i\subseteq\mathcal C$. Smooth sections are defined only into admitted smooth subbundles. The full state of the agent also includes the internal variables and access mechanisms read by its update rule.

The associated-bundle construction is that of \citet[Section I.5]{KobayashiNomizu1963}. Its measurable form needs the joint measurability of the action. If each $g$ acts bimeasurably but $(g,y)\mapsto gy$ is not jointly Borel, for example a translation of $\R$ by a discontinuous additive function of $g\in\R$, the Borel class of a section can depend on the trivialization in which it is written.

\lemmaheading{Measurable structure of the statistical bundle}{lem:measurable-bundle}
Under the stated hypotheses, $(g,\zeta)\mapsto g_\#\zeta$ is Borel on $G\times\Prob(\mathsf Y_i)$, and $\mathcal B_i$ carries a standard Borel structure, independent of the atlas, for which every local trivialization is a Borel isomorphism onto its image. Transition maps preserve fiberwise mixtures, null sets, mutual absolute continuity, and relative entropy.
\begin{proof}
For a Borel set $A$, $(g_\#\zeta)(A)=\int\mathbf 1_A(gy)\zeta(dy)$. The integrand is Borel in $(g,y)$ by joint measurability, and the integral of a bounded jointly Borel function against $\zeta$ is Borel in $(g,\zeta)$: this holds for products $f(g)h(y)$ and extends by the monotone class theorem. Since $\mathcal C$ is second countable, countably many trivializing patches cover it, and their transition functions are Borel. The gluing maps $(c,\zeta)\mapsto(c,g_{\alpha\beta}(c)_\#\zeta)$ are therefore Borel bijections with Borel inverses. The space $\Prob(\mathsf Y_i)$ with the sigma-algebra generated by the evaluations is standard Borel \citep[Section~17.E]{Kechris1995}, so $\mathcal B_i$ is a countable Borel union of standard Borel pieces glued by Borel isomorphisms; a second atlas yields the same structure because its transitions to the first are Borel by the same argument. Pushforward is affine and preserves null sets and absolute continuity, and \cref{prop:kl-coordinate} gives invariance of relative entropy.
\end{proof}

At a fixed context, disintegration gives
\begin{equation}
\zeta_i(dk,dm)=q_i(dk\mid m)s_i(dm),
\qquad q_i(dk)=\int q_i(dk\mid m)s_i(dm).
\label{eq:agent-disintegration}
\end{equation}
Here $q_i$ is the state marginal and $s_i$ is the law over model presentations. Regular conditional laws exist on the stated spaces, with versions defined up to the appropriate marginal null sets \citep{Kallenberg2021}. Disintegration commutes with the product action: the conditional law of $g_\#\zeta_i$ given $m'$ is $g_\#q_i(\cdot\mid g^{-1}m')$ for $g_\#s_i$-almost every $m'$, so \cref{eq:agent-disintegration} descends to bundle points. A model presentation acquires predictive content through a normalized kernel; its name or coordinate value alone supplies no prediction (\cref{sec:generative-models}).

The product restriction $\zeta_i=q_i\otimes s_i$ removes dependence between the two sectors within an agent. A product over agents makes a further restriction on represented contents. Neither restriction prevents the update of one law from depending on other laws. Coupling is a property of the dynamics; factorization is a property of a chosen probability description. The coupled population flows developed below impose the product restriction. The general joint law enters through its Fisher metric in \cref{ch:geometry} and through the local conditional-prediction construction of \cref{sec:conditional-prediction,app:conditional-memory}. The restriction is not neutral, as \cref{ch:dynamics} shows: it changes the local optimum of a belief, and the joint-law flow does not preserve it.

Smoothness is an additional hypothesis. When derivatives of laws are required, use a declared regular statistical family stable under the relevant coordinate changes. The whole space $\Prob(\mathsf Y_i)$ is not assigned a smooth manifold structure by notation. \Cref{ch:geometry} equips an admitted family with the Fisher metric of the full joint law and pulls that metric back to contextual directions through a covariant derivative. The product restriction has a simpler metric, but marginal metrics alone do not measure changes of dependence in a general joint family. A collection of pointwise marginals also does not determine a probability law on fields over $\mathcal C$; \cref{sec:content-laws} supplies such a law as a separate object.

The population at a context consists of the agents whose domains contain that context. Most of the dynamics below is formulated at a common $c_*$. This retains the ambient bundle and local sections without requiring parallel transport between distinct base points. Independent interactions between agents at $c_*$ are defined in \cref{ch:interactions}. Contextual statistical geometry is developed in \cref{ch:geometry}; \cref{app:connection} records the transformation law of connection data in a moving presentation. \Cref{ch:link-base-transport} constructs such data from regular contextual laws, interfaces, and links with declared comparison rules; selection of those rules and continuation through singular regions remain open. The potentials of \cref{ch:dynamics} contain no coupling between contexts. The formation constructions retain these context-local flows and compute connections as diagnostics. The separate extension in \cref{ch:contextual-coupling} adds an aligned contextual energy and derives its neighboring-context Fisher force on declared regular families.

Parents in later chapters are maps between statistical descriptions of different agents. The following definition supplies the corresponding morphism of bundles.

\definitionheading{Morphism of statistical bundles}{def:bundle-morphism}
Let $\tilde f:P\to\bar P$ be a principal bundle map covering $f:\mathcal C\to\bar{\mathcal C}$ with homomorphism $\phi:G\to\bar G$, so that $\tilde f(ug)=\tilde f(u)\phi(g)$. Let $\kappa:\mathsf Y\rightsquigarrow\bar{\mathsf Y}$ be a Markov kernel with $\kappa(gy,\cdot)=\phi(g)_\#\kappa(y,\cdot)$. The induced morphism is
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\tilde f,\kappa}[u,\zeta]=[\tilde f(u),\zeta\kappa].
\label{eq:bundle-morphism}
\end{equation}

\propositionheading{Statistical bundle morphisms}{prop:bundle-morphism}
The map \cref{eq:bundle-morphism} is well defined, fiberwise affine, and contracts relative entropy within each fiber. In a linear realization, let $A:V_q\to\bar V_q$ and $\tilde A:V_s\to\bar V_s$ be linear maps with $A\varrho_q(g)=\bar\varrho_q(\phi(g))A$ and $\tilde A\varrho_s(g)=\bar\varrho_s(\phi(g))\tilde A$ for all $g\in G$. Then the same principal map induces well-defined morphisms of the content bundles,
\begin{equation}
\Phi[u,k]=[\tilde f(u),Ak],\qquad
\tilde\Phi[u,m]=[\tilde f(u),\tilde Am],
\label{eq:content-morphisms}
\end{equation}
and the deterministic kernel $\kappa((k,m),\cdot)=\delta_{(Ak,\tilde Am)}$ induces the corresponding statistical morphism.
\begin{proof}
Equivariance of $\kappa$ gives $(g_\#\zeta)\kappa=\phi(g)_\#(\zeta\kappa)$. Hence $[u,g_\#\zeta]\mapsto[\tilde f(u),\phi(g)_\#(\zeta\kappa)]=[\tilde f(u)\phi(g),\zeta\kappa]$, which is the image of the equivalent representative $[ug,\zeta]$. The map $\zeta\mapsto\zeta\kappa$ is affine, and $\KL(\zeta\kappa\Vert\zeta'\kappa)\leq\KL(\zeta\Vert\zeta')$ is the data-processing inequality for relative entropy under a Markov kernel on standard Borel spaces \citep[Chapter~5]{Gray1990}. For the content morphisms, the representative $[ug,\varrho_q(g)^{-1}k]$ is sent to $[\tilde f(u)\phi(g),A\varrho_q(g)^{-1}k]=[\tilde f(u)\phi(g),\bar\varrho_q(\phi(g))^{-1}Ak]=[\tilde f(u),Ak]$, and likewise for $\tilde\Phi$. The deterministic kernel is equivariant by the two intertwining relations.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies verbatim to a Borel family of kernels $\kappa_c$ indexed by the base point, equivariant at each $c$, that is, to an equivariant kernel from the sample bundle of the fine description to the pullback by $f$ of the coarse sample bundle; the constant kernel of \cref{def:bundle-morphism} is its simplest case. A retention whose kernel varies over the base or depends on the configuration is a morphism only in this fibered sense, and a retention that reads several fibers at once is not a fiberwise morphism at all.

The belief and model morphisms share the principal map $\tilde f$. Two principal maps covering the same $f$ with the same homomorphism $\phi$ differ by a gauge transformation $h$ of $\bar P$, and the pair of sector maps built from them corresponds to the base-point-dependent kernel obtained by composing with $h(u)$ in one sector; it is covered by the fibered form above. Only sector maps with different homomorphisms $\phi_1\neq\phi_2$ would make independent groups act in the two sectors, and these are not admitted.

A parent is an agent of a declared type when its retained state is again a section of such a bundle, carries an admitted family, a register with a covariant readout, a generative model in the sense of \cref{def:generative-model} (presentation prior, evaluator, and record kernel), and interacts with exterior agents through channels of \cref{def:int-channel}. Proving that a retention is closed in this sense is part of the formation problem of \cref{ch:inheritance}, not a consequence of the definition.

\section{Presentation and probability}

Fix $c_*$ and a reference point $\sigma\in P_{c_*}$. A frame of agent $i$ is a point $u_i\in P_{c_*}$, and $u_i=\sigma U_i^{-1}$ defines $U_i\in G$. The point $u_i$ identifies the fiber of $\mathcal B_i$ at $c_*$ with $\Prob(\mathsf Y_i)$, and the law displayed by agent $i$ is the coordinate expression of its bundle point in that frame. In a linear realization $\mathsf K_i=V_q$ and $\mathsf M_i=V_s$ carry linear representations $\varrho_q,\varrho_s$ of $G$, the sector frames are $U_i^q=\varrho_q(U_i)$ and $U_i^s=\varrho_s(U_i)$, and $E_i^q,E_i^s$ denote agent $i$'s coordinate copies of $V_q,V_s$. Laws written on $E_i^a$, as in \cref{ch:interactions}, are the displayed laws. The reference laws are
\begin{equation}
\bar q_i=((U_i^q)^{-1})_\#q_i,
\qquad
\bar s_i=((U_i^s)^{-1})_\#s_i.
\label{eq:reference-laws}
\end{equation}
They are the coordinate expressions in the reference frame $\sigma$, since $[\sigma U_i^{-1},\zeta]=[\sigma,(U_i^{-1})_\#\zeta]$. A passive change sends $u_i$ to $u_ig_i^{-1}$, hence $q_i$ to $(g_i^q)_\#q_i$ and $U_i^q$ to $g_i^qU_i^q$, with the corresponding model-sector rule. The reference laws are unchanged. This is a change of description, not an inference step. One frame induces both sector frames, and a passive change is one element $g_i$ with $g_i^q=\varrho_q(g_i)$ and $g_i^s=\varrho_s(g_i)$. Independent sector changes would require the product group $G_q\times G_s$; the theory does not use them.

Because every frame-dependent object can be written through its reference law, covariance under independent passive changes at each agent holds for any rule expressed in reference laws and constrains nothing by itself. The frames act here as a dressing field in the sense of \citet{FournelEtAl2014}. A substantive symmetry requirement is a statement about the reference rules themselves, such as equivariance of an interaction under a common change of reference at one context. \Cref{ch:interactions} states it as \cref{eq:int-reference-equivariance}, and a result uses it only where it says so. Two forms must be kept apart. If every ingredient of a rule that is not invariant, such as a metric, a code, or a fixed direction, is declared as supplied data and re-expressed together with the reference laws, the rule is unchanged by the common reference change automatically; this naturality is the dressing argument applied to the reference point $\sigma$ and again constrains nothing. Active equivariance holds the rule, the references, the evaluators, and every supplied datum fixed and requires the dynamics to commute with the common action of $G$. A supplied datum carried with the reference reduces the group under which the model is actively symmetric to the stabilizer of that datum, for example to an orthogonal group for a fixed inner product. A symmetry that holds only because such data are re-expressed is artificial in the sense of \citet{Francois2019}; a result that needs active equivariance says so.

An active transformation changes the reference laws at fixed frames, for example $\bar q_i\mapsto a_\#\bar q_i$ for a bimeasurable bijection $a$ of $\mathsf K_i$. Whether two configurations related in this way are distinguishable depends on a declared observable algebra.

\definitionheading{Observable algebras and gauge distinctions}{def:observable-algebra}
The population algebra $\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pop}}$ of a construction consists of the measurable functions of the configuration that the agents themselves read: their source rows, the outputs of their channels and the costs built from them, their evaluators and record kernels, and the laws of their record readouts. A response protocol is a declared family of external queries together with their response kernels; the protocol algebra $\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{prot}}$ adds to $\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pop}}$ the response laws of the admitted queries of a declared protocol. An element of an algebra is evaluated at the configuration reached at any time along the declared dynamics from the configuration in question. Two configurations, or two configurations related by an active transformation, differ by a distinction that is gauge relative to an algebra $\mathfrak A$ when no element of $\mathfrak A$ separates them at any time. A comparison made at a single instant is called instantaneous and is not used to declare a distinction gauge.

A law functional that no agent, channel, record, or admitted query reads, for example a child-level diagnostic of a law used only to assess a retention, belongs to no observable algebra. A distinction that only such a functional resolves is gauge relative to every declared algebra; the text then names the functional rather than calling the distinction physical. When only an external protocol resolves a distinction, the text names that protocol rather than calling the distinction physical without qualification, and the words physical and public are used only relative to a named algebra. The time-evolved form matters: a law-level comparison can fail to separate two configurations at one instant while the dynamics separates them later (\cref{prop:loop-blindness} and the remark after it).

For probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on one measurable space, relative entropy is
\begin{equation}
\KL(\mu\Vert\nu)=\int\log\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}d\mu,
\label{eq:relative-entropy}
\end{equation}
when $\mu\ll\nu$, and is $+\infty$ otherwise. In this formula and below, a displayed integral is understood with its declared measure; no common Lebesgue density is required. Relative entropy compares distinguishability of laws \citep{KullbackLeibler1951}.

\propositionheading{Coordinate invariance}{prop:kl-coordinate}
For an injective Borel map $f$ between standard Borel spaces, in particular a bimeasurable bijection, including divergences with value $+\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\KL(f_\#\mu\Vert f_\#\nu)=\KL(\mu\Vert\nu).
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
By the Lusin--Souslin theorem \citep[Theorem 15.1]{Kechris1995} the image of $f$ is Borel and $f$ is a Borel isomorphism onto it, so it suffices to treat a bimeasurable bijection onto a Borel set carrying both pushforwards. Absolute continuity is preserved in both directions. If $h=d\mu/d\nu$, the transformed density is $h\circ f^{-1}$. Integrating its logarithm against $f_\#\mu$ gives the original integral. If absolute continuity fails, a set of zero $\nu$-measure and positive $\mu$-measure is carried to a set with the same property.
\end{proof}

\definitionheading{Normalized channel}{def:channel}
A Markov kernel $K:X\rightsquigarrow Y$ assigns a probability measure $K(x,\cdot)$ to each $x$, measurably in $x$. Its action on a law is
\begin{equation}
(\mu K)(A)=\int K(x,A)\mu(dx).
\label{eq:kernel-action}
\end{equation}
A deterministic measurable map $f:X\to Y$ is represented by $K_f(x,\cdot)=\delta_{f(x)}$. Then $\mu K_f=f_\#\mu$.

An invertible frame change preserves all distinctions. A channel that discards information or adds noise need not. Both are well-defined operations on general probability laws, but their roles in an agent interaction differ.

\section{Internal records}

At a fixed context, let $\mathsf S_i$ be the admitted internal-state space of agent $i$, and let $\mathsf S$ be the complete configuration space. It contains the statistical laws, the source rows defined in \cref{ch:dynamics}, interfaces, and memory variables that the dynamics reads. Write $\operatorname{pr}_i:\mathsf S\to\mathsf S_i$ for the local state projection. For the measurable constructions, take these spaces to be standard Borel. A finite collection of probability-law spaces and finite or Euclidean memory spaces supplies such a realization. A flow, retention, or response used in a measurable construction must be Borel for these structures. Smoothness in a Banach chart of log densities does not by itself ensure this, because the norm-Borel sigma-algebra of $L^\infty(\lambda)$ for nonatomic $\lambda$ is strictly finer than the trace of the evaluation sigma-algebra; where such flows are used inside history laws, joint Borel measurability of $(t,x)\mapsto\Phi_t(x)$ for the evaluation structure is a stated hypothesis. It holds without further argument for continuous flows on finite-dimensional families and on separable charts whose chart map into the law space is continuous and injective, by the Lusin--Souslin theorem \citep[Theorem~15.1]{Kechris1995}. Policies and record densities entering such history laws are subject to the measurability conditions of \cref{app:records}.

\definitionheading{Record and readout}{def:record}
A record readout is a measurable map
\begin{equation}
\rho_i:\mathsf S_i\longrightarrow\mathsf O_i,
\label{eq:record-readout}
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf O_i$ is a standard Borel alphabet of internally distinguishable outcomes. If an interaction with source $j$ produces configuration $Z_{t+1}$, its record variable is
\begin{equation}
O_{ij,t}=\rho_i(\operatorname{pr}_i Z_{t+1}).
\label{eq:record-variable}
\end{equation}
For stochastic dynamics this is a measurable function from the probability space of realizations to $\mathsf O_i$. A realized value is denoted $o_{ij,t}$. The source label is part of the record only when the internal state retains it.

A record can be transient. Persistence means that the distinction remains accessible through a specified later readout. A list kept outside the agent does not supply this accessibility. A memory coordinate is therefore an explicit part of the model unless the required distinction is already represented in its state.

\propositionheading{Induced record law}{prop:record-law}
Let $\Gamma_{ij}:\mathsf S\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ be a normalized interaction transition. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_{ij}(x,A)
=\Gamma_{ij}\left(x,\{x':\rho_i(\operatorname{pr}_i x')\in A\}\right),
\label{eq:record-kernel}
\end{equation}
is a normalized record kernel. If $\Gamma_{ij}(x,\cdot)=\delta_{F_{ij}(x)}$, the record is the deterministic function $\rho_i\circ\operatorname{pr}_i\circ F_{ij}$.
\begin{proof}
The right side is the pushforward of a probability measure through the measurable map $\rho_i\circ\operatorname{pr}_i$. Its total mass is one, and measurability in $x$ follows from the defining property of a kernel. The deterministic case follows by evaluating a Dirac measure.
\end{proof}

A readout that reads frame-dependent coordinates can change under a mere change of description. For example, a readout of the first coordinate of the displayed mean of $q_i$ returns $1$ for the law $\mathcal N(e_1,I)$ in one frame and $0$ after a passive rotation by $\pi/2$, although the configuration is the same. Records therefore carry a covariance requirement. A passive change is a tuple $\mathbf g=(g_i)_i$ of elements of $G$. It acts on $\mathsf S$ by a Borel automorphism $x\mapsto\mathbf g\cdot x$, on laws by pushforward, on frames by left multiplication, and on interface data by their declared rule, with $\operatorname{pr}_i(\mathbf g\cdot x)=g_i\cdot\operatorname{pr}_ix$. A readout is invariant if $\rho_i(g_i\cdot x_i)=\rho_i(x_i)$, and equivariant if $\rho_i(g_i\cdot x_i)=\tau_i(g_i)\rho_i(x_i)$ for a declared jointly Borel action $\tau_i$ of $G$ on $\mathsf O_i$. A transition is covariant if $\Gamma_{ij}(\mathbf g\cdot x,\mathbf g\cdot B)=\Gamma_{ij}(x,B)$ for all measurable $B$. Every readout, record kernel, and response kernel of this text is required to be invariant or declared equivariant, and every interaction transition covariant.

By the dressing argument after \cref{eq:reference-laws}, a readout written through reference laws satisfies this requirement automatically: the first coordinate of the reference mean $U_i^{-1}\mu_i$ is unchanged by every passive change, yet it changes under a common change of the reference point $\sigma$ at the same bundle point. A readout, record kernel, or response kernel is reference consistent if re-expressing all reference laws and supplied data under a common change of reference point leaves it unchanged, or changes its outcome by the declared action. This is the condition that \cref{hyp:int-reference-consistency} imposes on channels; without it a record law is not a function of the bundle data. Results whose conclusions concern bundle data assume it for the records they use.

\propositionheading{Covariant record laws}{prop:record-covariance}
If $\Gamma_{ij}$ is covariant and $\rho_i$ is equivariant with action $\tau_i$, then $\mathcal R_{ij}(\mathbf g\cdot x,A)=\mathcal R_{ij}(x,\tau_i(g_i)^{-1}A)$. For an invariant readout, $\mathcal R_{ij}(\mathbf g\cdot x,A)=\mathcal R_{ij}(x,A)$.
\begin{proof}
Write $B_A=\{x':\rho_i(\operatorname{pr}_ix')\in A\}$. Covariance gives $\mathcal R_{ij}(\mathbf g\cdot x,A)=\Gamma_{ij}(x,\mathbf g^{-1}\cdot B_A)$, and $\mathbf g^{-1}\cdot B_A=\{x':\tau_i(g_i)\rho_i(\operatorname{pr}_ix')\in A\}=B_{\tau_i(g_i)^{-1}A}$.
\end{proof}

This definition introduces no observing agent outside the population. It specifies where an outcome is available and how it can enter later dynamics. It does not derive the transition $\Gamma_{ij}$ or decide whether its stochasticity represents objective chance. Those are further modeling choices. A response protocol queried from outside the population, as in \cref{ch:open-formation}, is a separate declared device and is named wherever it is the only element of the observable algebra that resolves a distinction.

For example, a vector comparison can be represented by
\begin{equation}
r_{ij}:E_i^q\times E_j^q\times\R^r\to\R^r,
\qquad
r_{ij}(k_i,k_j,\epsilon)=A_i k_i-B_{ij}k_j+\epsilon.
\label{eq:sample-comparison}
\end{equation}
Under passive changes $k_i\mapsto g_ik_i$ and $k_j\mapsto g_jk_j$ the comparison matrices transform as $A_i\mapsto A_ig_i^{-1}$ and $B_{ij}\mapsto B_{ij}g_j^{-1}$, so the comparison value is invariant. A noise law turns this map into a record kernel on represented contents. A joint content law determines its predicted outcomes. A transition on agent states must still specify how an outcome is registered and how the agent changes. A predictive likelihood and an internal update thus have different domains.

\section{Beliefs and generative models}
\label{sec:generative-models}

An agent holds beliefs and generative models. The joint law $\zeta_i$ describes the first; the second is the mechanism that gives a model presentation predictive content.

\definitionheading{Generative model of an agent}{def:generative-model}
At a fixed context, a generative model of agent $i$ consists of a proper presentation prior $r_i$ on $\mathsf M_i$, a normalized evaluator $G_i:\mathsf M_i\rightsquigarrow\mathsf K_i$, and a normalized record kernel $\ell_i:\mathsf K_i\times\mathsf M_i\rightsquigarrow\mathsf O_i$. They define the normalized joint
\begin{equation}
 P_i(dm,dk,do)=r_i(dm)G_i(m,dk)\ell_i(k,m;do).
 \label{eq:generative-model}
\end{equation}
Each presentation $m$ indexes the generative model $G_i(m,dk)\ell_i(k,m;do)$: a prior over represented contents together with a likelihood for records. The presentation prior $r_i$ is the prior over presentations; the name reference law is reserved for the reference-frame laws \cref{eq:reference-laws}. When $\ell_i$ does not depend on $m$, it is the record kernel $H_i$ of \cref{ch:prediction}. The model is the self-only case of the per-agent model \cref{eq:dyn-per-agent-model} of \cref{ch:dynamics}, in which peers' communicated laws are added as further sources.

The model is specified in reference coordinates by $(\bar r_i,\bar G_i,\bar\ell_i)$ and displayed through the frame,
\begin{equation}
 r_i=(U_i)_\#\bar r_i,\qquad
 G_i(m,\cdot)=(U_i)_\#\bar G_i(U_i^{-1}m,\cdot),\qquad
 \ell_i(k,m;\cdot)=\tau_i(U_i)_\#\bar\ell_i(U_i^{-1}k,U_i^{-1}m;\cdot),
 \label{eq:model-display}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_i$ trivial for invariant readouts. The record kernel has a declared density, $\ell_i(k,m;do)=l_i(o\mid k,m)\lambda_{\mathsf O_i}(do)$, with $l_i$ jointly measurable in $(o,k,m)$ and $\lambda_{\mathsf O_i}$ a declared sigma-finite reference measure invariant under $\tau_i$. Changing $\lambda_{\mathsf O_i}$ to $w\lambda_{\mathsf O_i}$ shifts the free energy below by $\log w(o)$, so the reference measure is part of the model.

Given a record value $o$ with $0<P_i(o)<\infty$, where $P_i(o)=\int l_i(o\mid k,m)G_i(m,dk)r_i(dm)$ is the evidence density, the recognition law $\zeta_i$ is a candidate for the posterior $P_i(dm,dk\mid o)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_i(\zeta_i;o)=\KL\left(\zeta_i\Vert P_i(\cdot\mid o)\right)-\log P_i(o)
 \label{eq:agent-free-energy}
\end{equation}
is its variational free energy \citep{Friston2010,BleiEtAl2017}. In this reading $q_i$ is the agent's belief about represented contents, its perception, and $s_i$ is its belief over generative models. The model law alone predicts nothing: by \cref{eq:generative-model}, prediction requires the evaluator and the record kernel. Write
\begin{equation}
 K_i^{\mathrm{rec}}(m,do)=\int G_i(m,dk)\ell_i(k,m;do)
 \label{eq:composite-record-kernel}
\end{equation}
for the composite record kernel of a presentation. An evaluator and record kernel whose composite does not depend on $m$ make the model sector predictively inert; a constant evaluator suffices when $\ell_i$ does not read $m$, and does not suffice otherwise. Without records, the unique minimizer of $\KL(\zeta_i\Vert r_iG_i)$ is the prior joint itself, whose belief conditional $q_i(dk\mid m)$ equals the evaluator $G_i(m,dk)$; the conditional in \cref{eq:agent-disintegration} is the agent's current departure from that prior. With records, the posterior form \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} equals $\KL(\zeta_i\Vert r_iG_i)-\E_{\zeta_i}\log l_i(o\mid k,m)$ whenever the right side is not of the form $\infty-\infty$.

These generative models contain no action, policy, or expected free energy, and their presentation priors and evaluators are held fixed in every result of the text. The free energies studied below are therefore perceptual: records are supplied or registered by declared mechanisms, and neither the choice of the next record nor the learning of $r_i$ and $G_i$ is modeled.

\propositionheading{Frame invariance of the agent free energy}{prop:agent-fe-invariance}
Let the model be displayed by \cref{eq:model-display}. Under a passive change $U_i\mapsto g_iU_i$, with $\zeta_i\mapsto(g_i)_\#\zeta_i$ and $o\mapsto\tau_i(g_i)o$, the value of \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} is unchanged. If the reference data are re-expressed under a common change of reference point, the displayed model, and hence the value, is unchanged as well.
\begin{proof}
The displayed joint transforms by the pushforward under $(m,k,o)\mapsto(g_im,g_ik,\tau_i(g_i)o)$. Invariance of $\lambda_{\mathsf O_i}$ gives the transformed density $l_i(\tau_i(g_i)^{-1}o\mid g_i^{-1}k,g_i^{-1}m)$, so the evidence density at $\tau_i(g_i)o$ equals $P_i(o)$, and the posterior given the transformed record is the pushforward of $P_i(\cdot\mid o)$ under $(m,k)\mapsto(g_im,g_ik)$. \Cref{prop:kl-coordinate} gives invariance of the divergence. Under a common change of reference point the frames and the reference data change by inverse elements, which cancel in \cref{eq:model-display}.
\end{proof}
The second statement is naturality in the sense described after \cref{eq:reference-laws}. Holding $(\bar r_i,\bar G_i,\bar\ell_i)$ fixed while the reference laws change is an active transformation, and it changes $\mathcal F_i$ in general: with $\mathsf K_i=\R$, a standard normal evaluator, no record, and $\zeta_i$ standard normal, a translation of the reference law by $a$ moves $\mathcal F_i$ from $0$ to $a^2/2$.

\definitionheading{Shared generative model}{def:shared-model}
Let agents $i$ and $j$ have $\mathsf M_i=\mathsf M_j$, $\mathsf K_i=\mathsf K_j$, $\mathsf O_i=\mathsf O_j$, with the same actions of $G$. Their generative models are isomorphic if there are bimeasurable bijections $\phi$ of $\mathsf M_i$ and $\psi$ of $\mathsf K_i$ with
\begin{equation}
 r_j=\phi_\#r_i,\qquad
 G_j(\phi(m),\cdot)=\psi_\#G_i(m,\cdot),\qquad
 \ell_j(\psi(k),\phi(m);\cdot)=\ell_i(k,m;\cdot).
 \label{eq:shared-model}
\end{equation}
They share a generative model up to frame if the reference models of \cref{eq:model-display} are related by one element $g\in G$ acting on both sectors,
\begin{equation}
 \bar r_j=g_\#\bar r_i,\qquad
 \bar G_j(gm,\cdot)=g_\#\bar G_i(m,\cdot),\qquad
 \bar\ell_j(gk,gm;\cdot)=\tau(g)_\#\bar\ell_i(k,m;\cdot),
 \label{eq:shared-model-frame}
\end{equation}
with $\tau$ the common declared action on records. Sharing up to frame implies isomorphism, with $\phi$ and $\psi$ the actions of $U_jgU_i^{-1}$ in the two sectors when records are invariant; the converse fails, since \cref{eq:shared-model} admits independent bijections of the two sectors that are not actions of a group element, and for nonatomic presentation priors the condition $r_j=\phi_\#r_i$ alone constrains nothing. A population whose members pairwise share a generative model up to frame is called a species. Statements that use channels, codes, or dictionaries within a species require the frame version, because channels are covariant only under the group action. Codes and dictionaries are not components of \cref{eq:generative-model}; a shared language is a separately declared datum.

Sharing a generative model is a statement about mechanisms. Agreement of beliefs over models, $\KL(s_j\Vert\phi_\#s_i)=0$, is a statement about states. The two are independent, and agents with a shared model can hold different beliefs. What records can test is weaker than either form of sharing. For records that are conditionally independent given $(k,m)$, write $K_i^{(n)}(m,do_{1:n})=\int G_i(m,dk)\prod_{t=1}^n\ell_i(k,m;do_t)$ for the composite kernel of $n$ records, so that $K_i^{(1)}=K_i^{\mathrm{rec}}$.

\propositionheading{Predictive equivalence}{prop:predictive-equivalence}
Fix $n\ge1$, a bimeasurable bijection $\phi$ of $\mathsf M_i$, and the preparation and accessible record history.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If the models are isomorphic through $(\phi,\psi)$, then $\int K_j^{(n)}(m',\cdot)(\phi_\#s)(dm')=\int K_i^{(n)}(m,\cdot)s(dm)$ for every model belief $s$, and after a common history of $n$ records the posteriors correspond under $\psi\times\phi$.
\item[(ii)] Equality of the predicted laws of $n$ records for every model belief $s$ and its image $\phi_\#s$ holds exactly when $K_j^{(n)}(\phi(m),\cdot)=K_i^{(n)}(m,\cdot)$ for every $m$; this condition also makes the model posteriors correspond under $\phi$ after every history of $n$ records.
\item[(iii)] Isomorphism is strictly stronger than the condition of (ii) for every $n$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
For (i), substituting the three relations of \cref{eq:shared-model} gives $P_j=(\phi\times\psi\times\id)_\#P_i$ record by record, which yields both statements. For (ii), sufficiency is integration against $s$ and Bayes' rule with the same kernel; necessity follows by taking $s=\delta_m$. For (iii), let $\mathsf K=\mathsf M=\mathsf O=\{0,1\}$ with equal presentation priors, $\ell_i(k,m;\cdot)=\ell_j(k,m;\cdot)=\mathrm{Ber}(p_m)$ independent of $k$ with $p_0\ne p_1$, $G_i(m,\cdot)$ uniform and $G_j(m,\cdot)=\delta_0$. Then $K_i^{(n)}=K_j^{(n)}$ is the law of $n$ independent $\mathrm{Ber}(p_m)$ records for every $n$, with $\phi=\id$, while no bijection $\psi$ carries the uniform law to $\delta_0$; the content posteriors stay uniform for $i$ and equal $\delta_0$ for $j$.
\end{proof}
Under the recognition reading, in which agent $i$ predicts a record by $\int\ell_i(k,m;\cdot)\zeta_i(dk,dm)$, equal model beliefs do not suffice even for one shared model, since the belief conditionals also enter; predictions agree when the models are isomorphic through $(\phi,\psi)$ and $\zeta_j=(\psi\times\phi)_\#\zeta_i$. A record registered by one agent can change its posterior, and therefore its predictions, without changing those of an agent that did not register it; a record whose likelihood is constant in $(k,m)$ changes nothing.

The free energy \cref{eq:agent-free-energy} is a single-agent object. Interactions add a further choice. In one choice all agents are parts of one normalized joint over their contents and records. In another each agent's generative model treats the communicated laws of its peers as data. \Cref{ch:dynamics} develops both choices and a third, a direct interaction potential between laws. With fixed positive rows and an edge compared through an identity channel, \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} proves two restricted statements about the third: on finite alphabets no normalized model over finitely many finite-valued variables reproduces it for any rule assigning recognition laws, and with fixed channels no normalized model reproduces it on an open set when recognition is the product of the agent laws themselves. Neither statement covers coupled recognition laws on infinite spaces or law-dependent channels; the limits of the obstruction are described after \cref{prop:content-law}.

\section{Content laws on fields}
\label{sec:content-laws}

The global choice needs a joint law over the contents of all agents and, where contexts are compared, over contents at different contexts. Write $E_i=P\times_G\mathsf Y_i$ for the associated sample bundle. A point $[u,\zeta]$ of $\mathcal B_i$ at $c$ is identified with the law $(y\mapsto[u,y])_\#\zeta$ on the fiber of $E_i$ at $c$; this identification does not depend on the representative.

\definitionheading{Content law on fields}{def:content-law}
A content model consists of a standard Borel coefficient space $\mathsf Z$, a proper prior $P_0$ on $\mathsf Z$, and jointly measurable sample-section maps $f_i:\mathsf Z\times\mathcal U_i\to E_i$ with $f_i(z,c)$ in the fiber over $c$. For a recognition law $Q$ on $\mathsf Z$, the agent sections and finite-design laws are
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_i^Q(c)=(f_{i,c})_\#Q,\qquad
 Q_D=(F_D)_\#Q,\qquad
 F_D(z)=\bigl(f_i(z,c)\bigr)_{(i,c)\in D},
 \label{eq:content-law}
\end{equation}
where $f_{i,c}=f_i(\cdot,c)$ and $D$ is a finite set of pairs with $c\in\mathcal U_i$. A record mechanism is a normalized kernel $K_a(do_a\mid z)=k_a(o_a\mid z)\lambda_a(do_a)$ with a declared sigma-finite reference measure $\lambda_a$ and a density $k_a$ jointly measurable in $(o_a,z)$; it may read field evaluations, model presentations, communication maps, and parallel transports along declared paths. A record kernel must be specified through bundle points, equivalently it must transform with every fiber datum it reads. With conditionally independent records, $\mathbb P(dz,do)=P_0(dz)\prod_aK_a(do_a\mid z)$. At a record value $o$ write $L_o(z)=\prod_ak_a(o_a\mid z)$ for the likelihood on $\mathsf Z$; when $0<Z_o=\int L_odP_0<\infty$, the posterior is $\Pi_o=L_oP_0/Z_o$ and the free energy of a recognition law is $\mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o$, as in \cref{eq:dyn-fixed-vfe}.

The sample bundle $E_i$ carries a standard Borel structure by the argument of \cref{lem:measurable-bundle}, since its transition maps $(c,y)\mapsto(c,g_{\alpha\beta}(c)y)$ are Borel by joint measurability of the action. In a trivialization, $\zeta_i^Q(c)(B)=\int\mathbf 1_B(f_i(z,c))Q(dz)$ is Borel in $c$ by Tonelli's theorem, so $c\mapsto\zeta_i^Q(c)$ is a Borel section of $\mathcal B_i$.

\propositionheading{Consistency and projected free energy}{prop:content-law}
For $D\subseteq D'$ let $r_{D'D}$ delete the added coordinates. Then $(r_{D'D})_\#Q_{D'}=Q_D$, and the marginal of $Q_D$ at $(i,c)$ is $\zeta_i^Q(c)$. For a record value $o$ with $0<Z_o<\infty$, and whenever the free energy is finite,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(Q;o)
 =\KL(Q_D\Vert(F_D)_\#\Pi_o)-\log Z_o
 +\int\KL\bigl(Q(dz\mid F_D=w)\Vert\Pi_o(dz\mid F_D=w)\bigr)Q_D(dw).
 \label{eq:content-projected}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Since $F_D=r_{D'D}\circ F_{D'}$, pushforwards compose, which gives consistency, and the marginal statement is the case of one pair. The second identity is the chain rule for relative entropy applied to $Q$ and $\Pi_o$ disintegrated along the measurable map $F_D$, which is the deterministic-kernel case of \cref{thm:predictive-chain}, combined with $\mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o$.
\end{proof}

The last term of \cref{eq:content-projected} is the exact cost of the information that a finite description omits. It need not vanish even when $D$ contains every evaluation the records read, because recognition can have the wrong conditional law in unobserved directions. The law-valued configuration of the agents at the contexts in $D$ is the list of marginals of $Q_D$; it does not determine $Q_D$. The reciprocal potential of \cref{ch:dynamics} is a separate constitutive object defined on such lists. Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe}, it is not $\mathcal F(Q;o)$ when $Q$ is the product of these marginals and the channels are fixed, nor, on finite alphabets, for any rule assigning recognition laws. On infinite content spaces no such statement holds for coupled recognition laws without a restriction on how $Q$ depends on the configuration. Take $\mathsf Z$ the product of the agents' spaces, $f_i$ the coordinate maps, $P_0$ the product of the reference joints of \cref{ch:dynamics}, and no record. Then $\KL(Q\Vert P_0)$ is the sum of the marginal divergences and the total correlation of $Q$, and a coupling of atomless marginals can carry any prescribed nonnegative total correlation; a Gaussian copula with correlation $\rho$ carries $-\tfrac12\log(1-\rho^2)$ for two agents. Choosing the coupling of the agent laws at each configuration so that its total correlation equals the excess of the potential over its single-agent terms reproduces the potential with fixed rows, up to a constant, as $\mathcal F(Q(x);o)$ with the agent laws as marginals, at every configuration with atomless laws and finite potential. This construction is degenerate, since the coupling only stores the cost, but it shows that the separation of the reciprocal objective from the global one rests on the product recognition family and on the kinetic metric, not on normalization alone. On a finite alphabet the total correlation of a coupling is bounded by the logarithm of the alphabet sizes, which is the boundedness used in part (a) of the proposition.

\section{What correlations describe}

Suppose two represented bits have the independent uniform law. Register the event that they agree. Their conditional joint law assigns probability $1/2$ to each of $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. Each marginal remains uniform, while the conditional expectation of their product changes from $1/4$ to $1/2$. An exact joint posterior has acquired dependence that its two marginals cannot report.

This example identifies a concrete need for a joint law: predicting an observable that reads both bits after the shared record. It does not imply that every admissible agent dynamics must propagate that posterior. A coupled product-family dynamics is another precise model, provided it is declared, but it does not preserve the marginal symmetry. Against the hard agreement record, a product recognition law has finite free energy only when it is a point mass on $(0,0)$ or on $(1,1)$, at relative entropy $\log2$ from the posterior. For a soft record $\Pi\propto e^{J\sigma_1\sigma_2}$ in spin variables, the symmetric product optimum has magnetization $\bar\sigma$ solving $\bar\sigma=\tanh(J\bar\sigma)$, which is zero for $J\leq1$ and nonzero for $J>1$. Similarly, two routes that inspect the same source must share its identity; replacing it by independent redraws specifies a different experiment.

The same distinction governs what a comparison of laws can detect along a loop of routes.

\propositionheading{Law comparisons and shared samples}{prop:loop-blindness}
Let $H,H'$ be bimeasurable bijections of a standard Borel space $E$, for example two loop transports, and let $\nu\in\Prob(E)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $H_\#\nu=H'_\#\nu$, every functional of the pair of laws $(\nu,H_\#\nu)$ takes the same value for $H$ and $H'$. In particular $\KL(\nu\Vert H_\#\nu)=0$ exactly when $H$ preserves $\nu$.
\item[(ii)] The law $(\id,H)_\#\nu$ of the shared-sample pair $(Y,HY)$, $Y\sim\nu$, determines $H$ up to $\nu$-null sets.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
Part (i) is immediate because the pair of laws is the same. For (ii), suppose $(\id,H)_\#\nu=(\id,H')_\#\nu$. The complement of the graph of $H'$ is a Borel set $B$. Its measure under the first law is $\nu\{H\neq H'\}$ and under the second law is zero.
\end{proof}

Each interaction cost of \cref{ch:interactions,ch:dynamics}, and each receiver force, depends on a transport only through the transported law, so at one configuration no law-level comparison separates two transports with the same image law. A rotation $R$ leaves a centered Gaussian $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$ unchanged exactly when $R\Sigma R^T=\Sigma$; in dimension at least two this stabilizer always contains nonidentity rotations, for example the half-turn in the plane of two eigenvectors, and for isotropic $\Sigma$ it is the whole rotation group. The dynamics is not blind in the same way. The source-role force of a cost $\KL(\nu_i\Vert H_\#\nu_j)$ is the receiver likelihood ratio pulled back through $H$ itself, $y\mapsto-(d\nu_i/dH_\#\nu_j)(Hy)$, which depends on $H$ pointwise. For a self channel $H$ on a finite alphabet and an initial law $\nu_0$ with $H_\#\nu_0=\nu_0$, the first variation of $\KL(\nu\Vert H_\#\nu)$ vanishes at $\nu_0$, so the initial velocity of the unit Fisher flow of $\KL(\nu\Vert r)+\KL(\nu\Vert H_\#\nu)$ does not depend on $H$ within the stabilizer; with $v$ that common initial log-velocity, $\nu(t)=\nu_0(1+tv)+O(t^2)$ and the quadratic expansion of relative entropy give
\begin{equation}
\KL(\nu(t)\Vert H_\#\nu(t))=\frac{t^2}{2}\sum_y\nu_0(y)\bigl(v(y)-v(H^{-1}y)\bigr)^2+O(t^3).
\end{equation}
The loop classes of $H$ and of the identity are therefore separated along the trajectory at second order whenever the record-free drift breaks the stabilizer, and on a two-cycle through two agents it can appear at first order, because the source forces at the initial configuration already differ. A record that is sensitive to such a transport at the instant of comparison must read a coupling of the same sample along the routes, as the route mark of the supplement does; under a frame change $g$ at the base point the pair transforms diagonally, $(Y,HY)\mapsto(gY,gHg^{-1}gY)$, so invariant records are diagonal-invariant functions of the pair. Such records require a content law in the sense of \cref{def:content-law}.

The configuration $x$ of law-valued agents, a sample tuple $(k_i,m_i)_i$ used in a probabilistic model, and an ensemble of possible configurations are three different objects. A deterministic dynamics on $x$ needs no probability law over configurations. A preparation ensemble can be introduced to assess predictions or compression, but it is then stated explicitly. These distinctions will matter when a parent retains a law over contents while discarding information needed to evolve that law.
