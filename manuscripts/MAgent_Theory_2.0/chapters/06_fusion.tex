\chapter{Finite Fusion with Exact Statistical Inheritance}
\label{ch:fusion}

We now construct a population whose dynamics selects persistent blocks and
then fuses the resulting parents. Pairwise fusion penalties occur in convex
clustering \cite{Hocking2011}, and suitable nonsmooth consensus protocols
produce finite-time agreement \cite{Cortes2006}. The construction here also
specifies the represented laws, model evaluator, gauge transport, parent
metric, and boundary kernels. Its peer potential is a new nonsmooth
square-root-KL interaction. It is distinct from the smooth KL potential of
the preceding dynamics chapter.

\section{A law family closed under geometric averaging}

Work over one context, with state and model spaces
$\mathsf K=\mathsf M=(0,\infty)$. Write
$\operatorname{LN}(\mu,\sigma^2)$ for the law of $e^Z$ when
$Z\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$. An agent of integer multiplicity $n\geq1$ carries
the product law
\begin{equation}
 q_n=\operatorname{LN}(\mu^q,1/n),\qquad
 s_n=\operatorname{LN}(\mu^s,1/n),\qquad
 \zeta_n=q_n\otimes s_n.
 \label{fus:law-family}
\end{equation}
Multiplicity is retained state information. The variances are fixed within
each type; only the two log means evolve. Laws are first written in a common
reference coordinate. A fixed positive frame
$U_i^q=U_i^s=:U_i\in\GL^+(1)$ represents both sectors locally by
multiplication. Frames do not evolve in this example.

Choose constants $a>c>0$ and define the normalized reference and evaluator
\begin{equation}
 r_n=\operatorname{LN}\left(0,\frac{a}{n(a^2-c^2)}\right),\qquad
 G_n(m,dk)=\operatorname{LN}\left(\frac ca\log m,\frac1{an}\right)(dk).
 \label{fus:evaluator}
\end{equation}
The two occurrences of a lognormal expression in this equation denote
ordinary probability laws and kernels. In logarithmic coordinates the joint
reference $r_n(dm)G_n(m,dk)$ has precision
\begin{equation}
 nA,\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}a&-c\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{fus:precision}
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues $n(a-c)$ and $n(a+c)$ are positive. The predictive law is
\begin{equation}
 s_nG_n=\operatorname{LN}\left(
 \frac ca\mu^s,\frac{(c/a)^2+1/a}{n}\right).
 \label{fus:predictive-law}
\end{equation}
Thus the evaluator depends on the represented model value. Physical
references and evaluators are obtained by pushing both coordinates through
the common local frame.

\propositionheading{Local energy and metric}{fus:local-inheritance}
For every $n\geq1$ and every pair of real means, let
\begin{equation}
 C=a-1-\frac12\log(a^2-c^2),\qquad
 \mathcal U(\mu^q,\mu^s)
 =\frac a2\bigl((\mu^q)^2+(\mu^s)^2\bigr)-c\mu^q\mu^s.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 E_{0,n}=\KL(q_n\otimes s_n\|r_nG_n)
 =C+n\mathcal U,\qquad
 g_n=n\bigl((d\mu^q)^2+(d\mu^s)^2\bigr).
 \label{fus:local-formulas}
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The logarithm is a measurable bijection, so it preserves KL divergence.
The recognition law in log coordinates has covariance $I_2/n$, while the
reference has covariance $A^{-1}/n$. The Gaussian KL formula gives
\begin{equation}
 E_{0,n}=\frac12\left[
 \operatorname{tr}A-2-\log\det A
 +n(\mu^q,\mu^s)A(\mu^q,\mu^s)^{\mathsf T}\right],
\end{equation}
which is the stated expression. The score for either mean is
$n(Z-\mu)$, whose squared expectation is $n$. The product law has zero
cross score expectation, giving the displayed Fisher metric. $\square$

For independent synchronized children of multiplicities $n_j$, set
$n_I=\sum_{j\in I}n_j$. Their retained reference samples are
\begin{equation}
 k_I=\prod_{j\in I}k_j^{n_j/n_I},\qquad
 m_I=\prod_{j\in I}m_j^{n_j/n_I}.
 \label{fus:sample-retention}
\end{equation}
Weighted log averages have variance
$\sum_j(n_j/n_I)^2/n_j=1/n_I$. The same calculation for the joint
reference gives $r_{n_I}G_{n_I}$. Conditional log observation noise has
variance $\sum_j(n_j/n_I)^2/(an_j)=1/(an_I)$, so the evaluator itself
is inherited. Physical samples are first returned through $U_j^{-1}$ and
the retained sample is expressed in a chosen parent frame $U_I$.

\section{Messages and state-dependent scalar links}

Define a normalized message kernel by
\begin{equation}
 B_n(k,dw)=\operatorname{LN}(\log k,1-1/n)(dw),
 \label{fus:message-kernel}
\end{equation}
interpreting $B_1(k,dw)=\delta_k(dw)$. It restores unit log variance:
$q_nB_n=\operatorname{LN}(\mu^q,1)$ and
$s_nB_n=\operatorname{LN}(\mu^s,1)$. These messages give all multiplicities
a common comparison family.

Model prediction also commutes with this interface. Put
$h=(c/a)^2+1/a$ and define
$B_n^h(k,dw)=\operatorname{LN}(\log k,h(1-1/n))(dw)$, again using the
identity at $n=1$. Both routes in the kernel identity
\begin{equation}
 G_nB_n^h=B_nG_1
 \label{fus:evaluator-square}
\end{equation}
have conditional log mean $(c/a)\log m$ and variance
$h-(c/a)^2/n$. Integrating against $s_n$ gives
\begin{equation}
 (s_nB_n)G_1=(s_nG_n)B_n^h
 =\operatorname{LN}\left(\frac ca\mu^s,h\right).
 \label{fus:predictive-square}
\end{equation}
Thus a parent can emit the original predictive message by either normalized
route.

For each sector $\nu\in\{q,s\}$, choose a fixed $0<\chi_\nu<1$ and set
\begin{equation}
 L_{ij}^\nu=\chi_\nu(\mu_i^\nu-\mu_j^\nu),\qquad
 \Theta_{ij}^\nu=U_i\exp(L_{ij}^\nu)U_j^{-1}.
 \label{fus:links}
\end{equation}
Under a local change of frame $U_i\mapsto h_iU_i$, the physical transport
changes to $h_i\Theta_{ij}^\nu h_j^{-1}$. The reference means and
$L_{ij}^\nu$ remain unchanged. This supplies nonidentity, law-dependent
positive scalings when means differ. The chosen scalar links have trivial
cycle product; no curvature claim is needed.

Transport of the reference message from $j$ shifts its log mean to
$\mu_j^\nu+\chi_\nu(\mu_i^\nu-\mu_j^\nu)$. Consequently the directed
comparison cost
\begin{equation}
 d_{ij}^\nu=
 \frac{\sqrt{2\KL\bigl(
 \operatorname{LN}(\mu_i^\nu,1)\|
 (\exp L_{ij}^\nu)_{\#}\operatorname{LN}(\mu_j^\nu,1)
 \bigr)}}{1-\chi_\nu}
 =|\mu_i^\nu-\mu_j^\nu|
 \label{fus:distance}
\end{equation}
is gauge invariant. The equality includes the dependence of the link on
both endpoint laws. For unequal means, differentiating its log-mean
residual gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_{\mu_i^\nu}
 \bigl[\mu_i^\nu-\mu_j^\nu-L_{ij}^\nu\bigr]=1-\chi_\nu.
\end{equation}
This factor cancels the denominator in \eqref{fus:distance}. Holding the
link fixed while differentiating would give a different dynamics.

\section{The continuous subgradient dynamics}

For multiplicities $n_i$ with total $N=\sum_i n_i$, choose $\lambda>0$ and
the potential
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(\boldsymbol\mu)
 =\sum_i n_i\bigl[C+\mathcal U(\mu_i^q,\mu_i^s)\bigr]
 +\lambda\sum_{i<j}n_in_j
 \bigl(|\mu_i^q-\mu_j^q|+|\mu_i^s-\mu_j^s|\bigr).
 \label{fus:potential}
\end{equation}
All contacts are fixed and present. If source rows are recorded, take
$\beta_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}=n_j/N$, including self entries. Summing child
entries gives exactly the same parent row. Self comparison costs vanish,
while their row mass records the corresponding contact multiplicity.
There is no Gibbs row adaptation. The local term has the stated KL origin;
no fixed sample-model ELBO identity is asserted for the entire potential.

With $M=\operatorname{diag}(n_1,n_1,\ldots)$, define the flow on
$\R^{2r}$, for $r$ current agents, by
\begin{equation}
 M\dot{\boldsymbol\mu}\in-\partial\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\mu).
 \label{fus:inclusion}
\end{equation}
Solutions are absolutely continuous in the external time $t\geq0$ and
satisfy this inclusion almost everywhere. There are no boundaries or
stochastic driving terms. Both law sectors have the same basic mobility;
the factor $1/n_i$ comes from their inherited Fisher metric. In coordinates,
\begin{align}
 \dot\mu_i^q&=-a\mu_i^q+c\mu_i^s
 -\lambda\sum_{j\ne i}n_j\sigma_{ij}^q,\\
 \dot\mu_i^s&=-a\mu_i^s+c\mu_i^q
 -\lambda\sum_{j\ne i}n_j\sigma_{ij}^s,
 \label{fus:coordinate-flow}
\end{align}
where $\sigma_{ij}^\nu=-\sigma_{ji}^\nu$ belongs to
$\operatorname{Sign}(\mu_i^\nu-\mu_j^\nu)$ and
$\operatorname{Sign}(0)=[-1,1]$.

\lemmaheading{Uniqueness and persistence}{fus:uniqueness}
Two solutions of \eqref{fus:inclusion} with the same masses obey
\begin{equation}
 \|\boldsymbol\mu(t)-\widetilde{\boldsymbol\mu}(t)\|_M
 \leq e^{-(a-c)t}
 \|\boldsymbol\mu(0)-\widetilde{\boldsymbol\mu}(0)\|_M.
 \label{fus:contraction}
\end{equation}
On any interval where a solution exists, synchronized unit-mass children
remain synchronized in both sectors.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The quadratic part is $(a-c)$-strongly convex in the $M$ metric and the
absolute-value part is convex. For subgradients $\xi,\widetilde\xi$ at
two configurations, monotonicity gives
\begin{equation}
 \langle\xi-\widetilde\xi,
 \boldsymbol\mu-\widetilde{\boldsymbol\mu}\rangle
 \geq(a-c)\|\boldsymbol\mu-\widetilde{\boldsymbol\mu}\|_M^2.
\end{equation}
Substitution from \eqref{fus:inclusion} and integration proves
\eqref{fus:contraction}. Swapping two equal unit-mass children preserves
the initial configuration, potential, and metric. Uniqueness therefore
preserves their equality. This argument concerns the full two-sector
system, not a restricted scalar equation. $\square$

\section{An invariant class with explicit global solutions}

\theoremheading{Finite sticking on a common-offset family}{fus:sticking}
Let all initial masses be one and suppose
\begin{equation}
 \mu_i^q(0)=u_i^0+v_0,\qquad
 \mu_i^s(0)=u_i^0-v_0,
 \label{fus:admitted-class}
\end{equation}
where the $u_i^0$ are distinct real numbers and $v_0\ne0$. There is a
unique global solution of the full inclusion for these initial data.
Both sectors have the form
\begin{equation}
 \mu_i^q=u_i+v,\qquad \mu_i^s=u_i-v,\qquad
 v(t)=v_0e^{-(a+c)t}.
 \label{fus:invariant-form}
\end{equation}
Blocks formed by equal $u_i$ persist and follow the mass-weighted version
of the same equation.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Set $\kappa=a-c>0$. Equal sector orderings reduce
\eqref{fus:coordinate-flow} to
\begin{equation}
 \dot u_i\in-\kappa u_i-\lambda\sum_{j\ne i}n_j
 \operatorname{Sign}(u_i-u_j),\qquad
 \dot v=-(a+c)v.
\end{equation}
Introduce the increasing auxiliary time and rescaled positions
\begin{equation}
 \tau(t)=\frac{e^{\kappa t}-1}{\kappa},\qquad
 u_i(t)=e^{-\kappa t}z_i(\tau(t)).
 \label{fus:time-change}
\end{equation}
Since $\dot\tau=e^{\kappa t}>0$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 z_i'\in-\lambda\sum_{j\ne i}n_j\operatorname{Sign}(z_i-z_j).
 \label{fus:sticky-flow}
\end{equation}
An ordered block has constant velocity
$\lambda(M_{\rm right}-M_{\rm left})$ between collisions. Adjacent blocks
approach with relative speed $\lambda(n_I+n_J)>0$. At their collision,
join their masses, retain the common position, and use the new velocity
$\lambda(M_{\rm right}-M_{\rm left})$. This equals the mass-weighted
average of their velocities just before collision. Choosing zero internal
subgradients verifies the original inclusion for every constituent child
after collision. Each event reduces the number of blocks, so at most
$N-1$ events occur, and the resulting piecewise-linear curve exists for
all $\tau\geq0$. Reversing \eqref{fus:time-change} constructs a global
full-system solution. \Cref{fus:uniqueness} makes it unique. $\square$

The class \eqref{fus:admitted-class} has a common difference between its
two sector means. This is an initial-data restriction, not an imposed
constraint force. It is a proper submanifold of the full parameter space.
The two laws of each agent remain distinct at every finite time because
$v(t)\ne0$. The auxiliary $\tau$ is a monotone reparameterization of this
solution, not a geometric or operational clock.

\section{Two formation stages without a supplied partition}

\theoremheading{Four agents form two parents and then one}{fus:two-stages}
In \cref{fus:sticking}, take four agents with the unordered initial set
\begin{equation}
 \{u_i^0\}=\{-3d,-2d,2d,3d\},\qquad d>0.
\end{equation}
Two size-two blocks form at $t_1$ and remain distinct until $t_2>t_1$, where
they fuse under their inherited equations, with
\begin{equation}
 t_1=\frac1\kappa\log\left(1+\frac{\kappa d}{2\lambda}\right),\qquad
 t_2=\frac1\kappa\log\left(1+\frac{5\kappa d}{4\lambda}\right).
 \label{fus:collision-times}
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
In sorted order, the initial $z$ velocities are
$(3\lambda,\lambda,-\lambda,-3\lambda)$. The two outer gaps close at
$\tau_1=d/(2\lambda)$, leaving parent positions $-3d/2$ and $3d/2$.
Their inherited masses are two and their velocities are $2\lambda$ and
$-2\lambda$. Their gap closes after another $3d/(4\lambda)$, giving
$\tau_2=5d/(4\lambda)$. Inverting \eqref{fus:time-change} gives
\eqref{fus:collision-times}. $\square$

Exact reflection symmetry is unnecessary. For four sorted initial positions,
let their adjacent gaps be $g_1,g_2,g_3>0$, and put $H=\max(g_1,g_3)$.
If
\begin{equation}
 D=g_2+\frac{g_1+g_3}{2}-2H>0,
 \label{fus:open-gap-condition}
\end{equation}
both outer pairs form by $\tau_*=H/(2\lambda)$. To check that no middle
collision intervenes, first note that $D>0$ implies $g_2>H$, so a middle
collision cannot occur first. Suppose $g_1\leq g_3$. At the first outer collision
the middle gap is $g_2-g_1$; afterward it decreases at speed $3\lambda$
until the other outer pair collides at $g_3/(2\lambda)$. Its value there
is $D>0$. The case $g_3\leq g_1$ is the reflected calculation. The two
parent gap then decreases at speed $4\lambda$, and the final time is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\rm final}=\tau_*+\frac{D}{4\lambda}
 =\frac{2g_2+g_1+g_3}{8\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Condition \eqref{fus:open-gap-condition} is strict and defines an open
set relative to the ordered common-offset family. Taking all label
permutations gives an admitted class with no assigned labels for blocks.
The initial laws are unequal across agents, and the interaction rule
contains no partition. Membership is selected by attained equality.

\section{The inherited handoff and its scope}

At a collision, restrict a block $I$ to its common means. Summing its local
energies and pulling back its metric gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i\in I}n_i(C+\mathcal U)
 =n_I(C+\mathcal U)=E_{0,n_I}+(n_I-1)C,\qquad
 g_I=n_I\bigl((d\mu_I^q)^2+(d\mu_I^s)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Every external pair term sums to
$n_In_J|\mu_I^\nu-\mu_J^\nu|$. Zero internal subgradients therefore
lift each solution of the inherited equation to a fine solution. Uniqueness
identifies that lift with the original trajectory. This also proves
persistence for inherited masses by expanding a mass into synchronized
unit children. With $R$ retaining block means, masses, and frames, the
corresponding parameter flows satisfy the following identity. For a fixed
formed partition, keep both component index sets fixed even when further
components coincide; a later merger is a separate retention composed with
$R$. Then
\begin{equation}
 R\circ\Phi_t=\Phi_t^c\circ R
 \label{fus:flow-square}
\end{equation}
on every synchronized stratum reached by the construction, for all future
times. Weighted geometric averaging is associative, so later collisions
give the same retained laws as direct averaging. The state is continuous
at a collision; the velocity changes through the inherited mass sum.

Along the constructed continuous trajectory, ordinary differentiation on
each interval between collisions gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\mathcal F}{dt}
 =-\sum_i n_i\bigl((\dot\mu_i^q)^2+(\dot\mu_i^s)^2\bigr)
 \quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\end{equation}
The objective is continuous at collisions when the constants above are
retained. Comparing with the stationary configuration having every mean
zero in \eqref{fus:contraction} proves convergence to that configuration
for the admitted initial data. No discrete numerical update has been
specified, so this calculation makes no discrete-step assertion.

Sample retention is genuinely noninjective: different positive sample
vectors have the same geometric mean. For $n$ synchronized unit children,
their log samples conditional on the retained log mean $\bar Z$ have law
\begin{equation}
 (Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\mid\bar Z
 \sim N\left(\bar Z\mathbf1,
 I_n-\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}n\right).
 \label{fus:reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The residual is independent of the mean: their joint Gaussian cross
covariance is zero. Exponentiation gives a normalized joint reconstruction
kernel. Its one-child marginal is \eqref{fus:message-kernel}. Independent
residuals for all children would be incorrect because the off-diagonal
conditional covariances are $-1/n$. Generative joint samples similarly use
the covariance of $(\log m,\log k)$ under $s_1G_1$; their
residual kernel is determined by that covariance, not by the recognition
product law. The local KL residual $(n-1)C$ records the corresponding
state-independent information loss under the reference comparison.

Parameter retention has a narrower meaning. On an exactly synchronized
stratum, child means are redundant copies; retaining their common value
does not identify different nonsynchronized configurations. Away from that
stratum, a block average need not determine its external sign forces.
Equation~\eqref{fus:flow-square} makes no claim about such hidden parameter
fibers.

The admitted boundary protocol draws fresh messages through $B_n$, fresh
predictive messages through \eqref{fus:predictive-square}, or complete
fresh represented samples through the joint reconstruction. To type an
accessible stored readout, augment the complete population configuration by
$o\in\mathsf O=(0,\infty)$ and set $\rho(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol n,o)=o$.
At declared epochs, the transition $\Gamma$ advances the deterministic
parameter flow and overwrites $o$ using the selected normalized scalar
output kernel. Between epochs, $o$ is fixed, and it never enters the mean
equation. Retention copies this accessible $o$ exactly; it does not claim
access to cached private residual samples. For vector readouts the same
definition uses the corresponding finite product space and joint kernel.
Equal parameter trajectories and conditional output kernels imply equal
joint accessible readout laws for every finite sequence of such queries,
by multiplying successive conditional kernels. Reusing persistent latent
samples or introducing record feedback requires another state specification.

\openproblemheading{Formation under smooth peer KL dynamics}{fus:smooth-open}
The finite mergers above depend on a constitutive nonsmooth interaction.
For a locally Lipschitz vector field with an invariant synchronization
manifold, an interior trajectory cannot first reach that manifold in finite
time: local backward uniqueness would identify its preceding segment with
the manifold trajectory. Establishing recursive formation for the smooth
KL dynamics therefore requires a different formation criterion or an
additional mechanism, together with its own inherited closure calculation.
