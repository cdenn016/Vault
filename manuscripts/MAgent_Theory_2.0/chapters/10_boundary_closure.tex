\chapter{Boundary Closure and Recursive Composition}
\label{ch:boundary-closure}

This chapter concerns the reciprocal law potential of \cref{def:dyn-potential} and its Fisher flow, with identity peer channels, all-source uniform priors, and a coupled evaluator. It supplies a binary hierarchy together with an invariant family of agent laws, and proves that the family carries an exact hierarchy of public compressions. Each level preserves fresh uniform-descendant responses, their subsequent evolution, and a declared class of record assimilations. The chapter makes no selection claim. Membership and the hierarchy are supplied, so it is an inheritance construction without selection: measured against \cref{inh:recursive-formation}, (F1) holds in the family grade for the declared invariant family, (F3) and (F4) hold at every join of the supplied hierarchy, and (F2), (F5), and (F6) are not addressed. The source sets exclude self, so the two nulls of \cref{inh:null-flow} coincide. That null deletes every peer cost, is equivariant under the same symmetry group, preserves the same family, descriptors, and descended interface, and has the profile equation $\dot f=-f(\log f+V^q-c_q)$, along which every bit contrast decays at rate one. The exact hierarchy is therefore a property of the prepared family and its symmetry, not of the interaction, which contributes the class costs $D_k$, the incoming term, and a faster decay of the discarded contrast. Within this construction the gauge structure supplies the orbits that a parent forgets; it does no dynamical work. The mechanism is an equivariant symmetry reduction (\cref{sec:boundary-symmetry}). The public partition data that the hierarchy discards are invisible to the fine energy, the Fisher metric, and the admitted records, and near the partition-independent laws their contrast decays exponentially under the same flow (\cref{prop:boundary-contrast-decay}).

\section{A general-law family on a supplied tree}
\label{sec:boundary-tree-family}

Let $A$ and $B$ be probability references on standard Borel spaces with coordinates $r$ and $w$. Choose a positive joint density $\rho(r,w)$ against $A\otimes B$, normalized to one, with bounded logarithm. Its disintegration supplies a proper model reference and normalized evaluator as in \cref{eq:dyn-local-joint}. It may couple its arguments; for example, balanced signs $\phi(r),\psi(w)$ give $\rho=\exp(J\phi\psi)/\cosh J$.

Fix a binary tree of depth $d\ge1$ with $N=2^d$ leaves. Each leaf is a primitive agent. For every internal tree node $v$, append to the belief sample space an independent public coordinate $\theta_v$ with law $\lambda_v$, and append a separate set of such coordinates to the model sample space. The references extend by these product laws. Each node carries a measurable sign $\sigma_v(r,\theta_v)\in\{-1,1\}$. The profile equations of \cref{sec:boundary-profile-equations} and the descent computations of \cref{sec:boundary-tree-inheritance} use only one property of these signs: conditional on the base, they are independent and fair. The symmetry interpretation of \cref{sec:boundary-symmetry} uses more, namely node laws that admit reference-preserving sign reversals and maps acting transitively within each sign cell; the two realizations below have this property, while a node law with atoms of unequal mass inside a sign cell does not. Two realizations are used. In the coin realization, $\lambda_v$ is uniform on $\{-1,1\}$ and $\sigma_v(r,\theta_v)=\theta_vs_v(r)$ with a measurable orientation $s_v(r)\in\{-1,1\}$. In the circle realization, $\lambda_v$ is Haar measure on a circle, and a measurable phase function $\alpha_v(r)$ defines
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_v(r,\theta_v)=\operatorname{sign}\cos(\theta_v-\alpha_v(r)).
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-signs}
\end{equation}
The sign on its null zero set is immaterial. The atomless circle realization is needed only where a continuum of balanced partitions is used, namely for the continuous phase families and the total-variation and Hellinger statements of \cref{sec:boundary-tree-loss}. In both realizations the coordinates $\theta_v$ are original public sample coordinates. Different phase functions or orientations change the laws in those same coordinates; they are held fixed along a trajectory.

Label the left and right edges by $\epsilon=+1,-1$. A leaf $i$ defines a signed path
\begin{equation}
 u_j(i,\theta)=\epsilon_j(i)\sigma_{v_{j-1}(i)}(r,\theta_{v_{j-1}(i)}),
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,d.
 \label{eq:boundary-path-bits}
\end{equation}
Let $U_d$ be the uniform law on $\{-1,1\}^d$. Choose any positive bounded profile $f(r,u)$, with bounded logarithm and $\int f dA dU_d=1$, and set
\begin{equation}
 q_i(r,\theta)=f(r,u(i,\theta)),\qquad
 f_0(r)=\E_{U_d}f(r,u).
 \label{eq:boundary-profile-lift}
\end{equation}
These are densities against $A$ times all the node laws. Each leaf has the same base marginal $f_0A$, but its full public law can differ from every other leaf law. Define model densities from an independent profile $g(w,z)$ and model coordinates in the same way. The base spaces and the profile functions are unrestricted within the stated regularity domain; no Gaussian or finite-parameter family is imposed.

All physical peer channels are identities, every other agent is an allowed source, and the original source priors are uniform. If two leaves share exactly $k$ initial edges, there are $n_k=2^{d-k-1}$ possible sources in that class for each receiver. Give each such source weight $b_k/n_k$, where $b$ is a probability vector on $k=0,\ldots,d-1$. The belief and model vectors $b^q,b^s$ evolve separately. Uniform original rows are included by taking
\begin{equation}
 \pi_k=\frac{n_k}{N-1},\qquad b_k^q=b_k^s=\pi_k.
 \label{eq:boundary-class-priors}
\end{equation}
The fine system is therefore the reciprocal potential \cref{eq:dyn-potential} with identity channels, the evaluator determined by $\rho$, and uniform all-source priors. The tree enters only the prepared relation among the agent laws and row classes. The fine potential and its uniform priors contain no additional preference for a chosen pair, and they are invariant under every permutation of the agents.

\section{The exact inherited profile equations}
\label{sec:boundary-profile-equations}

For each $k$, define an operator $P_k$ on bit profiles: keep the first $k$ bits, reverse bit $k+1$, and average all later bits. It is a Markov operator, self-adjoint for $U_d$. It describes an average over a class of original source labels; it is not a replacement physical communication channel. Define
\begin{equation}
 D_k(f)=\int f(r,u)[\log f(r,u)-(P_k\log f)(r,u)] dA(r) dU_d(u).
 \label{eq:boundary-class-cost}
\end{equation}

The Fisher flow of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow} is proved on finite tables. The fine system here lives on general sample spaces. Its well-posedness is the identity-channel case of \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow}, whose sufficient conditions (identity channels between agents sharing a reference, evaluators with bounded log densities) it meets; the lemma below restates that case in unnormalized log-density coordinates and records the explicit first variation used later.

\lemmaheading{Fine identity-channel flow in bounded log-density charts}{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed}
Let $(\mathsf X,\lambda)$ and $(\mathsf Y,\mu)$ be standard Borel probability spaces whose coordinates include the base coordinates $r$ and $w$, with base marginals $A$ and $B$. Consider finitely many agents with normalized belief densities $q_i=e^{\ell_i}$ against $\lambda$ and model densities $s_i=e^{m_i}$ against $\mu$, where $\ell_i\in L^\infty(\lambda)$ and $m_i\in L^\infty(\mu)$. Suppose the peer channels are identities on a fixed directed edge set with nonempty source sets and positive priors, $T_q,T_s>0$, and each local term is $\KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i)$, where $R_i$ has a density $\rho_i(r,w)$ against $\lambda\otimes\mu$ that depends only on the base coordinates and has bounded logarithm. Up to additive constants, the first variation of \cref{eq:dyn-potential} with respect to $q_i$ is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h_i^q&=\log q_i+V_i^q
 +\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i^q}\beta_{ij}(\log q_i-\log q_j)
 -\sum_{l:i\in\mathcal N_l^q}\beta_{li}\frac{q_l}{q_i},\\
 V_i^q(r)&=-\int s_{i,0}(w)\log\rho_i(r,w) dB(w),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:boundary-fine-force}
\end{equation}
where $s_{i,0}$ is the base marginal density of $s_i$; the model variation $h_i^s$ follows by exchanging the sectors. Then the system
\begin{equation}
 \dot\ell_i=-(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q),\qquad
 \dot m_i=-(h_i^s-\E_{s_i}h_i^s),
 \label{eq:boundary-fine-flow}
\end{equation}
together with the row equations of \cref{eq:dyn-fisher-flow}, has for every initial condition with interior rows a unique maximal solution in $L^\infty(\lambda)^N\times L^\infty(\mu)^N$ times the row coordinates, with interior rows; if its existence time is finite, the solution leaves every bounded set of the chart with rows bounded away from the boundary. Along it the laws stay normalized, $\dot q_i=-q_i(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q)$, the right side is the Fisher gradient of $\mathcal E$ for the metric $\int(\delta q)^2/q$ in each block, and $\dot{\mathcal E}=-\sum\operatorname{Var}\le0$ as in \cref{eq:dyn-dissipation}.

\begin{proof}
For identity channels, \cref{eq:dyn-kl-differential} gives the receiver variation $\log q_i-\log q_j+1$ and the source variation $-q_i/q_j$ of $\KL(q_i\Vert q_j)$. The local term has variation $\log q_i+1+V_i^q$, because $\rho_i$ reads only the base coordinates. This gives \cref{eq:boundary-fine-force}. Define the field off the normalized set through $q_i=e^{\ell_i}/\int e^{\ell_i}d\lambda$. On a ball of radius $L$ in the sup norm, every density lies between $e^{-2L}$ and $e^{2L}$. The maps $\ell\mapsto\log q_i$, $\ell\mapsto q_l/q_i$, the base marginals $s_{i,0}$ as maps into $L^1(B)$, the linear map $s_{i,0}\mapsto V_i^q$ from $L^1(B)$ into $L^\infty(A)$ with norm at most $\Vert\log\rho_i\Vert_\infty$, the costs $D_{ij}$, and the expectations $\E_{q_i}h_i^q$ are therefore locally Lipschitz, and the row field is smooth on the interior of the simplices. Finite sums and products of bounded locally Lipschitz maps are locally Lipschitz, so the Picard--Lindel\"of theorem in the Banach space gives a unique maximal solution and the continuation alternative, as in \cref{prop:dyn-chart-flow}: a field that is Lipschitz on bounded sets is bounded there, so a solution confined to such a set on a finite interval is Cauchy at its end and extends. Since $\E_{q_i}\dot\ell_i=0$, the integral of $e^{\ell_i}$ is constant, so the normalized set is invariant and $\dot q_i=q_i\dot\ell_i$. For a tangent vector $v$ with $\int v d\lambda=0$, the metric pairing of $q_i(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q)$ with $v$ is $\int h_i^qv d\lambda$, which identifies the Fisher gradient exactly as in the proof of \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow}. The chain rule in the chart gives the dissipation identity.
\end{proof}

\theoremheading{Exact coupled hierarchy dynamics}{thm:boundary-tree-flow}
Consider the reciprocal potential \cref{eq:dyn-potential} with the data of \cref{sec:boundary-tree-family}, in either realization of the signs, and its Fisher flow in the chart of \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed}, including source-row evolution. The family \cref{eq:boundary-profile-lift}, with its model analogue and class-constant rows, is invariant under this flow. Its inherited profile energy and metric are the original restrictions divided by their common factor $N$. The complete profile equations retain the incoming source derivatives, and their solutions exist for all $t\ge0$.

\begin{proof}
For any two leaves in class $k$, the first $k$ path bits agree, bit $k+1$ is opposite, and later bits lie on disjoint branches. Integrating their independent signs gives exactly $\KL(q_i\Vert q_j)=D_k(f)$. Every leaf path has uniform bit law. Each local product law therefore has the same base marginals $f_0,g_0$ in its evaluator term. Direct substitution gives $\mathcal E=NF$, with
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 F={}&\int f\log f dA dU_d+\int g\log g dB dU_d
       -\int f_0(r)g_0(w)\log\rho(r,w) dA dB\\
 &+\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}b_k^qD_k(f)+T_q\KL(b^q\Vert\pi)
  +\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}b_k^sD_k(g)+T_s\KL(b^s\Vert\pi).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-energy}
\end{equation}
The metric restricts to $N$ times
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal G^c=\int\frac{(\delta f)^2}{f} dA dU_d
       +\int\frac{(\delta g)^2}{g} dB dU_d
       +\sum_{a=q,s}\sum_k\frac{(\delta b_k^a)^2}{b_k^a}.
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-metric}
\end{equation}
Thus no new mobility is introduced.

Self-adjointness gives the exact derivative
\begin{equation}
 \delta D_k(f)=\int\delta f
 \left[\log f+1-P_k\log f-\frac{P_k f}{f}\right]dA dU_d.
 \label{eq:boundary-class-variation}
\end{equation}
The last ratio is the incoming source contribution. Define
\begin{equation}
 V^q(r)=-\int g_0(w)\log\rho(r,w) dB(w),\qquad
 V^s(w)=-\int f_0(r)\log\rho(r,w) dA(r).
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-local}
\end{equation}
Up to an irrelevant additive constant, the belief force and evolution are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h^q&=2\log f+V^q-\sum_kb_k^q\left[P_k\log f+\frac{P_kf}{f}\right],\\
 c_q&=\int fh^q dA dU_d,\qquad
 \dot f=-f(h^q-c_q).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-flow}
\end{equation}
Exchange the sectors for $\dot g$. With $D_k^q=D_k(f)$ and $D_k^s=D_k(g)$, the rows obey
\begin{equation}
 \dot b_k^a=-b_k^a\left[
 D_k^a+T_a\log\frac{b_k^a}{\pi_k}
 -\sum_l b_l^a\left(D_l^a+T_a\log\frac{b_l^a}{\pi_l}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-rows}
\end{equation}

To establish full-flow invariance, fix a receiver and a public sample point. The leaves in its class-$k$ source subtree run through every suffix bit string once, regardless of the physical signs at that point. Their averages of $\log q_j$ and $q_j$ are therefore $P_k\log f$ and $P_kf$. Incoming weights have the same class values. The fine force \cref{eq:boundary-fine-force} at that leaf is precisely \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow} evaluated at its path bits. All leaf normalizations agree because every path-bit vector is uniform under the reference. Source costs also agree within their classes, preserving the row form. Hence the full field is tangent to the profile family, and the restricted gradient is its actual evolution. The profile equations are locally Lipschitz in the bounded-profile chart by the argument of \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed}, so they have a unique maximal solution. Its lift solves the fine equation by the preceding computation, and by the uniqueness in \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed} it is the fine solution. Global existence is \cref{lem:boundary-global}.
\end{proof}

In the coin realization with a finite base, the fine laws are positive tables on the base times $\{-1,1\}^{N-1}$, and \cref{thm:dyn-finite-flow} applies to the fine system directly; the theorem is then a statement about finite tables that can be checked by exact computation.

\lemmaheading{Global bounded-log existence of the profile flow}{lem:boundary-global}
Let $F(0)$ be the initial profile energy \cref{eq:boundary-tree-energy} and $C_K=F(0)+3\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty+2$. Along the solution of \cref{thm:boundary-tree-flow},
\begin{equation}
 \min\left(\operatorname*{ess\ inf}\log f(0),-C_K\right)
 \le\log f(t)\le
 \max\left(\operatorname*{ess\ sup}\log f(0),C_K\right),
 \label{eq:boundary-global-bound}
\end{equation}
with the same bound for $\log g$, and $\log(b_k^a(t)/\pi_k)\ge\min(\log(b_k^a(0)/\pi_k),-C_a/T_a)$, where $C_a$ is twice the bound \cref{eq:boundary-global-bound} on the absolute value of the corresponding log profile. The solution therefore exists for all $t\ge0$.

\begin{proof}
Since $\sum_kb_k^q=1$ and $P_k$ preserves $U_d$, the normalizer in \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow} is $c_q=\int f\log f dA dU_d+\sum_kb_k^qD_k(f)+\int f_0V^q dA-1$. Every term of $F$ except the evaluator term is nonnegative, the evaluator term is at least $-\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty$, and $F$ decreases along the flow. Hence the first two terms of $c_q$ lie between $0$ and $F(0)+\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty$, and $|V^q|\le\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty$ gives $|c_q|\le F(0)+2\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty+1$. Given the continuous scalar inputs $c_q(t)$, $b^q(t)$ and the base function $V^q(t,\cdot)$, the profile equation is, for each base point, a finite system of ordinary differential equations for $l=\log f(r,\cdot)\in\R^{2^d}$:
\begin{equation*}
 \dot l=-2l-V^q+\sum_kb_k^q\left[P_kl+\frac{P_ke^l}{e^l}\right]+c_q.
\end{equation*}
At a bit string where $l$ attains its maximum $M_r$, one has $P_kl\le M_r$ and $P_ke^l\le e^{M_r}$, so the upper Dini derivative of $M_r$ is at most $-M_r+C_K$. At a minimizing bit string, $P_kl\ge m_r$ and $P_ke^l\ge e^{m_r}$, so the lower Dini derivative of the minimum $m_r$ is at least $-m_r-C_K$. Comparison gives \cref{eq:boundary-global-bound} uniformly in $r$. For the rows, $y_k=\log(b_k/\pi_k)$ satisfies $\dot y_k=-D_k-T y_k+\sum_lb_l(D_l+Ty_l)$, and the last sum is nonnegative because $D_l\ge0$ and $\sum_lb_ly_l=\KL(b\Vert\pi)\ge0$. Since $D_k\le\sup l-\inf l\le C$, we obtain $\dot y_k\ge-C-Ty_k$. The model sector is identical. The solution thus stays in a bounded set of the chart with rows uniformly interior, and the continuation alternative of \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed} extends it to $[0,\infty)$. This is the profile form of the maximum-principle argument of \cref{prop:dyn-chart-global}, which gives global existence of the fine flow under the same hypotheses.
\end{proof}

The same retentions apply to the original Gibbs-reduced flow. Replace each row by $b_k^{a,*}=\pi_k\exp(-D_k^a/T_a)/\sum_l\pi_l\exp(-D_l^a/T_a)$ and use the matter metric. The envelope identity in \cref{eq:dyn-envelope} gives the displayed profile force with these weights, including its incoming term. All symmetry and response arguments below remain unchanged. The Gibbs-reduced flow and the flow with evolving rows are different flows. At frozen costs the row log-odds in \cref{eq:boundary-tree-rows} relax at rate $T_a$, so their transients differ most when $T_a$ is small; the retention statements hold for each of them.

The inherited cost is still a relative entropy. Let
\begin{equation}
 J_k(dr,du,dv)=A(dr)f(r,u)U_d(du)P_k(u,dv),
\end{equation}
and let $J_k^{\mathrm{rev}}$ exchange $u,v$. Reversibility gives
$D_k(f)=\KL(J_k\Vert J_k^{\mathrm{rev}})$. Since $J_k$ and $J_k^{\mathrm{rev}}$ are linear in $f$, joint convexity of relative entropy makes each $D_k$ convex in $f$. Equivalently, $D_k(f)=\tfrac12\iiint(f(r,u)-f(r,v))(\log f(r,u)-\log f(r,v))A(dr)U_d(du)P_k(u,dv)$ is the Dirichlet form of the reversible kernel $P_k$ evaluated at $(f,\log f)$, the entropy-production functional of modified logarithmic Sobolev inequalities \citep{BobkovTetali2006}. The class cost differs from the divergence to the averaged source law by a Jensen term:
\begin{equation}
 D_k(f)-\KL(f A\otimes U_d\Vert(P_kf) A\otimes U_d)
 =\int f[\log(P_kf)-P_k\log f]dA dU_d\ge0.
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-jensen}
\end{equation}
Because $f$ is positive and $P_k(u,\cdot)$ is uniform on its support, the right side vanishes exactly when $f(r,\cdot)$ does not depend on bits $k+2,\ldots,d$ for $A$-almost every $r$. It vanishes identically for $k=d-1$, where $P_{d-1}$ is the deterministic reversal of the last bit, and is positive for $k<d-1$ whenever $f$ depends on a later bit. Replacing the exact inherited cost by the averaged-source divergence would discard this term and change the dynamics.

\section{The hierarchy as an equivariant symmetry reduction}
\label{sec:boundary-symmetry}

The inheritance statements below are instances of classical facts about equivariant gradient flows. Let $\mathcal S$ be the group of pairs $\omega=(\Phi,\varpi)$ in which $\Phi=(\Phi^q,\Phi^s)$ consists of fibered bimeasurable maps of the belief and model sample spaces, $\Phi^q(r,\theta)=(r,\varphi_r(\theta))$ and similarly for $\Phi^s$, that preserve the references, and $\varpi$ is a permutation of the agents that preserves the edge set, the priors, and the evaluators. The group acts on configurations by $(\omega\cdot q)_i=q_{\varpi(i)}\circ\Phi^q$, $(\omega\cdot s)_i=s_{\varpi(i)}\circ\Phi^s$, and $(\omega\cdot\beta)_{ij}=\beta_{\varpi(i)\varpi(j)}$, with the same rule for model rows. The product is $\omega_1\omega_2=(\Phi_2\circ\Phi_1,\varpi_2\circ\varpi_1)$, componentwise in each sector; with this law the formulas define a left action, $\omega_1\cdot(\omega_2\cdot x)=(\omega_1\omega_2)\cdot x$. The finite-dimensional statement \cref{inh:equivariant-descent} does not apply here: $\mathcal S$ is not a Lie group, it acts on a Banach chart, and the sets used below are fixed-point sets of actions that are not free, so no quotient manifold or quotient metric is asserted.

\lemmaheading{Equivariant reduction}{lem:boundary-equivariance}
In the setting of \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed}, every $\omega\in\mathcal S$ acts on the chart by a linear isometry of the sup norm, preserves $\mathcal E$ and the Fisher metric, and commutes with the flow: if $x(t)$ solves the flow from $x_0$, then $\omega\cdot x(t)$ solves it from $\omega\cdot x_0$. Consequently the following hold.
(i) For every subgroup $H\le\mathcal S$, the fixed-point set $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ is invariant under the flow, and on it the flow is the Fisher gradient flow of the restriction of $\mathcal E$ for the restricted metric.
(ii) Every $\omega\in\mathcal S$ maps $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ onto $\operatorname{Fix}(\omega H\omega^{-1})$ and conjugates the restricted flows. A subgroup $K\le\mathcal S$ that maps $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ into itself, for example a subgroup of the normalizer of $H$, acts on $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ commuting with the flow, and the flow descends to the orbit space $\operatorname{Fix}(H)/K$. More generally, if $X$ is a flow-invariant union of fixed-point sets, the flow descends to $X$ modulo the equivalence relation induced on $X$ by $\mathcal S$.
(iii) A response kernel $Q$ from configurations to laws on an output space with $Q(\omega\cdot x)=Q(x)$ for $\omega\in K$ descends to $\operatorname{Fix}(H)/K$. If an assimilation map $A_o$ of \cref{def:dyn-record-interrupted} satisfies $A_o(\omega\cdot x)=\omega\cdot A_o(x)$ for $\omega\in K$, and the law of its record is $K$-invariant, then every finite adaptive history law of the record-interrupted dynamics descends as well.

\begin{proof}
A reference-preserving bimeasurable $\Phi^q$ preserves relative entropy, so $\KL(q_{\varpi(i)}\circ\Phi^q\Vert q_{\varpi(j)}\circ\Phi^q)=\KL(q_{\varpi(i)}\Vert q_{\varpi(j)})$, and $\varpi$ preserves the edge set and priors, so the reindexed peer sums agree. The entropy part of each local term is preserved, and because $\Phi^q$ is fibered and reference-preserving, $q\circ\Phi^q$ has the same base marginal as $q$. The evaluator term reads only base marginals, and $\varpi$ preserves the evaluators, so $\mathcal E(\omega\cdot x)=\mathcal E(x)$. The metric is preserved since $\int(\delta q\circ\Phi^q)^2/(q\circ\Phi^q)=\int(\delta q)^2/q$. On log densities the action is $\ell_i\mapsto\ell_{\varpi(i)}\circ\Phi^q$, a linear isometry of $L^\infty$. Direct substitution in \cref{eq:boundary-fine-force}, including the incoming term, gives $h_i^q(\omega\cdot x)=h_{\varpi(i)}^q(x)\circ\Phi^q$, so the field is equivariant, and uniqueness in \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed} gives the commutation. For (i), if $x_0\in\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ and $\eta\in H$, then $\eta\cdot x(t)$ is a solution from $x_0$ and equals $x(t)$. At a point of $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ the field is $H$-fixed, hence tangent to $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$, and its metric pairing with every tangent vector $v$ of $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ equals the derivative of the restricted energy along $v$; it is therefore the restricted gradient. Statement (ii) follows from $\omega\cdot\operatorname{Fix}(H)=\operatorname{Fix}(\omega H\omega^{-1})$ and the commutation: if $y_0=\omega\cdot x_0$ with $\omega\in K$, then $y(t)=\omega\cdot x(t)$, so orbits are carried to orbits; the same commutation applies to any two points of $X$ related by an element of $\mathcal S$. For (iii), $Q(y(t))=Q(x(t))$, and an equivariant assimilation keeps related states related while assigning the same law to the record. Induction over the record times, with the flow between them, gives the history statement.
\end{proof}

Statement (i) is the flow form of the principle of symmetric criticality \citep{Palais1979}, and the invariance of fixed-point subspaces of equivariant vector fields is standard in equivariant bifurcation theory and dynamics \citep{GolubitskyStewartSchaeffer1988,Field2007}. The lemma adds nothing beyond these classical facts; it fixes the form in which they are used here. The following proposition identifies the profile family as a fixed-point set.

\propositionheading{The profile family is a fixed-point set}{prop:boundary-fixed-family}
Fix a realization of the signs. Let $H_\alpha\le\mathcal S$ consist of the pairs $(\Phi,\varpi)$ such that $\varpi$ is an automorphism of the rooted binary tree acting on the leaves, and $u(\varpi(i),\Phi^q(\xi))=u(i,\xi)$ for every leaf $i$ and almost every belief sample point $\xi$, with the analogous condition on $\Phi^s$. Then $\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$ consists exactly of the configurations of the form \cref{eq:boundary-profile-lift} in both sectors, with class-constant rows $\beta_{ij}=b_k^q/n_k$ and $\gamma_{ij}=b_k^s/n_k$ for pairs in class $k$.

\begin{proof}
If $q_i=f(r,u(i,\xi))$ and $(\Phi,\varpi)\in H_\alpha$, then $q_{\varpi(i)}(\Phi^q\xi)=f(r,u(\varpi(i),\Phi^q\xi))=f(r,u(i,\xi))=q_i(\xi)$, and class-constant rows are preserved because tree automorphisms preserve the common-prefix length. Conversely, let a belief configuration be fixed by $H_\alpha$. Elements $(\Phi,\id)$ whose $\Phi^q$ preserves every sign belong to $H_\alpha$. In the coin realization the signs determine the node coordinates given $r$. In the circle realization, choose $\Phi^q$ to act on each cell $\{\sigma=\mathrm s\}$, in arc coordinates measured from $\alpha_v(r)$, by rotations of the arcs through fractions $c_v$ of their length such that $1$ and the $c_v$ are linearly independent over the rationals. This map is ergodic on each cell for the normalized Haar product by the Kronecker--Weyl criterion \citep[Chapter 1]{Walters1982}; rational independence of the $c_v$ alone does not suffice, since $c_1=\sqrt2-1$ and $c_2=2-\sqrt2$ leave the character $e^{2\pi i(x_1+x_2)}$ invariant, so by Fubini's theorem each invariant $q_i$ is almost everywhere a function $F_i(r,\sigma)$ of the base and the node signs.

For an internal node $w$, let $\varpi_w$ exchange the two subtrees below $w$ by their mirror map, and let $\Phi_w^q$ reverse $\sigma_w$ (a half-period shift of $\theta_w$, or a coin flip) and exchange the coordinates of mirror nodes in the two subtrees, adjusted by their phases or orientations so that mirror signs are carried to mirror signs. Following the edge labels along each path shows that $(\Phi_w,\varpi_w)\in H_\alpha$. For a leaf $i$ not below $w$, $\varpi_w(i)=i$, so $F_i$ is invariant under reversing $\sigma_w$ and permuting the signs strictly below $w$. Induction from the lowest internal nodes upward shows that $F_i$ does not depend on the sign of any node that is not an ancestor of $i$. The ancestral signs of $i$ determine $u(i,\xi)$ bijectively, so $q_i=f_i(r,u(i,\xi))$. For $(\Phi,\varpi)\in H_\alpha$, fixedness gives $f_{\varpi(i)}(r,u(i,\xi))=f_i(r,u(i,\xi))$, and $u(i,\cdot)$ is uniform given $r$, so $f_{\varpi(i)}=f_i$. The node elements generate a group that acts transitively on the leaves and on the pairs of leaves with a given common-prefix length. Hence $f_i=f$ for all $i$, and the rows are class constant. The model sector is identical.
\end{proof}

Two further subgroups give the quotient. For a measurable map $r\mapsto\tau(r)$ into the automorphisms of bit strings, let $\Phi_\tau$ shift $\theta_v$ by a half period exactly when $\tau(r)$ reverses the bit that follows the original signed prefix of $v$. This map is fibered, bimeasurable, and reference-preserving, since each shift depends only on $r$ and on ancestor coordinates, and $u(i,\Phi_\tau\xi)=\tau(r)u(i,\xi)$ for every leaf. Hence $(\Phi_\tau,\id)$ maps the family with profile $f$ to the family with profile $f(r,\tau(r)u)$. Phase rotations $\theta_v\mapsto\theta_v+\alpha_v(r)-\alpha'_v(r)$ map $\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$ onto $\operatorname{Fix}(H_{\alpha'})$ with the profile unchanged, and orientation changes act in the same way in the coin realization. These maps do not form one subgroup acting on a single $\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$, since phase rotations change $\alpha$ and $\Phi_\tau$ depends on it. The union $X=\bigcup_\alpha\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$ is flow invariant, and by the general form of \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance} (ii) the flow descends to $X$ modulo the relation $(\alpha,f)\sim(\alpha',f(r,\tau(r)u))$, which is the unordered profile tree of \cref{sec:boundary-tree-inheritance}.

The data discarded by this quotient, namely the phases or orientations and the base-dependent ordering of branches, are orbit data of a group of exact symmetries of the fine energy and the Fisher metric. Relative to the observable algebra generated by the fine energy and metric, the fresh uniform-descendant responses, and the admitted base-only broadcast records, they are gauge: no agent, channel, record, or energy term of this construction reads them. They are resolved only by the protocols excluded in \cref{sec:boundary-tree-inheritance}, namely reused children, shared labels, and child-specific or unrestricted identity-channel probes. Symmetry does not supply the explicit class costs $D_k$ or their pair-law form, the incoming term of \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow}, the identification \cref{eq:boundary-tree-response} of a fresh response with a subtree mean, the contrast decay of \cref{prop:boundary-contrast-decay}, or the observability obstruction of \cref{sec:boundary-observability}; these are computed directly.

The root-level statement holds for every identity-channel configuration, not only on the profile family.

\propositionheading{Descent of the joint conditional spectrum}{prop:boundary-joint-spectrum}
In the setting of \cref{lem:boundary-fine-wellposed}, let $\mu^q$ be the law of $(r,q_1(\xi),\ldots,q_N(\xi))$ for $\xi\sim\lambda$, and $\mu^s$ the law of $(w,s_1,\ldots,s_N)$ under $\mu$. For any edge set, priors, and initial rows, two configurations with equal $\mu^q$, $\mu^s$, and rows have solutions with equal $\mu^q(t)$, $\mu^s(t)$, and rows throughout their common existence interval. Response kernels that read only base coordinates of a fresh sample from a fixed agent, and assimilations by a base-only likelihood applied to every agent, descend to these spectra.

\begin{proof}
By \cref{eq:boundary-fine-force}, the force $h_i^q$ at $\xi$ is a function of $r$, of the value vector $(q_1(\xi),\ldots,q_N(\xi))$, and of quantities determined by the spectra and rows: $V_i^q$ reads the base marginals $s_{i,0}(w)=\E[s_i\mid w]$, and the expectations $\E_{q_i}h_i^q$ and the costs $D_{ij}=\int q_i\log(q_i/q_j)d\lambda$ are integrals against $\mu^q$. Fix the solution $x(t)$ from the first configuration, and let $\Psi_t(r,\cdot)$ be the flow of the finite-dimensional equations $\dot v_i=-v_i(h_i^q(r,v;t)-c_i(t))$ with the scalar inputs of $x(t)$, and similarly in the model sector. Two routine identifications make this precise. A continuously differentiable curve in $L^\infty$ has a jointly measurable version that is absolutely continuous in $t$ for almost every $\xi$ and satisfies the pointwise equation, so $x(t)$ is the pointwise flow almost everywhere; conversely, since $\Psi_t$ is locally Lipschitz in $v$ uniformly on the common essential range of the values, which is bounded and bounded away from zero, $\xi\mapsto\Psi_t(r,v(\xi))$ is a continuously differentiable curve in $L^\infty$. Applying $\Psi_t$ pointwise to the second configuration gives a path whose spectra are the push-forwards of the common initial spectra, hence equal to those of $x(t)$. Its scalar inputs therefore coincide with those of $x(t)$, so it solves the fine flow, and by uniqueness it is the solution from the second configuration. The rows read only the costs, which agree. A base-only response reads only $(r,\mu^q)$. A base-only assimilation sends the value vector at $r$ to $(L(o\mid r)v_i/Z_i)_i$ with $Z_i=\int L(o\mid r)q_{i,0}(r)dA(r)$; these factors depend on the agent unless the base marginals agree, but they are determined by $\mu^q$, so the update acts on the spectra.
\end{proof}

On the profile family, the conditional law of the value vector given $r$ is uniform on the orbit of the labeled vector $(f(r,u(i)))_i$ under the tree automorphisms, so $\mu^q$ and the unordered profile tree determine each other. The root case of \cref{thm:boundary-tree-inheritance} is therefore an instance of \cref{prop:boundary-joint-spectrum}. What the hierarchy adds is that responses at intermediate nodes, which read physical subtree membership, also descend; there the relevant descriptors are the conditional spectra of subtrees given ancestor coordinates. This is the identity-channel analogue of the conditional spectrum of \cref{ch:smooth-closure}.

\remarkheading{Compression requires the symmetry}{rem:boundary-heterogeneous}
Suppose the leaves of the two root branches carry different profiles $f^L,f^R$, a configuration outside $\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$. With $f_1^X(r,u_1)=\E f^X(r,u_1,\cdot)$, the fresh root response density is
\begin{equation}
 \tfrac12\left[f_1^L(r,\sigma_0)+f_1^R(r,-\sigma_0)\right],
 \label{eq:boundary-hetero-root}
\end{equation}
which depends on the root sign, and hence on the root phase, unless $f_1^L(r,s)+f_1^R(r,-s)$ does not depend on $s$. Discarding the join phase under the fresh interface therefore needs this conjugacy condition, which holds automatically on the symmetric family. The two-block family is invariant: it is $\operatorname{Fix}(H'_\alpha)$ for the subgroup $H'_\alpha\le H_\alpha$ of elements whose permutation preserves the two root branches, by the argument of \cref{prop:boundary-fixed-family} without the root node element, so \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance} (i) applies. When the flow preserves the conjugacy condition, so that phase compression at a heterogeneous join holds along the whole trajectory and not only at one time, is not established here.

\remarkheading{Other symmetric hierarchies}{rem:boundary-wreath}
The proofs use, at each node, a group of permutations of the children that acts 2-transitively on them, for example the full symmetric group, which gives an iterated wreath product of symmetric groups on the leaves, together with public coordinates that realize at each node a uniformly random element of that group, fair given the base and ancestor coordinates. Transitivity alone does not suffice: with cyclic arrangements of three children the two sibling costs of a receiver differ in general, so class-constant rows are not preserved. Rooted trees with larger branching, realized by uniform random child permutations, class-constant priors with an edge set that is a union of common-prefix classes, and phases that depend measurably on ancestor coordinates fall under \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance} in the same way. The operator $P_k$ is then replaced by the average that keeps the first $k$ digits, replaces digit $k+1$ by a uniformly chosen different digit, and averages the rest. We do not write out these cases.

\section{Recursive retention and the response process}
\label{sec:boundary-tree-inheritance}

At each base point, arrange the values of $f(r,u)$ in their binary profile tree. Retain this tree with unordered children at every internal node. Its leaves still carry density values, and its nested grouping is retained. Flattening it into a multiset would lose which values share a prefix and could change $D_k$.

For a physical subtree rooted at depth $l$, apply this retention only to its descendant values, keeping the dependence on the base and physical ancestor coordinates. This defines its parent descriptor. Include the corresponding model descriptor, the shared row-class data, primitive counts, and the remaining tree connections. At a lowest pair, the unordered values $p_+,p_-$ can equivalently be written as a mean $(p_++p_-)/2$ and a squared contrast $((p_+-p_-)/(p_++p_-))^2$. A first parent therefore retains more than its mixture law, but does not retain the two full child laws on their original node coordinates.

\theoremheading{Recursive boundary inheritance on the supplied hierarchy}{thm:boundary-tree-inheritance}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:boundary-tree-flow}, these parent descriptors inherit the joint controlled dynamics at every level of the same fine system. Joining two sibling descriptors gives their unordered pair. The join commutes with evolution and with fresh uniform-descendant response kernels. Record likelihoods that depend only on the base and are applied to every agent, used as the assimilation events of \cref{def:dyn-record-interrupted}, preserve these statements under arbitrary finite adaptive sequences, whenever the updates remain in the regular domain.

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:boundary-fixed-family}, the family is $\operatorname{Fix}(H_\alpha)$, and the maps $(\Phi_\tau,\id)$ and the phase rotations of \cref{sec:boundary-symmetry} act on it as stated there. At a fixed physical point, changing an internal sign exchanges the two branches below that node. Thus the map from physical leaf labels to their bit strings is an automorphism of the rooted binary tree. Forgetting its realization removes the phase at the joined node and leaves exactly the unordered profile subtree. As a direct check of \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance}(ii), tree automorphisms preserve common-prefix length, $U_d$, and every $P_k$. They consequently preserve \cref{eq:boundary-tree-energy,eq:boundary-tree-metric} and commute with \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow,eq:boundary-tree-rows}, also when they depend measurably on the base, since the equations have no derivatives in the base coordinate, the local evaluator reads only $f_0,g_0$, and the global integrals are unchanged. The quotient flow is therefore independent of the chosen ordered representatives.

The quotient admits Borel representatives. Order $\R^{2^m}$ lexicographically. At each internal node, from the lowest level upward, place first the child subtree whose current vector of values is lexicographically smaller. Each step is a finite composition of Borel comparisons and coordinate permutations, and applying it at each base point to the measurable map $r\mapsto f(r,\cdot)$ gives a measurable canonical profile that depends only on the unordered tree. On a finite base this also gives measurability of the descended updates, since the chart is then finite dimensional and the profile flow is continuous; on a general base the record statements below assume, as \cref{def:dyn-record-interrupted} does, that the flow is jointly Borel in time and state.

For partial retentions, the same argument applies to the product of automorphism groups of the retained subtrees. The complement supplies the retained sibling and ancestor data needed for their interactions; the joint parent network closes. Thus this is controlled closure for complements in the stated prepared family, rather than autonomy under every external law. Combining sibling trees and then forgetting their ordering is the same operation as retaining their union directly. Repeated compatible joins give the same descriptor, and joins of disjoint subtrees commute. These are representation changes inside one fixed $N$-agent interaction graph. They do not add or delete fine edges, and do not authorize an arbitrary reassociation of the leaves into another hierarchy.

For a response at a node of depth $l$, choose a descendant freshly and uniformly, draw its full original public sample, and conceal its label. At every physical sample point the descendant labels run through every suffix bit string. The response density is exactly
\begin{equation}
 f_l(r,u_1,\ldots,u_l)
 =\E_{u_{l+1},\ldots,u_d}f(r,u_1,\ldots,u_d).
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-response}
\end{equation}
It is the mean of the retained subtree values, with all unused node factors left in the original output space, and it is invariant under the automorphisms acting below the node, as \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance}(iii) requires. At the root it is $f_0(r)$. Choosing a sibling parent uniformly and then a descendant uniformly gives exactly the same full sample law. The model sector follows the same rule with a separate fresh label.

Fix a normalized positive record kernel $L_e^q(do\mid r)$ with bounded positive density on the admitted update domain, jointly Borel in $(o,r)$, applied as a readout to the original samples of every agent. Every child has base marginal $f_0A$, so a common record has the same component normalization
\begin{equation}
 Z_o=\int f_0(r)L_e^q(o\mid r) dA(r).
\end{equation}
Conditioning every mixture component preserves uniform labels and gives
\begin{equation}
 f^+(r,u)=\frac{L_e^q(o\mid r)f(r,u)}{Z_o}.
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-feedback}
\end{equation}
This multiplies every value in a profile tree at the given base point by the same factor. It commutes with every element of $\mathcal S$ whose maps are fibered, and in particular with all retentions and joins, and its record law depends only on $f_0$. The model update is identical in form, and rows remain unchanged at the record update. Equality of retained states gives equality of the next response law and the retained update. Induction, using the inherited continuous flow between records, proves equality of every finite history law under policies choosing the next admitted experiment from the observed history; the policies are Borel kernels from finite histories to admitted experiments and waiting times, and the history laws are those of \cref{def:adaptive-history}. At a representation join, the already observed history is carried forward as controller state; no past record is erased.
\end{proof}

The records enter as assimilation events in the sense of \cref{def:dyn-record-interrupted}. The potential is unchanged by them, and after an update the force \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow} depends on the record only through the updated profile. The admitted records are those whose likelihoods are invariant under the compression group and that are applied to every agent, which here means base-only and broadcast likelihoods; every admitted record history is a functional of the root trajectory $f_0(t)$. A likelihood that reads a public coordinate, for example $L(o\mid r,\sigma_0)$ at the root node, multiplies the profiles of the two root branches by $L(o\mid r,u_1)$ and $L(o\mid r,-u_1)$ respectively, and the next fresh root response \cref{eq:boundary-hetero-root} then depends on the root phase. A base-only record delivered to one subtree produces the same asymmetry. Records reading ancestor path bits would require retaining the ancestor phases and give a different descriptor.

\remarkheading{A persistent base-only evidence term}{rem:boundary-evidence}
Add to the potential a fixed evidence term $\sum_i\int q_i(d\xi)[-\log L(o\mid r)]$ for a recorded base-only likelihood with bounded logarithm, and analogously in the model sector. This evidence-augmented potential is not the reciprocal potential of \cref{def:dyn-potential}. Every leaf has base marginal $f_0A$, so the term equals $N\int f_0(-\log L(o\mid r))dA$, and its force is the base-only function $-\log L(o\mid r)$, which enters $h^q$ exactly as $V^q$ does. The term is invariant under $\mathcal S$, so \cref{thm:boundary-tree-flow,thm:boundary-tree-inheritance} and \cref{lem:boundary-global} hold with $V^q$ replaced by $V^q-\log L(o\mid r)$ and $\Vert\log\rho\Vert_\infty$ increased by $\Vert\log L\Vert_\infty$, where $\Vert\log L\Vert_\infty$ is the sum of the bounds in the two sectors when both carry evidence. The added term is the case of \cref{eq:dyn-evidence-potential} with $o_i=o$ for every agent and record kernels that read only the base coordinates, $\ell_i(o\mid k,m)=L^q(o\mid r(k))L^s(o\mid w(m))$. The stationary laws then depend on the accumulated evidence, while the compression is preserved.

The fresh-sampling condition matters. For a pair $q_\pm=p(1\pm a\sigma)$, two samples $\xi,\xi'$ from one reused child have joint density
\begin{equation}
 p(\xi)p(\xi')[1+a(\xi)a(\xi')\sigma(\xi)\sigma(\xi')],
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-reuse}
\end{equation}
which can reveal the discarded phase. A shared child label for a joint belief/model response can likewise expose a relative contrast. Unrestricted external identity-channel probes can distinguish these laws, as in \cref{ch:public-compression}. These protocols are outside the stated inherited interface.

\remarkheading{The root interface does not see base-uniform shapes}{rem:boundary-root-blind}
Let $f=f_0(r)F(u)$ with $\E_{U_d}F=1$. Since $\sum_kb_k^q=1$, \cref{eq:boundary-tree-flow} gives $h^q=\log f_0+V^q+\beta(u)$ with $\beta=2\log F-\sum_kb_k^q(P_k\log F+P_kF/F)$, so $\partial_t\log F=-(\beta-\E_{U_d}[F\beta])$ is independent of $r$, the product form is invariant, and $\partial_tf_0=-f_0[(\log f_0+V^q)-\int f_0(\log f_0+V^q)dA]$ involves neither $F$ nor the rows; the shape enters only the row equations, through $D_k(f)=D_k(F)$. The model sector is the same, $V^q$ and $V^s$ read only base marginals, a base-only broadcast record multiplies $f_0$ by $L(o\mid r)/Z_o$ and leaves $F$ unchanged, and its law depends on $f_0$ alone. Two base-uniform shapes $F,F'$ in different tree-automorphism orbits therefore give identical histories of the root responses under every admitted adaptive record policy. The unordered profile tree is not minimal for the root interface alone.

\openproblemheading{Minimality of the unordered profile tree}{op:boundary-minimality}
Decide whether the unordered profile tree is minimal for the fresh-response interface of the hierarchy: whether the response histories of all nodes, under admitted adaptive record policies, separate the base-dependent tree-automorphism orbits of profiles, at least generically. By \cref{rem:boundary-root-blind} the root histories alone do not, so a minimal root retention identifies every base-uniform shape with the flat profile. \Cref{prop:boundary-observable} proves local injectivity for a different two-agent binary model; for $d=1$ and a two-point base, the analogous Jacobian computation for the root response and its time derivatives is a natural first case.

\section{Public distinctions discarded at every depth}
\label{sec:boundary-tree-loss}

Choose, only as an initial witness,
\begin{equation}
 f(r,u)=f_0(r)\prod_{j=1}^d[1+a_j(r)u_j],\qquad 0<a_j(r)<1,
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-witness}
\end{equation}
with regular bounds and $\int f_0 dA=1$. The evolution subsequently uses the unrestricted positive profile $f$, and is not assumed to preserve this product. Before joining at depth $l$, the two child-parent response densities differ by their factor $1\pm a_{l+1}\sigma_v$. Rotating $\alpha_v$ changes these full public laws, while their joined unordered profile tree stays fixed. Strict differences persist on a nonzero time interval by continuity. The same construction works at every depth and in both sectors. Under the same flow, however, these differences decay near the partition-independent profiles.

\propositionheading{Decay of public contrast}{prop:boundary-contrast-decay}
Under the profile flow of \cref{thm:boundary-tree-flow}, the following hold in each sector.
(i) The set of profiles that do not depend on $u$ is invariant, and on it the rows converge to the class priors \cref{eq:boundary-class-priors}.
(ii) Along a solution in this set, write a nearby profile as $f=f_0(1+\varphi)$ with $\E_{U_d}\varphi=0$. To first order in $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
 \dot\varphi=-\left(3-2\sum_kb_k^qP_k\right)\varphi.
 \label{eq:boundary-contrast-linear}
\end{equation}
For the Walsh function $\chi_S(u)=\prod_{j\in S}u_j$ with largest index $m$, the rate is $\lambda_m=3+2b_{m-1}^q-2\sum_{k\ge m}b_k^q\ge1$. At the class priors every rate equals $3+2/(N-1)$.
(iii) Among profiles with a fixed base marginal, the belief part $\int f\log f dA dU_d+\sum_kb_k^qD_k(f)$ of the profile energy is minimized exactly by the profile that does not depend on $u$.

\begin{proof}
If $f$ does not depend on $u$, then $P_k\log f=\log f$ and $P_kf/f=1$, so $h^q$ does not depend on $u$ and the set is invariant. There $D_k=0$, and \cref{eq:boundary-tree-rows} is the Fisher flow of $T_a\KL(b^a\Vert\pi)$, whose log-odds relative to $\pi$ decay at rate $T_a$. For (ii), $\log f=\log f_0+\varphi+O(\varphi^2)$, $P_k\log f=\log f_0+P_k\varphi+O(\varphi^2)$, and $P_kf/f=1+P_k\varphi-\varphi+O(\varphi^2)$. The $u$-dependent part of $h^q$ is therefore $(3-2\sum_kb_kP_k)\varphi$, which has zero $U_d$-mean, while $D_k$ is of second order and the rows and $V^q$ are unchanged at first order. Writing $\dot f=\dot f_0(1+\varphi)+f_0\dot\varphi$ and taking the $U_d$-mean of $\dot f=-f(h^q-c_q)$ gives \cref{eq:boundary-contrast-linear}. On $\chi_S$, $P_k\chi_S=0$ when $m>k+1$, $P_k\chi_S=-\chi_S$ when $m=k+1$, and $P_k\chi_S=\chi_S$ when $m\le k$, which gives $\lambda_m$; since $\sum_kb_k=1$, $\lambda_m\ge1$. At the class priors, $\pi_{m-1}-\sum_{k\ge m}\pi_k=[2^{d-m}-(2^{d-m}-1)]/(N-1)=1/(N-1)$. For (iii), $\int f\log f dA dU_d=\int f_0\log f_0dA+\int f_0\KL(f(r,\cdot)/f_0(r)\Vert U_d)dA$, the conditional term vanishes exactly at the $u$-independent profile, and each $D_k$ is nonnegative and vanishes there.
\end{proof}

The common rate $3+2/(N-1)$ equals $1+\kappa_N$ with $\kappa_N=2N/(N-1)$, the rate of the complete-graph difference mode in \cref{eq:public-phase-linearization} at $J=0$. The proposition is local. Two global statements follow from the lemmas above.

\corollaryheading{No finite-time loss of contrast, and global decay on a finite base}{cor:boundary-contrast-global}
Under the profile flow of \cref{thm:boundary-tree-flow}, a profile that depends on $u$ at $t=0$ depends on $u$ at every finite time. If the base spaces are finite, every solution has bit contrast tending to zero in both sectors and rows tending to the class priors \cref{eq:boundary-class-priors}.
\begin{proof}
The set of $u$-independent profiles is a closed linear subspace of the log chart, invariant by \cref{prop:boundary-contrast-decay} (i). The field is locally Lipschitz, so solutions are unique backward as well as forward, and if a solution entered the subspace at a time $t^*$, the solution inside the subspace through that point would coincide with it on $[0,t^*]$; this is the argument of \cref{inh:smooth-no-finite-entry}. On a finite base the chart is finite dimensional, and by \cref{lem:boundary-global} the solution stays in a compact subset of the interior. The profile energy decreases at the rate of the summed Fisher variances, which vanish exactly at equilibria, so by LaSalle's invariance principle the $\omega$-limit set consists of equilibria. At an equilibrium $h^q=c_q$, so $f$ is a critical point, and since $\int f\log f$ and each $D_k$ are convex and the evaluator term is linear in $f$, a minimizer, of $f\mapsto F(f,g,b)$ on normalized profiles. Averaging $f$ over $u$ at fixed $r$ leaves $f_0$ and the evaluator term unchanged and, by \cref{prop:boundary-contrast-decay} (iii), lowers the rest strictly unless $f$ is already $u$-independent. Every equilibrium therefore has $u$-independent profiles in both sectors, hence $D_k=0$ and rows equal to the class priors.
\end{proof}

The distinctions discarded at every depth are therefore the orbit data of \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance}. Their effects through the excluded protocols decay near the partition-independent profiles, and on a finite base along every solution; on a general base global decay is not established, because bounded sets of $L^\infty$ are not compact.

In the circle realization these losses are continuous in total variation. For a phase shift $\delta$ with $|\delta|\le\pi$, the sign disagreement has Haar mass $|\delta|/\pi$. If the two branch response densities at a node are $p_+,p_-$, then, including the base and ancestor integrations, the total variation distance induced in one child-parent response is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|\delta|}{2\pi}\int\E_{U_l}|p_+(r,U_l)-p_-(r,U_l)|dA(r).
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-tv}
\end{equation}
For $\pi<|\delta|\le2\pi$, the factor $|\delta|$ is replaced by $2\pi-|\delta|$. With the squared Hellinger distance $H^2=\frac12\int(\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{p'})^2$, the same computation gives
\begin{equation}
 H^2=\frac{|\delta|}{2\pi}\int\E_{U_l}\left(\sqrt{p_+}-\sqrt{p_-}\right)^2dA,
 \qquad |\delta|\le\pi.
 \label{eq:boundary-tree-hellinger}
\end{equation}
The Fisher--Rao distance between the two laws is $2\arccos(1-H^2)$, which lies between $2\sqrt{2H^2}$ and $\pi\sqrt{2H^2}$. Hence every phase path along which $p_+\ne p_-$ on a set of positive measure is Lipschitz in total variation and H\"older of order one half in the Fisher--Rao distance, and it is not rectifiable in that distance: the chord sum over $n$ equal steps of a fixed phase interval grows like $\sqrt n$. These paths have no finite Fisher tangent and are not continuous in the bounded-log-density norm. In the coin realization, orientation changes are discrete.

Each phase can be an arbitrary measurable function of the base. The quotient therefore discards spatial patterns in the original public laws, beyond a permutation of primitive agents. It retains the amplitude profile tree, whose size can grow with the number of descendants; no fixed-size parent representation is asserted.

The sample-coordinate density is piecewise constant in the node coordinates, while the law-valued Fisher evolution remains smooth in time on the regular profile domain. The result does not give a smooth dimension reduction on an open law chart. It gives exact recursive public compression on a prepared invariant class, and that compression is a symmetry reduction. The general base laws and the coupled evaluator enter only through the base marginals $f_0,g_0$, which every level retains; the compressed data are orthogonal to them. The tree and the class are supplied, and no endogenous formation time has been derived.

The mechanism relies on complementary child laws whose equal mixture is flat in the public coordinate. Equal mixtures of Gaussian children do not have this property: with $\phi_d$ the standard normal density, $\frac12[\phi_d(y-v)+\phi_d(y+v)]=\phi_d(y)e^{-|v|^2/2}\cosh(y\cdot v)$ depends on the direction of $v$ unless $v=0$. A continuous phase of the kind used here therefore has no analogue for Gaussian child laws with a common isotropic covariance.

The reference coordinates $r,w$ in this construction belong to represented sample spaces. They are not identified with the contextual base $\mathcal C$. Applying \cref{ch:geometry} would additionally require an admitted smooth context-parameterized family and compatible connections. The measurable retention proved here does not supply the collective-connection condition in \cref{geo:retained-connection}.

\section{Why ordinary response completion can remove all compression}
\label{sec:boundary-observability}

The preceding restrictions cannot generally be removed by adding a few response moments to the phase parent. Observation derivatives can already recover a whole fine configuration, as in the local observability method of \citet{HermannKrener1977}. The following calculation uses only the inverse function theorem.

\propositionheading{Fresh responses locally identify a binary pair}{prop:boundary-observable}
Consider two agents under the reciprocal potential with identity peer channels, binary state and model spaces, the uniform model reference, and the coupled binary evaluator $G_J(\kappa\mid\kappa')=e^{J\kappa\kappa'}/(2\cosh J)$ for any real $J$; this includes the range $|J|<1$ used in \cref{ch:public-compression}. There is an open heterogeneous region where any retention preserving both fresh belief/model mixture responses and their future evolution is locally injective.

\begin{proof}
Write $q_i=p_{x_i}$, $s_i=p_{y_i}$, where $p_x(\kappa)=(1+\kappa x)/2$. The exact original equations, including incoming derivatives, are
\begin{equation}
 \dot x_i=-(1-x_i^2)[2\operatorname{atanh}x_i-\operatorname{atanh}x_j-Jy_i]-(x_i-x_j),
 \label{eq:boundary-binary-flow}
\end{equation}
and the exchanged equation for $y$; a nonuniform model reference would add its own term to the model equation. Set $x_{1,2}=m\pm a$, $y_{1,2}=n\pm e$. Fresh mixture responses are $p_m,p_n$. Their velocities have the convergent local expansions
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 F_x&=-(1-m^2)(\operatorname{atanh}m-Jn)+(6m-Jn)a^2-2Jmae+O(5),\\
 F_y&=-(1-n^2)(\operatorname{atanh}n-Jm)+(6n-Jm)e^2-2Jnae+O(5).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:boundary-response-derivatives}
\end{equation}
Here $O(5)$ denotes terms of total degree at least five. To check the coefficient, the uncoupled part of $F_x$ is
\begin{equation}
 -\tfrac12\sum_{\pm}[1-(m\pm a)^2]\operatorname{atanh}(m\pm a)
 +2ma[\operatorname{atanh}(m+a)-\operatorname{atanh}(m-a)],
\end{equation}
whose expansion is $-m+2m^3/3+6ma^2+O(5)$.

For $\mathcal H=(m,n,F_x,F_y)$, its Jacobian determinant at $m=n=\varepsilon c_1$, $a=e=\varepsilon c_2$, $c_1c_2\ne0$, is
\begin{equation}
 \det D\mathcal H
 =4\varepsilon^4c_1^2c_2^2(6-3J)(6-J)+O(\varepsilon^6).
 \label{eq:boundary-observability-det}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the lower-right block is $2\varepsilon^2c_1c_2$ times the matrix with diagonal $6-2J$ and off-diagonal $-J$, plus $O(\varepsilon^4)$. Its leading coefficient vanishes only at $J=2$ and $J=6$. At a general base point $(m,n,a,e)=\varepsilon(c_m,c_n,c_a,c_e)$ the same block gives the leading coefficient $\varepsilon^4Q(c;J)$ with $Q=[2(6c_m-Jc_n)c_a-2Jc_mc_e][2(6c_n-Jc_m)c_e-2Jc_nc_a]-4J^2c_mc_nc_ac_e$, which equals $-32$ at $J=2$, $c=(1,0,1,1)$ and $-36$ at $J=6$, $c=(1,0,1,1/2)$. For every real $J$ there is therefore a base point at which the determinant is nonzero for sufficiently small nonzero $\varepsilon$, and it remains nonzero nearby. If two states in that neighborhood have the same inherited parent, their response curves agree. Equality at time zero and of their first derivatives gives equality of $\mathcal H$, hence equality of the states by local invertibility. No differentiability assumption on the retention is needed.
\end{proof}

This is a local obstruction in one original model. It neither removes the global child-exchange ambiguity nor excludes the selective protocols and invariant classes constructed above.
