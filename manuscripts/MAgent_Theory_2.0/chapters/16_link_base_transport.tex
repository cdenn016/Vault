\chapter{Base Transport from Contextual Agent Data}
\label{ch:link-base-transport}

A smooth family of agent interactions can determine a connection once its cross-context comparison rule is specified. This chapter constructs such rules from law moments, receiving interfaces, and directed links. A full-rank message matrix gives displacement maps with identity at zero displacement; their first derivative gives the connection, and products along refining paths give its parallel transport. A second construction aligns the contextual link data directly and admits nonzero curvature even when every agent backtrack is the identity.

The comparison data are constitutive choices. The message metric and an augmented link metric need not be the Fisher metric of the joint law in \cref{geo:collective}. The new selectors therefore need not equal that chapter's collective Fisher connection. They supply a conditional construction for \cref{open:conn-derived}; they add no evolution equation or formation result to the contextual-flow extension in \cref{ch:contextual-coupling}. The selectors are kinematic: they take supplied fields as input and belong to none of the three objectives of \cref{ch:dynamics}.

\section{Contextual data and their transformation laws}

Let $\mathcal C$ be a smooth finite-dimensional contextual base, $P\to\mathcal C$ a principal matrix-group bundle, and $E=P\times_G V$ an associated vector bundle. Fix one sector temporarily. The explicit construction first uses $G=\GL(K,\R)$ and $V=\R^K$. \Cref{ibt:sec:group} treats a smaller declared matrix group. A context is denoted $c$; agent labels $i,j$ are not base coordinates. All derivatives below are contextual derivatives at fixed evolution time.

On a patch choose a reference presentation of $E$. Agent $i$ displays vectors in a coordinate copy $E_i$ through an invertible map $U_i:V\to E_i$. Its normalized law $\nu_i$ may be a belief or model marginal. A passive change of displayed coordinates and a change of common reference act differently:
\begin{align}
 U_i'&=g_iU_i,&\nu_i'&=(g_i)_\#\nu_i,
 \label{ibt:eq:passive}\\
 U_i'&=U_i h,&\bar\nu_i'&=(h^{-1})_\#\bar\nu_i,
 &\bar\nu_i&=(U_i^{-1})_\#\nu_i.
 \label{ibt:eq:reference}
\end{align}
The presentation maps and coordinate changes are $C^2$ and may vary with context. Reference-independent statements must satisfy both rules.

A directed, invertible interaction at one context is
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_{ij}=U_i \bar\Theta_{ij}U_j^{-1}:E_j\longrightarrow E_i,
 \qquad \bar\Theta_{ij}'=h^{-1}\bar\Theta_{ij}h.
 \label{ibt:eq:point-link}
\end{equation}
For example, $\bar\Theta_{ij}=\exp(L_{ij})$, where $L_{ij}=O_iT_j$ is a receiving--sending product on $V$ that may depend on the current laws. General normalized channels need not be invertible; the factorization arguments here apply to invertible links only. Neither $\Theta_{ij}$ nor its exponential generator is already a displacement map between $c$ and $c+\delta c$.

Our convention for a reference connection is $D^a=d+a$, with
\begin{equation}
 a'=h^{-1}ah+h^{-1}d h.
 \label{ibt:eq:affine}
\end{equation}
In agent $i$'s displayed coordinates the same connection is
\begin{equation}
 B_i=U_i aU_i^{-1}-d U_iU_i^{-1},\qquad
 F_i=U_i F^aU_i^{-1},\qquad F^a=d a+a\wedge a.
 \label{ibt:eq:dressed}
\end{equation}
Thus $B_i'=g_iB_ig_i^{-1}-d g_i g_i^{-1}$ under \cref{ibt:eq:passive}, and $B_i$ is unchanged by \cref{ibt:eq:reference} together with \cref{ibt:eq:affine}. Products of matrix-valued forms include matrix multiplication; $(a\wedge a)(Y,Z)=[a(Y),a(Z)]$.

The issue is to derive $a$ from stated contextual agent data. Merely declaring $a_\mu$ and inserting $\exp(-a_\mu\delta c^\mu)$ supplies the answer as additional data. The construction below begins with finite cross-context messages and differentiates their normalized alignment.

\section{What an agent frame does determine}

\propositionheading{A square frame selects flat local transport}{ibt:frame}
Let $Q(c)$ be a $C^2$ invertible matrix whose columns are declared marked frame vectors, expressed in one reference coordinate system. Put
\begin{equation}
 a_Q=-d Q Q^{-1},\qquad
 T_Q(c',c)=Q(c')Q(c)^{-1}.
 \label{ibt:eq:frame}
\end{equation}
Then $T_Q(c,c)=I$, $D^{a_Q}Q=0$, $F^{a_Q}=0$, and
$T_Q(c'',c')T_Q(c',c)=T_Q(c'',c)$. If $Q'=h^{-1}Q$, then $a_Q$ obeys \cref{ibt:eq:affine} and $T_Q'=h(c')^{-1}T_Qh(c)$.
\begin{proof}
Identity, composition and covariance follow by multiplication. Taylor expansion gives $T_Q(c+\delta c,c)=I-a_Q(\delta c)+O(|\delta c|^2)$. The identity $d Q^{-1}=-Q^{-1}(d Q)Q^{-1}$ gives $d a_Q=-a_Q\wedge a_Q$, proving flatness.
\end{proof}

An agent frame can therefore select a connection if declaring that frame parallel is part of the model. When the frame is only a coordinate choice, that declaration changes the model under an arbitrary change of frame unless the connection is transformed with it. In particular $B_i=-d U_iU_i^{-1}$, with $U_i$ as in \cref{ibt:eq:reference}, holds the reference connection $a=0$ fixed. Replacing $U_i$ by $U_i h(c)$ while again setting $a=0$ changes $B_i$ by $-U_id h h^{-1}U_i^{-1}$. This rule is not independent of the common reference.

Several individually flat connections can give a curved affine average. They must already be connections on the same bundle. For two such connections $a_1,a_2$, put $\Delta=a_1-a_2$ and let $\lambda$ be a smooth scalar. Then $a=a_2+\lambda\Delta$ has
\begin{equation}
 F^a=d\lambda\wedge\Delta-\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta\wedge\Delta.
 \label{ibt:eq:average-curvature}
\end{equation}
Flatness gives $D^{a_2}\Delta=-\Delta\wedge\Delta$, which substituted into $F^{a_2+\lambda\Delta}$ proves the formula. Weight variation can produce Abelian curvature, and noncommuting disagreements can produce non-Abelian curvature even at constant weight. The weights and identification of the individual connections remain constitutive inputs. Averaging square frames and applying \cref{ibt:eq:frame} still gives a flat connection.

\section{Retaining reciprocal and backtrack information}

Suppose each backtrack $N_{ij,j}=\Theta_{ji}\Theta_{ij}$ has spectrum disjoint from $(-\infty,0]$. The pair $ij$ names the interacting pair and the last subscript the agent at which the loop is based, so $N_{ij,j}$ and $N_{ij,i}$ are the two loops of one pair. The following is the split of \cref{prop:int-reciprocal}, written with these subscripts. Define
\begin{equation}
 S_{ij,j}=N_{ij,j}^{1/2},\qquad
 \Omega_{ij}=\Theta_{ij}S_{ij,j}^{-1},\qquad
 \Theta_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}S_{ij,j}.
 \label{ibt:eq:reciprocal}
\end{equation}
The principal real matrix root is smooth on this spectral domain and commutes with similarity~\citep{Higham2008}. Since $N_{ij,i}=\Theta_{ij}N_{ij,j}\Theta_{ij}^{-1}$, multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_{ji}=\Omega_{ij}^{-1},\qquad
 \Omega_{ij}'=g_i\Omega_{ij}g_j^{-1},\qquad
 S_{ij,j}'=g_jS_{ij,j}g_j^{-1}.
 \label{ibt:eq:reciprocal-law}
\end{equation}
This factorization retains the original directed pair exactly. It does not impose inverse relations on the original $\Theta$ fields. An interaction loop at one context is also distinct from a loop in the contextual base.

The construction may use raw $\Theta$ fields directly, without any square-root branch. Where the branch exists, including both $\Omega$-transformed and $\Theta$-transformed messages makes their different roles explicit. Storing $\Omega$ and $S$ retains all directed links, even though their eventual connection need not encode them injectively.

\section{A full-rank contextual message construction}
\label{ibt:sec:messages}

Fix a finite census and persistent message labels. Their message space is $H=\R^r$, with a stated, context-independent Euclidean inner product. A fixed positive message metric can be absorbed into the columns. Identifying these labels between contexts is an assumption; agent labels or fixed receiving-interface columns give one realization. \Cref{ibt:sec:message-connection} treats changing message coordinates.

For a normalized law with a finite first moment, let $\mu_i=\E_{\nu_i}[v]\in E_i$ and $\bar \mu_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i$. The law need not be Gaussian. Smoothness of its moments is an assumption on the law family, not a consequence of normalization. Define
\begin{equation}
 R_{ij}=U_i^{-1}\Omega_{ij}U_j,\qquad
 \bar\Theta_{ij}=U_i^{-1}\Theta_{ij}U_j.
\end{equation}
One permissible collective message matrix is a chosen concatenation
\begin{equation}
 X(c)=\bigl[\{\bar \mu_i\}_i,\ \{R_{ij}\bar \mu_j\}_{(i,j)},\
                    \{\bar\Theta_{ij}\bar \mu_j\}_{(i,j)}\bigr]:H\longrightarrow V.
 \label{ibt:eq:X-construction}
\end{equation}
Each column has a stated label. Receiving-interface columns $O_i:H_i\to V$, or their link-transformed columns, can replace or supplement the means. Gauge-invariant scalar column weights may depend smoothly on laws and rows; their derivatives are included in $d X$. Column selection, weights and message metric specify the rule. The identity self link does not imply that these columns span $V$.

Under passive agent re-expression every reference column is unchanged. Under reference rechoice it obeys
\begin{equation}
 X'=h^{-1}X.
 \label{ibt:eq:X-law}
\end{equation}
For example $(h^{-1}R_{ij}h)(h^{-1}\bar \mu_j)=h^{-1}R_{ij}\bar \mu_j$. The same calculation applies to raw link and interface columns. Thus the construction is reference consistent when its original link and interface rules are reference consistent. This is naturality: the rule is built equivariantly from the laws and from declared data that are re-expressed with the reference. It is not an active symmetry of the model with those data held fixed, which fixed receiving axes or labels generally break.

\hypothesisheading{Regular message region}{ibt:regular}
On an open region $\mathcal U\subset\mathcal C$, $X$ is $C^2$ with row rank $K$. Its columns refer to one fixed labeled message space. Local expressions on overlaps obey \cref{ibt:eq:X-law}. No spatial connection on $E$ is included in these data.

Put
\begin{equation}
 H_X=XX^T,\qquad Q_X=X^T H_X^{-1},\qquad P_X=Q_XX.
 \label{ibt:eq:right-inverse}
\end{equation}
Here $H_X$ is a positive-definite $K\times K$ Gram matrix, $Q_X:V\to H$ is a right inverse of $X$, and $P_X$ is the orthogonal projector in message space onto $\operatorname{im}X^T$.

\theoremheading{Displacement links and their connection}{ibt:displacement}
Under the regular message assumption, the map
\begin{equation}
 C(c',c)=X(c')Q_X(c)
 \label{ibt:eq:cross-map}
\end{equation}
equals $I$ at $c'=c$, is invertible on a neighborhood of the diagonal, and obeys
\begin{equation}
 C'(c',c)=h(c')^{-1}C(c',c)h(c).
 \label{ibt:eq:cross-endpoint}
\end{equation}
Its first derivative determines a $C^1$ connection:
\begin{equation}
 C(c+\delta c,c)=I-a_c(\delta c)+O(|\delta c|^2),\qquad
 a=-d X Q_X.
 \label{ibt:eq:derived-a}
\end{equation}
The derivative is linear in the tangent displacement. The local forms obey \cref{ibt:eq:affine} and define a connection wherever the data have the stated overlap laws. In agent coordinates
\begin{equation}
 C_i(c',c)=U_i(c')C(c',c)U_i(c)^{-1}
 \label{ibt:eq:display-cross}
\end{equation}
has connection \cref{ibt:eq:dressed}, is reference independent, and transforms by $g_i(c')$ and $g_i(c)^{-1}$ at its two endpoints.
\begin{proof}
Full row rank gives $XQ_X=I$. Continuity of the determinant makes $C$ invertible near each diagonal point. Under \cref{ibt:eq:X-law},
\begin{equation}
 H_X'=h^{-1}H_Xh^{-T},\qquad Q_X'=Q_Xh,\qquad P_X'=P_X.
\end{equation}
This proves the endpoint law. Differentiating the first argument of \cref{ibt:eq:cross-map} gives \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a}; Taylor's theorem gives its remainder locally uniformly on compact subsets. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 -d(h^{-1}X)Q_Xh=h^{-1}(-d XQ_X)h+h^{-1}d h.
\end{equation}
The affine law and the bundle transition cocycle glue the local covariant derivatives. Expanding \cref{ibt:eq:display-cross} gives $B_i$; substitution proves passive covariance and reference independence.
\end{proof}

\propositionheading{Global message surjections}{ibt:global}
Let $E\to\mathcal C$ be a rank-$K$ vector bundle and $X:\mathcal C\times\R^r\to E$ a $C^2$ fiberwise surjective bundle map whose local forms obey \cref{ibt:eq:X-law}. Put $W(c)=\operatorname{im}X(c)^T\subset\R^r$. Then the local forms $a=-dXQ_X$ define a global $C^1$ connection, with covariant derivative
\begin{equation}
 \nabla\sigma=Xd(Q_X\sigma).
 \label{ibt:eq:global-nabla}
\end{equation}
It is the connection induced on the moving subspace $W$ by orthogonal projection of the trivial connection of $\R^r$, transported to $E$ by the isomorphism $X|_W$. The maps $C(c',c)=X(c')Q_X(c)$ are defined for all pairs, equal the identity on the diagonal, and are invertible near it. The map $c\mapsto W(c)$ into the Grassmannian of $K$-planes in $\R^r$ classifies $E$; in particular $r=K$ forces $E$ to be trivial.
\begin{proof}
The affine law follows from the identity in the proof of \cref{ibt:displacement}, so the local forms glue. Since $XQ_X=I$, $XdQ_X=-dXQ_X$, and therefore $Xd(Q_X\sigma)=d\sigma-dXQ_X\sigma=D^a\sigma$. Because $Q_X$ is an isomorphism onto $W$ with inverse $X|_W$, and $X$ annihilates $W^\perp$, this is $X|_W$ applied to the orthogonal projection onto $W$ of the derivative of the $W$-valued section $Q_X\sigma$. The statements about $C$ were proved in \cref{ibt:displacement}. Finally $X|_{W(c)}:W(c)\to E_c$ is an isomorphism depending continuously on $c$, so $E$ is isomorphic to the pullback of the tautological bundle along $c\mapsto W(c)$; for $r=K$ that pullback is trivial.
\end{proof}

When $r>K$, such a surjection need not trivialize $E$. For instance, on the tangent bundle of the unit sphere $S^2\subset\R^3$, the tangential projections of the three coordinate vectors give a surjection with $r=3$; the induced connection is the Levi-Civita connection, and the Euler number $2$ shows that this bundle is nontrivial. A global square frame is the more restrictive case $r=K$.

A receiver's displayed columns $\mu_i,\Omega_{ij}\mu_j,\Theta_{ij}\mu_j$ also transform by left multiplication by $g_i$. Applying the formula gives a receiver-selected connection. Different receivers' selections need not agree. The collective reference construction \cref{ibt:eq:X-construction} instead selects one $a$, displayed by all agents through \cref{ibt:eq:dressed}.

\section{Reversal, finite paths, and zero displacement}

The cross-map $C$ generally fails both reversal and composition. These failures do not invalidate its first jet as a connection, but matter before calling a finite map parallel transport. Finite maps on links whose ordered products refine to path-ordered transport are the link variables of lattice gauge theory and their continuum limit \citep{Wilson1974}; a discrete-connection formalism is developed in \citet{LeokMarsdenWeinstein2005}.

\propositionheading{Reciprocal displacement maps with the same derivative}{ibt:reciprocal}
After shrinking the neighborhood of the diagonal, define
\begin{equation}
 N(c',c)=C(c,c')C(c',c),\qquad
 T(c',c)=C(c',c)N(c',c)^{-1/2}.
 \label{ibt:eq:discrete-reciprocal}
\end{equation}
Then $T(c,c)=I$, $T(c,c')=T(c',c)^{-1}$, and $T$ obeys \cref{ibt:eq:cross-endpoint}. Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
 N(c+\delta c,c)=I+O(|\delta c|^2),\qquad
 T(c+\delta c,c)=I-a_c(\delta c)+O(|\delta c|^2).
 \label{ibt:eq:same-jet}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
At the diagonal $N=I$, so its principal root is smooth nearby. The calculation in \cref{ibt:eq:reciprocal-law} proves reversal and endpoint covariance. Differentiating $XQ_X=I$ gives $Xd Q_X=-d XQ_X$, so the two factors of $N$ have opposite linear terms. Its root is $I+O(|\delta c|^2)$, proving the expansion.
\end{proof}

\propositionheading{Principal angles and the reciprocal map}{ibt:grassmann}
Let $W(c)=\operatorname{im}X(c)^T$ and let $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_K\in[0,\pi/2]$ be the principal angles between $W(c)$ and $W(c')$. Then the eigenvalues of $N(c',c)$ are $\cos^2\theta_k$. Wherever $C(c',c)$ is invertible, that is, wherever every $\theta_k<\pi/2$, the operator $N(c',c)$ is similar to a positive operator, $T(c',c)$ is defined without further shrinking, and $T(c',c)$ is an isometry from $(E_c,H_X(c)^{-1})$ to $(E_{c'},H_X(c')^{-1})$. Read through these isometries, $T(c',c)$ is the direct rotation of $W(c)$ onto $W(c')$, which equals parallel transport of the connection \cref{ibt:eq:global-nabla} of the moving subspace along the Grassmannian geodesic from $W(c)$ to $W(c')$.
\begin{proof}
Since $Q_X^TQ_X=H_X^{-1}$, the map $\iota_c=Q_X(c)$ is an isometry from $(E_c,H_X(c)^{-1})$ onto $W(c)$, with inverse $X(c)|_{W(c)}$, and $X(c')$ vanishes on $W(c')^\perp$. Hence $C(c',c)=\iota_{c'}^{-1}\Pi'\iota_c$, where $\Pi'$ is the orthogonal projection onto $W(c')$ restricted to $W(c)$, and similarly $C(c,c')=\iota_c^{-1}\Pi\iota_{c'}$. Thus $N(c',c)$ is conjugate to $\Pi\Pi'$ restricted to $W(c)$, a positive semidefinite self-adjoint operator with eigenvalues $\cos^2\theta_k$ \citep{BjorckGolub1973}; it is invertible exactly when $\Pi'$ is injective on $W(c)$. The map $T=CN^{-1/2}$ is then conjugate to the orthogonal polar factor of $\Pi'$, an isometry of $W(c)$ onto $W(c')$, which is the direct rotation \citep{DavisKahan1970}. Choose principal vectors $w_k\in W(c)$ and $w'_k\in W(c')$ with $\langle w_j,w'_k\rangle=\delta_{jk}\cos\theta_k$, and for $\theta_k>0$ put $n_k=(w'_k-\cos\theta_k w_k)/\sin\theta_k$, an orthonormal family in $W(c)^\perp$. Let $\Xi$ be the skew map with $\Xi w_k=\theta_kn_k$, $\Xi n_k=-\theta_kw_k$, and zero on the remaining complement. The curve $W(t)=\exp(t\Xi)W(c)$ is a Grassmannian geodesic from $W(c)$ to $W(c')$, and the vectors $\exp(t\Xi)w_k=\cos(t\theta_k)w_k+\sin(t\theta_k)n_k$ have derivatives orthogonal to $W(t)$, so they are parallel for the projected connection. At $t=1$ they equal $w'_k$, and the polar factor of $\Pi'$ also sends $w_k$ to $w'_k$, because $\Pi'w_k=\cos\theta_kw'_k$.
\end{proof}

The Grassmannian geodesic need not be the image of any path in the base, so $T(c',c)$ is exact transport along a path that the base may not contain; this is the precise sense of the remark below that a finite endpoint rule contains additional higher-order data. Because $T(c,c')=T(c',c)^{-1}$, ordered products of $T$ form a symmetric one-step rule, and for $C^3$ data their local errors cancel at second order, whereas products of $C$ are first-order accurate.

The new $N(c',c)$ is a cross-context reconstruction defect. It differs from the agent backtrack $N_{ij,j}(c)$, retained in the input state through \cref{ibt:eq:reciprocal}. Reciprocalizing a displacement map does not delete the backtrack data that determined $X$.

\theoremheading{Parallel transport from the derived first jet}{ibt:path}
For a piecewise $C^2$ path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal U$, the equation
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mathcal T}(t)=-a_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t))\mathcal T(t),\qquad
 \mathcal T(0)=I
 \label{ibt:eq:path-ode}
\end{equation}
has a unique invertible solution. It composes under concatenation, inverts under reversal, and has the endpoint law. Ordered products of $C$ or $T$ along partitions of $\gamma$ converge to $\mathcal T(1)$ as the maximum time step tends to zero.
\begin{proof}
On each smooth segment the coefficient in \cref{ibt:eq:path-ode} is continuous and bounded. Picard iteration on a sufficiently short interval is a contraction, and finitely many intervals cover the path. The solution of $\dot{\mathcal S}=\mathcal Sa(\dot\gamma)$, $\mathcal S(0)=I$, satisfies $\frac{d}{d t}(\mathcal S\mathcal T)=0$, proving invertibility. Uniqueness proves concatenation and reversal. Differentiating $h(\gamma(t))^{-1}\mathcal T(t)h(\gamma(0))$ verifies its transformed equation.

On each step contained in one smooth segment the exact short propagator and discrete factor equal $I-a(\dot\gamma)\Delta t+O(\Delta t^2)$ uniformly on the compact path image. Their norms are bounded by $1+L\Delta t$. Telescoping the product difference bounds the contribution of these steps by $c_0\exp(2L)\sum_k\Delta t_k^2$, which tends to zero because $\sum_k\Delta t_k^2\leq\max_k\Delta t_k$. A step that straddles one of the finitely many corners has both factors equal to $I+O(\Delta t)$, so these steps add at most a constant times the number of corners times $\max_k\Delta t_k$. This proves convergence for both discrete factors and every partition sequence. Local trivializations can be joined by transition maps; the endpoint laws make the result independent of this description.
\end{proof}

An unrefined $C(c',c)$ or $T(c',c)$ need not equal exact transport along a selected path. Its first jet determines \cref{ibt:eq:path-ode}, while the finite endpoint rule contains additional, nonunique higher-order data.

\section{Curvature and a curved law-dependent example}
\label{ibt:sec:curvature}

\propositionheading{Curvature of the rectangular construction}{ibt:curvature}
For \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a},
\begin{align}
 F^a&=d X\wedge d P_X Q_X
      =d X\wedge(I-P_X)d Q_X\nonumber\\
    &=d X\wedge(I-P_X)d X^T H_X^{-1},
 \label{ibt:eq:curvature}\\
 d H_X+aH_X+H_Xa^T&=0,\qquad
 \Tr a=-\tfrac12d\log\det H_X,\qquad \Tr F^a=0.
 \label{ibt:eq:metric-compatibility}
\end{align}
The connection preserves the fiber metric $H_X^{-1}$. When $r=K$, $P_X=I$ and the curvature vanishes. Redundant columns permit nonzero curvature.
\begin{proof}
Exterior differentiation of $a=-d XQ_X$ gives $d a=d X\wedge d Q_X$. Differentiating $P_X=Q_XX$ and multiplying by $Q_X$ gives $d P_XQ_X=d Q_X+Q_Xd XQ_X$. This proves the first equality after adding $a\wedge a$. Differentiating $P_XQ_X=Q_X$ proves the second. In $d Q_X=d X^TH_X^{-1}+X^Td H_X^{-1}$ the second term is killed by $I-P_X$, giving the last expression. Finally $aH_X=-d XX^T$ and $H_Xa^T=-Xd X^T$, proving metric compatibility. Taking its trace after multiplying by $H_X^{-1}$ gives $2\Tr a=-d\log\det H_X$. The trace of a commutator vanishes, so $\Tr F^a=d\Tr a=0$.
\end{proof}

\corollaryheading{Orthogonal holonomy of the rectangular selector}{ibt:orthogonal-holonomy}
Along every path, the transport of \cref{ibt:eq:path-ode} for \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a} is an isometry between the fiber metrics $H_X^{-1}$ at its endpoints, so every holonomy at $c_0$ lies in the orthogonal group of $H_X(c_0)^{-1}$. On a loop along which $E$ is trivialized, its determinant is $1$. Each curvature value $F^a(Y,Z)$ is skew for $H_X^{-1}$ and therefore has purely imaginary spectrum. The same holds when a message connection $\Gamma$ that preserves the message metric is used in \cref{ibt:eq:message-gamma}.
\begin{proof}
With $M=H_X^{-1}$, \cref{ibt:eq:metric-compatibility} gives $dM=Ma+a^TM$. For $\dot{\mathcal T}=-a(\dot\gamma)\mathcal T$, $\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal T^TM\mathcal T)=\mathcal T^T(-a^TM+dM(\dot\gamma)-Ma)\mathcal T=0$. In a trivialization along the loop, $\det\mathcal T(1)=\exp(-\oint\Tr a)=\exp(\tfrac12\oint d\log\det H_X)=1$. Metric compatibility gives $MF^a+(F^a)^TM=0$, so $M^{1/2}F^aM^{-1/2}$ is skew. A skew $\Gamma$ in \cref{ibt:eq:message-gamma} gives $aH_X+H_Xa^T=-dH_X$ by the same computation.
\end{proof}

Under the structure group $\GL(K)$ the rectangular selector therefore reduces to the $H_X^{-1}$-orthonormal frames and never produces shear or dilation holonomy; noncompact holonomy requires a selector that does not preserve a fiber metric. The curvature formula also shows that $F^a$ depends on $dX$ only through $(I-P_X)dX^T$, which represents the derivative of $c\mapsto W(c)$; $F^a(c)=0$ wherever that map has rank below two.

This projector construction is an instance of induced subbundle geometry: by \cref{ibt:global} it is the pullback along $c\mapsto W(c)$ of the connection that the trivial bundle induces on the tautological subbundle of a Grassmannian, the setting of universal connections \citep{NarasimhanRamanan1961,NarasimhanRamanan1963,MassambaThompson2003}. By \cref{ibt:orthogonal-holonomy} every connection produced by this real rule preserves a fiber metric; whether every metric connection arises for some finite $r$ is the universal-connection question, which is not settled here for this rule. The choice here is how agent laws and directed links supply the columns.

On $\R^2$ with coordinates $(x,y)$ take
\begin{equation}
 X=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&x\\0&1&y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix},\qquad \chi=1+x^2+y^2.
 \label{ibt:eq:witnessX}
\end{equation}
It has rank two everywhere. Substitution gives
\begin{align}
 a&=-\frac{(d v)v^T}{\chi},\label{ibt:eq:witnessA}\\
 F^a&=\frac{1}{\chi^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}-xy&1+x^2\\-(1+y^2)&xy\end{pmatrix}
 d x\wedge d y.
 \label{ibt:eq:witnessF}
\end{align}
At the origin $a=0$ but $F^a_{xy}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0$. Here $W(c)$ is the plane orthogonal to $(v,-1)$, so $c\mapsto W(c)$ is the inverse central projection onto the unit sphere, and the connection is the Levi-Civita connection of $S^2$ pulled back by it.

An explicit realization uses normalized non-Gaussian laws and an invertible link. Let a receiver carry receiving columns $e_1,e_2$. Let a source law be the product Laplace law of unit coordinate variances on $\R^2$, translated to mean $(x,1)^T$. Choose
\begin{equation}
 \bar\Theta_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\y&1\end{pmatrix}
        =\exp(yE_{21}),\qquad \bar\Theta_{ji}=\bar\Theta_{ij}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The linked mean is $(x,1+xy)^T$. Replacing $v$ in \cref{ibt:eq:witnessX} by this vector gives a full-rank, link-dependent matrix. Its curvature is the pullback of \cref{ibt:eq:witnessF} under $(x,y)\mapsto(x,1+xy)$, nonzero wherever $x\ne0$ because the Jacobian determinant is $x$. The labels, receiving axes, column selection, and message metric are declared data, and the law enters only through its mean: any law with mean $(x,1)^T$ gives the same connection. No Gaussian closure or evolving distributional ansatz is used.

Backtracks can affect the gauge class of the connection while the reciprocal agent link stays fixed. Take receiving columns $e_1,e_2$, a source mean $(1,1)^T$ in reference coordinates, and $\Theta_{ij}=\Theta_{ji}=\operatorname{diag}(e^x,e^y)$. Then $N_{ij,j}=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2x},e^{2y})$, $S_{ij,j}=\operatorname{diag}(e^x,e^y)$, and $\Omega_{ij}=I$. The raw linked column $(e^x,e^y)^T$ gives the matrix \cref{ibt:eq:witnessX} with $v=(e^x,e^y)^T$, whose curvature is the pullback of \cref{ibt:eq:witnessF} by a map with Jacobian determinant $e^{x+y}\neq0$, hence nonzero everywhere; at the origin it equals $\frac19\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}dx\wedge dy$. The reciprocal column $\Omega_{ij}(1,1)^T$ is constant and gives $a=0$. Curvature spectra are gauge invariant, so the backtrack factor alone changes the gauge class of the selected connection. This proves that a backtrack contribution can survive the selector, without asserting that every backtrack distinction is recoverable from one connection. In rank one the rectangular selection is always pure gauge, $a=-\tfrac12d\log H_X$; for example, a scalar link $\Theta_{ij}=\Theta_{ji}=\vartheta(c)>0$ with raw column gives $a=-\vartheta d\vartheta/(1+\vartheta^2)$, which differs from the reciprocal selection $a=0$ only by a gauge transformation.

\section{A declared structure group and joint sector data}
\label{ibt:sec:group}

The explicit matrix \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a} need not lie in an arbitrarily chosen subalgebra $\mathfrak g\subset\mathfrak{gl}(K)$. A $\GL(E)$ connection is not automatically a connection on a prescribed $G$-reduction.

Let $M(c)$ be a $C^1$ positive-definite fiber metric with $M'=h^TMh$, and let $X$ have full row rank and \cref{ibt:eq:X-law}, with reference changes $h(c)\in G$. The choice $M=H_X^{-1}$ is available. For $Y\in T_c\mathcal C$, minimize over $\xi\in\mathfrak g$
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_Y(\xi)=\tfrac12\Tr\bigl[M(d X(Y)+\xi X)
                            (d X(Y)+\xi X)^T\bigr].
 \label{ibt:eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
\propositionheading{A selector in the declared Lie algebra}{ibt:group}
For a closed matrix group $G$, \cref{ibt:eq:constrained} has a unique minimizer $a(Y)\in\mathfrak g$, linear in $Y$, $C^1$ in $c$, and satisfying \cref{ibt:eq:affine}. It solves
\begin{equation}
 \Tr[M(a(Y)X)(\eta X)^T]
 =-\Tr[Md X(Y)(\eta X)^T]\quad(\eta\in\mathfrak g).
 \label{ibt:eq:group-normal}
\end{equation}
For $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{gl}(K)$ the solution is \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a}.
\begin{proof}
The Hessian is positive: $\xi X=0$ implies $\xi=0$ after multiplying by $Q_X$. Its matrix in any basis of $\mathfrak g$ is invertible, giving existence, uniqueness and linear, smooth dependence. Under reference rechoice the candidate $\xi'=h^{-1}\xi h+h^{-1}d h(Y)$ belongs to $\mathfrak g$ and $d X'(Y)+\xi'X'=h^{-1}(d X(Y)+\xi X)$. The norm is unchanged; uniqueness gives the affine law. In the unrestricted algebra the normal equation is $(d X+aX)X^T=0$, yielding \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a}. The affine law uses $h^{-1}dh\in\mathfrak g$, that is, $h$ valued in $G$.
\end{proof}

For a proper subalgebra the minimizer depends on $M$, which is then an additional comparison choice. For $G=\mathrm{SL}(K)$ and $M=H_X^{-1}$ the Hessian form is $\Tr(H_X^{-1}\xi H_X\eta^T)$, for which the identity is orthogonal to every traceless $\eta$; the minimizer is therefore the traceless part $a+\tfrac1{2K}(d\log\det H_X)I$ of \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a}, with the same curvature, since the removed part is an exact scalar form.

For a proper subalgebra, the induced-metric and explicit curvature formulas of \cref{ibt:curvature} need not hold for the constrained minimizer. Finite $G$-valued transport follows by \cref{ibt:eq:path-ode} along every path in the regular region with this derived $a$. Belief and model sectors carry representations $\varrho_q,\varrho_s$ of one group. To select one principal connection from both, sum their costs with a declared relative sector weight, replacing $\xi X$ by $d\varrho_a(\xi)X^a$, and minimize over the same $\xi\in\mathfrak g$; the weight changes the selected generator. The required nondegeneracy is
\begin{equation}
 \{\xi:d\varrho_q(\xi)X^q=0,
       \ d\varrho_s(\xi)X^s=0\}=\{0\}.
\end{equation}
The same proof applies to the summed normal equation. Two unrelated sector connections do not automatically arise from one principal connection. The two interaction link fields can remain distinct; equality of the belief and model links is a separate hypothesis.

\section{Direct alignment of contextual link data}
\label{ibt:sec:alignment}

The rectangular construction is sufficient, but a full-rank vector collection is not necessary when the group action on links is itself identifiable and an invariant metric on the link target exists. For compact groups the second condition is automatic; for noncompact groups it is restrictive, as explained after \cref{ibt:alignment}. Let $G$ be a finite-dimensional Lie group and let $\mathcal Z$ be a finite-dimensional Riemannian target of laws, interfaces and links with a smooth isometric left $G$-action and metric $\mathcal H$. Under reference rechoice the target obeys $Z'(c)=h(c)^{-1}\cdot Z(c)$. Write $Z(c)\in\mathcal Z$ and $J_Z\xi=\left.\frac{d}{d t}\right|_0\exp(t\xi)\cdot Z$. Suppose $Z$ is smooth and $J_Z$ is injective on the region considered.

\propositionheading{Local link alignment}{ibt:alignment}
Near each diagonal point there is a unique smooth branch $t(c',c)$ near $e$ of strict local minimizers of
\begin{equation}
 g\longmapsto\tfrac12 d_{\mathcal H}(Z(c'),g\cdot Z(c))^2.
 \label{ibt:eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
It satisfies $t(c,c)=e$, reversal by inversion, and the endpoint law. Its connection solves
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H(J_Za(Y),J_Z\eta)=-\mathcal H(d Z(Y),J_Z\eta).
 \label{ibt:eq:augmented-normal}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Use squared distance inside a normal neighborhood. At a diagonal point the group gradient vanishes and its Hessian is the positive locked form $\mathcal H(J_Z\xi,J_Z\eta)$. The implicit function theorem gives a unique smooth critical branch, whose Hessian remains positive nearby. Expanding $t(c+\delta c,c)=e-a(\delta c)+O(|\delta c|^2)$ gives residual $d Z(Y)+J_Za(Y)$ and hence \cref{ibt:eq:augmented-normal}. Isometry makes a reference rechoice carry the selected branch to $h(c')^{-1}t(c',c)h(c)$. Symmetry of distance and isometry identify the reversed objective at $g^{-1}$ with the original at $g$; local uniqueness proves inversion.
\end{proof}

This is the mechanical connection of the declared target metric \citep{Smale1970}, as in the rotation--shape separation of \citet{LittlejohnReinsch1997}. With $\mathcal Z$ the declared law family, $\mathcal H$ its product Fisher metric, and $h$ replaced by $h^{-1}$ (\cref{ch:contextual-coupling} writes target changes, this chapter reference changes), $t(c',c)$ is the near-identity branch of \cref{ctx:f:ass:branch} and its first jet is the collective connection of \cref{geo:collective}; the new content here is the general target. For a free action the argument of \cref{geo:shape-pullback} shows that the curvature is the pullback of the curvature of the target's mechanical connection by $Z$, so it depends only on the derivative of the shape map $c\mapsto[Z(c)]$ and vanishes where that map has rank below two. The statement concerns the branch continuous from the identity. Infinitesimal identifiability does not exclude distant competing minima; \cref{ctx:f:eq:cusp} is an example of a branch singularity under a free action. On overlaps the local identity branches agree after restriction by uniqueness. Its first jet gives path transport by the preceding ODE argument.

For noncompact $G$ the isometric target is a substantive hypothesis, and injectivity of $J_Z$ does not supply it. If $z$ has stabilizer $G_z$, each element of $G_z$ acts on $T_z\mathcal Z$ by an isometry of $\mathcal H_z$, so the isotropy representation of $G_z$ must have relatively compact image. For $\GL(2)$ acting by conjugation on one link $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ with real $\lambda_1\neq\lambda_2$, the element $\operatorname{diag}(t,1)$ fixes the link and scales the tangent vector $E_{12}$ by $t$, which no positive inner product allows for all $t>0$. Even where the action is free, an invariant metric can fail to exist because the action is not proper. For $\mathrm{SL}(2)$ acting by conjugation on pairs, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)$, $z=(D,I+E_{12})$, $z'=(D,I+E_{21})$, $z_t=\bigl(D,\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1+t^2&1\\t^2&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\bigr)$ and $g_t=\operatorname{diag}(t,1/t)$. Both $z$ and $z'$ have trivial infinitesimal stabilizer, $z_t\to z$ and $g_tz_tg_t^{-1}\to z'$ as $t\to0$, while $g_tzg_t^{-1}\to(D,I)$, a point with a one-dimensional stabilizer. An invariant metric on an invariant open set of free points containing $z$ and $z'$ would give $d(g_tz,z')\leq d(z,z_t)+d(g_tz_tg_t^{-1},z')\to0$, which is impossible. Invariant metrics do exist on invariant open sets where the action is proper \citep{Palais1961}, but none is canonical in general. For the defining representation of $\GL(K)$, scalar matrices fix every conjugation link (\cref{ibt:invisible}), so link-only targets are never identifiable and the link term must be dressed by law or interface data, as in the example metric below. The link-only curved example below accordingly uses a compact group.

The target can retain reciprocal link matrices and backtrack factors, both transforming by conjugation, as well as laws and interfaces. Its invariant metric is an additional comparison choice: it is one smooth metric on the augmented target, including any dependence on the law or interface coordinates of that target. For example on a full-rank matrix region the terms
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(H_X^{-1}\delta X\delta X^T)
 +\sum_e\Tr(H_X^{-1}\delta\bar\Theta_e H_X\delta\bar\Theta_e^T)
\end{equation}
are positive and invariant under $X\mapsto h^{-1}X$, $\bar\Theta_e\mapsto h^{-1}\bar\Theta_eh$. Unlike the rectangular selector, the selector of this augmented metric need not preserve $H_X^{-1}$, so it is not subject to \cref{ibt:orthogonal-holonomy}. If the target retains reciprocal parts of tied links from both sectors, the sector-wise principal roots of \cref{ibt:eq:reciprocal} need not be the images of one element of $G$ when the two representations have different charges; a reciprocal part computed in $G$ and then represented in each sector avoids this. One can include Fisher terms for regular statistical families. An interface or link norm is not automatically the Fisher metric of a normalized statistical experiment.

\subsection{A curved connection from reciprocal links alone}

There is a concrete link-only example for $G=SO(3)$. Identify its Lie algebra with vectors by $\widehat v w=v\times w$, and use the product bi-invariant metric on $SO(3)^2$, normalized by $\langle\widehat v,\widehat w\rangle=v\cdot w$. On $(x,y)\in(0,\pi)^2$, fix $\beta\in(0,\pi)$ and set
\begin{equation}
 Q_1=R_x(x),\qquad Q_2=R_z(y)R_x(\beta)R_z(-y).
 \label{ibt:eq:SO3-links}
\end{equation}
These can be the two edges of a three-agent star, with the reverse links set to their inverses. All agent backtracks are the identity. Under reference change the pair transforms by simultaneous conjugation.

Let $\mathsf C_r=I-\operatorname{Ad}_{Q_r}$ and $V_r=d Q_r Q_r^{-1}$. The right-trivialized infinitesimal conjugation is $\mathsf C_r\xi$, so \cref{ibt:eq:augmented-normal} reads
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak k a=-\sum_{r=1}^2 \mathsf C_r^*V_r,\qquad
 \mathfrak k=\sum_{r=1}^2 \mathsf C_r^*\mathsf C_r.
 \label{ibt:eq:SO3-normal}
\end{equation}
For a rotation through angle $\alpha$ about unit axis $n$,
$(I-Q)^T(I-Q)=4\sin^2(\alpha/2)(I-nn^T)$. Here the axes are $e_1$ and $n(y)=(\cos y,\sin y,0)^T$, which are nonparallel on the stated region. Thus $\mathfrak k$ is positive definite. Write $w_x=4\sin^2(x/2)$ and $w_\beta=4\sin^2(\beta/2)$. The identities
$V_1=\widehat e_1d x$ and $V_2=\mathsf C_2\widehat e_3d y$ imply $\mathsf C_1^*V_1=0$ and $\mathsf C_2^*V_2=w_\beta\widehat e_3d y$. Since $\mathfrak k\widehat e_3=(w_x+w_\beta)\widehat e_3$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
 a=-\frac{w_\beta}{w_x+w_\beta}\widehat e_3d y,\qquad
 F^a=\frac{2w_\beta\sin x}{(w_x+w_\beta)^2}\widehat e_3d x\wedge d y\ne0.
 \label{ibt:eq:SO3-curvature}
\end{equation}
The wedge square vanishes because $a$ has only one base component. This proves nonzero curvature from smoothly varying reciprocal agent links and a declared bi-invariant comparison metric, without adding directional generators or a spatial connection as input. The connection and its curvature take values in the line spanned by $\widehat e_3$, the common normal of the two axes, so the connection reduces to the rotations about $e_3$ and its holonomy is Abelian; the example exhibits no non-Abelian contextual holonomy. Normalized rotationally invariant agent laws may be attached with a product metric on laws and links; their orbit terms are zero and do not alter this example. The axes are selected by the links, not by those invariant laws.

\section{What pointwise links do not determine}

\propositionheading{An invisible gauge subgroup}{ibt:invisible}
Suppose the only input fields are reference links and their contextual derivatives, with links transforming by simultaneous conjugation. If a nonzero $\zeta\in\mathfrak g$ commutes with every admitted link field, then no full connection selector from these inputs alone can obey \cref{ibt:eq:affine} for every smooth reference change $h(c)=\exp(f(c)\zeta)$.
\begin{proof}
Every link field is unchanged as a function of $c$, so all its derivatives are also unchanged. A selector of those data returns the same $a$. Choose a point $c_0$ and $f$ with $f(c_0)=0$ and $d f(c_0)\ne0$. At that point the affine law requires $a'=a+\zeta d f$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is pointwise for selectors that read only values and first derivatives of the links, as the normal equation \cref{ibt:eq:augmented-normal} does. If a nonzero $\zeta$ commutes with every link value $\bar\Theta_e(c_0)$ at one point, take $h=\exp(f\zeta)$ with $f(c_0)=0$ and $df(c_0)\neq0$. Then $\bar\Theta_e'(c_0)=\bar\Theta_e(c_0)$ and $d\bar\Theta_e'(c_0)=d\bar\Theta_e(c_0)+[\bar\Theta_e(c_0),\zeta]df(c_0)=d\bar\Theta_e(c_0)$, while the affine law requires $a'(c_0)=a(c_0)+\zeta df(c_0)$. No such selector is covariant at a point of nonzero common infinitesimal stabilizer.

For group-valued links the Lie algebra of the center is always invisible to conjugation. For arbitrary endomorphism-valued links, the relevant ambiguity is their actual common commutant within the represented group; a central group element need not commute with arbitrary maps between inequivalent representation blocks. In the defining $\GL(K)$ representation scalar matrices are invisible to every conjugation link. An Abelian group acting on its own group-valued links is entirely invisible. The $SO(3)$ example avoids this obstruction because the two nonparallel axes have zero common infinitesimal stabilizer.

Laws, labeled vectors, or interfaces can resolve an ambiguity if their combined infinitesimal action is identifiable. Full row rank of $X$ is sufficient in the fundamental representation. It is not implied by many links: centered invariant laws and deficient interfaces can fail it.

Even after identifiability is supplied, several covariant comparison rules can select different connections. Column sets, weights, message metrics, augmented target metrics, the auxiliary fiber metric $M$ of a proper subalgebra, and the relative sector weight affect the selector. Gauge covariance does not choose these data. A finite link collection at one context supplies no contextual derivative; the constructions require a smooth contextual field of such data.

\section{Message coordinates, dynamics, and remaining scope}
\label{ibt:sec:message-connection}

The fixed message space assumption has an intrinsic formulation. If $X$ maps a message bundle into $E$ and the message bundle has connection $\Gamma$, use
\begin{equation}
 a=-(d X-X\Gamma)Q_X.
 \label{ibt:eq:message-gamma}
\end{equation}
For a change $w'=\Lambda(c)w$ of message coordinates, $X'=X\Lambda^{-1}$, $\Gamma'=\Lambda\Gamma\Lambda^{-1}-d\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}$ and $Q_X'=\Lambda Q_X$, provided the message metric is transformed as well. Hence $d X'-X'\Gamma'=(d X-X\Gamma)\Lambda^{-1}$, and \cref{ibt:eq:message-gamma} is unchanged. Fixed labeled coordinates with $\Gamma=0$ give \cref{ibt:eq:derived-a}. Resetting $\Gamma$ to zero after a context-dependent message re-expression changes the model. A supplied nonflat message connection can contribute additional curvature beyond \cref{ibt:eq:curvature}.

The contextual laws, rows, interfaces and links remain supplied fields; no evolution theorem is implied. If the selected connection is inserted into a contextual energy, its complete constitutive dependence must be varied. An envelope simplification requires stationarity for that same energy. Agreement with a law-Fisher selector requires matching its target, action, metric and inputs; the shared affine law alone transfers no Fisher-flow theorem.

Dynamic selection of the comparison rule, preservation or recovery of rank, singular continuation, and inheritance of the link sector remain open. The base, its dimension and topology are supplied; no formation result follows.
