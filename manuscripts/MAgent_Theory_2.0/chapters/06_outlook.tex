\chapter{The Remaining Formation Problem}
\label{ch:outlook}

The constructions describe agents that compare laws, receive internal records, and inherit descriptions across scales. The quantifiers in \cref{inh:recursive-formation} distinguish a supplied partition from a selected one, a transient block from a persistent one, and agreement of one record law from agreement of subsequent responses. An exact autonomous trajectory is only one requirement.

\Cref{ch:fusion} supplies two stages of finite-time equality through a nonsmooth square-root-KL interaction. Local models, Fisher metrics, contact multiplicities, and fresh-response kernels are inherited on its restricted initial class. This changes the interaction and does not establish formation under the smooth potential of \cref{ch:dynamics}.

\Cref{ch:smooth-closure} gives general-law reductions under specified conditional reconstruction channels. Log-density spectra retain the hidden information entering the marginal force; record marks complete the state for declared feedback. Equal spectra allow distinct internal conditionals while public marginals follow the coarse Fisher flow. That equality is an invariant preparation.

\Cref{ch:smooth-erasure} lets an internal gate enter a prescribed flat channel region, after which private laws can be discarded. Source preferences change into persistent pairs with distinct public states. This finite-product example supplies the erasing architecture and retains every public child role. Its two invariant public regions do not merge again.

\Cref{ch:public-compression} removes continuous public differences through an exact collective-phase projection of the original smooth flow. It uses general bounded log densities near a common reference and no erasure gate. The fine children remain unequal at finite times, but a fresh uniform-child sample distinguishes states with the same phase parent. Unrestricted identity-channel Gibbs responses identify every distinct exposed child law. Restricted responses may admit compression, but their incoming forces must also close.

The unresolved problem is a dynamically selected family of interfaces for which public compression preserves both evolution and response processes, and remains compatible with a subsequent joining of parents. Missing information can concern conditional dependence, the response to an external law, or memory of a reused child. It need not belong to a predetermined distribution family.

Deriving a base geometry also remains separate. State-dependent interactions at one context do not specify compatible transports between nearby contexts. A curved connection requires that additional structure; a common geometry across agents requires compatibility between their structures.
