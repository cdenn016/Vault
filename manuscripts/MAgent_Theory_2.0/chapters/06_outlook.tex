\chapter{Beyond the Existence Constructions}
\label{ch:outlook}

The communication and link constructions of \cref{ch:communication-links,ch:link-dynamics,ch:curvature-hierarchy} add a declared observation model to the dictionary sector. General-law reconstruction and identifiability statements distinguish a code, its stabilizer, and the receiver's conditional reconstruction; identification of dictionaries modulo stabilizers assumes a known code (\cref{it1:identifiability}). The normalized tangent prior and Gaussian recognition formulas give a bounded model of learning those dictionaries. The global, plug-in per-agent, law-level per-agent, and reciprocal calculations retain different forces and metrics; equality of one derivative does not identify their flows. The per-agent link model of \cref{it2:per-agent-model} is a record-likelihood model, distinct from the mixture-source per-agent model of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent}, so the manuscript contains two per-agent constructions that are not yet related. The induced curvature cost is a quadratic cost of an affine coded residual and includes its record-dependent linear term; its cohomological part is stated in the small-noise limit at fixed anchors and, within the tangent model, in the weak-anchor limit, where the omitted log-determinant dominates outside that model (\cref{it3:weak-anchor-determinant}). Rigid-cluster calculations assume a supplied partition and do not establish its dynamical formation. The Gaussian tangent extension in \cref{it2:coupled-flow,it2:coupled-global} includes joint covariance and source-row evolution with global forward existence under fixed proper references and convergence of each trajectory to one stationary point. Its three-agent example has, for its reference data, the local subcritical pitchfork of \cref{it2:critical-pitchfork}, with nearby asymmetric saddles; the sign of the quartic coefficient depends on those data. Nonlinear dictionary learning, distant branches and destinations on general graphs, unrestricted law-space dynamics, and inheritance of a learned link sector remain open.

The theory distinguishes three objectives for interacting agents. In the global picture one normalized joint covers the contents and records of every agent, and local updates are coordinate descent of one free energy (\cref{prop:dyn-global-local}). In the per-agent picture each agent has its own generative model in which its own evaluator and its peers' communicated laws compete as sources of prior information (\cref{prop:dyn-per-agent}); with fixed channels its forces agree with the mean-field block forces of a global model on an open set only when every attended peer channel is constant in its input (\cref{prop:dyn-per-agent-not-global}). Under Gaussian references, affine evaluators, linear-Gaussian channels, and log-quadratic records both free-energy flows are tangent to the Gaussian product family (\cref{prop:dyn-gaussian-closure}), whereas the reciprocal flow is not (\cref{prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance}). The reciprocal law potential is a third object. With fixed positive rows on finite alphabets, \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} shows that it is not an affine function of the free energy of any finite normalized model under any recognition rule when an attended edge is an identity channel, and that with fixed input-dependent channels it is not, on any open set, an affine function of the free energy of a normalized model with product recognition by the agent laws. \Cref{rem:dyn-no-global-scope} records exact identifications outside these hypotheses: constant-output channels, countable auxiliary latents, and coupled recognition on atomless spaces. Whether the obstruction extends to the Gibbs-reduced potential, to law-dependent channels, or to declared charts is open. Every construction of \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation} concerns the reciprocal potential or, in \cref{ch:fusion}, a nonsmooth modification of it. \Cref{ch:per-agent-inheritance,ch:collective-local-parent} concern the normalized per-agent free energies under their Gibbs-reduced dynamics. No construction states a selection result for the global free energy, and no objective is declared primary; which objective carries the formation program, and whether any conclusion transfers between objectives, is part of \cref{op:dyn-three-pictures}.

\Cref{app:interacting-memory} supplies a different composition: exact correlated inference under a common preparation, private records, and a known posterior-sampling communication protocol. Its joint private-record posterior is a product tilt of the fixed preparation coupling (\cref{imem:product-tilt}): every message multiplies the factor of its speaker, the cross-agent dependence of the coupling never changes, and with the preparation fixed the two private-record marginals determine the posterior. Together with the fixed conditional reconstruction it preserves the declared future messages; complete local beliefs alone need not do so. The affine singleton-presentation Gaussian case closes, whereas multiple presentations need not remain a finite Gaussian mixture. Smaller sufficient representations, new private measurements over time, adaptation of the common model, and interactions between agents using different models remain open. None of those extensions follows merely from composing normalized local evaluators. The generative models of \cref{ch:foundations} contain no action, policy, or learning rule, so every free energy in the manuscript is perceptual; agents that choose their queries or adapt their models are a further construction.

Measured against the formation standard \cref{inh:recursive-formation}, with the nulls and the frozen-environment control of \cref{inh:null-flow} and the counterfactual class of \cref{inh:counterfactual-class}, no construction in this manuscript is proved to satisfy every clause. The calibration example \cref{inh:anchored-consensus} passes interaction necessity and fails the counterfactual clause. The nonsmooth fusion construction of \cref{ch:fusion} is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance: its fused children are identical, so compression fails; it meets interaction necessity only through its exact-equality selector, since on the common-offset family its partition sequence is the normalized-centroid linkage of the initial data for every coupling (\cref{fus:ncl}) and a threshold version of the selector gives the same sequence along the nulls; and its second stage forms from every counterfactual handoff. The gated erasure construction of \cref{ch:smooth-erasure} is a single-stage selection with exact inheritance whose interaction-necessity verdict is not determined, since the proved results fix only its first and last partitions (\cref{sec:erase-status}). The Wishart construction of \cref{ch:conditional-formation} is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance: its membership schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the initial belief shapes at every coupling, the renormalized null and the frozen-environment control reproduce it, the label-preserving null has agent-dependent clocks already at zero coupling (\cref{form:lp-null}) and integrations indicate that it changes the schedule on an open set, so the verdict of interaction necessity is split and the clause fails, and the union forms from every counterfactual handoff. The six-agent construction of \cref{ch:open-formation} admits a full open neighborhood of positive laws and inherits a moving-representative flow, but its theorem establishes neither interaction necessity nor counterfactual dependence; integrations of its limit field indicate that the null flow forms the same blocks (\cref{open:null-control}), its block relations are transient, the selector eventually merges the exterior pair (\cref{open:final-merge}), and its handoff loss is created by the response interface rather than by the selection. It is not shown to be a recursive formation. The angular per-agent construction of \cref{ch:per-agent-inheritance} and the collective construction of \cref{ch:collective-local-parent} are single-stage selections with exact inheritance whose retentions fail compression. In \cref{ch:per-agent-inheritance} the persistent grouping passes interaction necessity against both nulls only for ratio thresholds in $(1/8,1/3)$, the frozen-environment control reproduces it at every such threshold, and the four-state and persistent-record groupings carry no compressive parent. The retention constructions of \cref{ch:smooth-closure,ch:public-compression,ch:boundary-closure} make no selection claim, and the smooth constructions supply their compression architectures. Their quotients exist before membership occurs, so the dynamics selects when a description is used without creating a new operational equivalence. Every exact compression realized here erases data that no agent, channel, or energy term reads, and no mechanism is given by which an interaction creates an exact symmetry or decoupling; a graded notion of inheritance measured by the exact defect functionals of the inheritance chapter is not developed.

The supplied-architecture results retain their scopes. The profile hierarchy of \cref{ch:boundary-closure} is an equivariant symmetry reduction with a supplied tree; the data it discards are invisible to the fine energy, metric, and admitted records. Its two nulls coincide and preserve the same family, descriptors, and descended interface, so the exact hierarchy is a property of the prepared family and its symmetry rather than of the interaction. The collective phase of \cref{ch:public-compression} is an asymptotic-phase projection that loses ordinary child sampling. The erasure construction of \cref{ch:smooth-erasure} keeps public child states and its heterogeneity is transient. The per-agent parent of \cref{pa:phase-parent} preserves complete admitted histories and persistent records, while \cref{pa:geometry-inheritance,pa:nonflat-window} preserve the actual collective geometry on sections with context-constant discarded phases. Its phase quotient is supplied before grouping, and the phase is gauge relative to every algebra the chapter declares. Its grouping holds against both nulls for ratio thresholds in $(1/8,1/3)$ and fails above $1/3$ (\cref{pa:selector-threshold}). Adding a future hidden-angle probe destroys that quotient, and joining already canonical parents erases no new coordinate. Response sufficiency depends on the interface: an unrestricted identity-channel Gibbs interface identifies distinct exposed laws, and child-specific probes, exact constituent-law records, or feedback can require discarded distinctions.

The collective alternative of \cref{ch:collective-local-parent} exposes every proper block through fixed relative-angle probes and permits one common orientation, gauge relative to every declared algebra, to be erased only by the whole-block retention. Its reference-memory realization closes a local two-jet in the original time and preserves the actual normalized-law Fisher cost. A finite preparation generator yields a complete conditional whole-patch realization, with the full chronological record retained. The causal accumulator of \cref{lp:causal-flow} instead integrates the local angular velocity from the actual preparation time. It preserves the original native dynamics and every admitted joint history, and \cref{lp:causal-coherence} proves actual geometric inheritance under initial collective-phase coherence, a sufficient condition that is not shown to be necessary. That condition is not generated by the memory. \Cref{lp:initial-phase-datum} identifies the missing initial contextual phase relation for arbitrary preparations. \Cref{lp:path-memory-obstruction,lp:causal-jet-obstruction} exclude order-zero rotation-invariant value-path retentions and smooth bounded-jet causal memories on the class of \cref{lp:rich-class}, in addition to the finite-order and finite global-encoding obstructions. They refer to the declared marked structure group; with the common rotation added to the group they lose their force (\cref{lp:group-dependence}). They leave the unrestricted causal-local-parent question inconclusive (\cref{lp:local-parent-open}). More general admissible memory mechanisms, interaction-created invisibility and a new second compressive handoff remain unresolved.

The remaining problems are more specific than an existence question. \Cref{inh:formation-open} asks for a construction that meets every clause of the standard with a derived selector, and whether type closure and an action of the parent on its children should be required. The per-agent example meets interaction necessity at one stage for a range of selector constants, but still needs a compressive retention of a distinction that some declared probe resolves, a selection that the frozen-environment control does not reproduce, and a second compressive handoff with counterfactual dependence. Under the reciprocal potential with shared targets and additive moment channels, every pairwise law comparison relaxes on a common clock (\cref{inh:common-clock}) and selectors invariant under common tilts fail interaction necessity (\cref{inh:common-tilt}); interaction-sensitive selection read by such selectors requires that a hypothesis of \cref{inh:common-clock} fail, while selectors that threshold a divergence between agent laws can pass. \Cref{op:dyn-three-pictures} asks how the three objectives are related and which conclusions survive a change of objective. Within the formation constructions the gauge structure supplies the orbits that a parent forgets and does no dynamical work, and the connection remains diagnostic. \Cref{ch:contextual-coupling} separately gives it a dynamical role through auxiliary alignment: the finite graph has a regular-branch Fisher ODE whose reduced cost is the minimized cost only while the branch stays globally minimizing (\cref{ctx:f:global-branch}), and a flat periodic base has a locally well-posed shape PDE coupled to frame and source-row ODEs. After elimination no locally gauge-invariant contextual energy diffuses frames; independent smoothing of rows, frames, and communication links, which would need a declared row or link energy, global continuation, and graph refinement remain open. \Cref{ch:link-base-transport} supplies displacement maps and their connection from regular contextual agent data with declared comparison rules, including a curved reciprocal-link example; the rectangular selector has holonomy orthogonal for its induced metric (\cref{ibt:orthogonal-holonomy}). The message or target metric remains a declared comparison rule. Selecting that rule, continuing across rank or orbit degeneracy, and proving dynamical or parent inheritance of the resulting link sector remain open. Full permutation symmetry still constrains deterministic law-only selection as in \cref{inh:selector-symmetry}. Persistent membership under the fixed per-agent record episode does not extend its geometric theorem beyond the declared finite window. None of the results establishes an indefinite spacetime metric, a thermodynamic limit, typicality under random initialization, or unbounded recursive formation.
