\chapter{Beyond the Existence Constructions}
\label{ch:outlook}

Two constructions satisfy the formation obligation in \cref{inh:recursive-formation} under different assumptions within the original smooth KL model. \Cref{ch:conditional-formation} uses common-precision matrix laws, global moment channels, and addition, with original-time parent Fisher dynamics. \Cref{ch:open-formation} admits independently variable positive laws in a full open neighborhood. Native routing selects pairs and then a pair of parents, while an exterior parent remains. Physical orientation loss, inherited dynamics, admitted histories, and curved geometry hold over the same finite horizon.

The open-law construction uses a fixed bit channel, whole-block angular responses, contextual profiles, and primitive ports. Its autonomous flow includes a moving-representative correction; its statistical Fisher metric differs from the kinetic quotient metric. A fixed anchor selects one partition for the contextual patch, with local phase charts.

Both architectures have sufficient quotients before membership occurs. Dynamics selects when those descriptions are used; it does not create a new operational equivalence. Thresholds are declared constitutive choices. A nonempty open class does not establish typicality under random initialization, and finite persistence and curvature do not imply indefinite depth or a uniform infinite-time bound.

Response sufficiency depends on the interface. An unrestricted identity-channel Gibbs interface identifies distinct exposed laws; the finite binary inverse in \cref{ch:open-formation} demonstrates this obstruction. Child-specific probes, exact constituent-law records, and feedback can require discarded distinctions. Extending an interface requires a new joint-kernel and update calculation.

Initial score sufficiency need not propagate: incoming peer forces can generate hidden contextual scores, with the quadratic loss in \cref{open:generated-loss}. The anchored representation preserves the entire minimizing cost on its declared sections. It does not preserve every state tangent. Pointwise centering, context-dependent handoff times, and global phase roots require separate arguments.

The earlier constructions retain their scopes. The profile hierarchy in \cref{ch:boundary-closure} uses identity channels and a supplied tree. The collective phase in \cref{ch:public-compression} loses ordinary child sampling. \Cref{ch:smooth-erasure} keeps public child states, and \cref{ch:fusion} changes the interaction to a nonsmooth one.

The remaining formation problem concerns the selection of sufficient architectures and interfaces from broader physical conditions, the prevalence and stability of their organizing regimes, and recursive continuation beyond a fixed finite construction. Full permutation symmetry still constrains deterministic law-only selection as in \cref{inh:selector-symmetry}. None of the results establishes an indefinite spacetime metric, a thermodynamic limit, or unbounded recursive formation.
