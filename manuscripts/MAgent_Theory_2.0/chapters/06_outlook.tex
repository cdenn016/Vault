\chapter{Formation Beyond the Prepared Examples}
\label{ch:outlook}

The formation obligation in \cref{inh:recursive-formation} combines selected persistent membership with inherited evolution and responses. \Cref{ch:conditional-formation} realizes it conditionally under the original smooth KL potential. Four matrix-law agents form two parents and then their union through one retained-state rule. The full channel derivative, additive parent laws, original-time Fisher evolution, admitted adaptive response histories, and collective contextual geometry are proved together.

Its assumptions identify what remains to be derived. Common precision is prepared, and unequal precisions do not first agree at finite time in this model. The normalized channels read a global moment; they do not create preferential source groups. The threshold is fixed constitutive data, and every additive quotient exists before its selection event. The construction therefore establishes state-selected use of sufficient parent descriptions, not the first appearance of new operational redundancy.

The response class is also substantive. Fresh extensive queries and readouts of their full joint vector descend. Child-specific probes, reused hidden children, or Bayesian interventions can require distinctions that the parent discards. A broader formation mechanism must specify its actual interface and show that the resulting conditional kernels and updates close, rather than infer this from a static sum law.

The geometry in \cref{form:geometry} is inherited because all contextual and orbit scores are measurable from the sums, and the infinitesimal stabilizers agree. Discarded shape allocations are constant over context. Allowing those allocations to vary can give positive fine contextual length with zero parent length. Formation with inherited geometry from less restricted contextual families requires satisfying the corresponding score and connection conditions, not merely Fisher contraction.

The preceding constructions retain their separate scopes. The profile hierarchy in \cref{ch:boundary-closure} uses identity channels and a supplied tree, with a different response protocol and no Fisher tangent for its discarded phases. The phase reduction in \cref{ch:public-compression} loses ordinary child sampling. The erasure example in \cref{ch:smooth-erasure} keeps every public child state, and \cref{ch:fusion} changes the interaction to a nonsmooth one. Their conclusions do not transfer to one another without checking the specified laws, channels, and retentions.

The outstanding problem is to obtain persistent sufficient organization from less structured preparations, with a justified membership criterion and an appropriate interaction architecture. Full permutation symmetry still constrains law-only deterministic selection as in \cref{inh:selector-symmetry}. None of the finite constructions establishes an indefinite spacetime metric, a thermodynamic limit, or unbounded recursive formation.
