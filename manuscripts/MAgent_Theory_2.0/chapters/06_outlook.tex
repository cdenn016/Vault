\chapter{Beyond the Existence Constructions}
\label{ch:outlook}

The theory now distinguishes three objectives for interacting agents. In the global picture one normalized joint covers the contents and records of every agent, and local updates are coordinate descent of one free energy (\cref{prop:dyn-global-local}). In the per-agent picture each agent has its own generative model in which its own evaluator and its peers' communicated laws compete as sources of prior information (\cref{prop:dyn-per-agent}). The reciprocal law potential is a third object, and \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} shows that it is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws. Every construction of \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation} concerns the reciprocal potential or, in \cref{ch:fusion}, a nonsmooth modification of it. Whether their conclusions hold under the per-agent dynamics or a global free energy is part of \cref{op:dyn-three-pictures}.

Measured against the formation standard \cref{inh:recursive-formation}, no construction in this manuscript is yet a recursive formation under the smooth potential. The Wishart construction of \cref{ch:conditional-formation} selects two parents and then their union with exact original-time inheritance, but its membership schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the initial belief shapes and does not depend on the coupling; it is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance. The six-agent construction of \cref{ch:open-formation} admits a full open neighborhood of positive laws and inherits a moving-representative flow, but in the regime of its proof the null flow reproduces its membership history, and its handoff loss is created by the response interface rather than by the selection. Both smooth constructions supply their compression architectures. Their quotients exist before membership occurs, so the dynamics selects when a description is used without creating a new operational equivalence. The nonsmooth fusion construction of \cref{ch:fusion} meets interaction necessity at the cost of changing the interaction, and its fused parents compress nothing. Every construction is therefore a single-stage or two-stage selection with exact inheritance, and each chapter states which clauses of the standard fail.

The supplied-architecture results retain their scopes. The profile hierarchy of \cref{ch:boundary-closure} is an equivariant symmetry reduction with a supplied tree; the data it discards are invisible to the fine energy, metric, and admitted records. The collective phase of \cref{ch:public-compression} is an asymptotic-phase projection that loses ordinary child sampling. The erasure construction of \cref{ch:smooth-erasure} keeps public child states and its heterogeneity is transient. Response sufficiency depends on the interface: an unrestricted identity-channel Gibbs interface identifies distinct exposed laws, and child-specific probes, exact constituent-law records, or feedback can require discarded distinctions.

The remaining problems are therefore sharper than an existence question. \Cref{inh:formation-open} asks for a construction that meets every clause of the standard, including interaction necessity and compression, with a derived rather than declared selector and interface. Under the reciprocal potential, interaction-sensitive selection requires receiver-dependent targets, since shared references with additive moment channels make every pairwise law comparison relax on a common clock. \Cref{op:dyn-three-pictures} asks how the three objectives are related and which conclusions survive the change from the reciprocal flow to free-energy dynamics. The connection of \cref{ch:geometry} does dynamical work only where a vector field, row, channel, or record depends on it; in the present constructions it is a diagnostic of the evolved sections, and giving it a dynamical role is open. Full permutation symmetry still constrains deterministic law-only selection as in \cref{inh:selector-symmetry}. None of the results establishes an indefinite spacetime metric, a thermodynamic limit, typicality under random initialization, persistence beyond finite windows, or unbounded recursive formation.
