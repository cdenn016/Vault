\chapter{The Remaining Formation Problem}
\label{ch:outlook}

The formation problem in \cref{inh:recursive-formation} requires the same population dynamics to select persistent memberships and give their parents enough state to inherit subsequent evolution and responses. An exact autonomous collective trajectory satisfies only part of that requirement.

\Cref{ch:fusion} supplies two inherited formation stages under a nonsmooth square-root-KL interaction. Its altered interaction and restricted initial class distinguish that result from formation under the smooth potential of \cref{ch:dynamics}.

For the original smooth dynamics, \cref{ch:smooth-closure} closes conditional forces through spectra under specified reconstruction channels and preparations. \Cref{ch:smooth-erasure} discards private laws through an engineered gate while retaining every public child role. \Cref{ch:public-compression} compresses public differences through a collective phase, but loses ordinary child sampling. Their distinct assumptions identify which state or interface information remains to be obtained dynamically.

\Cref{ch:boundary-closure} preserves the original coupled identity-channel dynamics, fresh uniform-descendant responses, and common record conditioning at arbitrary finite binary depth. It retains a nested amplitude tree while discarding public partition fields on a supplied invariant family. Obtaining such admissible organization from less structured preparations remains open. Statistical independence partitions do not by themselves select agents; \cref{inh:selector-symmetry} also states the limitation of a law-only deterministic selector under full permutation symmetry.

The pullback in \cref{ch:geometry} gives contextual geometry on a regular statistical family with a compatible connection. Collective minimization specifies a connection class relative to declared comparisons and weights. Its stabilizer components, actual aggregate rank, and persistence require the corresponding additional hypotheses.

Geometric inheritance requires the mixed orthogonality in \cref{geo:retained-connection}, beyond Fisher contraction. The profile-tree example has a different measurable scope: its sample coordinates are not contextual coordinates, and its discarded phases have no Fisher tangent. The next geometric construction must supply a smooth parent family with compatible dynamics, responses, and connections, then establish whether the interaction dynamics selects and maintains that organization.
