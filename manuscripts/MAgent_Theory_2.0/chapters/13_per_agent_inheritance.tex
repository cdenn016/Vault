\chapter{Per-Agent Grouping with Persistent Records}
\label{ch:per-agent-inheritance}

This chapter concerns the per-agent objective, the normalized per-agent model of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent} under its Gibbs-reduced dynamics, rather than the reciprocal potential used in \cref{ch:fusion} to \cref{ch:open-formation}. Each agent changes its own recognition laws while treating the communicated laws of its peers as current model data. Records remain in its free energy. The first construction uses a four-state channel and exhibits a grouping on a finite window that neither null of \cref{inh:null-flow} selects. A second construction gives persistent grouping against a conflicting record. Its verdict under interaction necessity depends on the selector constant: it holds for every same-sign ratio threshold in $(1/8,1/3)$ and fails for every threshold in $(1/3,1)$, and the frozen-environment control reproduces the selected sequence. A third construction adds an angular sector and a parent that quotients a hidden phase, with exact original-time dynamics, admitted response histories, and nonflat collective geometry on a declared family. That phase is gauge relative to every algebra the chapter declares, so the parent fails compression (F4). Its interface is supplied before selection, and no second compressive handoff is established. The angular construction is a single-stage selection with exact inheritance, the other two have no compressive parent, and none is a recursive formation.

\section{What changes under an own-objective derivative}

All source priors below are positive and normalized. Source rows are minimized exactly at unit temperature at every state: the dynamics is the Gibbs-reduced per-agent dynamics of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent-flow}, not its finite-mobility row evolution. The two share equilibria but not transients, and no transfer of stability between the two row conventions is proved for the non-gradient per-agent field. On the circle spaces below the dynamics is the chart version of the same definition, whose well-posedness is checked where it is used. At a fixed configuration let $D^q_{ii}=\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)$ and $D^s_{ii}=\KL(s_i\Vert r_i)$, with the nonself costs defined by the communicated laws. These self costs are the evaluator and reference divergences of the per-agent model, the costs written $D_{ii}$ in \cref{inh:null-self-row}; they are not the identity-channel self divergences of the reciprocal potential, which vanish. Then
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{ij}=\frac{\pi^q_{ij}e^{-D^q_{ij}}}{\sum_k\pi^q_{ik}e^{-D^q_{ik}}},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{ij}=\frac{\pi^s_{ij}e^{-D^s_{ij}}}{\sum_k\pi^s_{ik}e^{-D^s_{ik}}}.
 \label{pa:rows}
\end{equation}
The envelope derivative is the row-weighted derivative of the costs. It does not differentiate the optimized row again. Each law follows its own agent's Fisher field, so derivatives of another agent's free energy are absent. The probability-coordinate form is the replicator equation associated with the Shahshahani metric \citep{Shahshahani1979}; the construction here concerns its particular normalized sources, records, and exact inheritance.

Every peer cost below has a compared law that depends on the sender's law, so \cref{inh:null-flow} deletes all of them. The label-preserving null (N1) keeps their labels and priors. If $a=\pi^q_{ii}$, its content self row is the instance
\begin{equation}
 \beta^0_{ii}=\frac{a e^{-D^q_{ii}}}{a e^{-D^q_{ii}}+1-a}
 \label{pa:null-row}
\end{equation}
of \cref{inh:null-self-row}. The model row has the same form. The renormalized null (N2) removes the deleted labels, so both self weights equal one. Since every self cost here is nonzero, the two nulls differ, and each (F5) verdict below is stated against both. The frozen-environment control (N3) holds every peer law at its initial value in every cost, row, and channel. Every example uses the same records, selector, and initial state along the full flow and along all three controls, and the selector reads public laws only, so the readout convention of \cref{inh:null-flow} is met trivially.

\subsection{A conditional four-state channel}

Let $k=(a,x)$ and $m=(b,u)$ range over $\{-1,1\}^2$ with counting measure, and let $q_i,s_i$ be arbitrary positive normalized tables. Fix $J>1$ and $0<\alpha,\kappa<1$. Take
\begin{equation}
 r(b,u)=\frac14,\qquad
 G((b,u),(a,x))=\frac{e^{Jab}}{4\cosh J}.
 \label{pa:four-evaluator}
\end{equation}
For a positive table $p$, define $\mu_p(x)=\sum_a p(a,x)$ and $L_p(b\mid x)=p(b,x)/\mu_p(x)$. The nonself kernel is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 W_b(y\mid x)&=\frac{1+\kappa bxy}{2},\\
 K[p]((a,x),(b,y))
 &=\bigl[\alpha\mathbf1_{\{a=b\}}+(1-\alpha)L_p(b\mid x)\bigr]W_b(y\mid x).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:four-channel}
\end{equation}
Each row sums to one and is positive. The first operation has determinant $\alpha$ on each conditional two-state block, and the second has determinant $\kappa b$ on each content block, so $K[p]$ is invertible. Nevertheless its compared output simplifies exactly:
\begin{equation}
 (pK[p])(b,y)=\sum_xp(b,x)W_b(y\mid x)=:(pW)(b,y),
 \qquad D(pK[p])[v]=vW.
 \label{pa:four-substitution}
\end{equation}
The first identity follows by summing over $a$; its derivative includes the channel derivative. No loss of channel rank or frozen derivative is used. In the per-agent dynamics an own-block variation holds the sender fixed, and the receiver sees the sender only through $pK[p]=pW$, so the law-dependent part of $K[p]$ is inert here: every result below holds verbatim with the fixed channel $W$. The channel derivative would enter only the source-role forces of the reciprocal potential, which the per-agent dynamics omits.

Let the homogeneous self priors be $\rho_q,\rho_s\in(0,1)$ and divide each remaining prior equally over the $N-1$ peers. Write $m_i=\E_{q_i}a$, $n_i=\E_{s_i}b$, $\nu_j^q=q_jW$, and $\nu_j^s=s_jW$. One retained record $(o_q,o_s)\in\{-1,1\}^2$ has normalized likelihood
\begin{equation}
 \ell(o_q,o_s\mid a,x,b,u)
 =\frac{e^{h_qo_qa}}{2\cosh h_q}
  \frac{e^{h_so_sb}}{2\cosh h_s},
 \qquad h_q,h_s>0.
 \label{pa:four-record}
\end{equation}
For finitely many retained records put $H_i^q=\sum_t h_{q,t}o_{q,t}$ and $H_i^s=\sum_t h_{s,t}o_{s,t}$. Their negative expected log likelihood is a constant minus $H_i^qm_i+H_i^sn_i$. The ordered record word is retained whenever it remains an accessible response.

\propositionheading{Transferred own-block and null fields}{pa:four-transfer}
With product recognition $q_i\otimes s_i$, the normalized per-agent model with sources \cref{pa:four-evaluator,pa:four-channel} has the original-time equations
\begin{equation}
 \dot q_i=-q_i(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q),\qquad
 \dot s_i=-s_i(h_i^s-\E_{s_i}h_i^s),
 \label{pa:four-flow}
\end{equation}
where, modulo constants,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h_i^q(a,x)&=\log q_i(a,x)-\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_{ij}\log\nu_j^q(a,x)
             -\beta_{ii}Jn_i a-H_i^q a,\\
 h_i^s(b,u)&=\log s_i(b,u)-\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}\log\nu_j^s(b,u)
             -\beta_{ii}Jm_i b-H_i^s b.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:four-potentials}
\end{equation}
Writing $b_i^0=\beta^0_{ii}$ and $g_i^0=\gamma^0_{ii}$, the null potentials are
\begin{equation}
 h_i^{q,0}=b_i^0(\log q_i-Jn_i a)-H_i^q a,\qquad
 h_i^{s,0}=g_i^0\log s_i-b_i^0Jm_i b-H_i^s b.
 \label{pa:four-null}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Integrating the record, content source, presentation source, and source priors in the local model proves normalization. Its content self cost is
$\sum q_i\log q_i-Jm_in_i+\log(4\cosh J)$, and its presentation self cost is $\sum s_i\log s_i+\log4$. Every peer is fixed in the own-block variation. Differentiating the row-weighted costs and the persistent record term gives \cref{pa:four-potentials}; the coefficients of each full-law entropy sum to one. In particular both derivatives of the evaluator interaction carry $\beta_{ii}$, not $\gamma_{ii}$. After deletion only the self entropy coefficients remain, giving \cref{pa:four-null}. Centering these first variations in the Fisher metric gives \cref{pa:four-flow}.
\end{proof}

On the synchronized family $q_i(a,x)=(1+ma)/4$, $s_i(b,u)=(1+nb)/4$, every peer cost and normalized peer tangent vanishes. Full and null fields coincide there. With no records, stationarity requires
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{atanh}m=Jn,\qquad
 \gamma_{ii}\operatorname{atanh}n=\beta_{ii}Jm.
 \label{pa:four-stationarity}
\end{equation}
Thus a symmetric synchronized equilibrium $m=n=r_*$ with $\operatorname{atanh}r_*=Jr_*$ additionally requires equality of the two self rows. Synchronized bistability cannot by itself establish interaction necessity.

\propositionheading{Interaction-dependent selection on a full open table class}{pa:four-grouping}
For $N=2$, $J=3/2$, $\rho_s=1/2$, and $\rho_q=\cosh J/(1+\cosh J)$, there are positive record strengths and a nonempty full open set of initial positive tables for which a permutation-covariant conditional-sign selector selects the pair for a nonzero time window, while neither the label-preserving nor the renormalized null selects the pair at any time.
\begin{proof}
At the record-free uniform table the self rows equal $1/2$ and their first variations vanish. The sign linearization is
\begin{equation}
 \dot m_i=-m_i+\frac12m_j+\frac34n_i,\qquad
 \dot n_i=-n_i+\frac12n_j+\frac34m_i,\qquad j\ne i.
 \label{pa:four-linear}
\end{equation}
Its common sign eigenvalue is $1/4$ and the matching difference eigenvalue is $-3/4$. Other sign eigenvalues are $-5/4,-9/4$, and the content modes have eigenvalues $-1\pm\kappa/2$, hence are stable. Start with $m=n=\eta(3,-1)$ and uniform conditional content. The relevant linear solution is
\begin{equation}
 m_i(t)=n_i(t)=\eta\bigl[e^{t/4}+(a_i-1)e^{-3t/4}\bigr],
 \qquad (a_1,a_2)=(3,-1).
 \label{pa:four-crossing}
\end{equation}
At $t_*=\log4$ its values are $\eta(3/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)$. The analytic field is its linearization plus a quadratic remainder. Variation of constants on a fixed finite interval gives an $O(\eta^2)$ state and velocity error for sufficiently small $\eta>0$. Now fix such an $\eta$ and add small records with $H_1^q=H_1^s=h>0$ and $H_2^q=H_2^s=-h$. Continuous dependence preserves the strict positive crossing and a subsequent common interval, also under arbitrary small positive-table perturbations.

For the null, let $c_i^q(x)=\E[a\mid x]$ and $c_i^s(u)=\E[b\mid u]$. Conditional Fisher centering gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot c_i^q(x)&=(1-(c_i^q(x))^2)
 \{b_i^0[Jn_i-\operatorname{atanh}c_i^q(x)]+H_i^q\},\\
 \dot c_i^s(u)&=(1-(c_i^s(u))^2)
 \{b_i^0Jm_i-g_i^0\operatorname{atanh}c_i^s(u)+H_i^s\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:four-cone}
\end{equation}
At a first zero of a conditional initially aligned with its record, all other signed conditionals are nonnegative and the signed derivative is strictly positive. The two opposite cones are therefore invariant. All null log ratios have linear growth bounds because the self rows lie in $(0,1]$ and $|m_i|,|n_i|\leq1$, so this conclusion holds for every finite time. The renormalized null is \cref{pa:four-cone} with $b_i^0=g_i^0=1$, and the same first-zero argument applies to it.

Declare the selector family $S_c$: the pair is selected when all eight conditional means exceed $c$, or all are below $-c$. For every $c\in(0,\eta/(8\sqrt2)]$ the full flow meets this condition on the common interval; neither null does for any $c\geq0$, since its two agents stay in opposite cones. Initially there is no selected pair for any $c\geq0$. The condition is strict on a full open neighborhood of the chosen initial tables and is preserved by simultaneous relabeling of agents and records. The partition sequences differ after deleting their event times, against both nulls and for every such $c$.
\end{proof}

The open set is a small neighborhood of a near-uniform unstable equilibrium, of size of order $\eta$, and the constant set $(0,\eta/(8\sqrt2)]$ scales with the initial amplitude. Numerical integration of the full flow from the base point indicates that the pair, once selected, persists and that the state converges to a hyperbolic sink inside the selection region; this is not proved, and the theorem asserts only a finite window. The frozen-environment control is not analyzed here. On the product-content family through the base point, a numerical integration at $\eta=0.02$ and record strength $2\times10^{-4}$ shows the frozen control also selecting the pair, on a window beginning near $t=0.7$, and losing it near $t=3.6$, while the full flow keeps it. Within the theorem's finite window the selection therefore does not distinguish the full flow from the response of each agent to the initial laws of the other. The construction has no compressive retention.

\section{A persistent group against conflicting evidence}

A simpler local evaluator yields indefinite persistence with the initial records held fixed. Take three agents with positive Bernoulli belief and presentation laws of log odds $l_i,n_i$, uniform self reference and evaluator, and identity peer channels. Set $\pi_{ii}=a=1/5$ and $\pi_{ij}=p=2/5$ for $j\ne i$. Their common binary record likelihood, relative to counting measure, is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_\epsilon(o\mid z)=\frac{e^{\epsilon oz/2}}{2\cosh(\epsilon/2)},
 \qquad o,z\in\{-1,1\},\quad \epsilon>0.
 \label{pa:bit-record}
\end{equation}
Condition on $o=(1,1,-1)$ and retain it in the objective. The species are identical; the different observations are data.

For a constant $c\in(0,1)$, the selector $S_c$ connects $i,j$ when $l_i,l_j$ have the same nonzero sign and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\min(|l_i|,|l_j|)}{\max(|l_i|,|l_j|)}>c,
 \label{pa:selector}
\end{equation}
and takes connected components. It reads public laws only, treats labels symmetrically, and is the same function along every flow compared below.

\theoremheading{Persistent grouping for selector constants below one third}{pa:persistent-grouping}
Let $\mathsf C$ be a compact subset of $(1/8,1/3)$ and $u^{\rm in}=(4,1/2,-1)$. There are $\epsilon_0>0$ and $r>0$ such that for every $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0$ the following holds for all initial belief log odds $l(0)\in\epsilon B(u^{\rm in},r)$ and all positive initial presentation laws. Under every $S_c$ with $c\in\mathsf C$ the configuration starts with three singleton blocks, and the per-agent flow selects the three-agent block at a finite time after which the block persists for all time, whereas neither the label-preserving nor the renormalized null selects that block at any time.
\begin{proof}
The exact log-odds equations are
\begin{equation}
 \dot l_i=-l_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_{ij}l_j+\epsilon o_i,\qquad
 \dot n_i=-n_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}n_j.
 \label{pa:bit-flow}
\end{equation}
For a Bernoulli law, the Fisher inverse cancels $dl/dq(+1)$, so the peer KL derivative contributes $l_i-l_j$ and the log-likelihood ratio contributes $\epsilon o_i$. The rows use the corresponding Bernoulli costs. The field is analytic with at most linear growth. The presentation sector is retained but does not enter the evaluator.

Put $l=\epsilon u$. Then exactly
\begin{equation}
 \dot u=M u+o+R_\epsilon(u),\qquad
 M=-\frac75 I+\frac25\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
 u_*=-M^{-1}o=\left(\frac{15}7,\frac{15}7,\frac57\right),
 \label{pa:bit-limit}
\end{equation}
with $R_\epsilon(u)_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(\beta_{ij}-\pi_{ij})u_j$. The matrix $M$ is symmetric with eigenvalues $-1/5,-7/5,-7/5$. The log-partition function $\log(2\cosh(l/2))$ of the Bernoulli family has second derivative at most $1/4$, so every Bernoulli divergence obeys $\KL\leq(l-l')^2/8$. Let $\rho=13/4$ and let $\mathsf B$ be the closed ball of radius $\rho$ about $u_*$. On $\mathsf B$ we have $|u_i|<6$, so every cost lies in $[0,18\epsilon^2]$, each ratio $\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij}$ lies in $[e^{-18\epsilon^2},e^{18\epsilon^2}]$, and
\begin{equation}
 |R_\epsilon(u)|\leq\bar R_\epsilon:=\frac{24\sqrt3}5\bigl(e^{18\epsilon^2}-1\bigr)
 \qquad(u\in\mathsf B).
 \label{pa:bit-remainder}
\end{equation}
For $e=u-u_*$, symmetry of $M$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}|e|\leq-|e|/5+|R_\epsilon(u)|$, so $\mathsf B$ is forward invariant whenever $\bar R_\epsilon<\rho/5$, in particular for $\epsilon\leq1/20$. Since $|u^{\rm in}-u_*|=\sqrt{1781}/14<3.02$, the initial set lies in $\mathsf B$ for $r\leq1/5$. Let $\bar u$ solve the limit equation $\dot{\bar u}=M\bar u+o$ with $\bar u(0)=u(0)$. Then $\frac{d}{dt}(u-\bar u)=M(u-\bar u)+R_\epsilon(u)$ and $\|e^{Mt}\|=e^{-t/5}$ give
\begin{equation}
 |u(t)-\bar u(t)|\leq5\bar R_\epsilon\qquad\text{for all }t\geq0.
 \label{pa:bit-shadow}
\end{equation}
This bound is uniform in time, so persistence needs no separate attracting-ball argument.

From $u(0)=u^{\rm in}$ the limit solution is $\bar u(t)=u_*-\tfrac12e^{-t/5}\mathbf1+\tfrac1{14}e^{-7t/5}(33,-16,-17)$. For an edge between agents with $u_i\geq u_j>0$ the selector margin is $u_j-cu_i$, and along this solution
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar u_2-c\bar u_1&=\tfrac{15}7(1-c)-\tfrac{1-c}2e^{-t/5}-\tfrac{16+33c}{14}e^{-7t/5},\\
 \bar u_3-c\bar u_2&=\tfrac{5-15c}7-\tfrac{1-c}2e^{-t/5}-\tfrac{17-16c}{14}e^{-7t/5},\\
 \bar u_3-c\bar u_1&=\tfrac{5-15c}7-\tfrac{1-c}2e^{-t/5}-\tfrac{17+33c}{14}e^{-7t/5},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:bit-margins}
\end{equation}
while $\bar u_1-\bar u_2=\tfrac72e^{-7t/5}>0$, $\bar u_2-\bar u_3>10/7$, and $\bar u_2\geq1/2$. For $c\in(0,1)$ every time-dependent coefficient is negative, so each margin increases strictly in time. For small $r$ these signs persist uniformly over the initial set and over $c$ in a compact subset of $(0,1)$, and the limit margins move by $O(r)$. The margins are $\sqrt2$-Lipschitz in $u$, so by \cref{pa:bit-shadow} the exact margins differ from those of $\bar u$ by at most $5\sqrt2\bar R_\epsilon$.

Initially $u_2-cu_1=1/2-4c+O(r)<0$ and $u_3<0$, so all blocks are singletons for $c>1/8$ and small $r$. For $c\in\mathsf C$ the limits $\tfrac{15}7(1-c)$ and $(5-15c)/7$ of the margins are bounded below by a positive constant $m_{\mathsf C}$. Choose $\epsilon_0\leq1/20$ with $5\sqrt2\bar R_\epsilon<m_{\mathsf C}/4$ and then $T$ such that every margin of $\bar u$ exceeds $m_{\mathsf C}/2$ for $t\geq T$, uniformly over the initial set and $\mathsf C$. For $t\geq T$ the exact edges $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ are present in both orientations of each pair, so the full block persists. The equilibrium ratio $1/3$ and the initial ratio $1/8$ are the two quantities that bound the admissible constants.

The label-preserving null is
\begin{equation}
 \dot l_i^0=-\beta_{ii}^0(l_i^0)l_i^0+\epsilon o_i,\qquad
 \beta_{ii}^0=\frac{a e^{-\KL(q_i\Vert q_0)}}{a e^{-\KL(q_i\Vert q_0)}+2p},
 \label{pa:bit-null}
\end{equation}
and the renormalized null is $\dot l_i^0=-l_i^0+\epsilon o_i$. In both, the derivative at $l_i=0$ has the strict sign of $o_i$. The first two initial odds remain positive and the third remains negative for all time. Thus the full block never appears under any $S_c$, irrespective of event timing. Independent presentation-law perturbations do not change the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\propositionheading{The verdict depends on the selector constant}{pa:selector-threshold}
Let $\mathsf C'$ be a compact subset of $(1/3,1)$ and $\mathsf C$ as in \cref{pa:persistent-grouping}. For sufficiently small $\epsilon$ and $r$ and the same initial set, the full flow and both nulls produce the same partition sequence $1|2|3,\ 12|3$ under every $S_c$ with $c\in\mathsf C'$. Under every $S_c$ with $c\in\mathsf C$, the full flow and the frozen-environment control both produce $1|2|3,\ 12|3,\ 123$.
\begin{proof}
For $c\in\mathsf C'$ the limit $(5-15c)/7$ is negative and the last two margins of \cref{pa:bit-margins} lie below it for all $t\geq0$; by \cref{pa:bit-shadow} the exact margins $u_3-cu_1$ and $u_3-cu_2$ stay negative once $5\sqrt2\bar R_\epsilon<(15c-5)/14$. If $u_3>0$ then $u_3<cu_j<u_j$, so the ratio of agent $3$ with either peer is below $c$, and agent $3$ is never connected. The first margin increases strictly to $\tfrac{15}7(1-c)>0$ with a positive derivative at its unique zero, so for small $\epsilon$ the exact margin has exactly one zero, and the opposite-orientation margin $u_1-cu_2$ stays positive. The full sequence is therefore $1|2|3,\ 12|3$.

Along the renormalized null, $l_i=\epsilon o_i+(l_i(0)-\epsilon o_i)e^{-t}$, and the ratio of agents $1$ and $2$ increases strictly to one. Along the label-preserving null put $r_i=o_il_i/\epsilon$, so $\dot r_i=f(r_i)$ with $f(r)=1-\beta^0(r)r$ for one function $\beta^0$. Since $\KL(q_i\Vert q_0)\leq\epsilon^2r^2/8$, its $r$-derivative is at most $\epsilon^2r/4$, and $|d\beta^0/d\KL|\leq1/4$, the interval $1/4\leq r\leq12$ is forward invariant for $\epsilon\leq1/100$, it contains the initial values, and on it $f(r)/r$ and $f$ are strictly decreasing. Hence agents $1$ and $2$ keep their order $r_2<r_1$, $\frac{d}{dt}\log(r_2/r_1)=f(r_2)/r_2-f(r_1)/r_1>0$, and both converge to the unique zero of $f$. In both nulls the ratio of agents $1$ and $2$ crosses $c$ exactly once and agent $3$ stays negative, giving $1|2|3,\ 12|3$.

The frozen-environment control is $\dot u_i=-u_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\beta^{\rm fr}_{ij}u_j(0)+o_i$ with rows computed from the own current law and the initial peer laws, so $\beta^{\rm fr}_{ij}=\pi_{ij}+O(\epsilon^2)$ and its solution stays within $O(\epsilon^2)$ of the limit $u_i(t)=w_i+(u_i(0)-w_i)e^{-t}$, $w_i=o_i+\tfrac25\sum_{j\ne i}u_j(0)$, uniformly in time. From $u^{\rm in}$, $w=(4/5,11/5,4/5)$, and with $x=e^{-t}$ the margins $u_2-cu_1$, $u_3-cu_1$, $u_3-cu_2$ are affine in $x$ and vanish at $x_{12}=2(11-4c)/(17+32c)$, $x_{13}=4(1-c)/(9+16c)$, $x_{23}=2(4-11c)/(18-17c)$, each crossing once, while the opposite-orientation margins stay positive for $c<4/11$. For $c\in(1/8,1/3)$ one has $x_{12}>58/83>4/9\geq\max(x_{13},x_{23})$, so the sequence is $1|2|3,\ 12|3,\ 123$. For the full flow, at $e^{-t/5}=5/6$ the limit solution has $\bar u_3<0$ and $\bar u_2-c\bar u_1>0$ for every $c\leq1/3$, so the pair $\{1,2\}$ forms before agent $3$ turns positive, and by the proof of \cref{pa:persistent-grouping} agent $3$ then joins once and stays. All crossings are transversal, so the sequences persist for small $\epsilon$ and $r$.
\end{proof}

The (F5) report for this construction under \cref{inh:recursive-formation} is therefore the following. Its objective is the per-agent free energy under the Gibbs-reduced dynamics on positive Bernoulli tables, a full-grade admitted class. Against both nulls (F5) holds for every compact constant set inside $(1/8,1/3)$, at every point of the initial set of \cref{pa:persistent-grouping}, and fails for every constant set that meets $(1/3,1)$; the two endpoints are the initial ratio and the equilibrium ratio, which are properties of the flow rather than of the constitutive data. The selector-free discrepancy is the sign of agent $3$'s public odds: it converges to approximately $5\epsilon/7>0$, against its own record, under the full flow, and stays negative under both nulls, near $-5\epsilon$ and $-\epsilon$. The frozen-environment control reproduces the full partition sequence on the same constant set and also reverses agent $3$'s sign, with limit near $4\epsilon/5$ from $u^{\rm in}$: the selection already follows from each agent's response to the initial laws of the others, and feedback among the agents is not needed for it. For $c=1/4$ the limit solution meets the last selector margin at $t=-5\log y_*\approx3.83$, where $y_*\approx0.465$ is the root in $(0,1)$ of $\tfrac5{28}-\tfrac38y-\tfrac{13}{14}y^7$. In the limit $\epsilon\to0$, \cref{pa:bit-limit} is continuous-time averaging \citep{DeGroot1974} with each agent anchored to its fixed record, the model of \citet{FriedkinJohnsen1990}, whose equilibrium is the same linear solve; its stationary points are logarithmic pools of private-signal likelihoods, as in log-linear non-Bayesian social learning \citep{JadbabaieEtAl2012}. What is new here is its derivation from normalized per-agent free energies with exact Gibbs rows, the uniform-in-time bound \cref{pa:bit-shadow} for the exact field, and the inheritance of the selected block below. The construction discards no child law.

\section{A fixed-model angular extension}

Let $\mathsf K=\{-1,1\}\times S^1\times S^1$, with uniform binary reference and independent Haar references $H$ on both circles. Let $\mathsf M=\{-1,1\}$ with counting measure. Write $e(\phi)=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$ and
$h(A)=\log I_0(|A|)$, where $I_0(r)=\int e^{r\cos\phi}H(d\phi)$. The invariant belief family is
\begin{equation}
 q_i(z,\phi,\psi)=
 \frac{\exp[l_i z/2+A_i\cdot e(\phi)+zC_i\cdot e(\phi)
                    +b_i\cos(\psi-\theta_i)]}{Z_i},
 \label{pa:angular-family}
\end{equation}
where $A_i,C_i\in\R^2$, $b_i>0$, $\theta_i\in S^1$, and
\begin{equation}
 Z_i=I_0(b_i)
 \frac{e^{l_i/2}I_0(|A_i+C_i|)+e^{-l_i/2}I_0(|A_i-C_i|)}2.
 \label{pa:angular-normalizer}
\end{equation}
The Cartesian hidden parameter $b_i e(\theta_i)$ is regular at zero; the phase chart used for compression is restricted to $b_i>0$. Retain the independent Bernoulli presentation laws and priors of \cref{pa:persistent-grouping}.

Fix $J=\epsilon^3$ and the normalized self evaluator
\begin{equation}
 G_i(m;z,\phi,\psi)=\frac{e^{Jz\cos\phi}}{I_0(J)}.
 \label{pa:angular-evaluator}
\end{equation}
It is independent of $m$. Each nonself channel copies $z$ and resamples both circles from Haar measure. Thus its output is $\mu_j\otimes H\otimes H$, where $\mu_j$ is the public binary marginal. Its density is relative to the uniform binary reference, not counting measure:
\begin{equation}
 \mu_i(z)=\frac{e^{L_i z/2}}{\cosh(L_i/2)},\qquad
 L_i=l_i+h(A_i+C_i)-h(A_i-C_i).
 \label{pa:public-odds}
\end{equation}
The record space is a bit $o$ with counting measure and an angle $\omega$ with Haar measure, with likelihood
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{\epsilon}(o,\omega\mid z,\phi,\psi,m)
 =\frac{e^{\epsilon oz/2}}{2\cosh(\epsilon/2)}
  \frac{e^{\epsilon\cos(\omega-\phi)}}{I_0(\epsilon)}.
 \label{pa:angular-record}
\end{equation}
Integrating each circle and summing the record bit proves all normalizations. The channels, evaluator, and likelihood are fixed during an own-block variation. In particular this example does not reconstruct the receiver's changing conditional law inside its generative model.

\propositionheading{Exact angular field}{pa:angular-flow}
The full own-law Fisher field preserves \cref{pa:angular-family} and is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot l_i&=-l_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_{ij}L_j+\epsilon o_i,&
 \dot A_i&=-A_i+\epsilon e(\omega_i),\\
 \dot C_i&=-C_i+\beta_{ii}\epsilon^3e_1,&
 \dot b_i&=-b_i,\qquad \dot\theta_i=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:angular-ode}
\end{equation}
The presentation equation remains the second equation of \cref{pa:bit-flow}. If $m_i=\tanh(L_i/2)$ and $t_i=\E_{q_i}[z\cos\phi]$, the unnormalized content row weights are
\begin{equation}
 w_{ii}=\frac{a e^{Jt_i}}{I_0(J)},\qquad
 w_{ij}=\frac{p e^{L_jm_i/2}}{\cosh(L_j/2)},\qquad
 \beta_{ij}=\frac{w_{ij}}{\sum_r w_{ir}}.
 \label{pa:angular-weights}
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Put $S_i=\E_{q_i}\log q_i$. The costs are
$D_{ii}=S_i-Jt_i+\log I_0(J)$ and
$D_{ij}=S_i-L_jm_i/2+\log\cosh(L_j/2)$.
The common entropy cancels in the Gibbs normalization, giving \cref{pa:angular-weights}. The centered negative own derivative is the logarithmic replicator velocity. It lies in the span of $z,e(\phi),ze(\phi),e(\psi)$ and constants. Matching coefficients gives \cref{pa:angular-ode}. The covariance of these seven sufficient statistics is positive definite: a linear combination constant under a positive density must be constant under the reference, and its Fourier modes and sign parity force every nonconstant coefficient to vanish. This gives tangency: the chart field of \cref{def:dyn-per-agent-flow}, evaluated on the family, is the family velocity. Invariance needs uniqueness of that chart flow. Take the bounded log-density chart of \cref{def:dyn-chart-flow} on $\mathsf K$ relative to $\lambda=\tfrac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)\otimes H\otimes H$, with log densities $f_i\in L^\infty_0(\lambda)$. The evaluator \cref{pa:angular-evaluator} and the likelihood \cref{pa:angular-record} have bounded log densities. The peer output has density $E_\lambda[e^{f_j}\mid Z=z]/E_\lambda e^{f_j}$; since $f\mapsto\log E_\lambda e^f$ and its conditional version are $1$-Lipschitz in the supremum norm, its logarithm is a $2$-Lipschitz function of $f_j$. The Gibbs rows are smooth functions of bounded costs. The sufficient conditions stated after \cref{def:dyn-per-agent-flow} therefore hold, the chart flow has unique maximal solutions, and the family curve, which solves it, is that solution. The family is invariant, and \cref{pa:angular-ode} is the exact Fisher velocity on it rather than a Euclidean update or a projected approximation. The same argument applies to the null and frozen-environment fields.
\end{proof}

The marked sector participates in the public force. At $A=\alpha e_1$ and $C=ce_1$ with $c>0$, $\partial_\alpha L=h_1'(\alpha+c)-h_1'(\alpha-c)>0$, since $h_1''(r)=\operatorname{Var}_r(\cos\phi)>0$. Starting with $C=0$ generates positive $c$ at positive time. Also $\partial_{L_j}\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ij}(1-\beta_{ij})(m_i-m_j)/2$. Thus angular data can change source competition and the receiver's force. The separate hidden phase does not enter those quantities.

\theoremheading{Grouping survives the angular extension}{pa:angular-grouping}
Let $\mathsf C$ be a compact subset of $(1/8,1/3)$. There is $\epsilon_0>0$ such that for every $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0$, every selector $S_c$ of \cref{pa:selector} with $c\in\mathsf C$, applied to the actual public odds $L_i$, selects a persistent full block from a nonempty relatively open initial set in \cref{pa:angular-family}, with records $o=(1,1,-1)$ held fixed. Neither the label-preserving nor the renormalized null selects that block at any time. Initial marked and hidden concentrations can lie in a fixed small positive compact patch independent of $\epsilon$, and the conclusion is uniform over the initial angular record values.
\begin{proof}
The null equations are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot l_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0l_i^0+\epsilon o_i,&
 \dot A_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0A_i^0+\epsilon e(\omega_i),\\
 \dot C_i^0&=\beta_{ii}^0(\epsilon^3e_1-C_i^0),&
 \dot b_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0b_i^0,\qquad\dot\theta_i^0=0,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:angular-null}
\end{equation}
with \cref{pa:null-row} evaluated at the complete self cost. Its angular entropy cannot be canceled after deleting the nonself costs.

For $\epsilon\leq1/100$, consider
\begin{equation}
 \frac14\leq\frac{o_i l_i}{\epsilon}\leq12,\qquad
 |A_i|\leq\frac1{10},\qquad |C_i|\leq2\epsilon^3,\qquad
 0\leq b_i\leq\frac1{10}.
 \label{pa:null-box}
\end{equation}
In natural parameters $\eta=(l/2,A,C,b e(\theta))$ the sufficient-statistic vector has squared norm four, so the log-partition Hessian has operator norm at most four. Taylor's integral remainder for the exponential-family divergence, relative to $\eta_G=(0,0,\epsilon^3e_1,0)$, gives
\begin{equation}
 D_{ii}\leq2|\eta-\eta_G|^2
 \leq72\epsilon^2+\frac1{25}+18\epsilon^6<\log2,
 \qquad \frac19\leq\beta_{ii}^0\leq\frac15.
 \label{pa:null-bound}
\end{equation}
For $r_i=o_i l_i/\epsilon$, $\dot r_i=1-\beta_{ii}^0r_i$ points inward at both endpoints. The outward derivatives of the $A$ and $C$ norms are at most $\epsilon-1/90<0$ and $-\beta_{ii}^0\epsilon^3<0$ on their respective boundaries, while $b$ decreases. Thus \cref{pa:null-box} is forward invariant. Since $\nabla h(0)=0$ and $\|\nabla^2h\|\leq1$,
\begin{equation}
 |L_i-l_i|\leq2|C_i|(|A_i|+|C_i|)
 \leq\frac25\epsilon^3+8\epsilon^6<\frac\epsilon8.
 \label{pa:null-sign-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently $o_iL_i\geq\epsilon/8$ for all finite null times.

For the full flow put $u=l/\epsilon$ and $c=C/\epsilon^3$, leaving $A$ unscaled. On bounded sets,
$L_i/\epsilon=u_i+O_{C^1}(\epsilon^2)$ and $m_i,t_i=O(\epsilon)$.
Equation \cref{pa:angular-weights} implies $\beta_{ij}=\pi_{ij}+O_{C^1}(\epsilon^2)$ even for fixed nonzero initial angular amplitudes. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \dot u=Mu+o+O_{C^1}(\epsilon^2),\qquad
 \dot c_i=-c_i+a e_1+O_{C^1}(\epsilon^2),\qquad
 \dot A_i=-A_i+\epsilon e(\omega_i).
 \label{pa:angular-limit}
\end{equation}
At $\epsilon=0$ this is a stable affine field with equilibrium
$(u_*,c_i=a e_1,A_i=0)$ and symmetric negative-definite derivative. The implicit function theorem supplies a nearby equilibrium with
$u=u_*+O(\epsilon^2)$, $c_i=a e_1+O(\epsilon^2)$, and $A_i=\epsilon e(\omega_i)$.
On a sufficiently small common ball its symmetric derivative is at most $-I/10$. After recentering, the derivative of squared distance is at most minus one fifth of that distance squared. This proves an invariant attracting neighborhood with all public signs positive and all pair ratios above $\max\mathsf C$.

Choose $u(0)$ near $(4,1/2,-1)$, $|C_i(0)|<\epsilon^3$, and $|A_i(0)|,b_i(0)<1/20$ with positive hidden amplitudes. The limiting affine flow enters the attraction neighborhood after a fixed finite time, uniformly over compact initial marked patches. Finite-time continuous dependence gives the same entry for small positive $\epsilon$ and a relatively open initial set. Initially $L_i=l_i+O(\epsilon^3)$, so the strict singleton margins persist. The angular records range over a compact torus and enter only through bounded constant forcings of size $\epsilon$; the same entrance time and smallness threshold work uniformly over their values. The label-preserving null retains opposite signs by \cref{pa:null-sign-bound}. The renormalized null is \cref{pa:angular-null} with $\beta^0_{ii}=1$; along it $o_il_i/\epsilon\geq\min(o_il_i(0)/\epsilon,1)\geq1/4$, $|A_i|\leq\max(|A_i(0)|,\epsilon)$ and $|C_i|\leq\epsilon^3$, so \cref{pa:null-sign-bound} holds there as well.

Finally $A,C,b$ have finite-interval bounds directly from their equations, $|L-l|\leq2|C|$, and both $l$ and the presentation log odds have at most linear growth. Solutions therefore continue for every finite time. The subsequent group persistence uses the invariant attraction neighborhood, not indefinite extrapolation of finite-time continuity.
\end{proof}

The smallness of the marked and hidden amplitudes is used only by the null estimate \cref{pa:null-bound}. In the full flow the marked amplitudes enter the scaled public equation only through $|L_i/\epsilon-u_i|\leq2\epsilon^2(|A_i|+|C_i|)$, and the hidden concentrations do not enter it at all. The scaled public equation differs from \cref{pa:bit-limit} by a remainder of order $\epsilon^2$ that is bounded uniformly on the invariant ball, so the uniform-in-time comparison \cref{pa:bit-shadow} and the proof of \cref{pa:selector-threshold} apply with $L$ in place of $l$. The (F5) report after \cref{pa:selector-threshold} therefore holds for this theorem, with the same constant sets, the same frozen-environment comparison and the same sign reversal of agent $3$'s public odds.

\section{A hidden-phase quotient with inherited records}

Fix the operational protocol before selection. It admits fresh public and marked-coordinate queries on the primitive ports, normalized generative or recognition-predictive record transactions with the likelihood family \cref{pa:angular-record}, and adaptive choices of ports, strengths and waiting times from the retained history. These two kinds of predictive law are declared separately and need not agree. A prediction is not automatically assimilated. An evidence transaction appends its actual outcome and adds its likelihood to the persistent objective. Every finite continuation has finite record coefficients. Hidden absolute-angle queries are excluded at all stages. An external source-label readout does not change the agent's conditioning convention; revealing such a label to the inferring agent would specify another source-row update. Policies are Borel kernels, independent of context, from finite histories and fixed lab data to admitted actions and waiting times, and every record density is jointly Borel in the outcome and the state, as in \cref{def:adaptive-history}. For the collective geometry below, the expected number of transactions in $[0,t]$ is required to be bounded on compact contextual patches; without this bound a policy can issue unboundedly many context-sensitive fresh queries before a fixed time, and the history experiment is then not differentiable in quadratic mean. In the terms of \cref{inh:protocol-classes}, the parent protocol rule $A_I$ consists of these queries and transactions addressed to a block through its primitive ports, and the child-level probe rule $B_I$ consists of the same queries applied to individual members, or to sets of them, before the handoff, together with every channel, evaluator and record kernel of the construction. Neither rule depends on the retention or on the selected blocks, and neither contains a query whose response law depends on a hidden phase $\theta_i$.

\propositionheading{Canonical phase parent}{pa:phase-parent}
On the invariant family \cref{pa:angular-family}, replace each hidden phase by zero and retain $l_i,A_i,C_i,b_i$, the presentation laws, primitive port labels, all constitutive data, event information, and the full record history. For a selected block take the product of these normalized canonical laws. This retention is noninjective at every finite time, intertwines the original-time full flow, both null flows and the frozen-environment control, and preserves every admitted finite adaptive joint response-record history, including its dependence with retained next states. The phase it erases is gauge relative to the probe rule $B_I$ and to every observable algebra of the construction, so the retention fails (F4).
\begin{proof}
All full and null costs and retained velocities are independent of $\theta_i$, and $\dot\theta_i=0$. The canonical laws therefore obey the exact inherited port equations. Positive initial $b_i$ remains positive at each finite time. The child diagnostic
\begin{equation}
 \E_{q_i}e^{i\Psi}=\frac{I_1(b_i)}{I_0(b_i)}e^{i\theta_i}
 \label{pa:hidden-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
separates a phase fiber because $I_1(b)/I_0(b)>0$ for $b>0$, so the retention is noninjective after event data and records are included. That diagnostic is a law functional that no agent, channel, evaluator, record kernel or admitted query reads. By \cref{def:observable-algebra} it belongs to no observable algebra and is not an element of $B_I$. Every cost, row, channel output, retained velocity and admitted response law is independent of $\theta_i$ at every time, so no element of $B_I$ separates two points of a fiber, and (F4) of \cref{inh:recursive-formation} fails.

The current retained state determines every admitted next response law. Conditional on its actual outcome, the same likelihood adds the same binary and marked-vector coefficients to the retained objective and the same entry to history. The joint response and retained next-state kernel therefore factors through retention. Deterministic waiting also factors by the exact equations. Induction over the causal product of these kernels proves every finite adaptive joint history. This is the predictive-equivalence requirement familiar from causal-state constructions \citep{ShaliziCrutchfield2001}; for input-driven processes its analogue is the $\epsilon$-transducer \citep{BarnettCrutchfield2015}, and for finite controlled chains it is probabilistic bisimulation \citep{LarsenSkou1991}. It is proved here directly for the declared controlled protocol.
\end{proof}

The retention is the equivariant quotient of \cref{inh:equivariant-descent} by the hidden torus acting by $\theta_i\mapsto\theta_i+g_i$ on the stratum $b_i>0$, with the quotient identified with the canonical section $\theta=0$; that action is free and proper and is a symmetry of the full field, of both nulls, of the Fisher metric and of every admitted kernel. The history induction follows the pattern of \cref{lem:boundary-equivariance}. What is new is the assembly of these general results for the per-agent model with persistent records. The two attributes required with (F4) are these: the fibers are orbits of that symmetry, and the separation they carry fades, since $b_i(t)=e^{-t}b_i(0)$ along the full flow, so that the excluded diagnostic has modulus $I_1(b_i)/I_0(b_i)\sim b_i/2$ and the phase Fisher information $b_iI_1(b_i)/I_0(b_i)$ decays like $e^{-2t}$; along the nulls the rate is $\beta^0_{ii}\in[1/9,1/5]$ or one.

The parent carries primitive ports and their directed own-objective field. No claim is made that it is a fixed-size agent with one new source row, or the Fisher gradient of the sum of its children's free energies. Such a sum would add source-role derivatives. Normalized inherited channels act by projecting to the selected primitive source port, applying the existing public channel, and appending normalized canonical factors on other output coordinates. No new parent force is fitted.

The diagnostic \cref{pa:hidden-diagnostic} lies outside $A_I$ and outside $B_I$. If a hidden-angle query is admitted in $A_I$, the quotient fails (B). A rule that admitted such a query in $B_I$ for singletons and withdrew it after the handoff would make (F4) hold, at the price of deleting a probe at the handoff; the chapter declares no such rule, and no agent of the construction reads the hidden angle. A persistent hidden-angle likelihood proportional to $e^{\lambda\cos(v-\psi)}$ also changes the hidden amplitude and phase:
\begin{equation}
 \dot b=-b+\lambda\cos(v-\theta),\qquad
 \dot\theta=\frac{\lambda}{b}\sin(v-\theta)
 \label{pa:hidden-record-obstruction}
\end{equation}
in the full fixed-model example. Two states with the same retained $b,v$ can then have different future $b$ velocities. Finite-record uncertainty about $\theta$ does not remove this dynamical obstruction.

\section{Collective geometry of the same parent}

The marked comparison group is a common $S^1$ acting on $\phi$ within the selected block. Under a frame change $g$, transport $\phi$ and the record angle by addition of $g$, rotate $A_i,C_i$ and the constitutive evaluator direction $e_1$ by the corresponding plane rotation, and leave $z,b,\psi,\theta$ unchanged. The presentation sector carries no marked orbit. This is passive covariance of the declared comparison data. Rotating only the beliefs while holding the numerical evaluator and record directions fixed is generally not a symmetry of the energy. The hidden phase rotations act outside the marked comparison group. They are symmetries of every constitutive datum and admitted kernel, and the distinctions they relate are gauge relative to every algebra declared in this chapter.

To include records in the actual statistical experiment, fix one finite initial episode and let $\Pi_c(dh)$ be its normalized generative predictive law at contextual point $c$. Conditional on the prepared configuration the agents' record kernels may be sampled independently; this defines an operational experiment and does not posit a global latent generative model. Let $Q_{c,t}^h$ be the product of fresh recognition and Gibbs-label laws conditional on the retained history $h$. The comparison law is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_{c,t}(dh,dy)=\Pi_c(dh)Q_{c,t}^h(dy),\qquad
 g^{\rm joint}=g^{\rm history}+\E_H[g^{\rm conditional}].
 \label{pa:joint-record-geometry}
\end{equation}
The metric identity is the chain rule for Fisher information \citep{Zamir1998}, compare \cref{geo:joint-fisher}: the conditional score has mean zero. It presupposes that $c\mapsto\mathcal P_{c,t}$ is differentiable in quadratic mean (\cref{geo:dqm}), which is where the bound on the expected number of transactions in the protocol is used. For the grouping witness one may condition on the positive-probability public pattern $(+,+,-)$, keeping all angular records random and including the conditioning normalizer in the history score. Source-label scores remain part of $Q^h$.

\propositionheading{Exact collective cost inheritance}{pa:geometry-inheritance}
On every regular contextual section, the canonical parent of \cref{pa:phase-parent} preserves the actual collective minimizing connection class and its curvature for \cref{pa:joint-record-geometry}. It preserves the minimized contextual Fisher tensor exactly when $\sum_i\mathcal I(b_i)(d\theta_i)^2=0$, with $\mathcal I(b)=bI_1(b)/I_0(b)$, in particular when every discarded $\theta_i$ is constant in context.
\begin{proof}
At each context the product shift $\psi_i\mapsto\psi_i-\theta_i$ is constant in time and history. It sends the fine joint law to the actual canonical parent law with unit Haar Jacobian and commutes with the marked action. The fine contextual score is the parent score at corresponding samples plus $\sum_i(X\theta_i)b_i\sin(\psi_i-\theta_i)$. Under $\mathcal P$ each $\psi_i$ is independent of the marked and public coordinates, of the history and of the source labels, because no likelihood, channel output or row reads it; and $\E\sin(\psi-\theta)=\E[(\cos(\psi-\theta)-\E\cos(\psi-\theta))\sin(\psi-\theta)]=0$. The added term is therefore orthogonal to every retained score and to the marked orbit score $s_{\rm orb}$, and for every tangent $X$ and comparison velocity $\xi$
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\rm fine}(X,\xi)
 =\E_{\mathcal P}(s_X+\xi s_{\rm orb})^2
 =\mathcal C_{\rm parent}(X,\xi)+\sum_i\mathcal I(b_i)(X\theta_i)^2.
 \label{pa:cost-isometry}
\end{equation}
The added term does not depend on $\xi$, so the minimizer class modulo isotropy, hence the connection and its curvature, agree, and the minima differ by $\sum_i\mathcal I(b_i)(X\theta_i)^2$. The parent metric is computed from its normalized law rather than assigned by copying a tensor.
\end{proof}

This is \cref{geo:retained-connection} with a vanishing horizontal defect, and the tensor gap is the exact defect of \cref{geo:minimal-contraction}. The common marked connection is a diagnostic of these evolved sections; it does not enter the present dynamical equations.

\theoremheading{A common nonflat grouping window}{pa:nonflat-window}
The initial set in \cref{pa:angular-grouping} admits a compact rank-two contextual patch and a fixed finite post-selection window on which the actual collective connection has nonzero curvature, its minimized tensor has rank two, and the canonical parent has exactly the same quantities.
\begin{proof}
Choose fixed small positive $a_-<a_+$, $d$, and $b_i$, independent of $\epsilon$. Use contextual coordinates $(a,\chi)$ with
$A_1(0)=a e(\chi)$, $a\in[a_-,a_+]$, $A_2(0)=d e_1$, and $C_i(0)=0$.
Other selected marked factors may have fixed positive concentrations. Keep hidden phases constant over context and take the public initial odds from \cref{pa:angular-grouping}. Choose a finite $T_+$ containing its common entrance time and a positive subsequent interval, with no additional assimilation during this window.

At $\epsilon=0$, the episode likelihood is uniform, public laws are uniform, and the full Gibbs rows equal the fixed priors. The history score and the score of the conditioning normalizer vanish identically in this witness, at every $\epsilon$: the generative predictive kernel of the initial episode reads the configuration only through the peers' initial public odds, which equal $l_j(0)$ because $C_i(0)=0$ and are independent of context. Label scores vanish at the limiting parameter. The solution is $A_i(t)=e^{-t}A_i(0)$, $C_i(t)=0$, $l_i(t)=0$, and $b_i(t)=e^{-t}b_i(0)$. Put
\begin{equation}
 I(x)=x\frac{I_1(x)}{I_0(x)},\qquad
 V(x)=\operatorname{Var}_{v_x}(\cos\phi),\qquad
 B(t)=\sum_{i\ne1}I(e^{-t}|A_i(0)|)>0.
 \label{pa:inertias}
\end{equation}
Here $v_x=e^{x\cos\phi}/I_0(x)$. Differentiating its partition function gives $I'(x)=I_1(x)/I_0(x)+xV(x)>0$ for $x>0$. Integration of the derivative of $\sin\phi e^{x\cos\phi}$ around the circle gives the phase Fisher identity $x^2\E\sin^2\phi=I(x)$. Minimizing the sum of marked phase-score costs gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A_{0,t}
 &=-\frac{I(e^{-t}a)}{I(e^{-t}a)+B(t)}d\chi,\\
 \mathcal F_{0,t}
 &=-\frac{B(t)e^{-t}I'(e^{-t}a)}
 {[I(e^{-t}a)+B(t)]^2}da\wedge d\chi,\\
 h^{0,t}_{aa}&=e^{-2t}V(e^{-t}a),\qquad
 h^{0,t}_{\chi\chi}=\frac{I(e^{-t}a)B(t)}{I(e^{-t}a)+B(t)},\qquad
 h^{0,t}_{a\chi}=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{pa:nonflat-limit}
\end{equation}
The locked inertia, both tensor eigenvalues, and the magnitude of the curvature coefficient have positive minima on the compact contextual patch and $0\leq t\leq T_+$.

The exact densities, episode kernels, and ODE coefficients are analytic on a common compact finite-time tube. The field is real-analytic jointly in the state and in $\epsilon$, and the initial section is analytic in context and $\epsilon$, so the flow and its first two contextual derivatives, which solve
$\dot X_c=DV(X)X_c$ and
$\dot X_{cc}=DV(X)X_{cc}+D^2V(X)[X_c,X_c]$,
depend continuously on $\epsilon$ uniformly on the compact tube \citep[Chapter V]{Hartman2002}; they converge to the displayed limit as $\epsilon\to0$. Compact record spaces and positive bounded densities justify differentiating the Fisher integrals. Conditioning on the public pattern is regular because its limiting probability is positive. Thus the locked inertia $K_\epsilon$, momentum $j_\epsilon$, and unminimized contextual tensor $B_\epsilon$ converge with the derivatives required in
$\mathcal A_\epsilon=-K_\epsilon^{-1}j_\epsilon$,
$h_\epsilon=B_\epsilon-j_\epsilon\otimes j_\epsilon/K_\epsilon$,
and $\mathcal F_\epsilon=d\mathcal A_\epsilon$.
The positive limiting margins persist for sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$, uniformly in the conditional integrands over the compact episode space. The grouping threshold can be imposed simultaneously by taking the smaller admissible $\epsilon_0$. Equation \cref{pa:cost-isometry} gives exact parent equality.
\end{proof}

The fixed positive initial marked amplitudes are needed in this finite-window argument: they keep the limiting inertia nonzero at the same finite entrance time as grouping. No numerical bound on $\epsilon_0$ is asserted. The group persists indefinitely with its initial record episode fixed, while nonflatness is asserted on the declared finite window. Later admitted record continuations preserve quotient closure but may alter the grouping or geometry.

The curvature is prepared rather than produced by the grouping. Context enters only through $A_1(0)=ae(\chi)$, the marked equation $\dot A_i=-A_i+\epsilon e(\omega_i)$ has no peer term, and \cref{pa:nonflat-limit} already holds at $t=0$, at $\epsilon=0$ with no coupling and no records, and along the null flows with the same limit. The window is a choice of interval containing the entrance time. At a fixed positive $\epsilon$ the evaluator-driven $C_i$ gives the locked inertia a positive floor of order $\epsilon^6$ while the contextual momentum decays like $e^{-t}$, so the curvature of the evolved section is expected to tend to zero; numerical evaluations of a reduced model indicate this, and the finite window is then necessary rather than a limitation of the proof. Neither statement is proved here.

\section{Report against the formation standard}

The angular construction concerns the per-agent objective, with the Gibbs-reduced per-agent dynamics in the bounded log-density chart on $\{-1,1\}\times S^1\times S^1$.
