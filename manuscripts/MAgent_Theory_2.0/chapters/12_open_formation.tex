\chapter{Preferential Formation from Open Law Classes}
\label{ch:open-formation}

The common-precision construction selects an exact additive description within a prepared family. This chapter gives a different existence construction whose initial laws range over a full open neighborhood. Native source preferences select pairs and then a pair of parents. A fixed selective channel makes a physical orientation symmetry available for compression, while an anchored representation preserves contextual Fisher geometry. The symmetry exists before the selected events. The construction derives the timing and membership of its use, not the first appearance of operational redundancy.

\section{A homogeneous selective-channel model}

Every primitive belief and model space is $\mathsf K=\mathsf M=\{-1,1\}\times S^1$. Use the probability measure
\begin{equation}
d\nu=\frac12\sum_{z=\pm1}\delta_z\otimes\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
All densities below are relative to this measure. The $2N$ normalized densities $q_i,s_i$ vary independently in a positive $C^r$ chart, with $r\ge3$. Internal bit-angle correlations are unrestricted. Recognition is the declared product $q_i\otimes s_i$. Take uniform proper references and evaluators, $r_i=G_i=1$, equal unit Fisher mobilities, a fixed temperature $T>0$, and all-source priors $\pi_{ij}=1/N$, including self.

Use the same fixed normalized comparison channel in both sectors:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K((z,\theta),d(z',\theta'))
=\delta_z(dz')\frac{d\theta'}{2\pi}.
\label{open:channel}
\end{equation}
It transmits the bit and replaces the angle by an independent uniform draw. There is no channel switch at formation. If
\begin{equation}
\mu_i(z)=\int q_i(z,\theta)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\eta_i(w)=\int s_i(w,\psi)\frac{d\psi}{2\pi},
\end{equation}
then $q_j\mathcal K$ has density $\mu_j(z)$, and the model output has density $\eta_j(w)$. The chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_i\|\mu_j\otimes\mathrm{Haar})
=H_i^q+\KL(\mu_i\|\mu_j),
\qquad
H_i^q=\KL(q_i\|\mu_i\otimes\mathrm{Haar}).
\label{open:cost}
\end{equation}
The receiver term $H_i^q$ cancels from the Gibbs normalization. Thus
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ij}
=\frac{\exp[-\KL(\mu_i\|\mu_j)/T]}
{\sum_k\exp[-\KL(\mu_i\|\mu_k)/T]},
\label{open:row}
\end{equation}
and $\gamma$ is the same expression in $\eta$. All entries are positive. The angular term remains in the reduced energy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{\rm red}
=\sum_i\bigl[\KL(q_i\|1)+\KL(s_i\|1)\bigr]
-T\sum_i\log\left(\frac1N\sum_j e^{-D^q_{ij}/T}\right)
-T\sum_i\log\left(\frac1N\sum_j e^{-D^s_{ij}/T}\right),
\end{equation}
where $D^q_{ij}$ is the left side of \cref{open:cost}.

\propositionheading{Complete density force}{open:force}
On the positive chart, the exact unit-Fisher field is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
h_i^q(z,\theta)
&=2\log q_i(z,\theta)-\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log\mu_j(z)
-\sum_k\beta_{ki}\frac{\mu_k(z)}{\mu_i(z)},\\
\partial_tq_i&=-q_i\bigl(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q\bigr),
\end{split}
\label{open:full-field}
\end{equation}
with the exchanged formula for $s,\eta,\gamma$. Every sum includes self.
\begin{proof}
The local KL contributes $\log q_i$ modulo a constant. Differentiating a reduced row by its envelope identity gives its weighted divergence derivatives. The normalized receiver row contributes another $\log q_i-\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log\mu_j$. Varying a source marginal in every incoming row gives $-\sum_k\beta_{ki}\mu_k/\mu_i$. The fixed channel has no law derivative. Fisher inversion on normalized densities subtracts the $q_i$ expectation and multiplies by $-q_i$.

For a self term the incoming ratio is one, but $D^q_{ii}=H_i^q$ need not vanish. Its constant incoming contribution and its angular entropy are both included. Logarithms and marginal ratios are smooth on a neighborhood bounded away from zero. Bounded integration, multiplication, and finite Gibbs normalization preserve $C^r$. The field is therefore locally Lipschitz on the normalized Banach chart and has a unique local interior flow. Its centering preserves normalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Two stages selected by native routing}

For a current partition $\mathcal P$, retain the actual block rows
\begin{equation}
B^q_{IJ}=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\beta_{ij},
\qquad
B^s_{IJ}=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\gamma_{ij}.
\label{open:block-row}
\end{equation}
Self mass remains in each normalized row. Its experiment chooses a receiver uniformly in $I$, draws its native source index, and reports the source block. If blocks are joined, finite summation gives
\begin{equation}
B^a_{CD}
=\frac1{|C|}\sum_{I\subset C}|I|\sum_{J\subset D}B^a_{IJ},
\qquad a\in\{q,s\}.
\label{open:row-recursion}
\end{equation}
This is associative aggregation with inherited primitive counts.

\definitionheading{Preferential pair rule}{open:selector}
Fix $\varepsilon=1/10$ and $\delta=1/20$. Distinct current blocks $I,J$ qualify when, in both sectors and directions,
\begin{equation}
B^a_{IJ}>\varepsilon,
\qquad
B^a_{IJ}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal P\setminus\{I,J\}}B^a_{IK}>\delta.
\label{open:contrast}
\end{equation}
The exterior excludes self and the proposed partner. A partner need not beat the self loop. Merge qualifying disjoint pairs at their first closed-threshold contact when the strict inequalities hold immediately afterward. A block cannot have two qualifying partners: each would have to exceed the other by more than $\delta$. The rule is therefore unambiguous at simultaneous disjoint events and respects relabeling. Persistence below means strict contrast over the stated positive time windows.

The routing calculation first uses binary densities $\mu_i(z)=1+zx_i$, $\eta_i(w)=1+wy_i$, with $(x,y)\in(-1,1)^{2N}$. Define $d(u,v)=\KL(\mu_u\|\mu_v)$. Its derivatives are
\begin{equation}
\partial_ud(u,v)=\operatorname{atanh}u-\operatorname{atanh}v,
\qquad
\partial_vd(u,v)=\frac{v-u}{1-v^2}.
\end{equation}
The exact binary field is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\dot x_i={}&(1-x_i^2)\left[-\operatorname{atanh}x_i
-\sum_j\beta_{ij}(\operatorname{atanh}x_i-\operatorname{atanh}x_j)\right]\\
&+\sum_k\beta_{ki}(x_k-x_i).
\end{split}
\label{open:binary-field}
\end{equation}
The model equation is identical and independent. Self summands vanish in this binary equation, consistently with $d(x_i,x_i)=0$.

\lemmaheading{Interior binary evolution}{open:interior}
Every binary initial configuration has a unique global interior solution, and all its means tend to zero.
\begin{proof}
At a maximum of $\vartheta_i=\operatorname{atanh}x_i$, both peer sums in the equation for $\dot\vartheta_i$ are nonpositive; at a minimum they are nonnegative. The finite maximum and minimum satisfy the corresponding one-sided differential inequalities. Scalar comparison yields
\begin{equation}
e^{-t}\min_i\vartheta_i(0)\le\vartheta_i(t)
\le e^{-t}\max_i\vartheta_i(0).
\label{open:logit-bound}
\end{equation}
The same holds for the model logits. The solution stays in a compact interior box, where the smooth field extends it past any proposed finite maximal time. The bounds imply convergence. This does not by itself establish the order of formation events.
\end{proof}

\propositionheading{Two-stage preferential routing}{open:routing}
For $N=6$, some fixed $T>0$ and a nonempty relabeling-invariant open set in $(-1,1)^{12}$ have the following behavior. Every initial off-diagonal native weight is below $1/10$. Three preferential pairs form and coexist for a positive interval. Two of their parents subsequently join while the third remains an exterior with strictly smaller routing masses. The first pairs and the selected parent pair satisfy \cref{open:contrast} over positive windows. All events occur at finite interior times under \cref{open:binary-field}, with no parameter change.
\begin{proof}
Choose $T=h^2$ and write $x_i=hu_i$. Taylor expansion on every fixed compact set gives
\begin{equation}
h^{-2}d(hu,hv)\longrightarrow\frac{(u-v)^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Dividing \cref{open:binary-field} by $h$, without changing time, gives the $C^1$ limiting field
\begin{equation}
\dot u_i=-u_i+\sum_j(w_{ij}+w_{ji})(u_j-u_i),
\qquad
w_{ij}=\frac{e^{-(u_i-u_j)^2/2}}{\sum_k e^{-(u_i-u_k)^2/2}}.
\label{open:limit}
\end{equation}
The self summand one bounds every denominator away from zero. Limit trajectories remain bounded on finite intervals by the same extrema comparison. On a compact trajectory tube, a uniform field error $e_h$ and Lipschitz constant $C$ give deviation at most $e_h(e^{Ct}-1)/C$, with $te_h$ when $C=0$. A sufficiently small error prevents exit from the tube. Thus strict windows and transverse crossings persist for one sufficiently small fixed $h>0$.

For four agents first take the reflection-invariant configuration
\begin{equation}
u=(-a-b,-a+b,a-b,a+b),\qquad a(0)=A,\quad b(0)=2.
\end{equation}
Set $w=e^{-2b^2}$, $v_0=e^{-2a^2}$, $v_\pm=e^{-2(a\pm b)^2}$,
$Z_\pm=1+w+v_0+v_\pm$, and
\begin{equation}
c_p=w(Z_+^{-1}+Z_-^{-1}),
\qquad c_0=v_0(Z_+^{-1}+Z_-^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Substitution into the full field gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\dot a&=-a-2ac_0
-2\left[\frac{(a+b)v_+}{Z_+}+\frac{(a-b)v_-}{Z_-}\right],\\
\dot b&=-b-2bc_p
-2\left[\frac{(a+b)v_+}{Z_+}-\frac{(a-b)v_-}{Z_-}\right].
\end{split}
\label{open:core}
\end{equation}
Both unequal row normalizers are retained.

For $a\ge4$ and $0\le b\le2$, put $c=1+w+v_0\ge1$. The function
$g_c(r)=r e^{-2r^2}/(c+e^{-2r^2})$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
|g_c'(r)|\le(1+4r^2)e^{-2r^2}.
\end{equation}
This bound decreases for $r\ge2$, so
$|g_c(a+b)-g_c(a-b)|\le34be^{-8}$.
Since $0\le c_p\le1$, \cref{open:core} implies
\begin{equation}
-4b\le\dot b\le-b/2,\qquad -2a\le\dot a\le-a.
\label{open:early-bounds}
\end{equation}
For the second inequality one can bound
$-\dot a/a\le1+4e^{-2a^2}+4e^{-2(a-b)^2}<2$.
The positive coordinates cannot first reach their invariant zero axes at finite time.

Take $A\ge4096$. The first time $\tau$ with $b=1/2$ satisfies
$\tau\le2\log4$, and $a(t)\ge A/256\ge16$ through this interval. Initial peer weights are at most $e^{-8}$ and cross weights much smaller. A directed within-pair weight is $p_\pm=w/Z_\pm$. While $b\ge1/2$,
$\dot w\ge w/2$, whereas
\begin{equation}
|\dot v_0+\dot v_\pm|
\le8a^2e^{-2a^2}
+4(a+2)(2a+8)e^{-2(a-2)^2}<1/100
\end{equation}
for $a\ge8$. Both positive terms decrease on that range. Differentiating $p_\pm$ therefore gives $\dot p_\pm>0$. At $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
p_\pm\ge\frac{e^{-1/2}}{1+e^{-1/2}+2e^{-8}}>1/4.
\end{equation}
Each peer threshold is crossed transversely, with exterior mass less than $2e^{-8}$. The contrast is strict at entry.

Until the first time $t_A$ with $a=4$, the pair weights remain above $1/4$ and every cross weight is at most $e^{-8}$. An unwanted parent source column has at most two such entries, excluding a premature join even if one pair has formed before the other. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
t_A\ge\frac12\log(A/4),
\qquad b(t_A)\le2(A/4)^{-1/4}.
\label{open:small-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus the pairs coexist while their residual internal gaps become arbitrarily small.

Starting at $a=4$, compare with the invariant trajectory $b=0$. Its exact inherited cross mass and primitive peer weight are
\begin{equation}
B=\frac{e^{-2a^2}}{1+e^{-2a^2}},\qquad
p=\frac{1-B}{2},\qquad
\dot a=-a(1+4B).
\end{equation}
Here $\dot B=4a^2e^{-2a^2}(1+4B)/(1+e^{-2a^2})^2>0$. The parent threshold is crossed at $a=\sqrt{\log9/2}$. Stop this comparison when $B=1/5$, at a finite time $S\le\log(4/\sqrt{\log4/2})$. Throughout it, $p\ge2/5$ and the primitive exterior mass is at most $1/5$, so first-pair contrast is at least $1/5$. The parent crossing has a positive derivative. Compact margins away from a small crossing interval, and a positive derivative inside it, persist for sufficiently small initial $b$. Choose one finite $A$ using \cref{open:small-gap} and fix $L=t_A+S$.

A third pair makes the second event have a genuine exterior. Add initial coordinates $R-2,R+2$. In the decoupled comparison their center is $Re^{-t}$ and half-gap $b_c$ obeys
\begin{equation}
\dot b_c=-b_c(1+4p_c),\qquad
p_c=\frac{e^{-2b_c^2}}{1+e^{-2b_c^2}},\qquad b_c(0)=2.
\end{equation}
Its threshold is transverse, and by $\log4$ its peer weight exceeds $1/4$.

Subtract $Re^{-t}$ from these last two coordinates. On a radius-one tube about the decoupled trajectories, every separation from the core is at least
$D_R=Re^{-L}-A-6$. Row denominators remain at least one. The Gaussian numerator and its force derivative satisfy
\begin{equation}
|(e^{-r^2/2})'|=|r|e^{-r^2/2},
\qquad |(re^{-r^2/2})'|\le(1+r^2)e^{-r^2/2}.
\end{equation}
The quotient rule, including changed row normalizers and incoming forces, bounds the full six-agent field difference and its first derivatives by
\begin{equation}
e_R\le C_A\sup_{r\ge D_R}(1+r^2)e^{-r^2/2}\longrightarrow0.
\label{open:distant-bound}
\end{equation}
The constant depends on the fixed within-core bound, not on $R$. Along moving trajectories the extra derivative is also controlled, since
$Re^{-t}|r|\le(|r|+A+6)|r|$.
A finite-time difference estimate in these moving coordinates prevents exit and preserves all crossings when $R$ is sufficiently large and finite. Choose it so that every aggregated mass between the core and the third pair is below $10^{-3}$ through $L$. At the core-parent crossing the contrast is then greater than $0.099>1/20$; the third pair cannot join either core block or their union.

Fix this $R$, then take one sufficiently small $h>0$ with $h|u_i|<1$ on the compact tube. The exact scaled binary field inherits the strict windows. Choose equal sector trajectories only as a reference, then perturb all twelve initial coordinates independently. Continuous dependence preserves the windows and the positive crossing derivatives. Each pair's event is the latest of its four directional-sector crossings, before the next separated window. The means can be initially distinct; their invariant equality diagonals and uniqueness preclude a first finite collision. Finally take the union of all population permutations of this open neighborhood. The initial configuration chooses the eventual blocks, while the constitutive data contain no block labels.
\end{proof}

\section{A full open domain of correlated laws}

The binary result embeds exactly into \cref{open:full-field}. For arbitrary positive angular profiles, the product reference family
\begin{equation}
q_i(z,\theta)=\mu_i(z)f_i(\theta),\qquad
s_i(w,\psi)=\eta_i(w)g_i(\psi)
\label{open:product-reference}
\end{equation}
is invariant. The full force separates into its binary part and $2\log f_i$, so
\begin{equation}
\partial_tf_i=-2f_i(\log f_i-\E_{f_i}\log f_i),
\qquad
f_i(\theta,t)=
\frac{f_i(\theta,0)^{e^{-2t}}}
{\int f_i(\psi,0)^{e^{-2t}}d\psi/(2\pi)}.
\label{open:angular-flow}
\end{equation}
The model formula is identical. This proves an exact reference trajectory, not an initial restriction for the following statement.

\propositionheading{Open-law transfer}{open:open-laws}
The routing behavior of \cref{open:routing} persists on a nonempty open set of independently variable positive $C^r$ belief and model densities on $\{-1,1\}\times S^1$, including correlated bit-angle laws.
\begin{proof}
Choose angular reference profiles with nonzero first circular moments through $[0,L]$, for example von Mises profiles
$p_\kappa(\theta-a)=e^{\kappa\cos(\theta-a)}/I_0(\kappa)$ with $\kappa>0$.
Equation \eqref{open:angular-flow} sends their concentrations to $\kappa e^{-2t}$ and keeps their phases fixed. The product trajectory has positive density and moment lower bounds on the finite horizon. The field in \cref{open:full-field} is locally Lipschitz on the full density chart, not merely on the product family. A compact regular tube gives a common constant $C$. Initial deviations smaller than its radius times $e^{-CL}/2$ remain inside the tube by the integral difference estimate. Rows, their time derivatives, and the finite block sums depend continuously on these densities. Shrinking this full neighborhood preserves the strict windows and crossing signs. Its perturbations independently change all densities and admit arbitrary sufficiently small bit-angle correlations.
\end{proof}

\section{Physical compression and inherited dynamics}

For an angle $a$, let $(\tau_af)(z,\theta)=f(z,\theta-a)$. Simultaneously rotate each agent's belief and model by its own constant $a_i$. The full energy, metric, rows, and \cref{open:full-field} are invariant or equivariant under this physical $(S^1)^N$ action. It changes laws in fixed physical coordinates; an absolute-angle sample distinguishes the change. It is different from a passive re-description.

For a block $I$ discard the subgroup
\begin{equation}
\mathsf H_I=\{(a_i)_{i\in I}:\sum_{i\in I}a_i=0\pmod{2\pi}\}.
\label{open:physical-group}
\end{equation}
Its dimension is $|I|-1$. A quotient alone does not give the required contextual statistical realization, so retain a whole local contextual section with fixed anchor $c_*$. Suppose $u_i(c_*)=\E_{q_i(c_*)}e^{i\theta}\ne0$, choose compatible local phase lifts, and define
\begin{equation}
\vartheta_i=\arg u_i(c_*),\qquad
\phi_I=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}\vartheta_i,\qquad
a_i=\phi_I-\vartheta_i.
\label{open:anchor}
\end{equation}
Apply $a_i$ to the entire section, independently of evaluation context:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde q_i(c)=\tau_{a_i}q_i(c),\qquad
\widetilde s_i(c)=\tau_{a_i}s_i(c),\qquad
q_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\widetilde q_i,\quad
s_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\widetilde s_i.
\label{open:parent-law}
\end{equation}
These are actual normalized parent laws. Their factors are recovered by marginalization. The relative phase profiles $\arg u_i(c)-\arg u_i(c_*)$ remain retained; only constant relative offsets are discarded. On this chart the fibers are precisely the constant $\mathsf H_I$ orbits.

Parent references and evaluators are products of their normalized primitive data. For each primitive port $i\in I,j\in J$, project a source-parent sample onto $j$, apply \cref{open:channel}, and compare with factor $i$. This is a normalized inherited channel. A full-parent version appends independent recipient factors on the other coordinates:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{IJ}^{ij}(\boldsymbol k_J,d\boldsymbol k'_I)
=\mathcal K(k_j,dk'_i)
\bigotimes_{\ell\in I\setminus\{i\}}\widetilde q_\ell(dk'_\ell).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\KL(q_I\|q_J\mathcal L_{IJ}^{ij})=D^q_{ij}$ by cancellation of the matching normalized factors. If this representation is differentiated, its recipient-fill channel derivatives must be retained. It represents the original primitive sums and rows, not one newly fitted parent KL cost.

At a join of $I,J$, rotate their factors further by $\phi_{I\cup J}-\phi_I$ and $\phi_{I\cup J}-\phi_J$, where
\begin{equation}
\phi_{I\cup J}=
\frac{|I|\phi_I+|J|\phi_J}{|I|+|J|}.
\label{open:phase-recursion}
\end{equation}
Each primitive total rotation becomes $\phi_{I\cup J}-\vartheta_i$, so direct and sequential retention agree. Primitive counts are inherited. Since
$\mathsf H_I\times\mathsf H_J\subsetneq\mathsf H_{I\cup J}$,
this join discards one further continuous relative block orientation. Native row trajectories and all selected event times are unchanged by these rotations, so recording the times does not recover the discarded offsets.

\propositionheading{Original-time parent flow}{open:parent-flow}
On the regular local chart the retained network of sections has an autonomous flow intertwining the original dynamics at the same time.
\begin{proof}
Equivariance and uniqueness imply that two sections related by a constant $\mathsf H_I$ rotation remain so for their common existence interval. This defines the quotient flow, the projectability condition underlying exact lumpability \citep{HorstmeyerAtay2016}. An explicit representative equation is also available. Let
\begin{equation}
\omega_i=\operatorname{Im}
\frac{\int e^{i\theta}V_i^q(c_*)d\nu}{u_i(c_*)},
\qquad
\overline\omega_I=|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}\omega_i.
\end{equation}
This ratio is invariant under rotation and is computable from retained factors. Since $\dot a_i=\overline\omega_I-\omega_i$ and $\partial_a\tau_af=-\partial_\theta\tau_af$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\widetilde q_i
=V_i^q(\widetilde x)
-(\overline\omega_I-\omega_i)\partial_\theta\widetilde q_i,
\label{open:moving-flow}
\end{equation}
with the same coefficient for $\widetilde s_i$. The product rule gives the parent-law derivative. Every term is retained. Successive correction coefficients telescope by \cref{open:phase-recursion}. For finite $C^r$ regularity the displayed derivative is in $C^{r-1}$; existence and uniqueness follow from the fine flow and equivariance, without assuming rotations are differentiable as maps $C^r\to C^r$.
\end{proof}

The Fisher metric of this canonical product slice need not be the kinetic quotient metric. For two location families with inertias $I_1,I_2>0$ and retained total phase $F$, minimizing lift norm subject to $\dot\vartheta_1+\dot\vartheta_2=\dot F$ gives
\begin{equation}
ds_{\rm quotient}^2=\frac{dF^2}{I_1^{-1}+I_2^{-1}},
\qquad
ds_{\rm slice}^2=\frac{I_1+I_2}{4}dF^2.
\label{open:two-metrics}
\end{equation}
They differ unless the inertias agree. The exact parent update is \cref{open:moving-flow}, not an uncorrected unit-Fisher gradient of a new single parent KL.

\section{Responses and retained histories}

Declare one recursive response rule before any selection. A query to current block $I$ draws independent constituent samples $(Z_i,\Theta_i)\sim q_i$ and returns
\begin{equation}
\left((Z_i)_{i\in I},\ \sum_{i\in I}\Theta_i\pmod{2\pi}\right).
\label{open:response}
\end{equation}
The model sector has its corresponding independent joint response. Native source draws, transmitted bit samples, and joint measurable functions of whole-block outputs are also admitted. A policy may depend on past records, but cannot split a compressed block to reveal an individual absolute angle. Queries are observational and do not reset the law state.

The kernel in \cref{open:response} is a normalized pushforward of a product law. Since $\sum_i a_i=0$, it is exactly the same pushforward of the parent law in \cref{open:parent-law}. Its first angular moment is $\prod_i u_i$, generally nonzero; a physical aggregate angle remains observable. Joining concatenates bit vectors and adds angle sums. Thus the same experiment composes at each level.

Equal retained states have equal waiting-time evolution and equal next-record joint kernels. Conditioning successively on the realized history proves equality of every finite adaptive joint-history law. Prior records are carried forward. A finite collection of sampled angular records does not deterministically identify the erased offsets: all the densities are positive, leaving continuously many compatible configurations for the same finite history. An exact record of constituent laws would instead invalidate the loss assertion. An individual absolute-angle query also fails descent immediately because its first moment changes under \cref{open:physical-group}. These experiments are outside the declared interface.

\section{Collective geometry on actual parent laws}

Take the common collective group to be $S^1$, acting on every belief and model angle simultaneously. It commutes with the independent physical rotations. Use the original sum Fisher metric and convention $D^A p=dp+J_pA$. At fixed time the constants $a_i$ in \cref{open:anchor} do not vary with evaluation context. Hence $d\widetilde q_i=\tau_{a_i}dq_i$ and similarly for $s_i$. A product score is a sum of centered independent factor scores, so all cross terms vanish. Rotation preserves the remaining integrals. For every contextual direction $X$ and scalar gauge velocity $\xi$ this proves
\begin{equation}
\overline g(dRpX+\overline J\xi,dRpX+\overline J\xi)
=g(dpX+J\xi,dpX+J\xi).
\label{open:cost-equality}
\end{equation}
Equality holds before minimization. The orbit inertia, contextual momentum, minimizing connection class, and minimized tensor are therefore identical. On a regular chart their curvature agrees. This is a calculation from normalized laws, rather than assigning the fine tensor to retained metadata. It is consistent with fixed-statistic Fisher sufficiency results \citep{AyJostLeSchwachhofer2015}, but the present retention is section valued.

Under a passive common rotation $\chi_0(c)$, each anchor phase shifts by $\chi_0(c_*)$, leaving every $a_i$ unchanged. Parent sections transform by the same $\chi_0(c)$, and $A'=A-d\chi_0$ gives the usual covariant derivative. The local Lie-algebra stabilizer is preserved. Globally the total phase has $|I|$ roots; a common rotation $2\pi/|I|$ lies in the discarded group. A single global root representative and equality of finite stabilizer groups are not asserted. The construction stays on compatible local charts.

A nonflat family accompanies the routing trajectory. Define
\begin{equation}
p_\kappa(\theta)=\frac{e^{\kappa\cos\theta}}{I_0(\kappa)},
\qquad
\mathcal I(\kappa)=\kappa\frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)}.
\end{equation}
Here $I_0$ normalizes Haar measure and $I_1/I_0=\E_{p_\kappa}\cos\theta$.
Integrating the derivative of $\sin\theta p_\kappa(\theta)$ around the circle gives rotation inertia
$\E_{p_\kappa}[\kappa^2\sin^2\theta]=\mathcal I(\kappa)$.
For $\kappa>0$ this mean cosine is positive, and its derivative is
$\operatorname{Var}_{p_\kappa}(\cos\theta)>0$, so $\mathcal I,\mathcal I'>0$.

Use contexts $(\kappa,\chi)$, belief factors $p_\kappa(\theta-\chi)$, and model factors $p_\lambda(\psi)$ with fixed $\lambda>0$. Binary coordinates are initially context independent. Put $\rho_t=e^{-2t}$,
$a_t=\mathcal I(\rho_t\kappa)$ and $b_t=\mathcal I(\rho_t\lambda)$. Parity makes concentration and rotation scores orthogonal. For a block of primitive count $n$ the common-gauge cost in its angular direction is
