\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
An agent holds beliefs and generative models, receives records, makes predictions, and changes its laws. We formulate these objects on associated statistical bundles, with independent directed interactions that may depend on the current belief and model laws. On regular statistical families, the joint-law Fisher metric and a connection induce a covariant pullback tensor on the contextual base. A collective least-squares construction selects a connection class from declared comparison data. Exact identities distinguish information lost under a channel from changes caused by incompatible connections, and characterize when a parent retention preserves the collective connection; when both levels use collective minimizers, the two retention identities reduce to one.

Interacting agents admit three objectives, which we keep separate. In a global free energy all agents are parts of one normalized joint, and local updates are coordinate descent of one functional. In per-agent free energies each agent's generative model treats its peers' communicated laws as competing sources of prior information. With Gaussian references, affine evaluators, linear-Gaussian channels, and log-quadratic records, both free-energy flows are tangent to the Gaussian product family. A reciprocal law potential generates coupled Fisher dynamics in which sources also move toward their receivers; with identity channels its flow leaves the Gaussian family as soon as a source is attended by a receiver with a different law. With fixed positive source rows on finite alphabets, we prove that this potential is not an affine function of the free energy of any normalized model over finitely many finite-valued variables, under any recognition rule, when some attended edge is an identity channel, and that with fixed channels that are not constant in their input it is not, on any open set, an affine function of the free energy of a normalized model whose recognition is the product of the agent laws. Constant-output channels, countable auxiliary latent variables, and coupled recognition on atomless spaces give exact identifications, so the separation rests on these hypotheses. Conditional-information identities describe communication, common recovery, and the cost of discarding record history. Parent dynamics and boundary responses must descend through the retained configuration.

A two-agent conditional-prediction experiment gives an exact interacting record model before posterior projection. Each speaker samples its result prediction from private evidence and a shared transcript; the receiver conditions on that actual mechanism. The joint private-record posterior is a product tilt of the fixed preparation coupling, so its cross-agent dependence never changes and, with the preparation fixed, its two marginals determine it. With a fixed reconstruction of represented states it preserves correlated model/content beliefs and the declared future communication laws. An affine Gaussian specialization with one presentation closes exactly, while finite Gaussian-mixture closure with several presentations and retention of local beliefs alone can fail. The common preparation and protocol are supplied, and the resulting fixed-history inference is distinct from the population flows.

A formation standard requires an open admitted class, selection by a selector family fixed before the flows are analyzed, exact inheritance, compression of a distinction that a declared child-level probe resolves, interaction necessity tested against two null flows, one keeping the deleted peer labels and one renormalizing the source rows, and reported beside a frozen-environment control, and a second stage that does not form from every handoff in a declared counterfactual class. An anchored linear consensus calibrates it: it passes interaction necessity and fails the counterfactual clause. Under the reciprocal potential, a profile construction preserves coupled identity-channel dynamics and a declared response interface through arbitrary finite binary depth on a supplied family; it is an equivariant symmetry reduction and makes no selection claim. A four-agent Wishart construction selects two parents and then their union with exact original-time inheritance, but its membership schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the prepared initial shapes at every coupling, the renormalized null reproduces it, integrations indicate that the label-preserving null, whose clocks are agent dependent already at zero coupling, changes it on an open set, and the union forms from every counterfactual handoff in the domain of the selector; it is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance. A six-agent construction admits a full open set of positive bit-circle laws, with preferential pairs, a later parent join, inherited moving-representative dynamics, adaptive response histories, and, on a declared contextual family with tied belief and model rotations, nonflat rank-two contextual geometry on a finite window. Its orientation loss is relative to the supplied interface, its block relations are transient, and interaction necessity and counterfactual dependence are not established; integrations of its limit field indicate that the null flow forms the same blocks. It is not shown to be a recursive formation. A nonsmooth extension gives finite-time fusion with exact inheritance; its explicit coalescence order is a coupling-independent linkage of the initial data, it meets interaction necessity only through its exact-equality selector, and fused parents compress nothing, so it is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance. A gated erasure construction is a single-stage selection whose interaction necessity is not determined.

For the normalized per-agent free energies, a separate construction gives persistent grouping against conflicting records; its small-signal limit is the anchored opinion pooling of Friedkin and Johnsen. Its interaction-necessity verdict depends on the declared selector constant: it holds against both nulls for ratio thresholds in $(1/8,1/3)$ and fails for thresholds in $(1/3,1)$, and the frozen-environment control reproduces the selected sequence. An angular extension admits a parent that quotients a hidden phase and preserves the original-time port dynamics, every admitted finite adaptive response-record history, and the actual collective connection and contextual tensor on a nonflat finite-window family. The quotiented phase is gauge relative to every declared observable algebra, so the retention fails compression; the construction is a single-stage selection with exact inheritance, and no second compressive handoff is established. No construction in this manuscript is proved to satisfy every clause of the formation standard.

A collective phase quotient preserves fixed relative probes and admits exact local two-jet evolution with finite reference memory. The common orientation it erases is gauge relative to every declared algebra, so this whole-block retention also fails compression and gives a single-stage selection with exact inheritance. A finite preparation generator gives another conditional realization. A causal local phase accumulator obeys the original-time native dynamics and preserves every admitted history; actual geometric inheritance is proved under initial collective-phase coherence across context, a sufficient condition that is not shown to be necessary. Relative to the declared marked structure group, scoped obstructions exclude order-zero rotation-invariant value-path retentions and smooth bounded-jet causal memories on a declared rich preparation class. The unrestricted causal-local-parent question remains inconclusive; neither interaction-created invisibility nor a second compressive handoff is proved.

A separate contextual extension adds to the reciprocal potential an auxiliary alignment energy that couples neighboring laws in declared finite-dimensional regular families. On finite graphs, a regular minimizing branch gives an explicit Fisher force and local uniqueness; the reduced cost equals the minimized cost only while the branch stays globally minimizing, and for general linear frames of dimension at least three the branch hypothesis excludes consensus contexts. On a supplied flat torus, a free proper isometric action reduces the contextual cost to a quotient Dirichlet energy. The resulting shape PDE and frame and source-row ODEs have a local Sobolev solution with exact total-energy dissipation, and no locally gauge-invariant eliminated energy diffuses frames. These results retain fixed-reference forces that need not descend to the quotient. They establish no global regularity, singular-orbit passage, independent row or link diffusion, or graph-refinement limit.

A separate geometric construction builds base transport from regular contextual law, interface, and directed-link data once comparison labels, columns, weights, and a metric are declared. A spanning message matrix gives displacement identity, affine connection covariance, an explicit curvature formula, and holonomy orthogonal for the induced metric. Local alignment under a declared bi-invariant metric gives a curved example from reciprocal rotation links, with Abelian holonomy. A single marked square frame instead gives flat local transport. Link-only alignment requires an invariant target metric, which conjugation targets of the general linear group do not admit, continuous invisible gauge sectors obstruct a link-only selector, and no new evolution or inheritance result is asserted.

Selective communication introduces a further distinction between the transmitted code and the invertible dictionary used to interpret it. Conditional-information identities quantify reconstruction loss for general laws. With a known code, grounded messages identify dictionaries modulo code stabilizers, while an anchored loop prior couples their unread directions. A Gaussian tangent model gives exact recognition formulas and separates global, receiver-owned, and reciprocal link dynamics; for its three-agent reference data the coupled reciprocal flow has a local subcritical pitchfork. Integrating out Gaussian contents produces a quadratic cost of an affine coded residual on reinterpretations, with a record-dependent linear term. Its relation to directed holonomy requires explicit cycle hypotheses, and its cohomological form holds in the small-noise limit at fixed anchors and, within the tangent model, in the weak-anchor limit. Supplied rigid clusters admit a restricted parent description under normalization and connectivity conditions; this reduction supplies no new formation theorem.

\section*{Reading the construction}
The mathematical objects are introduced in the order in which they are needed: agent laws, generative models and records, interactions, statistical and contextual geometry, free energies and dynamics, predictive compression, inherited organization, a contextual-coupling extension, and base transport derived from contextual agent data. The final three technical chapters develop selective communication, latent dictionary learning, and curvature costs in supplied hierarchies. The main results include their decisive proofs. The appendices give the internal history construction, conditional prediction and generative memory, exact interaction between conditional predictors, directional links and base connections, an optional determinant-metric and circle example, and notation. Secondary calculations are collected in a separate supplement.

Definitions specify the model's objects; constitutive choices specify how those objects interact. A theorem states a consequence of explicit hypotheses. An open problem identifies a further construction that is not supplied by those hypotheses. General probability measures are used whenever measurability suffices. Finite alphabets, smooth statistical families, and vector fibers enter only where an example, a differential calculation, or a stated hypothesis requires them; the obstruction to a global free-energy identification is one such hypothesis. The general definitions assume no Gaussian closure; the fusion example states its lognormal restriction explicitly.
