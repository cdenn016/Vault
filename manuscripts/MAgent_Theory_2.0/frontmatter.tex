\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
An agent holds beliefs and generative models, receives records, makes predictions, and changes its laws. We formulate these objects on associated statistical bundles, with independent directed interactions that may depend on the current belief and model laws. On regular statistical families, the joint-law Fisher metric and a connection induce a covariant pullback tensor on the contextual base. A collective least-squares construction selects a connection class from declared comparison data. Exact identities distinguish information lost under a channel from changes caused by incompatible connections, and characterize when a parent retention preserves the collective connection.

Interacting agents admit three objectives, which we keep separate. In a global free energy all agents are parts of one normalized joint, and local updates are coordinate descent of one functional. In per-agent free energies each agent's generative model treats its peers' communicated laws as competing sources of prior information. A reciprocal law potential generates coupled Fisher dynamics in which sources also move toward their receivers; we prove that it is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws. Conditional-information identities describe communication, common recovery, and the cost of discarding record history. Parent dynamics and boundary responses must descend through the retained configuration.

A formation standard requires selection, inheritance, compression, interaction necessity checked against a null flow, and a second stage that depends on the first. The existence constructions, all under the reciprocal potential, are measured against it. A profile construction preserves coupled identity-channel dynamics and a declared response interface through arbitrary finite binary depth on a supplied family; it is an equivariant symmetry reduction. A four-agent Wishart construction selects two parents and then their union with exact original-time inheritance, but its membership schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the prepared initial shapes and is the same at zero coupling. A six-agent construction admits a full open set of positive bit-circle laws, with preferential pairs, a later parent join, exact inherited moving-representative dynamics, declared adaptive response histories, and, on a declared contextual family with one group acting on belief and model angles, rank-two contextual geometry with nonzero curvature over a finite window. Its orientation loss is relative to the declared response interface, and in the regime of its proof the null flow reproduces its membership history. A separate nonsmooth extension gives finite-time fusion in a lognormal family; its grouping requires the interaction, but fused parents compress nothing. Each construction is classified as a single-stage or two-stage selection with exact inheritance, with the failed clauses stated. A construction meeting every clause of the standard remains open.

\section*{Reading the construction}
The mathematical objects are introduced in the order in which they are needed: agent laws, generative models and records, interactions, statistical and contextual geometry, free energies and dynamics, predictive compression, and inherited organization. The main results include their decisive proofs. The appendices give the internal history construction, directional links and base connections, an optional determinant-metric and circle example, and notation. Secondary calculations are collected in a separate supplement.

Definitions specify the model's objects; constitutive choices specify how those objects interact. A theorem states a consequence of explicit hypotheses. An open problem identifies a further construction that is not supplied by those hypotheses. General probability measures are used whenever measurability suffices. Finite alphabets, smooth statistical families, and vector fibers enter only where an example or a differential calculation requires them. The general definitions assume no Gaussian closure; the fusion example states its lognormal restriction explicitly.
