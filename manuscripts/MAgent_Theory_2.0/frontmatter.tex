\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
An agent carries a local probability law and mechanisms for receiving records, making predictions, and changing that law. We formulate these objects on associated statistical bundles, with independent directed interactions that may depend on the current belief and model laws. On regular statistical families, the joint-law Fisher metric and a connection induce a covariant pullback tensor on the contextual base. A collective least-squares construction selects a connection class from declared comparison data. Exact identities distinguish information lost under a channel from changes caused by incompatible connections, and characterize when a parent retention preserves the collective connection.

A declared interaction potential generates coupled Fisher dynamics. Conditional-information identities describe communication, common recovery, and the cost of discarding record history. Parent dynamics and boundary responses must descend through the retained configuration. Exact constructions separate state completion, public compression, and response closure. An atomless profile construction preserves coupled identity-channel dynamics and a declared response interface through arbitrary finite binary depth on a supplied family, while its discarded partition information has no Fisher tangent.

A conditional four-agent construction under the original smooth KL potential selects two parents and then their union. It uses non-Gaussian matrix laws with prepared common precision, moment-dependent channels, a fixed membership threshold, and fresh extensive responses. A separate six-agent construction admits a full open set of positive bit-circle laws. Native preferential pairs form before two parents join against a nonempty exterior. Fixed selective channels and anchored contextual profiles permit continuous physical orientation loss while preserving exact inherited dynamics, declared adaptive response histories, and rank-two contextual geometry with nonzero curvature. The children remain distinct at finite times. These results assume their comparison architectures and interfaces; they do not derive a preferred selector or newly created redundancy. The latter parents use an inherited moving-representative flow, and their persistence is proved on finite windows. A separate nonsmooth extension gives finite-time fusion in a lognormal family.

\section*{Reading the construction}
The mathematical objects are introduced in the order in which they are needed: agent laws and records, interactions, statistical and contextual geometry, dynamics, predictive compression, and inherited organization. The main results include their decisive proofs. The appendices give the internal history construction, directional links and base connections, an optional determinant-metric and circle example, and notation. Secondary calculations are collected in a separate supplement.

Definitions specify the model's objects; constitutive choices specify how those objects interact. A theorem states a consequence of explicit hypotheses. An open problem identifies a further construction that is not supplied by those hypotheses. General probability measures are used whenever measurability suffices. Finite alphabets, smooth statistical families, and vector fibers enter only where an example or a differential calculation requires them. The general definitions assume no Gaussian closure; the fusion example states its lognormal restriction explicitly.
