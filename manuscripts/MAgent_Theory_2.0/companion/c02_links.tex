\chapter{Frames, Messages, and Interactions}
\label{comp:links}

An agent's probability law and the coordinates used to describe that law are different objects. Communication introduces a third object: a rule for translating, filtering, or modifying what another agent supplies. Keeping these distinctions explicit prevents a coordinate change from being mistaken for an interaction, or a selective message from being mistaken for an invertible transport.

\section{Reading a link from source to receiver}

There are two sectors. Write $\nu_i^q=q_i$ for the belief marginal and $\nu_i^s=s_i$ for the model marginal. In a finite-dimensional realization, a sector $a\in\{q,s\}$ has a reference vector space $V_a$, an agent space $E_i^a$, and an invertible presentation
\begin{equation}
U_i^a:V_a\longrightarrow E_i^a,\qquad
\bar\nu_i^a=(U_i^a)^{-1}_\#\nu_i^a.
\end{equation}
The pushforward notation expresses the represented possibilities in reference coordinates. The space $E_i^a$ is agent $i$'s displayed copy of $V_a$: in this linear realization, at one context all agents' laws lie in one fiber of the statistical bundle, and the presentations are frames used to display them; in general the agents' laws lie in fibers over one point of bundles associated to one principal bundle. The two sectors carry two representations of one frame group, with different dimensions, laws, and interaction rules: a belief vector rotates by $\varrho_q(g)$ and a model presentation by $\varrho_s(g)$ under the same change of frame $g$. Think of one set of axes carried by the agent, with beliefs and models as two different kinds of object written on those axes. Each edge can carry a belief link and a model link, both valued in the same group. When they agree the edge transports beliefs and models the same way; when they differ, the conjugacy class of the mismatch $\tilde\theta_{ij}\theta_{ij}^{-1}$ is a property of the edge that no change of frame removes. If a representation is not faithful, two different group links can display the same transport, and the channels see the mismatch only modulo the kernels of the two representations. The collective geometry of \cref{comp:geometry} uses this single group.

A law-dependent reference generator and its displayed link are
\begin{equation}
L_{ij}^a=\mathcal L_{ij}^a(\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a),\qquad
\Theta_{ij}^a=U_i^a e^{L_{ij}^a}(U_j^a)^{-1}.
\label{eq:comp-link}
\end{equation}
The index order is receiver first, source second. Reading from right to left, the source is expressed in reference coordinates, the generator acts there, and the result is expressed at the receiver. Here $\bar{\boldsymbol\nu}^a$ is the tuple of all reference laws in the sector; dependence on the two endpoint laws alone is a special case. The function $\mathcal L_{ij}^a$ is a constitutive choice. The exponential parameterization does not derive it or equate the belief and model generators. It is also only one kind of interaction. The general interaction is a normalized Markov channel, possibly noisy or information-discarding; the exponential link is the special deterministic channel $y\mapsto\Theta_{ij}^ay$. Its values $e^{L}$ lie in $\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(V_a))$, which in dimension at least two misses some determinant-one matrices, such as $\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$, and every reflection \citep{Culver1966}.

\section{Changing coordinates}

For a passive change $y_i'=g_i^a y_i$, both the law and its presentation change: $\nu_i^{a\prime}=(g_i^a)_\#\nu_i^a$ and $U_i^{a\prime}=g_i^aU_i^a$. The reference law stays fixed, giving
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{ij}^{a\prime}=g_i^a\Theta_{ij}^a(g_j^a)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This endpoint covariance is familiar from gauge link variables \citep{Wilson1974}, here in display coordinates. It holds for every generator rule, because the reference laws do not change, and so it constrains nothing: the frames act as a dressing field \citep{FournelEtAl2014}. Every interaction cost can be computed entirely in reference coordinates. Metrics and additional model data must also be re-expressed when forming scalar predictions. Covariance alone supplies no physical symmetry or evolution law.

Changing the common reference presentation is a separate operation. Under $U_i^a\mapsto U_i^a h_a$, the reference laws become $(h_a^{-1})_\#\bar\nu_i^a$. Reference consistency requires the constitutive rule, including any interface data, to give $L_{ij}^{a\prime}=h_a^{-1}L_{ij}^a h_a$. The identity $e^{h_a^{-1}Lh_a}=h_a^{-1}e^Lh_a$ then leaves the displayed link unchanged. This requirement is easy to meet in one sense: any rule passes once each ingredient that singles out directions, such as a fixed metric or a fixed code, is declared as data and re-expressed together with the laws. That is bookkeeping. The substantive symmetry is active: holding the rule and all its data fixed, the interaction commutes with a common change of frame. The moment-dependent links below have it with no extra data, because the covariance supplies the metric; fixed codes and fixed lab directions do not, and they reduce the symmetry to the subgroup that fixes them. The main text names which of the two properties a result uses.

\section{Selective interfaces and an explicit pair}

Sending and receiving can be represented by
\begin{equation}
T_j^a:V_a\longrightarrow H_a,\qquad
O_i^a:H_a\longrightarrow V_a,\qquad
L_{ij}^a=O_i^aT_j^a.
\end{equation}
The sender $T_j^a$ extracts a message; the receiver $O_i^a$ inserts its effect. A direct content map $O_i^aT_j^a$ can lose dimensions. Its exponential is always invertible and acts as identity on $\ker T_j^a$. Thus exponentiating a low-rank generator retains unread directions, and the link compares complete laws. Sending only the extracted message is a different, information-discarding operation. General normalized Markov kernels allow communication without any linear realization.

One explicit law-dependent choice uses finite second moments and positive-definite covariance. Let $\mu_i^a,\Sigma_i^a$ be the reference mean and covariance. On nonself edges choose a scalar $\lambda_{ij}^a$ and set
\begin{equation}
L_{ij}^a=\lambda_{ij}^a\mu_i^a(\mu_j^a)^T(\Sigma_j^a)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The row $(\mu_j^a)^T(\Sigma_j^a)^{-1}$ sends a scalar message; the column $\lambda_{ij}^a\mu_i^a$ receives it. Under a reference change, the mean transforms by $h_a^{-1}$ and covariance by congruence. Their product consequently transforms by conjugation. These assumptions concern moments, not Gaussian shape. For these links the primitive self link is set to the identity separately. In general the self channel is constitutive data, and some later constructions use a nonidentity one.

For a concrete non-Gaussian example, let $Z$ have independent centered Laplace coordinates of variance one in two dimensions. Give two agents the laws of $e_1+Z$ and $e_2+Z$, with identity frames and unit coefficients. Then $\Sigma_1=\Sigma_2=I$, $L_{12}=E_{12}$, and $L_{21}=E_{21}$. Since each generator squares to zero,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{12}\Theta_{21}
=(I+E_{12})(I+E_{21})
=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here $E_{ab}$ has its only nonzero entry, one, in row $a$, column $b$. The directed communication loop is nontrivial. It establishes neither a connection over contexts nor a physical curvature. Writing $\Theta_{12}=\Omega_{12}N_2^{1/2}$ with $N_2=\Theta_{21}\Theta_{12}$, the reciprocal part $\Omega_{12}$ is here a rotation with $\Omega_{12}\Omega_{21}=I$, so the original two-cycle is carried entirely by the backtrack factor.

\Cref{comp:link-base-transport} turns smooth contextual fields of such laws, interfaces, and links into displacement transport under stated rank or identifiability assumptions. The additional comparison rule and contextual derivatives distinguish that construction from a link at one context.

A further limit concerns a comparison that reads a loop map $H$ only through the transported law $H_\#\nu$. If $H$ fixes the law, as a rotation fixes a centered isotropic Gaussian and a half-turn in an eigenplane fixes any centered Gaussian, that comparison cannot register it at that instant. The reciprocal dynamics can still respond later, because the force on the source pulls the receiver's likelihood ratio back through $H$ itself. The joint law of a sample $Y$ and its image $HY$ does determine $H$ almost everywhere. A record comparing the same sample along two routes can therefore resolve distinctions that the marginal laws omit. Grounded messages offer another route: sufficiently informative referents and codes can identify each edge dictionary and hence its loop product. A covariance-dressed matrix defect can also penalize every nonidentity loop, including those that fix the law. Its use as a prior is an additional modeling choice.
