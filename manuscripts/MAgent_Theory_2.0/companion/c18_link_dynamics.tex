\chapter{Learning Directed Links under Three Objectives}
\label{c18:link-dynamics}

A message can change a receiver's belief about the subject or its understanding of the speaker. These are separate variables. If one agent reports a higher temperature than another expects, the receiver may revise its temperature estimate, revise the conversion between their scales, or share the correction between the two. A comparison record alone may not distinguish those responses. The distinction becomes mathematical once the dictionary is included among the uncertain quantities.

\section{What changes when a dictionary changes}

Work at one context in reference coordinates. Agent $i$ represents content $y_i\in\R^K$. On a directed edge $e:j\to i$, the sender has a fixed code $T_e:\R^K\to\R^{n_e}$ of full row rank. The receiver owns an invertible dictionary $\theta_e=e^{\omega_e}$, where $\omega_e$ is a $K\times K$ matrix. According to this dictionary, the receiver's content $y_i$ would be expressed by the sender as $\theta_e^{-1}y_i$. A comparison record therefore reads
\begin{equation}
 o_e=T_e(\theta_e^{-1}y_i-y_j)+\varepsilon_e.
 \label{c18:nonlinear-record}
\end{equation}
The code can discard content coordinates even though the dictionary is invertible. The independently owned dictionary on the opposite attending edge need not be $\theta_e^{-1}$.

Fix an operating content $c_i$ and expand at the identity dictionary. Dropping terms quadratic in $\omega_e$ and $y_i-c_i$ gives
\begin{equation}
 o_e=T_e(y_i-y_j-\kappa_i\omega_e)+\varepsilon_e,
 \qquad \kappa_i\omega_e=\omega_ec_i,
 \qquad \varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,R_e),
 \label{c18:tangent-record}
\end{equation}
with $R_e$ positive definite. The Gaussian noise and tangent approximation make the following calculations explicit; the theory of normalized communication kernels does not require them.

Suppose $c_i\neq0$. For arbitrary content shifts, choose
\begin{equation}
 \delta\omega_e=(\delta y_i-\delta y_j)c_i^T/|c_i|^2.
 \label{c18:compensation}
\end{equation}
Then $\kappa_i\delta\omega_e=\delta y_i-\delta y_j$, so the comparison record has exactly the same law. This is an observational ambiguity of the declared record. Other records and priors can distinguish its two explanations. At $c_i=0$ this first-order dictionary response vanishes, which will matter in the ring example.

The ambiguity is not confined to Gaussian noise. If a record kernel depends only on $T_e(\theta_e^{-1}y_i-y_j)$, every change preserving that discrepancy preserves its law. For $K\geq2$ and dictionaries in $\GL^+(K,\R)$, put $v_e=\theta_e^{-1}y_i+y_j'-y_j$. Whenever $v_e$ and $y_i'$ are nonzero, an orientation-preserving invertible map can send $v_e$ to $y_i'$. Taking that map as $\theta_e'$ preserves the discrepancy. This is a pointwise statement; it does not prove a global nonlinear learning flow or its convergence.

\section{How one record divides the adjustment}

Take independent Gaussian priors for $y_i$, $y_j$, and $\omega_e$, with means $m_i$, $m_j$, $a_e$ and covariances $\Sigma_i^0$, $\Sigma_j^0$, $V_e$. Matrix coordinates of $\omega_e$ use the Frobenius inner product. The uncertainty in its action on the receiver's operating content is $C_\rho=\kappa_iV_e\kappa_i^T$. Define the record residual and its covariance by
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 r&=o_e-T_e(m_i-m_j-\kappa_i a_e),\\
 S&=R_e+T_e(\Sigma_i^0+\Sigma_j^0+C_\rho)T_e^T.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:residual}
\end{equation}
Gaussian conditioning multiplies each variable's covariance with the record by $S^{-1}r$. These cross-covariances have opposite signs for receiver and sender, giving
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta y_i&=\Sigma_i^0T_e^TS^{-1}r,\qquad
 \delta y_j=-\Sigma_j^0T_e^TS^{-1}r,\\
 \kappa_i\delta\omega_e&=-C_\rho T_e^TS^{-1}r.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:shares}
\end{equation}
The receiver adjusts its content, the sender adjusts its content, and the dictionary changes the receiver's predicted message. The noise covariance accounts for the remaining residual, so the three adjustments need not fit the record exactly. With isotropic dictionary uncertainty $V_e=\sigma^2I$, one has $C_\rho=\sigma^2|c_i|^2I$. Reinterpretation can then absorb more of the discrepancy farther from the reference origin.

In a per-agent model, the sender's law is data. Integrating $y_j$ out of \cref{c18:tangent-record} adds $T_e\Sigma_j^0T_e^T$ to the noise covariance. The receiver and dictionary updates in \cref{c18:shares} remain the same for this one-record calculation, but the sender is not updated by the receiver's inference. This exact agreement of two conditional means in the isolated Gaussian example does not equate population dynamics.

Grounded messages provide information about the dictionary by pairing a message with an independently specified referent. If the referents span the content space, their unobserved dictionary directions are $N_e=\{\delta:T_e\delta=0\}$. Give the observed directions precision $gG_e$ with $\ker G_e=N_e$, on top of a fixed positive-definite prior precision. Let $\Pi_e$ project onto $N_e^\perp$. Block inversion then gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_eV_e\Pi_e=O(g^{-1}),\qquad
 T_eC_\rho T_e^T
 =T_e\kappa_i\Pi_eV_e\Pi_e\kappa_i^TT_e^T=O(g^{-1}).
 \label{c18:grounding}
\end{equation}
Grounding suppresses reinterpretation through the identified coordinates. A direction satisfying only $T_e\delta c_i=0$ is invisible to the single operating content, but it can be visible to these varied grounded referents. The two experiments must be kept distinct.

\section{Objective derivatives and actual velocities}

A population model also needs self information and a prior on dictionaries. Give each content a self precision $P_i>0$ about $m_i$, each dictionary an anchor precision $s>0$, and the dictionaries a loop penalty. If $B\omega$ collects sums of generators around a declared finite set of simple directed loops, take the prior precision to be $sI+\lambda B^TWB$, where $W$ is positive definite and $\lambda\geq0$. Grounding adds its edgewise quadratic terms. Combining this proper prior with the Gaussian record likelihoods gives a proper global posterior
\begin{equation}
 p(x\mid o)\propto\exp\{-\tfrac12x^TJx+\eta^Tx\},
 \qquad x=(y,\omega),\quad J>0.
 \label{c18:global}
\end{equation}
The prior is a declared model of loop consistency, not evidence that conversation has measured every loop coordinate.

Group the variables into receiver blocks $x_i=(y_i,\omega_{\operatorname{in}(i)})$ and use product recognition $Q=\bigotimes_i\mathcal N(u_i,S_i)$. The global free energy, up to a constant, is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_G=\tfrac12(u-u^*)^TJ(u-u^*)
 +\tfrac12\sum_i[\Tr(J_{ii}S_i)-\log\det S_i],
 \qquad u^*=J^{-1}\eta.
 \label{c18:global-vfe}
\end{equation}
Thus $S_i=J_{ii}^{-1}$ at its covariance optimum, and the factor means equal the exact posterior mean. This is a feature of the Gaussian target; for non-Gaussian posteriors the factor means generally differ from the exact means. The exact marginal covariance is larger because integrating other blocks subtracts a positive-semidefinite term from the marginal precision. Factorization has removed correlations, not made the underlying variables better measured.

The Fisher metric for a Gaussian mean is its inverse covariance. With these optimized covariances held fixed, put $D=\bigoplus_iJ_{ii}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \dot u=-D^{-1}J(u-u^*).
 \label{c18:global-flow}
\end{equation}
The relaxation matrix is similar to the symmetric positive-definite matrix $D^{-1/2}JD^{-1/2}$. Every mode therefore decays with a real rate. Each record contributes derivatives at the receiver, the sender, and the receiver's dictionary.

The per-agent model contains received records only. In its plug-in version, peers are replaced by their current means. Its dictionary derivative equals the corresponding global derivative because the same edge likelihood, anchor, grounding, and loop factors occur in the owner's model. Its content derivative omits records for which that agent is the sender. Integrating peer laws instead gives the law-level version. For a record and a loop through receiver $i$, its effective covariances and precisions are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde R_e=R_e+T_e\Sigma_jT_e^T,
 \qquad
 \Omega_{i\gamma}=\left[(\lambda W_\gamma)^{-1}
       +\sum_{f\in\gamma\setminus\operatorname{in}(i)}V_f\right]^{-1}.
 \label{c18:widening}
\end{equation}
The loop expression assumes $\lambda>0$ and the declared product of peer dictionary marginals; at $\lambda=0$ the loop term is absent. This widening changes dictionary derivatives as well. With one scalar edge, $T=c=R=\Sigma_j=1$, zero record, unit content difference, and zero dictionary mean, the global record derivative in the dictionary is $-1$, whereas the law-level derivative is $-1/2$.

Even the plug-in equality is an equality of covectors. If $A_i$ is the own-block Hessian of the per-agent model, its optimized Gaussian recognition has covariance $A_i^{-1}$, and its velocity multiplies the derivative by that covariance. The global covariance $J_{ii}^{-1}$ need not equal $A_i^{-1}$; both can mix content and dictionary components. Matching derivative rows alone therefore does not match dictionary velocities.

\section{A ring that cannot be a gradient flow}

Consider $\ell\geq3$ scalar agents, each receiving from its successor. Let the self precision be $p>0$, the effective record precision be $q>0$, the dictionary anchor be $s>0$, and the common operating content be $c$. Omit grounding and loop penalties in this example. The receiver's quadratic energy is
\begin{equation}
 E_i=\tfrac12p(y_i-m_i)^2+\tfrac12s\omega_i^2
      +\tfrac12q(o_i-y_i+y_{i+1}+c\omega_i)^2.
 \label{c18:ring-energy}
\end{equation}
With the Gaussian own-block covariance at its optimum, the Hessian and the off-diagonal coupling are
\begin{equation}
 A=\begin{pmatrix}p+q&-qc\\-qc&s+qc^2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}-q&0\\qc&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{c18:ring-blocks}
\end{equation}
For a Fourier mode $x_i=\zeta^iv$ with $\zeta^\ell=1$, the linear flow is $-(I+\zeta A^{-1}C)v$. The second column of $A^{-1}C$ vanishes, and its first diagonal entry is $-w$, where
\begin{equation}
 w=\frac{qs}{ps+pqc^2+qs}
   =\frac{q_{\mathrm{eff}}}{p+q_{\mathrm{eff}}},
 \qquad q_{\mathrm{eff}}=(q^{-1}+c^2/s)^{-1}.
 \label{c18:ring-coupling}
\end{equation}
The two rates in each Fourier sector are $-1$ and $-(1-\zeta w)$. Since $0<w<1$, all modes decay, but nonreal roots give damped oscillation. The Jacobian of a Riemannian gradient flow at an equilibrium is self-adjoint in the metric there and has real spectrum. This ring therefore supplies a stable per-agent dynamics that cannot be made a gradient flow by choosing another metric, a familiar effect of non-reciprocal coupling \citep{FruchartEtAl2021}.

The formula also quantifies reinterpretation. For $c\neq0$, dictionary uncertainty adds effective variance $c^2/s$, and $w\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$. For $c=0$, however, the dictionary is absent from the record and $w=q/(p+q)$ for every positive $s$. The weak-anchor limit is taken from proper models with $s>0$; it does not assert normalization of a zero-anchor dictionary prior. In a strongly grounded population, the global and plug-in equations instead approach their known-dictionary limits. One retains sender and receiver derivatives and the other retains receiver derivatives only, so their limiting content estimates need not agree. Coupled law-level covariance updates require a separate limit argument.

\section{Why an unread loop can look certain}

Let one unread scalar dictionary coordinate $z_e$ live on each of $\ell\geq2$ edges with different owners. Give each an anchor variance $\sigma^2$ and use one factor $\exp[-\lambda(\sum_ez_e)^2/2]$, with $\lambda>0$. The exact precision and its marginal variance are
\begin{equation}
 J=\sigma^{-2}I+\lambda\mathbf1\mathbf1^T,
 \qquad
 v_{\mathrm{exact}}=\sigma^2\frac{1+(\ell-1)\lambda\sigma^2}
                                      {1+\ell\lambda\sigma^2}.
 \label{c18:loop-exact}
\end{equation}
This follows by inverting the rank-one perturbation. As $\lambda\to\infty$, the sum is pinned but $\ell-1$ independent directions remain, leaving marginal variance $\sigma^2(\ell-1)/\ell$. The constraint permits correlated fluctuations of opposite sign.

Product recognition instead gives variance $(\sigma^{-2}+\lambda)^{-1}$, which tends to zero. Its independent factors cannot carry the compensating fluctuations. In a symmetric law-level per-agent fixed point, each receiver convolves the loop factor with $\ell-1$ peer laws of variance $v$, obtaining
\begin{equation}
 v^{-1}=\sigma^{-2}+[\lambda^{-1}+(\ell-1)v]^{-1}.
 \label{c18:echo}
\end{equation}
Writing $\epsilon=\lambda^{-1}$ turns this into
$(\ell-1)\sigma^{-2}v^2+(\epsilon\sigma^{-2}+2-\ell)v-\epsilon=0$.
Its roots have opposite signs, so it has one positive symmetric solution. For $\ell\geq3$ that solution tends to $\sigma^2(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)$. For two edges it satisfies $v^2+\epsilon v-\epsilon\sigma^2=0$ and hence $v\sim\sigma\lambda^{-1/2}$. Each owner has recycled information that previously reached the partner through the same constraint.

A cavity message excludes the factor to which it is being sent. There is only one factor here, so the peer cavity laws are their anchors. The resulting variance is
\begin{equation}
 v_{\mathrm{cavity}}
 =\left[\sigma^{-2}+(\lambda^{-1}+(\ell-1)\sigma^2)^{-1}\right]^{-1}
 =v_{\mathrm{exact}}.
 \label{c18:cavity}
\end{equation}
This exactness is the tree property of the factor graph with one central factor and $\ell$ variable leaves \citep{Pearl1988}. It is consistent with a loop in the communication graph: the communication graph and factor graph are different objects. Overlapping loop factors need their own analysis. All three approximate schemes here agree on the zero mean, so a point-estimate comparison would miss their disagreement about uncertainty.

\section{The reciprocal potential and source uncertainty}

Now treat the content and dictionary laws as configuration variables of a reciprocal potential. Let $q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ and let the dictionary laws be independent Gaussians $\mathcal N(a_e,V_e)$. For edge $e:j\to i$, compare
\begin{equation}
 m_e^{\mathrm{rec}}(\omega_e)
 =\mathcal N(T_e(\mu_i-\kappa_i\omega_e),S_{i,e}),
 \qquad m_e^{\mathrm{src}}=\mathcal N(T_e\mu_j,S_{j,e}),
 \label{c18:message-laws}
\end{equation}
where $S_{k,e}=R_e+T_e\Sigma_kT_e^T$. Add their expected divergence with weight $\beta_e>0$ to content self divergences and the dictionary divergence from the declared prior. There is no registered comparison record in this potential.

At fixed covariances and fixed weights, its mean-dependent part is a quadratic global energy with zero comparison values and record covariances $S_{j,e}/\beta_e$. Its Gaussian mean flow consequently has real decay rates. This equality of mean quadratics does not equate the complete objectives or their covariance dynamics. Gibbs-optimized rows give the same pointwise derivative with the current weights, but moving weights do not yield the same constant-coefficient flow. A dictionary enters only its edge cost and its prior. A source content enters every edge on which it is heard, producing reciprocal source-role derivatives.

The covariance derivative exposes the distinction. Put $d_e=T_e(\mu_i-\mu_j-\kappa_i a_e)$ and $U_e=T_e\kappa_iV_e\kappa_i^TT_e^T$. The unweighted edge cost has sender-covariance derivative
\begin{equation}
 \frac12T_e^TS_{j,e}^{-1}
       [S_{j,e}-S_{i,e}-d_ed_e^T-U_e]
       S_{j,e}^{-1}T_e.
 \label{c18:source-covariance}
\end{equation}
Indeed, its relevant terms are $[\Tr(S_{j,e}^{-1}(S_{i,e}+d_ed_e^T+U_e))+\log\det S_{j,e}]/2$. Differentiation gives \cref{c18:source-covariance}. The source covariance is compared with the receiver's predicted second moment about the source mean, which includes their disagreement. More generally, let an exponential-family source $q_\eta$ with statistic $t$ be compared through a fixed kernel $\mathcal K$. Under regularity and finite moments, $\partial_\eta\KL(p\Vert q_\eta\mathcal K)=\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_{\tilde p_\eta}t$, where $\tilde p_\eta=\int p(dh)q_\eta(\cdot\mid h)$ is the source's own posterior reconstruction of the compared message law. Source stationarity is therefore the fixed-point condition of an EM step; only for an identity kernel is it the plain moment match $\E_{q_\eta}t=\E_pt$.

There is a precise scalar conclusion. Freeze the means, the receiver variance $v_k$, the noise $R>0$, and the dictionary variance $V_e\geq0$. Let the sender have only its self term of precision $p_j$ and one outgoing edge of unit weight. With $a=v_k+R+d^2+c_k^2V_e$, its stationary variance satisfies
\begin{equation}
 f(v,a)=p_j-v^{-1}+(v+R)^{-1}
                  -a(v+R)^{-2}=0.
 \label{c18:variance-stationary}
\end{equation}
For every $v>0$, $\partial_vf=[R^3+3R^2v+4Rv^2+2(a-R)v^2]/[v^2(v+R)^3]>0$, and $f$ runs from $-\infty$ to $p_j>0$, so there is exactly one stationary variance and it is the global minimizer. Since $\partial_af=-(v+R)^{-2}<0$, implicit differentiation gives $dv/da>0$. This establishes an increasing stationary uncertainty under these frozen conditions. It does not establish a monotone covariance increase along a flow in which means, other edge costs, and source rows all move. In linear-Gaussian inference the optimizing covariance is record-independent because the precision matrix is fixed; non-Gaussian Bayesian uncertainty can depend on the observed data. The coupled Gaussian calculation below allows source selection and all uncertainties to move, while retaining fixed codes, operating contents, and proper references.

\section{When content, interpretation, and attention all move}
\label{c18:coupled}

The expectation defining an edge cost has a general-law meaning. For a dictionary law $r_e$ and normalized message kernels, write $C_e=\E_{r_e}\KL(A_e(\omega_e)\Vert B_e)$, where $A_e$ is the receiver's conditional message law and $B_e$ is the source's law. Changing content changes either argument of the divergence, and changing the dictionary law changes the expectation. Averaging message laws before computing their divergence would erase part of this uncertainty cost. These definitions do not require Gaussian laws; the differentiable dynamics requires regular families and justified differentiation.

Take one content sector, with singleton model-presentation spaces, and set $F_0=\sum_i\KL(q_i\Vert p_i)+\KL(r\Vert\pi_\omega)$, where $r=\bigotimes_e r_e$ is product recognition and $\pi_\omega$ is a fixed proper joint dictionary prior. For each nonempty source set $\mathcal I_i$ choose a positive normalized prior $\pi_i$ and temperature $T_i>0$. The complete row objective is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_T&=F_0+\sum_i\left[\sum_{e\in\mathcal I_i}\beta_eC_e
                         +T_i\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i)\right],\\
 \beta_e^*&=\frac{\pi_e e^{-C_e/T_i}}{\sum_{f\in\mathcal I_i}\pi_f e^{-C_f/T_i}},
 \qquad
 E_T=F_0-\sum_iT_i\log\sum_{e\in\mathcal I_i}\pi_e e^{-C_e/T_i}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:coupled-objectives}
\end{equation}
The row prior and entropy oppose concentrating all attention on the cheapest source. Substituting $\log(\beta_e^*/\pi_e)=-C_e/T_i-\log\sum_f\pi_fe^{-C_f/T_i}$ gives $\mathcal E_T-E_T=\sum_iT_i\KL(\beta_i\Vert\beta_i^*)$ and $dE_T=dF_0+\sum_e\beta_e^*dC_e$. The row entropy is therefore part of the reduced scalar potential, even though its optimized derivative takes the weighted-cost form.

For the Gaussian tangent family, put $K_e=T_e\kappa_i$, $J_e=S_{j,e}^{-1}$, $d_e=T_e(\mu_i-\mu_j)-K_ea_e$, and $Q_e=S_{i,e}+d_ed_e^T+K_eV_eK_e^T$. Then
\begin{equation}
 C_e=\tfrac12[\Tr(J_eQ_e)-n_e+\log\det S_{j,e}-\log\det S_{i,e}],
 \label{c18:coupled-cost}
\end{equation}
where $n_e$ is the message dimension. The uncertainty of the dictionary contributes to the receiver's second moment through $K_eV_eK_e^T$; it does not enter the receiver covariance's log determinant because the conditional divergence was computed first.

Write the fixed joint dictionary prior as $\mathcal N(a_0,A_\omega^{-1})$ with $A_\omega\succ0$, and set $b_\omega=A_\omega a_0$. It may correlate different dictionaries even though recognition factors over edges. With $\operatorname{in}(i)$ and $\operatorname{out}(i)$ denoting receiver and source roles, differentiation gives the complete forces
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 g_{\mu_i}&=P_i(\mu_i-m_i)
 +\sum_{e\in\operatorname{in}(i)}\beta_eT_e^TJ_ed_e
 -\sum_{e\in\operatorname{out}(i)}\beta_eT_e^TJ_ed_e,\\
 g_{a_e}&=(A_\omega a-b_\omega)_e-\beta_eK_e^TJ_ed_e,\\
 2G_{\Sigma_i}&=P_i-\Sigma_i^{-1}
 +\sum_{e\in\operatorname{in}(i)}\beta_eT_e^T(J_e-S_{i,e}^{-1})T_e\\
 &+\sum_{e\in\operatorname{out}(i)}\beta_eT_e^T(J_e-J_eQ_eJ_e)T_e,\\
 2G_{V_e}&=(A_\omega)_{ee}-V_e^{-1}+\beta_eK_e^TJ_eK_e .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:coupled-forces}
\end{equation}
The $G$ symbols are symmetric trace-pairing covariance derivatives. The source term follows from $dJ_e=-J_e(dS_{j,e})J_e$; it includes receiver uncertainty, mean disagreement, and dictionary uncertainty together. The prior's full matrix acts on the dictionary means, while only its diagonal block enters a product factor's covariance derivative.

The Gaussian score metric has mean block $\Sigma_i^{-1}$ and covariance quadratic form $\Tr(\Sigma_i^{-1}\delta\Sigma_i\Sigma_i^{-1}\delta\Sigma_i)/2$. Its inverse converts the forces to velocities:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_i&=-\Sigma_i g_{\mu_i},&
 \dot\Sigma_i&=-2\Sigma_iG_{\Sigma_i}\Sigma_i,\\
 \dot a_e&=-V_eg_{a_e},&
 \dot V_e&=-2V_eG_{V_e}V_e .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:coupled-flow}
\end{equation}
This is the natural-gradient rule applied to the product of the declared Gaussian laws \citep{Amari1998}. At full row relaxation one also evolves
\begin{equation}
 \dot\beta_e=-\beta_e(h_e-\E_{\beta_i}h),\qquad
 h_e=C_e+T_i(\log(\beta_e/\pi_e)+1).
 \label{c18:coupled-rows}
\end{equation}
Instantaneous Gibbs reduction instead reevaluates $\beta^*$ at every state and uses \cref{c18:coupled-flow} with the same matter metric. Pulling back the full metric to the graph of the Gibbs row would add $\sum_e(d\beta_e^*)^2/\beta_e^*$ and give a different mobility. Finite-rate row relaxation and this reduced convention generally follow different paths. Near the critical point below, pullback-metric reduction reproduces the leading center coefficients of full relaxation.

At a stationary point every force in \cref{c18:coupled-forces} vanishes, and the full row is Gibbs. For example,
\begin{equation}
 V_e^{-1}=(A_\omega)_{ee}+\beta_eK_e^TJ_eK_e.
 \label{c18:coupled-variance}
\end{equation}
It is a coupled condition: source covariance determines $J_e$, and the row depends on costs that themselves contain $V_e$. Freezing either input changes the stationary problem.

\section{Why the Gaussian flow stays in its regular domain}
\label{c18:coupled-global}

For a finite graph with fixed codes and operating contents, positive-definite content reference precisions and joint dictionary precision, positive-definite message noises, and positive fixed source priors and temperatures, both conventions have unique solutions for all forward time from every interior initial state. The mechanism is the proper reference cost. Contracting \cref{c18:coupled-forces} with \cref{c18:coupled-flow} gives a negative squared Fisher-gradient norm; full row relaxation adds $-\sum_i\operatorname{Var}_{\beta_i}(h)$. Every self and dictionary KL remains bounded by the initial energy, since the full row terms and the reduced terms $-T_i\log Z_i$ are nonnegative.

For a content covariance let $\lambda_k$ be the eigenvalues of $P_i^{1/2}\Sigma_iP_i^{1/2}$. Its self KL is
\begin{equation}
 \tfrac12\|\mu_i-m_i\|_{P_i}^2+
 \tfrac12\sum_k(\lambda_k-\log\lambda_k-1).
 \label{c18:coercivity}
\end{equation}
The scalar function diverges when an eigenvalue collapses to zero or grows without bound. The mean quadratic also diverges at large displacement. The joint dictionary KL applies the same argument to $a-a_0$ and $A_\omega^{1/2}VA_\omega^{1/2}$, where $V=\operatorname{diag}(V_e)$. Thus all matter coordinates stay in a compact subset of the Gaussian domain. This conclusion does not assert a monotone change of any covariance.

Costs are bounded there, say $0\leq C_e\leq M$. Gibbs rows satisfy $\beta_e^*\geq\pi_e e^{-M/T_i}$. For relaxing rows, the adjusted log-ratio $\eta_{ef}=\log(\beta_e/\pi_e)-\log(\beta_f/\pi_f)$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 \dot\eta_{ef}=-T_i\eta_{ef}-(C_e-C_f).
 \label{c18:log-ratio}
\end{equation}
Variation of constants bounds its magnitude by $L_i=\max\{M/T_i,\max_{e,f}|\eta_{ef}(0)|\}$. Normalization gives $\beta_e\geq\pi_e e^{-L_i}$, so no row reaches the boundary. The vector field is smooth near the resulting compact trajectory closure and can therefore be continued for every forward time.

The reduced energy attains an interior minimum on a compact sublevel; its Gibbs row gives a full stationary point. Integrated dissipation is finite. On the compact trajectory closure its time derivative is bounded, so dissipation is uniformly continuous and tends to zero: sustained positive pulses would otherwise have infinite total area. Every accumulation point is consequently stationary. Because both potentials are real analytic on the interior and the Fisher metric is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one on the trajectory closure, the {\L}ojasiewicz inequality gives finite trajectory length, so every trajectory converges to one stationary point. The limit can depend on the initial state, since uniqueness of the equilibrium is not proved. They apply to this fixed Gaussian restriction and provide no uniform limit at zero temperature or singular reference precision.

\section{Uncertainty can change the stability verdict}
\label{c18:threshold}

Source selection adds a feedback to the curvature of the potential. If $H_{\mathrm{fixed}}=\nabla^2F_0+\sum_e\beta_e^*\nabla^2C_e$ is the Hessian with the current weights fixed, differentiating the Gibbs formula gives
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{red}}=H_{\mathrm{fixed}}
       -\sum_iT_i^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}_{\beta_i^*}(\nabla C_e).
 \label{c18:row-curvature}
\end{equation}
A perturbation that makes competing costs change differently causes attention to shift toward the decreasing cost. The shift subtracts a positive-semidefinite form from the fixed-row curvature. This compares the same state and weights; it does not order arbitrary stationary branches by temperature.

An explicit example makes the difference measurable without freezing uncertainty. There are three scalar content laws and two dictionaries on edges $1\to0$ and $2\to0$. Take unit codes and message noises, fixed $\kappa_0=1$, no loop penalty, content reference precisions $(1,5/4,5/4)$, content reference means $(0,-23/15,23/15)$, and dictionary references $\mathcal N(0,4/3)$. Receiver $0$ chooses exactly between these two peers with prior $(1/2,1/2)$ and temperature $T>0$. Its row has no identity self candidate. The two sources need no nontrivial rows; singleton identity self rows contribute zero.

Write $q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,v_i)$ and $r_j=\mathcal N(a_j,V_j)$, and order the ten matter coordinates as
\begin{equation}
 x=(\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,a_1,a_2,v_0,v_1,v_2,V_1,V_2).
 \label{c18:threshold-order}
\end{equation}
Every mean and variance varies. With $S_i=v_i+1$ and $d_j=\mu_0-\mu_j-a_j$, the costs are $C_j=[(S_0+d_j^2+V_j)/S_j-1+\log S_j-\log S_0]/2$. For every temperature,
\begin{equation}
 x_*=(0,-4/3,4/3,1/3,-1/3,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad b_*=1/2
 \label{c18:threshold-point}
\end{equation}
is stationary. Here $b$ is the weight of source $1$. Both costs equal $1/2$. Each source mean and dictionary mean has a self force canceled by its weighted edge force. The source variance forces cancel as $1/8-1/8$, the dictionary variance forces as $-1/8+1/8$, and receiver forces cancel or vanish.

Put $H=\nabla^2(F_0+(C_1+C_2)/2)(x_*)$ and $g=\nabla(C_1-C_2)(x_*)$. The complete ten-dimensional positive-definiteness check can be written as a sum of squares. For $\xi=(u_0,u_1,u_2,b_1,b_2,h_0,h_1,h_2,k_1,k_2)$, let $z_j=u_0-u_j-b_j$ and $\epsilon_1=1$, $\epsilon_2=-1$. Differentiation of the preceding cost gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \xi^TH\xi={}&u_0^2+\tfrac54(u_1^2+u_2^2)
       +\tfrac34(b_1^2+b_2^2)+\tfrac12h_0^2\\
 &+\sum_{j=1}^2\left[
       \tfrac14(z_j-\epsilon_jh_j/2)^2+\tfrac1{16}(h_0-h_j)^2
       +\tfrac{71}{128}h_j^2+\tfrac12(k_j-h_j/8)^2\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:threshold-positive}
\end{equation}
Every nonzero perturbation has positive cost in this quadratic form. With a semicolon separating the mean and variance blocks,
\begin{equation}
 g=(1,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2;0,-1/4,1/4,1/4,-1/4)^T.
 \label{c18:threshold-gradient}
\end{equation}
Solving the linear equations from \cref{c18:threshold-positive} gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 (H^{-1}g)_{\mu,a}&=(2400,-960,-960,-1600,-1600)^T/5059,\\
 (H^{-1}g)_{v,V}&=(0,-716,716,2440,-2440)^T/5059.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:threshold-direction}
\end{equation}
Thus $g^TH^{-1}g=6538/5059$. At the equal-cost point \cref{c18:row-curvature} is the rank-one correction $H_T=H-gg^T/(4T)$. Changing tangent coordinates by $H^{-1/2}$ leaves nine positive directions and one sign determined by $1-g^TH^{-1}g/(4T)$. The exact critical temperature is
\begin{equation}
 T_c=\frac{3269}{10118}.
 \label{c18:critical-temperature}
\end{equation}
The stationary Fisher Jacobian is similar to a symmetric matrix with the opposite Hessian inertia. Hence the equilibrium is exponentially stable for $T>T_c$ and has exactly one unstable direction for $0<T<T_c$.

Full row relaxation adds the Hessian row block $4T$ and mixed block $g$. Its quadratic form completes to
\begin{equation}
 \xi^TH_T\xi+4T\left(\delta b+\frac{g^T\xi}{4T}\right)^2.
 \label{c18:full-threshold}
\end{equation}
It has the same threshold and one extra positive direction, although the two flows have different rates. At $T_c$ the reduced null vector is $H^{-1}g$, while the full vector is $(H^{-1}g,-1)$. The covariance components in \cref{c18:threshold-direction} show that the neutral displacement includes changes of source and dictionary uncertainty.

If all five variances are fixed at one, only the first five coordinates remain. At fixed $t=g_{\mathrm{mean}}^T\xi_{\mathrm{mean}}$, exchange symmetry and convexity reduce the minimum of their quadratic form to $u_1=u_2=y$, $b_1=b_2=z$. Its minimizing values are $(u_0,y,z)=(15,-6,-10)t/31$ and its value is $61t^2/62$. Thus $g_{\mathrm{mean}}^TH_{\mathrm{mean}}^{-1}g_{\mathrm{mean}}=62/61$, giving the lower threshold $T_{\mathrm{mean}}=31/122$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \frac{31}{122}<\frac3{10}<\frac{3269}{10118}.
 \label{c18:threshold-contrast}
\end{equation}
At $T=3/10$ the frozen-variance model is stable and the complete model is unstable. Allowing uncertainty to respond changes the available perturbations and the response to source selection. The nonlinear calculation below determines what happens locally at the threshold, within the same Gaussian tangent potential.

\subsection{What the zero mode does nonlinearly}
\label{c18:critical-mode}

Keep all ten matter coordinates free and put $s=b-1/2$, $\delta=T-T_c$, $A=F_0+(C_1+C_2)/2$, and $\Delta=C_1-C_2$. The full potential is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_T(x,s)=A(x)+s\Delta(x)+Th(s),\qquad
 h(s)=(1/2+s)\log(1+2s)+(1/2-s)\log(1-2s).
 \label{c18:critical-potential}
\end{equation}
The two sources exchange under simultaneous reflection of every mean. This transformation fixes $x_*$ and sends $s$ to $-s$, so a reduced stationary potential must be even in the imbalance.

First let the matter coordinates equilibrate at each fixed row. Positivity of $H$ gives a unique local curve $X(s)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 A_x(X(s))+s\Delta_x(X(s))=0,\qquad X(0)=x_*,\qquad X'(0)=p=-H^{-1}g.
 \label{c18:critical-stationary-curve}
\end{equation}
This equation includes source and dictionary variances, whose components of $p$ are nonzero. Define $\Phi(s,T)=\mathcal E_T(X(s),s)$. With all derivatives evaluated at $x_*$, put $k=A_{xxx}[p,p]+2\Delta_{xx}p$. Two derivatives of \cref{c18:critical-stationary-curve} give $X''(0)=-H^{-1}k$, and four derivatives of $\Phi$ give
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{ssss}(0,T_c)=A_{xxxx}[p,p,p,p]+4\Delta_{xxx}[p,p,p]
                    -3k^TH^{-1}k+32T_c=6c,
 \label{c18:critical-quartic}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 c=-\frac{657534393590529472}{6223404588394626861}\simeq-0.1056551.
 \label{c18:critical-cubic}
\end{equation}
The explicit costs give
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 A_{xxxx}[p,p,p,p]&=\frac{236914859731152}{655026269697361},\\
 \Delta_{xxx}[p,p,p]&=-\frac{31633266640}{129477420379},\\
 3k^TH^{-1}k&=\frac{21485600170644766720}{2074468196131542287}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{c18:critical-contractions}
\end{equation}
The negative response term accounts for the stable matter coordinates moving as the row changes. A calculation on the straight zero-mode line would omit it and give the positive fourth derivative $6368971071507328/655026269697361\approx9.72$, predicting a supercritical pitchfork; the response term exceeds $32T_c$ by less than $0.2$ percent and reverses the classification. The resulting stationary force is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_s(s,T_c+\delta)=4\delta s+c s^3+O(|\delta||s|^3+|s|^5).
 \label{c18:critical-force}
\end{equation}
Evenness allows one to factor this as $sQ(s^2,\delta)$. Since $Q_z(0,0)=c<0$, the implicit-function theorem gives exactly two additional nearby stationary points for small positive $\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 s_\pm(\delta)=\pm\sqrt{-4\delta/c}+O(\delta^{3/2})
              \simeq\pm6.15297\sqrt{\delta}.
 \label{c18:critical-branches}
\end{equation}
There are no additional nearby equilibria for small $\delta\leq0$. On either asymmetric branch $\Phi_{ss}=-8\delta+O(\delta^2)<0$. The matter Hessian remains positive definite, so each branch is a saddle with one unstable direction. These branches lie on the high-temperature side, where the symmetric state is still stable. The sign of $c$ is a property of these reference data: a float64 continuation, not interval validated, gives $c\approx+0.023$ when the dictionary reference precision is $1$ instead of $3/4$, and then the pitchfork is supercritical.

Stationary elimination determines the equilibrium positions but not the speed of a moving row. The Fisher metric enters that second calculation. At the symmetric point the matter Fisher metric is $M_*^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,1,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)$, and
\begin{equation}
 D=p^TM_*^{-1}p=\frac{19189456}{25593481}.
 \label{c18:critical-length}
\end{equation}
The local analytic fields have reflection symmetry, a single zero eigenvalue, and otherwise stable spectra. A parameter-dependent equivariant center manifold therefore exists with any required finite smoothness \citep[Theorem~1.2]{Ruelle1973Symmetry}. For full relaxation use the physical row coordinate $s=b-1/2$; for Gibbs reduction use its observable $s=b^*(x,T)-1/2$. The latter is regular because its derivative along $p$ is $-g^Tp/(4T_c)=1$.

Let $Y(s,\delta)$ be the matter coordinates on an invariant center manifold and $f=\dot s$. Matter invariance requires $Y_s f=-M(Y)[A_x(Y)+s\Delta_x(Y)]$. It implies $Y_{ss}(0,0)=X''(0)$, but the two curves differ at the next order. Set $B=H^{-1}M_*^{-1}p$, so $g^TB=-D$. At each of the orders $\delta s$ and $s^3$, the displacement $Y-X$ is $-Bf$. The row force is consequently $4\delta s+c s^3+Df$ at these orders. Gibbs reduction sets that force to zero. Full relaxation multiplies it by the row mobility $-1/4$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \dot s=-\frac{4\delta s+c s^3}{d}
       +O(\delta^2|s|+|\delta||s|^3+|s|^5),\qquad
 d=\begin{cases}D,&\text{Gibbs reduction},\\D+4,&\text{full relaxation}.\end{cases}
 \label{c18:critical-dynamics}
\end{equation}
The extra $4$ is the row's Fisher squared length at equal weights. Full relaxation has $4797364/30390845$ times the leading Gibbs-reduced center speed, corresponding to a time scale about $6.34$ times longer. Adding the Gibbs row's pullback metric to the reduced flow changes the specified dynamics in a definite way: the row direction adds $1/(b_*(1-b_*))=4$ to the squared length of $p$, so the pullback-metric reduction has exactly the leading center coefficients of full relaxation. The factor $(D+4)/D$ is the kinetic cost of the row omitted by matter-metric reduction.

At the threshold, $\dot s=(-c/d)s^3+O(s^5)$ points away from zero on either side. The symmetric equilibrium is therefore nonlinearly unstable even though its linear center rate vanishes. Each nearby asymmetric branch has an unstable eigenvalue $8\delta/d+O(\delta^2)$, with nine stable directions for Gibbs reduction and ten for full relaxation. For these data this is a subcritical pitchfork with destabilizing parameter $T_c-T$. It supplies no nearby stable asymmetric group. The calculation compares local equilibria and leading center velocities; higher-order paths, distant attractors, folds, and inherited-parent formation remain separate questions.
