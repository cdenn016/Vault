\chapter{Free Energies, Coupled Relaxation, and Source Selection}
\label{comp:dynamics}

The dynamical variables are probability laws. Moving an agent therefore means changing its expectations, uncertainties, and dependencies, rather than moving a point through its sample space. The physical analogue developed here is overdamped motion on a probability manifold: an objective supplies the driving covector, and the Fisher metric converts that covector into a velocity. Neither ingredient determines the other.

\section{Three objectives}

Before any equation, one must say what is being minimized, and for interacting agents there are three different answers. In the global picture all agents are parts of one normalized joint law over their contents and records, as the spins of one lattice share one Boltzmann weight. Each agent's own free energy is then that single functional seen as a function of its own factor, peers act on it only through expectations of their contents, and every local update lowers the same total: coordinate descent, like a sequence of single-spin heat-bath moves.

In the per-agent picture each agent has its own generative model, in which its own evaluator and the laws its peers communicate compete as sources of prior. The peers' laws are data for the agent, like an external field it does not control. Each agent lowers its own free energy, but the free energies of the agents that listen to it change through terms it does not control. Such non-reciprocal forces need have no global Lyapunov function: on a directed ring of three Bernoulli agents, each pulled toward its successor, the linearized motion spirals, with complex eigenvalues that no gradient flow can have.

The third object, developed in this chapter, is the reciprocal law potential. It adds the divergences between agent laws directly, so each edge pulls its receiver toward the source and also pulls the source toward its receivers, like a spring that acts on both ends. All agents then descend one shared function, and the dynamics dissipates that function and cannot spiral into an equilibrium. The price is that this function is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are the beliefs, and records enter it only as separate assimilation events. The constructions of the later chapters use this third object; their conclusions are not claims about the other two.

\section{The potential and its source rows}

At a fixed context, let $q_i$ describe agent $i$'s represented state $k$, and let $s_i$ describe its model presentation $m$. For this construction their joint recognition law is the explicit product $q_i\otimes s_i$. Choose a model reference $r_i(dm)$ and a normalized evaluator $G_i(m,dk)$. With the reference joint $R_i(dk,dm)=r_i(dm)G_i(m,dk)$, their local contribution is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_0=\sum_i\KL(q_i\otimes s_i\Vert R_i)
 =\sum_i\left[\KL(s_i\Vert r_i)
       +\int\KL(q_i\Vert G_i(m,\cdot))s_i(dm)\right].
 \label{eq:comp-local-potential}
\end{equation}
The equality separates the model marginal from the conditional state law. Thus a product recognition family still has coupled dynamics: changing $s_i$ changes the evaluator against which $q_i$ relaxes, and changing $q_i$ changes the model costs.

The channels of \cref{comp:links} send peer laws into comparable spaces. Write $\nu^q_{ij}=q_j\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q]$ and $D^q_{ij}=\KL(q_i\Vert\nu^q_{ij})$, where $\mathbf q$ collects the belief laws. Define $\nu^s_{ij}$ and $D^s_{ij}$ similarly for model laws. With normalized source rows $\beta_i,\gamma_i$, positive reference rows $\pi_i^q,\pi_i^s$, and $T_q,T_s>0$, the potential is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E=\mathcal E_0
 &+\sum_i\left[\sum_j\beta_{ij}D^q_{ij}
                  +T_q\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i^q)\right]\\
 &+\sum_i\left[\sum_j\gamma_{ij}D^s_{ij}
                  +T_s\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\pi_i^s)\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:comp-potential}
\end{equation}
Each sum uses its declared source set. The row penalty balances preference for compatible peers against departure from the reference routing.

Hold the laws fixed. For either sector, minimize $f(\eta)=\sum_j\eta_jD_j+T\KL(\eta\Vert\pi)$ subject to $\sum_j\eta_j=1$. Stationarity gives $D_j+T[\log(\eta_j/\pi_j)+1]=\lambda$, hence
\begin{equation}
 \eta_j^*=\frac{\pi_je^{-D_j/T}}{Z},\qquad
 Z=\sum_j\pi_je^{-D_j/T},\qquad
 f(\eta)=-T\log Z+T\KL(\eta\Vert\eta^*).
 \label{eq:comp-source-row}
\end{equation}
The last identity proves that this stationary row is the unique minimum. Eliminating both sets of rows gives $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}=\mathcal E_0-T_q\sum_i\log Z_i^q-T_s\sum_i\log Z_i^s$. At fixed temperatures and reference rows,
\begin{equation}
 d\mathcal E_{\mathrm{red}}
 =d\mathcal E_0+\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}^*dD^q_{ij}
                   +\sum_{ij}\gamma_{ij}^*dD^s_{ij}.
 \label{eq:comp-envelope}
\end{equation}
This follows by differentiating each log partition sum. Optimized row derivatives disappear; derivatives inside their costs remain.

\section{Every interaction role contributes to motion}

For normalized densities $a,b$, variation of $\int a\log(a/b)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 d\KL(a\Vert b)=\int\log(a/b)da-\int(a/b)db,
 \qquad
 d\nu^q_{ij}=(dq_j)\mathcal K^q_{ij}
             +q_jD\mathcal K^q_{ij}(d\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:comp-all-role-variation}
\end{equation}
Here $da,db$ denote mass-preserving variations, and differentiation assumes a regular dominated family. An agent moves through its receiver role, its source role, and any dependence of the channels on its law. Directed routing does not remove incoming contributions to source motion. The source is pulled toward the pooled law of the agents that listen to it, so a source more confident than its listeners' spread is widened; a communicating population realizes this only if listeners send a reverse message back to their sources. For an identity self-channel, the two laws remain identical and their divergence stays zero. Holding an old self snapshot fixed instead creates a different potential after the current law changes.

For positive finite probability tables, collect all dynamical tables as $u^a$, including the source rows when they are not eliminated. The Fisher metric and its unit-mobility descent are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 g_u(v,v)&=\sum_{a,z}\frac{(v_z^a)^2}{u_z^a},\\
 \dot u_z^a&=-u_z^a(h_z^a-\E_{u^a}h^a),\qquad
 h_z^a=\frac{\partial\mathcal E}{\partial u_z^a},\\
 \dot{\mathcal E}&=-\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{u^a}(h^a).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:comp-fisher-descent}
\end{equation}
Centering preserves each normalization. Substituting the velocity into $\dot{\mathcal E}=\sum_{a,z}h_z^a\dot u_z^a$ proves the dissipation identity. The same construction uses the Fisher tensor on a regular smooth family \citep{Amari1998}. Both sectors have equal mobility; a slower model response must arise from the resulting dynamics. Equal mobility is what one gets by treating all tables as one product law of all represented variables and source labels, since Fisher information adds over independent factors. It has a cost for the source rows: under unit mobility a row's log odds relax at a rate equal to its temperature, so a row that selects sharply moves slowly, and replacing it by its instantaneous Gibbs optimum is a fast-row limit rather than the same flow.

A Bernoulli law makes the metric concrete. Its success probability $p$ has metric $dp^2/[p(1-p)]$, so the mobility is $p(1-p)$. For $\mathcal E(p)=\KL(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(p_0))$, with $p,p_0\in(0,1)$, write $l=\log[p/(1-p)]$ and $l_0=\log[p_0/(1-p_0)]$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \dot p=-p(1-p)(l-l_0),\qquad
 \dot l=-(l-l_0).
 \label{eq:comp-bernoulli-flow}
\end{equation}
Log odds relax exponentially, although probability does not follow a linear restoring equation. Here $t$ parametrizes the chosen relaxation, and the dimensionless $T_q,T_s$ weight routing penalties. They have not been identified with physical time or bath temperature.

\section{A record likelihood defines a different objective}

A fixed normalized prior $P_0$ and record likelihood $\ell_o$ give posterior $\Pi_o=\ell_oP_0/Z_o$ and the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(Q;o)=\KL(Q\Vert P_0)-\E_Q\log\ell_o
               =\KL(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o.
 \label{eq:comp-record-vfe}
\end{equation}
Source inference in a product recognition family then uses expected negative log likelihood as its cost. Peer coupling instead uses divergence between laws, whose source template can move during relaxation. Both yield Gibbs rows because both contain a categorical relative-entropy penalty, with coefficients $T_q$ and $1$ respectively; their costs and state derivatives differ. There is one setting in which the two meet exactly. If each agent receives $n$ messages from a source chosen by its prior and registers whether their empirical frequencies match its own law, the posterior over which source was heard is the Gibbs row at temperature $1/n$. The row temperature is then the reciprocal number of exchanged messages, as in a counting experiment in which a sharper verdict needs more counts. Identical independent Gaussian laws have zero peer divergence but nonzero expected squared sample difference. A normalized likelihood for that difference therefore describes an interaction distinct from direct agreement of the laws.
