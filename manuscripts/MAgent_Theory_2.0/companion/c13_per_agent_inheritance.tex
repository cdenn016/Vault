\chapter{Per-Agent Inference and Conditional Inheritance}
\label{cf:per-agent-inheritance}

An agent that changes its beliefs to fit a peer need not make that peer change in return. This chapter uses that per-agent picture: each agent minimizes the free energy of its own normalized model, taking communicated peer laws as data. A persistent record can then favor one sign locally while communication brings the agent into a group with the opposite sign. Three agents give an explicit example. Whether the grouping counts as interaction-dependent depends on the threshold of the selector, and the grouping already follows from each agent's response to the initial laws of the others. The selected group has an exact parent for a declared interface, including its record histories and a nonflat collective geometry, but the parent forgets only a hidden phase that no agent, channel, record or admitted query reads. That phase is gauge in the sense of the main text, so the parent fails the compression clause of the formation standard. The interface is supplied before selection, so this is a single-stage selection with exact inheritance rather than a recursive formation.

\section{Whose free energy supplies the force?}

Let $k\in\mathsf K$ be a represented content, $m\in\mathsf M$ a model presentation, and $j,g$ their respective source labels. Agent $i$ has a normalized evaluator $G_i(m,dk)$, a presentation reference $r_i(dm)$, and fixed positive source priors $\pi_i^q,\pi_i^s$. Its content sources are $T_{ii}=G_i$ and communicated peer laws $T_{ij}=\nu_j^q$ for $j\ne i$; its presentation sources are $R_{ii}=r_i$ and $R_{ig}=\nu_g^s$ for $g\ne i$. With a normalized record likelihood $\ell_i(do\mid k,m)$, its own model is
\begin{equation}
 P_i(dm,dk,j,g,do)
 =\pi_{ig}^sR_{ig}(dm)\pi_{ij}^qT_{ij}(m,dk)
   \ell_i(do\mid k,m).
 \label{cp:local-model}
\end{equation}
Integrating the record and content, summing $j$, and then integrating the presentation and summing $g$ gives one. The model stays fixed during an own-block variation with peers held fixed. It does not specify a common generative law for the population.

Restrict recognition to $Q_i=q_i\otimes s_i\otimes\beta_i\otimes\gamma_i$. For a retained record $o_i$, define $D_{ij}=\E_{q_i\otimes s_i}\log(q_i/T_{ij})$ and $E_{ig}=\KL(s_i\Vert R_{ig})$. All densities are taken against their declared common reference measures. The negative evidence lower bound is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 F_i={}&\sum_j\beta_{ij}D_{ij}+\KL(\beta_i\Vert\pi_i^q)
       +\sum_g\gamma_{ig}E_{ig}+\KL(\gamma_i\Vert\pi_i^s)\\
     &-\E_{q_i\otimes s_i}\log\ell_i(o_i\mid K,M).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:free-energy}
\end{equation}
The likelihood remains in this objective after observation. Exact source minimization at unit temperature gives
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{ij}=\frac{\pi_{ij}^qe^{-D_{ij}}}{\sum_k\pi_{ik}^qe^{-D_{ik}}},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{ig}=\frac{\pi_{ig}^se^{-E_{ig}}}{\sum_k\pi_{ik}^se^{-E_{ik}}}.
 \label{cp:gibbs}
\end{equation}
Indeed, differentiating the row objective subject to normalization gives $D_j+\log(\beta_j/\pi_j)+1+\eta=0$; its positive Hessian gives the unique minimum. The reduced content term is $-\log\sum_j\pi_{ij}^qe^{-D_{ij}}$, whose differential is $\sum_j\beta_{ij}dD_{ij}$. Minimizing the rows exactly at every state, rather than letting them relax at finite mobility, is the Gibbs-reduced per-agent dynamics of the main text; the two conventions share equilibria but not transients.

With the own-law Fisher metric and unit mobility, write $h_i^q=\delta F_i/\delta q_i$ and evolve by
\begin{equation}
 \dot q_i=-q_i(h_i^q-\E_{q_i}h_i^q),\qquad
 \dot s_i=-s_i(h_i^s-\E_{s_i}h_i^s).
 \label{cp:own-flow}
\end{equation}
This is natural-gradient descent in each agent's own block \citep{Amari1998}. There is no incoming derivative from the objectives of agents listening to $i$. Such a derivative belongs to the reciprocal potential of \cref{comp:dynamics}. If $G_i$ couples belief and presentation, both derivatives of its expected log density carry $\beta_{ii}$, because the evaluator is a content self source; the presentation reference carries $\gamma_{ii}$. A channel whose output depends on the sender's law adds a derivative only to the sender's role as a source of others, which the per-agent force omits; it does not convert the per-agent force into the reciprocal force or transfer reciprocal equilibria.

Three controls are compared with the full flow. The label-preserving null deletes the nonself costs while retaining every label, prior, and row entropy. Its content self weight is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{ii}^0=
 \frac{\pi_{ii}^qe^{-D_{ii}}}
 {\pi_{ii}^qe^{-D_{ii}}+\sum_{j\ne i}\pi_{ij}^q},
 \label{cp:null-row}
\end{equation}
with the corresponding formula for $\gamma_{ii}^0$. The renormalized null also removes the deleted labels, so each self weight is one. The two nulls differ here because the self costs are nonzero, and a verdict counts only if it holds against both. The frozen-environment control deletes nothing and holds every peer law at its initial value; it asks whether the grouping already follows from each agent's response to the others' initial laws, without feedback.

\section{A bit and two different circles}

Take three agents of one species with $\mathsf K=\{-1,1\}\times S^1\times S^1$. Write $k=(z,\phi,\psi)$, $e_\phi=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$, and let $H$ be Haar probability measure. Belief densities are relative to the product of the uniform bit measure and two copies of $H$. Define $I_0(v)=\int e^{v\cos\phi}H(d\phi)$ and $h(A)=\log I_0(|A|)$. The invariant family is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 q_i(z,\phi,\psi)
 &=\frac{\exp[l_i z/2+A_i\cdot e_\phi+zC_i\cdot e_\phi
                  +b_i\cos(\psi-\theta_i)]}{Z_i},\\
 Z_i&=\frac{I_0(b_i)}2
 \left[e^{l_i/2}I_0(|A_i+C_i|)
       +e^{-l_i/2}I_0(|A_i-C_i|)\right],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:family}
\end{equation}
where $A_i,C_i\in\R^2$ and $b_i>0$. The presentation law is $s_i(w)=e^{n_iw/2}/[2\cosh(n_i/2)]$ on $w\in\{-1,1\}$, with counting measure and uniform self reference. All source priors have self mass $1/5$ and mass $2/5$ for each peer.

Fix $\epsilon>0$, set $J=\epsilon^3$ and $\lambda=\epsilon$, and choose the presentation-independent evaluator
\begin{equation}
 G_i(w;z,\phi,\psi)=\frac{e^{Jz\cos\phi}}{I_0(J)}.
 \label{cp:evaluator}
\end{equation}
The circle integral proves its normalization for either value of $z$. A peer content channel copies $z$ and draws both circles independently from $H$; a peer presentation channel is the identity. The circle $\phi$ is marked by the evaluator direction $e_1=(1,0)$. The circle $\psi$ carries another orientation, but no channel, evaluator, record or admitted query reads it.

The transmitted bit has log odds
\begin{equation}
 L_i=l_i+h(A_i+C_i)-h(A_i-C_i).
 \label{cp:public-odds}
\end{equation}
Thus $l_i$ alone is not the public state. The peer density against the uniform bit reference is $\nu_j(z)=e^{L_jz/2}/\cosh(L_j/2)$; its probability mass is $\nu_j(z)/2$. Using that probability mass in the density KL would add $\log2$ to peer costs alone and change the rows.

A record is $(o,\omega)\in\{-1,1\}\times S^1$, with counting measure for $o$ and Haar measure for $\omega$. Choose
\begin{equation}
 \ell(o,\omega\mid z,\phi,\psi,w)
 =\frac{e^{\epsilon oz/2}}{2\cosh(\epsilon/2)}
  \frac{e^{\lambda\cos(\omega-\phi)}}{I_0(\lambda)}.
 \label{cp:likelihood}
\end{equation}
Both factors normalize over their recorded variable. The first records the bit imperfectly; the second records a noisy direction on the marked circle. Neither reads $\psi$. Their observed values remain in the free energy throughout the ensuing relaxation.

\section{Exact motion in the original time}

Put $m_i=\tanh(L_i/2)$, $t_i=\E_{q_i}[Z\cos\Phi]$, and $S_i=\E_{q_i}\log q_i$. The content costs are
\begin{equation}
 D_{ii}=S_i-Jt_i+\log I_0(J),\qquad
 D_{ij}=S_i-L_jm_i/2+\log\cosh(L_j/2).
 \label{cp:costs}
\end{equation}
The common entropy $S_i$ cancels from the full row. Its unnormalized weights are
\begin{equation}
 w_{ii}=\frac{e^{Jt_i}}{5I_0(J)},\qquad
 w_{ij}=\frac{2e^{L_jm_i/2}}{5\cosh(L_j/2)},\qquad
 \beta_{ij}=\frac{w_{ij}}{\sum_rw_{ir}}.
 \label{cp:weights}
\end{equation}
These weights read the marked circle through $L_i,L_j,t_i$, but read neither $b_i$ nor $\theta_i$. For example, when $A_i=\alpha e_1$ and $C_i=ce_1$ with $c>0$, the derivative of $L_i$ with respect to $\alpha$ is $h_1'(\alpha+c)-h_1'(\alpha-c)>0$, where $h_1(r)=\log I_0(r)$ and $h_1''(r)=\operatorname{Var}_r(\cos\Phi)>0$.

Modulo a normalization constant, the own belief force is $\log q_i-\beta_{ii}\log G_i-\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_{ij}\log\nu_j-\log\ell_i$. Its centered negative is the log-density velocity in \cref{cp:own-flow}. Matching the coefficients of the sufficient statistics in \cref{cp:family} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot l_i&=-l_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_{ij}L_j+\epsilon o_i,\\
 \dot A_i&=-A_i+\epsilon e_{\omega_i},&
 \dot C_i&=-C_i+\beta_{ii}\epsilon^3e_1,\\
 \dot b_i&=-b_i,&\dot\theta_i&=0,\\
 \dot n_i&=-n_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}n_j.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:parameters}
\end{equation}
The apparent linearity of several parameter equations comes from the Fisher metric, not a Euclidean gradient assumption. The sufficient-statistic covariance is positive definite in the Cartesian chart $B_i=b_ie_{\theta_i}$: no nonzero combination of the distinct Fourier and bit-parity modes is constant. The density-level flow has unique solutions in the bounded log-density chart, because every log density in the force is a Lipschitz function of the chart coordinates, so this family, to which the flow is tangent, is an exact invariant family of the own-law flow. Finite parameters give positive analytic densities and smooth parameter dependence. The displayed equations bound $A_i,C_i,B_i$ directly, while $|L_i-l_i|\leq2|C_i|$ gives a linear growth bound for $l_i$; these bounds prevent finite-time escape. No clock has been rescaled.

\section{How the records produce a persistent group}

Retain binary outcomes $o=(1,1,-1)$ and arbitrary angular outcomes $\omega_i$. Start with $l/\epsilon$ near $(4,1/2,-1)$, small $|A_i|,b_i<1/20$, $b_i>0$, and $|C_i|<\epsilon^3$. These strict conditions admit a relatively open set in \cref{cp:family}. Before either evolution, fix a threshold $c\in(0,1)$ and connect two agents exactly when their nonzero public odds have the same sign and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\min(|L_i|,|L_j|)}{\max(|L_i|,|L_j|)}>c.
 \label{cp:selector}
\end{equation}
The selected blocks are the connected components. This rule treats labels symmetrically. Initially the only same-sign pair has ratio close to $1/8$, so the partition consists of singletons whenever $c>1/8$.

Write $u_i=l_i/\epsilon$ and $c_i=C_i/\epsilon^3$ for the proof, retaining the original time. On bounded parameter sets, \cref{cp:public-odds,cp:weights} give $L_i/\epsilon=u_i+O(\epsilon^2)$ and $\beta_{ij}=\pi_{ij}+O(\epsilon^2)$, including first derivatives in these scaled variables. The limiting public equation is
\begin{equation}
 \dot u=Mu+o,\qquad
 M=-\frac75\id+\frac25\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
 u_*=-M^{-1}o=(15/7,15/7,5/7).
 \label{cp:affine}
\end{equation}
The common mode has eigenvalue $-1/5$ and the two difference modes have eigenvalue $-7/5$. Thus a positive shared component survives the conflicting third record. At equilibrium every sign is positive and the smallest pair ratio is $1/3$. This limit is the anchored averaging of \citet{FriedkinJohnsen1990}: each agent averages its peers and is pulled toward its own fixed record.

This limiting calculation gives an exact small-parameter result. Because $M$ is symmetric with top eigenvalue $-1/5$, the distance to $u_*$ decreases along the limit field, and the exact field differs from it only through the deviation of the rows from their priors, which is of order $\epsilon^2$ on bounded sets because a Bernoulli divergence is at most $(l-l')^2/8$. The exact trajectory therefore stays within a distance of order $\epsilon^2$ of the limit trajectory $\bar u$ for all time, with $R_\epsilon$ that row deviation times the peer odds:
\begin{equation}
 |u(t)-\bar u(t)|\leq5\sup|R_\epsilon|=O(\epsilon^2)\qquad(t\geq0).
 \label{cp:attraction}
\end{equation}
Along the limit trajectory every selector margin increases monotonically. If $1/8<c<1/3$, the margins of the pairs $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ become positive and stay so, and the exact flow selects the persistent full block. If $1/3<c<1$, agent $3$'s ratio stays below $1/3+O(\epsilon^2)<c$ forever, and the exact flow selects only the pair $\{1,2\}$. The argument is uniform in $\omega\in(S^1)^3$. It assumes that no additional record is assimilated during this episode.

The null has a different field, obtained by deleting the full nonself costs before row reduction:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta_{ii}^0&=\frac{e^{-D_{ii}}}{e^{-D_{ii}}+4},&
 \dot l_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0l_i^0+\epsilon o_i,\\
 \dot A_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0A_i^0+\epsilon e_{\omega_i},&
 \dot C_i^0&=\beta_{ii}^0(\epsilon^3e_1-C_i^0),\\
 \dot b_i^0&=-\beta_{ii}^0b_i^0,&
 \dot n_i^0&=-\gamma_{ii}^0n_i^0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:null-parameters}
\end{equation}
Its phase is also constant. Here the hidden concentration contributes to $D_{ii}$ and no longer cancels from the row. An all-time sign bound needs this dependence.

For $0<\epsilon\leq1/100$, consider
\begin{equation}
 \frac14\leq\frac{o_il_i}{\epsilon}\leq12,\qquad
 |A_i|\leq\frac1{10},\qquad |C_i|\leq2\epsilon^3,\qquad
 b_i\leq\frac1{10}.
 \label{cp:null-box}
\end{equation}
The sufficient-statistic vector has squared norm four, so the log-partition Hessian has operator norm at most four. Its integral Taylor remainder bounds the self divergence by $D_{ii}\leq72\epsilon^2+1/25+18\epsilon^6<\log2$. Hence $1/9\leq\beta_{ii}^0\leq1/5$. For $r_i=o_il_i/\epsilon$, the equation $\dot r_i=1-\beta_{ii}^0r_i$ points inward at both endpoints of \cref{cp:null-box}. The radial derivatives of $A_i$ and $C_i$ also point inward, and $b_i$ decreases. This proves invariance of the whole region.

Finally $\|\nabla^2h\|\leq1$ gives
\begin{equation}
 |L_i-l_i|\leq2|C_i|(|A_i|+|C_i|)
 \leq\frac25\epsilon^3+8\epsilon^6<\frac\epsilon8,
 \qquad o_iL_i\geq\frac\epsilon8.
 \label{cp:null-signs}
\end{equation}
The third null agent stays negative for every finite time. The renormalized null, with self weight one, gives $\dot l_i^0=-l_i^0+\epsilon o_i$ in the limit and keeps the same signs. For $1/8<c<1/3$ the full flow eventually selects the three-agent block, whereas neither null does, and deleting event times cannot remove this difference of partition histories. For $1/3<c<1$ the full flow and both nulls select the same pair $\{1,2\}$, so the comparison fails. The endpoints $1/8$ and $1/3$ are the initial and equilibrium ratios, which the flow determines; the threshold is not derived from the model. What the interaction does for every threshold is to reverse the sign of agent $3$'s public odds against its own record.

The frozen-environment control gives the limit $u_i\to o_i+\frac25\sum_{j\ne i}u_j(0)$, which is $(4/5,11/5,4/5)$ from $(4,1/2,-1)$. It also reverses agent $3$'s sign and, for $1/8<c<1/3$, produces the same sequence of partitions as the full flow. The grouping is therefore already the response of each agent to the initial laws of the others; feedback among the agents is not needed for it.

\section{The parent and its complete admitted histories}

For a selected block $I$, replace each hidden factor $e^{b_i\cos(\psi-\theta_i)}/I_0(b_i)$ by $e^{b_i\cos\psi}/I_0(b_i)$, and take the product of the resulting normalized belief laws and the retained presentation laws. The parent keeps $l_i,A_i,C_i,b_i,n_i$, all primitive ports and source rows, constitutive data, the complete ordered record history, and membership events and their times. Its retained vector field is exactly \cref{cp:parameters} with $\theta_i$ omitted. Every retained derivative is independent of the omitted phases, so this retention intertwines the flows in their original time.

For $I_1(b)=dI_0(b)/db$, a separate child diagnostic gives
\begin{equation}
 \E_{q_i}e^{\mathrm i\Psi}
 =\frac{I_1(b_i)}{I_0(b_i)}e^{\mathrm i\theta_i}.
 \label{cp:phase-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
It distinguishes the phase fibers whenever $b_i>0$, which holds at every finite full-flow time because $b_i(t)=e^{-t}b_i(0)$; the distinction it carries fades at that rate. The marked-frame group acts only on $\phi$, so changing $\theta_i$ is not its gauge transformation. But no agent, channel, record or admitted query reads $\theta_i$, and the diagnostic is a functional of the law that only an observer outside the population computes. Relative to every observable algebra the construction declares, the erased phase is therefore gauge, and the parent is a symmetry quotient by the hidden rotations, which leave every field and admitted kernel unchanged. The compression clause of the formation standard asks for a declared probe that resolves the erased distinction; none does, so that clause fails. The parent retains $b_i$, including the information carried by variations of the hidden shape.

Declare the boundary protocol before selection. It may query retained public and marked coordinates, primitive source labels, and transmitted bits, with actions and observation times chosen from the retained history. Assimilated records use \cref{cp:likelihood}, with declared strengths; each new outcome appends its likelihood and actual value without instantaneously resetting the recognition law. Their natural parameters add, so the same finite family remains invariant. Fresh predictive draws are not assimilated as new evidence. A retained source-label trace does not reveal that latent label to the inferring agent; revealing it would change the optimized-row conditioning convention. Reused samples must have their queried coordinates stored as part of the retained state. Policies are measurable rules of the observed history, and the expected number of transactions in a finite time is bounded; otherwise infinitely many fresh queries could accumulate before a fixed time and the history experiment below would have no finite Fisher information. No hidden absolute-angle query is in this protocol, before or after a handoff.

One must also specify how records are sampled. The normalized generative predictive kernel integrates \cref{cp:local-model}, whereas a recognition-predictive kernel integrates the likelihood against $q_i\otimes s_i$. These generally differ. Either declared kernel depends only on retained data here; the geometric construction below uses the former for its initial records. If $x$ is the complete configuration and $R$ its retention, the joint next-output and retained-update kernel has the form
\begin{equation}
 K_a(x,dy,dx_c')
 =\widehat K_a(Rx,dy,dx_c'),\qquad
 x_c'=U(Rx,a,y),
 \label{cp:joint-kernel}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is the retained action label and $U$ appends the output and any persistent likelihood coefficients. Equal parent states give equal waiting-time flows, equal next outputs, and equal next retained states conditional on those outputs. Successive conditioning proves equality of every finite adaptive joint history, including its dependence with future states. A $\psi$-reading sensor would violate this identity immediately by \cref{cp:phase-diagnostic}.

The parent therefore carries exact directed equations on all its child ports. It is not a fixed-size agent with one new source row. Nor is its field the Fisher gradient of $\sum_{i\in I}F_i$: differentiating that sum would add the source-role forces that the per-agent dynamics excludes.

\section{Geometry of histories and current states}

Use a common marked $S^1$ comparison frame for a block. A passive change through angle $g$ sends $\phi\mapsto\phi+g$, $\omega\mapsto\omega+g$, $A_i\mapsto R_gA_i$, and $C_i\mapsto R_gC_i$. It also sends the evaluator direction $e_1\mapsto R_ge_1$. The hidden variables $\psi,\theta_i$ and the presentation sector do not transform. Evaluators, record kernels, and recognition laws are then expressed in the same new coordinates. Rotating the beliefs while holding numerical evaluator and record directions fixed would instead change the model.

For a contextual section $c$, draw the initial records independently from the agents' normalized generative predictive kernels, conditional on their prepared laws. Let $\Pi_c$ be the law of the resulting history $H$. Condition on the positive-probability binary event $E=\{o=(1,1,-1)\}$, retain its label, and leave the angular records random. If $Q_{c,t}^{h}$ is the product of the selected children's current recognition laws and their Gibbs-label laws after history $h$, the actual experiment is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_{c,t}(dh,dy)=\Pi_c(dh\mid E)Q_{c,t}^{h}(dy).
 \label{cp:experiment}
\end{equation}
This experimental product introduces no global population free energy. Its score and Fisher metric satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 s_X&=\partial_X\log\Pi_c(H\mid E)+\partial_X\log Q_{c,t}^{H}(Y),\\
 g^{\mathrm{joint}}&=g^{\mathrm{history}}+\E_H g^{\mathrm{conditional}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:history-fisher}
\end{equation}
This is the chain rule for Fisher information. The cross term vanishes because each conditional density is normalized. The first score includes $-\partial_X\log\Pr_c(E)$, and the second includes the source-row label scores. A metric computed only at one fixed realized history would omit the first contribution. In the nonflat example below the history score is in fact identically zero: the generative predictive kernel reads the configuration only through the peers' initial public odds, which do not depend on context there.

Restrict now to sections whose erased phases are constant in context. Since they are also constant in time and history, the product shift $\psi_i\mapsto\psi_i-\theta_i$ is one fixed change of sample coordinates. It carries \cref{cp:experiment} to the parent's actual experiment, preserves Haar measure, and commutes with the marked-frame action. Consequently, for every contextual tangent $X$ and common angular comparison velocity $\xi$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{fine}}(X,\xi)
 =\E_{\mathcal P}(s_X+\xi s_{\mathrm{orb}})^2
 =\mathcal C_{\mathrm{parent}}(X,\xi),
 \label{cp:cost-isometry}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\mathrm{orb}}$ is the marked-orbit score. Equality before minimization gives the same minimizing connection and contextual tensor. This is the mechanical-connection construction for the declared statistical metric \citep{Smale1970}; the result here is its exact preservation by this parent. Context-varying erased phases add the terms $[b_iI_1(b_i)/I_0(b_i)](d\theta_i)^2$ to the fine cost. Their score is orthogonal to every retained score and to the marked orbit score, so the added terms do not depend on the comparison velocity: the minimizing connection and its curvature are still preserved, and only the minimized tensor loses these terms.

Nonzero curvature occurs in the same model. Choose a compact contextual patch $c=(a,\chi)$ with $A_1(0)=ae_\chi$, $A_2(0)=de_1$, and small $a,d\in(0,1/20)$ fixed independently of $\epsilon$. Keep $C_i(0)=0$, hidden phases and concentrations constant over context, and initial presentation laws uniform. Other selected children may have fixed small positive marked amplitudes. At $\epsilon=0$, the initial history is uniform and the row-label laws equal their context-independent priors, so both scores vanish; meanwhile $A_i(t)=e^{-t}A_i(0)$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I(v)=v\frac{I_1(v)}{I_0(v)},\qquad
 s=e^{-t}a,\qquad
 B(t)=\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{1\}}\mathcal I(e^{-t}|A_i(0)|)>0.
 \label{cp:inertias}
\end{equation}
The angular part of the cost is $\mathcal I(s)(d\chi+\xi)^2+B(t)\xi^2$. Minimization, followed by differentiation in context, gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A_{0,t}&=-\frac{\mathcal I(s)}{\mathcal I(s)+B(t)}d\chi,\\
 \mathcal F_{0,t}&=-\frac{B(t)e^{-t}\mathcal I'(s)}
 {[\mathcal I(s)+B(t)]^2}da\wedge d\chi,\\
 h_{aa}^{0,t}&=e^{-2t}\operatorname{Var}_{s}(\cos\Phi)>0,\\
 h_{\chi\chi}^{0,t}&=\frac{\mathcal I(s)B(t)}{\mathcal I(s)+B(t)}>0,
 \qquad h_{a\chi}^{0,t}=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:curvature}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I'(s)=I_1(s)/I_0(s)+s\operatorname{Var}_{s}(\cos\Phi)>0$. The phase inertia identity follows by integrating the derivative of $\sin\phi e^{s\cos\phi}$ around the circle. Thus the tensor has rank two and the connection has nonzero curvature on this patch.

Fix a finite time window containing the common grouping entrance time, and compare sections at the same physical time. The positive inertia, tensor eigenvalues, and curvature magnitude have positive lower bounds on the compact patch throughout that window. Analytic finite-time dependence of the flow and its contextual derivatives, together with positivity of the normalized history density, preserves those strict bounds for sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon>0$. \Cref{cp:cost-isometry} then gives exact equality with the compressed parent's geometry at that same positive parameter. No connection enters \cref{cp:parameters}; it diagnoses the evolved statistical sections. Nonflatness is asserted on the finite window, whereas group persistence concerns the entire fixed-record episode. Later admitted records preserve exact quotient closure but may change the group and its geometry. The curvature is also present at time zero, at $\epsilon=0$, and along the null flows: it is prepared in the initial section $A_1(0)=ae_\chi$, since the marked equation has no peer term, and the window is a choice of interval containing the entrance time rather than a geometry created by the grouping.

The remaining formation obligation is concrete. Every erased phase was already invisible to the admitted interface before any group was selected. The interaction chooses membership, but this construction does not make the sufficient interface appear. Joining already canonical parents concatenates their retained ports and histories and erases no new phase, so it gives neither a second compressive handoff nor the required counterfactual dependence of that second stage. A construction deriving such an interface and a genuinely new inherited loss remains open.

\Cref{cf:collective-local-parent} treats a different transmitted-angle model whose loss is collective rather than an invisible primitive factor. It gives conditional local realizations and scoped locality obstructions; the general local-parent and second-stage formation problems remain open.
