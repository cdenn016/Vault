\section{Proofs for Exact Agent-Network Coarse-Graining}
\label{sec:proofs-network-coarsegraining}

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus}.}
Positivity and unitality of $\mathsf U$ give the bounds in (a), the bound on
$\mathcal A(\psi)$, and the boundedness and positivity of $\mathsf U^\psi$;
substitution in \eqref{eq:rg-local-action-map} gives
\eqref{eq:rg-action-recentering-identity}. If $\lVert h\rVert_\infty<\log2$, then
$\lVert\mathsf U^\psi(e^{-h})-1\rVert_\infty\le e^{\lVert h\rVert_\infty}-1<1$, so
the exponential series in $L^\infty(\bar{\mathsf m}_o)$ and the series of
$\log(1+u)$ in the commutative Banach algebra $L^\infty(\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c)$
converge, and $h\mapsto\mathcal A(\psi+h)$ is real analytic on the ball of radius
$\log2$ about every $\psi$, which proves (a). The Fr\'echet chain rule for the
exponential, $\mathsf U^\psi$, and the logarithm gives
\eqref{eq:rg-action-first-frechet-general}; differentiating
$\int h(y)C^\dagger_{\psi+tk}(z,dy)$ at $t=0$ gives
\eqref{eq:rg-action-second-frechet-general}; and $\psi=0$ gives the origin. For (c),
$\psi\le\psi'+c$ implies $e^{-\psi}\ge e^{-c}e^{-\psi'}$, and positivity of
$\mathsf U$ gives the order statement; applying it in both directions with
$c=\lVert\psi-\psi'\rVert_\infty$ gives
\eqref{eq:rg-nonlinear-action-sup-contraction}, and
$\mathsf U(e^{-\psi-c})=e^{-c}\mathsf U(e^{-\psi})$ gives homogeneity, which with
$\mathsf U1=1$ gives (d). For (e),
$\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi C=(e^{-\psi}\mathsf m_o)C/\mathsf m_o(e^{-\psi})$ and
$\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c=\mathsf m_oC/M_o$ give the density
$e^{-\mathcal A(\psi)}/\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$; the first two derivatives of
$t\mapsto\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-th})$ at $t=0$ are $-\bar{\mathsf m}_o(h)$ and
$\operatorname{Var}_{\bar{\mathsf m}_o}(h)$, and polarization gives the covariance.
$\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-action-lp-contraction}.}
Conditional Jensen gives $\lvert\mathsf Uh\rvert^p\le\mathsf U\lvert h\rvert^p$ for
finite $p$, and integrating the disintegration
\eqref{eq:rg-normalized-reverse-kernel} proves
\eqref{eq:rg-action-lp-contraction} and preservation of the mean; $p=\infty$
follows from the pointwise bound. The conditional variance identity
\begin{equation}
\int h^2d\bar{\mathsf m}_o
=\int(\mathsf Uh)^2d\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c
+\int\operatorname{Var}_{C^\dagger(z,\cdot)}(h)\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c(dz),
\label{eq:rg-action-total-conditional-variance}
\end{equation}
the decomposition behind \Cref{thm:cg-fisher-contraction} applied to $h$, proves
\eqref{eq:rg-action-l2-conditional-defect}, and a nonnegative conditional variance
integrates to zero exactly when it vanishes almost everywhere. For the oscillation
bound, fix a representative of $h$ and let $N$ be the $\bar{\mathsf m}_o$-null set on which it
lies outside $[\operatorname{ess~inf}h,\operatorname{ess~sup}h]$. Disintegration gives
$\int C^\dagger(z,N)\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c(dz)=\bar{\mathsf m}_o(N)=0$, so
$C^\dagger(z,N)=0$ for almost every $z$. Thus this fixed $h$ lies between its essential bounds
almost surely under those kernels. For such a pair
$(z,z')$ the signed measure $\mu=C^\dagger(z,\cdot)-C^\dagger(z',\cdot)$ has total
mass zero and positive and negative parts of equal mass $t\le\delta$, so
$(\mathsf Uh)(z)-(\mathsf Uh)(z')=\int h(y)\mu^+(dy)-\int h(y)\mu^-(dy)
\le t(\operatorname{ess~sup}h-\operatorname{ess~inf}h)$. The sets where
$\mathsf Uh$ lies within any positive distance of its essential supremum or
infimum have positive $\bar{\mathsf m}_o^c$ measure, so the bound passes to
$\operatorname{ess~sup}\mathsf Uh-\operatorname{ess~inf}\mathsf Uh$; applying it
factor by factor gives the composition statement. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{cor:rg-perturbation-divergence-bounds}.}
Fix a representative of $\psi$ with values in $[\mathrm a,\mathrm b]$, where
$\mathrm a=\operatorname*{ess~inf}\psi$ and $\mathrm b=\operatorname*{ess~sup}\psi$, so
that $\mathrm b-\mathrm a=2\lVert[\psi]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}$. Then
$e^{-\mathrm b}\le\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})\le e^{-\mathrm a}$, which is the bracket
of the log evidence ratio, and the density $e^{-\psi}/\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$ of
$\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ with respect to $\bar{\mathsf m}_o$ is positive with a
logarithm in $[\mathrm a-\mathrm b,\mathrm b-\mathrm a]$. This gives equivalence and,
since each divergence is the mean of a logarithmic density ratio bounded by
$\mathrm b-\mathrm a$ in absolute value, the linear branch of
\eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-two-sided-kl}. For the quadratic branch, the density of
$\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ gives $\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi(e^{\psi})=1/\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$, so
\begin{align*}
\KL\bigl(\bar{\mathsf m}_o\Vert\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi\bigr)
&=\bar{\mathsf m}_o(\psi)+\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})
=\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o\bigl(e^{-(\psi-\bar{\mathsf m}_o(\psi))}\bigr),\\
\KL\bigl(\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi\Vert\bar{\mathsf m}_o\bigr)
&=-\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi(\psi)-\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})
=\log\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi\bigl(e^{\psi-\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi(\psi)}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Since $\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ is equivalent to $\bar{\mathsf m}_o$, $\psi$ takes values in
$[\mathrm a,\mathrm b]$ almost surely under both laws, and
\citet[Sec.~4, Eq.~(4.16)]{Hoeffding1963}, applied under $\bar{\mathsf m}_o$ to $-\psi$ and under
$\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ to $\psi$ with $h=1$, bounds both divergences by
$(\mathrm b-\mathrm a)^2/8=\lVert[\psi]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}^2/2$.
Adding a constant to $\psi$ changes neither $\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ nor
$\lVert[\psi]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}$. For $Q\ll\bar{\mathsf m}_o$ one has
$\log(dQ/d\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi)=\log(dQ/d\bar{\mathsf m}_o)+\psi
+\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$ $Q$-almost surely; the last two terms are bounded and
the first has a $Q$-integrable negative part
(\Cref{def:prob-log-density-ratio-convention}), so
$\KL(Q\Vert\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi)=\KL(Q\Vert\bar{\mathsf m}_o)+\E_Q\psi
+\log\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$ in $(-\infty,+\infty]$, and the mass
$(e^{-\psi}\mathsf m_o)(\mathsf Y)=M_o\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})$ turns this into the
free-energy identity. Any other $Q$ charges a set that is null for both equivalent
laws, so both divergences are $+\infty$. Along the arrows, suppose
$\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell$ is essentially bounded, as it is for $\ell=0$, where it
equals $\psi_0$. Then $\widetilde{\mathsf m}_\ell=e^{-(\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell)}\mathsf
m_\ell$, so \eqref{eq:rg-local-action-map} gives
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_\ell C_\ell=e^{-\mathcal A_\ell(\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell)}\mathsf
m_{\ell+1}$ and
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}=e^{-\mathcal A_\ell(\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell)
-\psi_{\ell+1}}\mathsf m_{\ell+1}$; taking densities with respect to
$\varrho_{\ell+1}$ where $H_{\ell+1}$ is finite, which is $\mathsf m_{\ell+1}$-almost
everywhere, gives the recurrence in \eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-tower}. Since
$\mathcal A_\ell(0)=0$, part (c) of \Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus} gives
$\mathcal A_\ell(c)=c$ for constants $c$, and applied to the constant bounds of
$\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell$ it keeps $\mathcal A_\ell(\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell)$
between the essential infimum and supremum of $\widetilde H_\ell-H_\ell$. Adding
$\psi_{\ell+1}$ and inducting gives essential boundedness at every depth and the bounds
in \eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-tower}, and the first part of the proof applies at depth
$L$. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{cor:rg-contracted-perturbation-tower}.}
Fix $\ell<L$ and $\psi\in L^\infty(\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell)$ with
$\mathrm a=\operatorname*{ess~inf}\psi$, $\mathrm b=\operatorname*{ess~sup}\psi$, and
$x=\mathrm b-\mathrm a$, and fix a representative with values in
$[\mathrm a,\mathrm b]$ off a $\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell$-null set $N$. As in the proof of
\Cref{thm:rg-action-lp-contraction}, $C^\dagger_\ell(z,N)=0$ for
$\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}$-almost every $z$. When $\Delta_\ell<\infty$, the sets of
full $(\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1})^{\otimes2}$ measure on which
\eqref{eq:rg-birkhoff-diameter} holds for a sequence $t_n\downarrow\Delta_\ell$
intersect in a set of full measure on which it holds with $t=\Delta_\ell$. Hence the
pairs $(z,z')$ whose rows $\mu=C^\dagger_\ell(z,\cdot)$ and
$\mu'=C^\dagger_\ell(z',\cdot)$ both vanish on $N$, satisfy
$\sup_A\lvert\mu(A)-\mu'(A)\rvert\le\delta_\ell$, and, when $\Delta_\ell<\infty$,
satisfy \eqref{eq:rg-birkhoff-diameter} with $t=\Delta_\ell$, form a set of full
measure. For such a pair put $g=e^{\mathrm b-\psi}$, which takes values in $[1,e^x]$
almost surely under both rows, so that
$\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)(z')-\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)(z)=\log(\mu(g)/\mu'(g))$. Since
$\mu-\mu'$ has total mass zero and its positive part has mass at most $\delta_\ell$,
$\mu(g)-\mu'(g)=(\mu-\mu')(g-1)\le\delta_\ell(e^x-1)$, and $\mu'(g)\ge1$, so
\begin{equation*}
\log\frac{\mu(g)}{\mu'(g)}\le\log\bigl(1+\delta_\ell(e^x-1)\bigr).
\end{equation*}
For the second bound let $\Delta_\ell<\infty$, put $\lambda=\mu(g)/\mu'(g)$, let $P$
be a positive set of a Hahn decomposition of the signed measure
$\nu=\mu-\lambda\mu'$, and put $h=1+(e^x-1)1_P$. Because $0\le g-1\le e^x-1$ almost
surely under both rows, $0=\nu(g)=\nu(1)+\nu(g-1)\le\nu(1)+(e^x-1)\nu(P)=\nu(h)$, so
$\mu(h)\ge\lambda\mu'(h)$. With $p=\mu(P)$ and $q=\mu'(P)$,
\begin{equation*}
\log\frac{\mu(h)}{\mu'(h)}=\log\bigl((1-p)+pe^x\bigr)-\log\bigl((1-q)+qe^x\bigr),
\end{equation*}
which is positive only if $p>q$; otherwise $\log\lambda\le0$. If $p>q$, \eqref{eq:rg-birkhoff-diameter} with
$A=P$ and $B=\mathsf Y_\ell\setminus P$ reads $p(1-q)\le e^{\Delta_\ell}q(1-p)$, which
excludes $q=0$ and $p=1$, so $p,q\in(0,1)$ and
$\gamma=\log\bigl(p(1-q)/(q(1-p))\bigr)\in(0,\Delta_\ell]$. Put
$\beta=\log(q/(1-q))$ and $\operatorname{sg}(u)=(1+e^{-u})^{-1}$, the derivative of
$u\mapsto\log(1+e^u)$. Since $1-q=(1+e^\beta)^{-1}$ and $1-p=(1+e^{\beta+\gamma})^{-1}$,
\begin{equation*}
\log\frac{\mu(h)}{\mu'(h)}
=\int_0^x\bigl[\operatorname{sg}(\beta+\gamma+u)-\operatorname{sg}(\beta+u)\bigr]du.
\end{equation*}
The function $u\mapsto\operatorname{sg}(u+\gamma)-\operatorname{sg}(u)$ has
derivative $\operatorname{sg}'(u+\gamma)-\operatorname{sg}'(u)$, and
$\operatorname{sg}'=\operatorname{sg}(1-\operatorname{sg})$ is even and decreasing in
$\lvert u\rvert$, so the derivative is positive for $u<-\gamma/2$ and negative for
$u>-\gamma/2$; the maximum is
$\operatorname{sg}(\gamma/2)-\operatorname{sg}(-\gamma/2)=\tanh(\gamma/4)\le\tanh(\Delta_\ell/4)$.
Hence $\log(\mu(g)/\mu'(g))=\log\lambda\le\log(\mu(h)/\mu'(h))\le\tanh(\Delta_\ell/4)x$,
the form of the contraction ratio of Birkhoff's lemma on Hilbert's projective metric
\citep[Sec.~4, Lemma~1, Eq.~(9a)]{Birkhoff1957}. The sets
where $\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)$ lies within any positive distance of its essential
supremum or infimum have positive $\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}$ measure, so their product
meets the full-measure set of pairs, and letting the distance tend to zero gives
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname*{ess~sup}\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)-\operatorname*{ess~inf}\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)
\le\min\bigl\{\log\bigl(1+\delta_\ell(e^x-1)\bigr),\tanh(\Delta_\ell/4)x\bigr\},
\end{equation*}
the second term being $x$ when $\Delta_\ell=\infty$ by part (c) of
\Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus}.

(a) Take $\psi=\mathfrak r_\ell$, so $x=\omega_\ell$. The recurrence
$\mathfrak r_{\ell+1}=\mathcal A_\ell(\mathfrak r_\ell)+\psi_{\ell+1}$ of
\eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-tower}, subadditivity of the essential supremum, and
superadditivity of the essential infimum give
\eqref{eq:rg-contracted-width-recursion}. For $\omega_\ell>0$ one has
$\delta_\ell(e^{\omega_\ell}-1)<e^{\omega_\ell}-1$ exactly when $\delta_\ell<1$, and
$\tanh(\Delta_\ell/4)<1$ exactly when $\Delta_\ell<\infty$.

(b) For $\delta=0$ take two points $y_0,y_1$ of mass $1/2$, a channel sending each
to the uniform law on $\{z,z'\}$, and $\psi=x1_{\{y_1\}}$; both reverse rows equal the
fine law, so $\mathcal A(\psi)$ is constant. For $\delta\in(0,1]$ give
$(y_1,z)$, $(y_0,z')$, and $(y_1,z')$ the joint masses $1/2$, $\delta/2$, and
$(1-\delta)/2$; the fine law gives $y_0$ and $y_1$ the positive masses $\delta/2$
and $1-\delta/2$, the coarse law is uniform on $\{z,z'\}$, the channel is the
forward conditional law, and the reverse rows are given by $C^\dagger(z,\{y_1\})=1$, $C^\dagger(z',\{y_0\})=\delta$, and
$C^\dagger(z',\{y_1\})=1-\delta$. Their total variation distance, and hence the coefficient, is $\delta$. For
$\psi=x1_{\{y_1\}}$ one has $\mathcal A(\psi)(z)=x$ and
$\mathcal A(\psi)(z')=-\log(\delta+(1-\delta)e^{-x})$, whose difference is
$x+\log(\delta+(1-\delta)e^{-x})=\log(1+\delta(e^x-1))$. The pair $(z',z)$ carries
product mass $1/4$, and \eqref{eq:rg-birkhoff-diameter} with $A=\{y_0\}$ and
$B=\{y_1\}$ would require $\delta\le e^t\cdot0$, so no $t$ qualifies and the
diameter is infinite.

(c) The map $x\mapsto\log(1+\delta(e^x-1))$ is increasing in $x$ and in $\delta$,
so $\omega_{\ell+1}\le\log(1+\delta(e^{\omega_\ell}-1))+s$ for $\ell<L$. The
function $\mathrm k(x)=x-\log(1+\delta(e^x-1))$ has $\mathrm k(0)=0$ and
$\mathrm k'(x)=(1-\delta)/(1+\delta(e^x-1))>0$, and it tends to $\log(1/\delta)$ as
$x\to\infty$, since $\mathrm k(x)=-\log(\delta+(1-\delta)e^{-x})$; so it is a
continuous increasing bijection of $[0,\infty)$ onto $[0,\log(1/\delta))$, and
$x_{\delta,s}$ exists and is unique. Put $\mathrm B=\max\{\omega_0,x_{\delta,s}\}$.
If $\omega_\ell\le\mathrm B$, then
$\omega_{\ell+1}\le\log(1+\delta(e^{\mathrm B}-1))+s=\mathrm B-\mathrm k(\mathrm B)+s\le\mathrm B$,
because $\mathrm k(\mathrm B)\ge\mathrm k(x_{\delta,s})=s$; induction from $\ell=0$
gives the first bound. For the second put
$\mathrm B'=\max\{\omega_0,s/(1-\tanh(\Delta/4))\}$; if $\omega_\ell\le\mathrm B'$,
then $\omega_{\ell+1}\le\tanh(\Delta/4)\mathrm B'+s\le\mathrm B'$, because
$s\le(1-\tanh(\Delta/4))\mathrm B'$. The divergence bound is
\eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-two-sided-kl} at depth $L$ with
$\lVert[\mathfrak r_L]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}=\omega_L/2$.

(d) The joint law $\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell(dy)C_\ell(y,dz)$ is concentrated on the graph
of $\chi_\ell$, a Borel set, so disintegration gives
$\int C^\dagger_\ell(z,\chi_\ell^{-1}\{z\})\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}(dz)=1$ and
$C^\dagger_\ell(z,\chi_\ell^{-1}\{z\})=1$ for almost every $z$. Because
$\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}$ is not a point mass, the diagonal has
$(\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1})^{\otimes2}$ measure equal to the sum of the squared atom
masses, at most the largest atom mass, which is below one; so pairs $z\ne z'$ whose
rows are concentrated on the disjoint fibers $\chi_\ell^{-1}\{z\}$ and
$\chi_\ell^{-1}\{z'\}$ have positive measure. For such a pair, $A=\chi_\ell^{-1}\{z\}$
gives $C^\dagger_\ell(z,A)-C^\dagger_\ell(z',A)=1$, so $\delta_\ell=1$, and with
$B=\mathsf Y_\ell\setminus A$ the left side of \eqref{eq:rg-birkhoff-diameter} is one
and the right side zero, so $\Delta_\ell=\infty$. Both terms of the minimum in
\eqref{eq:rg-contracted-width-recursion} then equal $\omega_\ell$. For a bounded
Borel $f$ on $\mathsf Y_{\ell+1}$ the same concentration gives
$\mathcal A_\ell(f\circ\chi_\ell)(z)=-\log\int e^{-f(\chi_\ell(y))}C^\dagger_\ell(z,dy)=f(z)$
for almost every $z$. Put $F_L=F$, $F_\ell=F_{\ell+1}\circ\chi_\ell$, and
$\bar c_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}c_j$. Then $\mathfrak r_0=\psi_0=\bar c_0F_0$, and
if $\mathfrak r_\ell=\bar c_\ell F_\ell$, the recurrence gives
$\mathfrak r_{\ell+1}=\bar c_\ell F_{\ell+1}+c_{\ell+1}F_{\ell+1}=\bar c_{\ell+1}F_{\ell+1}$.
By \Cref{prop:rg-measure-pair-composition}, $\bar{\mathsf m}_L$ is the image of
$\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell$ under $\chi_{L-1}\circ\cdots\circ\chi_\ell$, so $F_\ell$ has
under $\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell$ the essential range of $F$ under $\bar{\mathsf m}_L$,
of some length $\mathrm W$. Since the $c_j$ are nonnegative,
$\omega^{\mathrm{st}}_j=c_j\mathrm W$ and
$\omega_\ell=\bar c_\ell\mathrm W=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\omega^{\mathrm{st}}_j$.

(e) By \eqref{eq:rg-local-action-map}, $e^{-\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)}$ is the density of
$(e^{-\psi}\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell)C_\ell$ with respect to
$\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell C_\ell=\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}$, and a Markov kernel preserves
total mass, so
$\bar{\mathsf m}_{\ell+1}(e^{-\mathcal A_\ell(\psi)})=\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell(e^{-\psi})$.
Since $\widetilde{\mathsf m}_L=e^{-\mathfrak r_L}\mathsf m_L$, the first equality of
\eqref{eq:rg-tower-offset-decomposition} holds. Mass preservation also gives
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_j(\mathsf Y_j)=\widetilde{\mathsf m}_{j-1}(\mathsf
Y_{j-1})\widehat{\mathsf n}_j(e^{-\psi_j})$ for $j\ge1$,
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_0(\mathsf Y_0)=M_0\bar{\mathsf m}_0(e^{-\psi_0})$, and
$M_L=M_0$ by \Cref{prop:rg-measure-pair-composition}; the product of these factors
gives the second equality. The proof of
\Cref{cor:rg-perturbation-divergence-bounds} gives
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_{j-1}C_{j-1}=e^{-\mathcal A_{j-1}(\mathfrak r_{j-1})}\mathsf m_j$
with a bounded exponent, so $\widehat{\mathsf n}_j$ is equivalent to
$\bar{\mathsf m}_j$, $\psi_j$ has the same essential bounds under both, and each
summand lies in the stated interval. Finally, by part (c) of
\Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus},
$\mathcal A_{\ell'}(\mathfrak r_{\ell'}+c)=\mathcal A_{\ell'}(\mathfrak r_{\ell'})+c$ for $\ell'\ge j$, so replacing $\psi_j$
by $\psi_j+c$ replaces $\mathfrak r_\ell$ by $\mathfrak r_\ell+c$ and
$\widetilde{\mathsf m}_\ell$ by $e^{-c}\widetilde{\mathsf m}_\ell$ for every
$\ell\ge j$ and changes nothing at smaller depths, for every choice of arrows. Widths
and normalizations are therefore unchanged, and
$\log\bar{\mathsf m}_L(e^{-\mathfrak r_L})$ decreases by $c$. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-hoeffding-action-isomorphism}.}
Fubini's theorem for the product $\nu_\ell$ gives commutation and
$\E_{\ell,B}\E_{\ell,B'}=\E_{\ell,B\cap B'}$; expanding the
product in \eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-mobius-projectors} with this rule gives the
alternating sum, and M\"obius inversion on the Boolean lattice gives the
orthogonality relations and the resolution of the identity. Every term of
$\mathsf P_{\ell,B}f$ is a function of $y_B$, and the factor
$I-\E_{\ell,V_\ell\setminus\{i\}}$ removes its mean in coordinate $i$; hence
the nonempty components recover $\mathsf g$ and sum to
$f-\E_{\ell,\varnothing}f$, the class $[f]$. The triangle inequality and
$\lVert[\psi]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}\le\lVert\psi\rVert_\infty$ give
$\lVert\operatorname{asm}_\ell\rVert\le1$. For nonempty $B$ the projector
$\mathsf P_{\ell,B}$ annihilates constants and is an alternating sum of
$2^{\lvert B\rvert}$ contractions, so
$\lVert\mathsf P_{\ell,B}f\rVert_\infty\le2^{\lvert B\rvert}\lVert f-c\rVert_\infty$
for every constant $c$, and
$\sum_{B\ne\varnothing}2^{\lvert B\rvert}=3^{\lvert V_\ell\rvert}-1$. For the
biased coordinates, expanding $\prod_i\bigl((Y_i-m)+m\bigr)$ with
$m=\E Y_i=2p-1$ gives the components $m^{n-\lvert B\rvert}\prod_{i\in B}(Y_i-m)$,
whose supremum norms $m^{n-\lvert B\rvert}(1+m)^{\lvert B\rvert}$ sum to
$(1+2m)^n-m^n=(4p-1)^n-(2p-1)^n$. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{prop:rg-interaction-intra-block-invariance}.}
Because $\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell$ is a product over agents and hence over blocks, the
joint law $\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell(dy)C_\ell(y,dz)$ is the product of the block laws
$\bar{\mathsf m}_\ell^{I}(dy_I)C_I(y_I,dz_I)$; its $z$-marginal is the product of
the block marginals, and the product of the block reverse kernels
$C_I^\dagger(z_I,dy_I)$ is a version of the reverse kernel. For $\mathsf
g\in\mathcal G_\ell^{\mathrm{intra}}$, grouping components by block writes
$\psi_{\mathsf g}=\sum_I\psi_I$ with $\psi_I$ a function of $y_I$, so Fubini's
theorem gives $\mathcal A_\ell(\psi_{\mathsf g})=\sum_If_I(z_I)$ with
$f_I=-\log\mathsf U_I(e^{-\psi_I})$. For $\lvert B\rvert\ge2$ choose
$j\in B\setminus\{I\}$; the function $\E_{\ell+1,B}f_I$ does not depend on
$z_j$, so the factor $I-\E_{\ell+1,V_{\ell+1}\setminus\{j\}}$ of the product
form of \eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-mobius-projectors} annihilates it. Hence
$\mathsf P_{\ell+1,B}f_I=0$, and linearity finishes the proof. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{prop:rg-interaction-rn-gauge-covariance}.}
Channel covariance gives $\widehat{\mathsf m}_\ell\widehat
C_\ell=(\kappa_{\ell+1})_\#(\mathsf m_\ell C_\ell)$ and
$(e^{-\mathsf K_\ell\psi}\widehat{\mathsf m}_\ell)\widehat
C_\ell=(\kappa_{\ell+1})_\#((e^{-\psi}\mathsf m_\ell)C_\ell)$. The Radon--Nikodym
derivative of two measures pushed through one Borel isomorphism is the transported
derivative, and applying $-\log$ gives \eqref{eq:rg-action-rn-gauge-covariance}
without selecting a pointwise reverse kernel. Differentiating at an arbitrary
bounded $\psi$ with part (b) of \Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus} gives
\eqref{eq:rg-tilted-derivative-gauge-covariance}. Composing with the intertwinings
\eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-gauge-covariance} gives
\eqref{eq:rg-nonlinear-interaction-gauge-covariance}, and inserting the intertwining
of the retained projections into $I-\mathsf P^{\mathrm{ret}}_{\ell+1}$ and into
\Cref{prop:rg-interaction-residual} gives the residual formulas. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{prop:rg-hyperedge-generation}.}
(a) is Tonelli's theorem applied to the product of nonnegative factors. (b) is
M\"obius inversion on the Boolean lattice of subsets of $V^c$, and the component
indexed by $B$ reads only $z_B$ because every term does. (c) follows by
substituting $gz$ and $gz^\circ$ in \eqref{eq:rg-mobius-potentials}. For (d), write
$f(u)=-\log2\cosh u$, so $f'''=2\operatorname{sech}^2\tanh$. With
$x=\sum_r\xi_ry_r$ and $y_r^2=1$, only odd powers of $x$ contain the monomial
$y_1y_2y_3$; the cubic term $f'''(\xi_0)x^3/6$ contributes
$f'''(\xi_0)\xi_1\xi_2\xi_3$, and higher odd powers are of order five in the
couplings. At $\xi_0=0$ the function $f(x)$ is even in $y$, so every odd Walsh
coefficient vanishes. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{prop:rg-bridge-kernels}.}
Adjointness is \eqref{eq:rg-agent-meta-bayes-adjoint} integrated against
$f(y_j)g(z_I)$. For the factorization witness take two blocks with one
$\{-1,1\}$-valued agent each, a uniform product baseline, a channel that flips each
coordinate independently with probability $1/5$, and the likelihood
$e^{Jy_1y_2}$. The baseline reverse probability of $y_1=1$ given $z_1=1$ is $4/5$
whatever $z_2$ is, while with $x=e^{2J}$ the posterior reverse probability is
$4(x+4)/(8x+17)$ at $z_2=-1$ and $4(4x+1)/(17x+8)$ at $z_2=1$, which differ exactly
when $J\ne0$. For a deterministic statistic, $\mathsf C\mathsf
Jf(z)=\E[f(\chi(Y))\given\chi(Y)=z]=f(z)$ almost everywhere. A channel whose output
does not depend on $y$ gives $\mathsf C\mathsf Jf=\boldsymbol\Pi^c_o(f)$, which
differs from $f$ for nonconstant $f$. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-strong-lumpability}.}
Assume \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel} and take $\mu=\delta_y$. Then
$\mathsf T(y,\chi^{-1}A)=\mathsf T^\chi(\chi(y),A)$ for every $y$ and Borel $A$,
whose right side depends on $y$ only through $\chi(y)$, and surjectivity determines
$\mathsf T^\chi$ at every $z$. Conversely, for fixed $z$ the map
$A\mapsto\mathsf T(\iota(z),\chi^{-1}A)$ is a probability measure because $\chi^{-1}$
commutes with countable disjoint unions, and for fixed $A$ it is Borel in $z$ as the
composition of $\iota$ with $y\mapsto\mathsf T(y,\chi^{-1}A)$. Since
$\chi(\iota(\chi(y)))=\chi(y)$, hypothesis \eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} gives the
same pointwise identity, and integrating it against $\mu$ gives
\eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel}. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-memory-projection}.}
At $t=0$ the empty context has mass one. If the context at $t$ has its
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_N$ marginal, multiplying by its row gives the correct
context-plus-symbol marginal, and marginalizing the oldest symbol gives the next
context marginal; by induction no positive prefix enters a null context, and
successive row sums give normalization. Expanding the logarithm of the products and
regrouping by contexts gives \eqref{eq:rg-memory-excess-risk}; a competitor that
excludes a positive word has infinite divergence. Equality forces every occupied row
to agree, and recursive reachability forces the same path law. Subtracting the
source chain-rule logarithm from the projected product leaves, in expectation, the
difference between entropy given the context and entropy given the full past, which
is the conditional mutual information \eqref{eq:rg-memory-information-defect} by the
chain rules of \citet[Sec.~2.5]{CoverThomas2006}. Conditioning on more history does
not increase entropy, so the defect decreases in $k$, and $k\ge N-1$ keeps the whole
past. The projection keeps its own defining marginals, and a shorter prefix only
clips the available history. The total-variation bound is Pinsker's inequality
\citep[Lemma~11.6.1]{CoverThomas2006}. For the cycle,
$\mathbb P(Z_0=0)=2/3$ and both one-step rows from $0$ put mass $1/2$ on $0$, so the
projection gives $000$ the mass $1/6$ although the cycle never produces it.
Subtracting the two finite log ratios gives
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recognition-vfe-difference}. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-memory-borel-projection}.}
Write $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t$ and $\widetilde{\mathbb P}_t$ for the laws of
$Z_{0:t-1}$ under $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_N$ and $\widetilde{\mathbb P}$, the case
$t=0$ living on a point, abbreviate $\mathbf f_t(Z_{0:t-1})$ to $\mathbf f_t$, and let
$\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$ and $\widetilde{\mathbb P}^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$ be the laws
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(dz_{0:t-1})\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t(\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1}),dz_t)$
and
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(dz_{0:t-1})\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t(\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1}),dz_t)$
on $\mathsf Z^{t+1}$. The kernel $\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t(\mathbf f_t(\cdot),\cdot)$ is a
version of the law of $Z_t$ given $Z_{0:t-1}$ both under $\widetilde{\mathbb P}$ and
under $\widetilde{\mathbb P}^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$, whose first marginal is
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t$, so part (a) of \Cref{lem:prob-kl-chain-rule} with first
coordinate $Z_{0:t-1}$, applied to both pairs, gives
\begin{equation*}
\KL\bigl(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}\Vert\widetilde{\mathbb P}_{t+1}\bigr)
=\KL\bigl(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t\Vert\widetilde{\mathbb P}_t\bigr)
+\KL\bigl(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}\Vert\widetilde{\mathbb P}^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}\bigr)
\end{equation*}
in $[0,+\infty]$, and summing over $t$ writes
$\KL(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_N\Vert\widetilde{\mathbb P})$ as the sum of the last
terms. Fix $t$ and let $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(\cdot\given\mathbf w)$ be a regular
conditional law of $Z_{0:t-1}$ given $\mathbf f_t=\mathbf w$. The map
$(z_{0:t-1},z_t)\mapsto(\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1}),z_t,z_{0:t-1})$ is Borel and the Borel
projection onto its last two components inverts it, so part (d) of the lemma, applied
to the map and to the projection, shows that pushing two laws through it preserves
their divergence; equivalently, the map is a bimeasurable bijection onto its image,
which is Borel as the preimage of the diagonal of the standard Borel statistic space
under a Borel map, and part (e) applies there. After the push, Fubini's theorem, and
the fact that under the two laws just defined $Z_t$ reads the history only through
$\mathbf f_t$, give the triple $(\mathbf f_t,Z_t,Z_{0:t-1})$ the law
$(\mathbf f_t)_\#\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(d\mathbf w)\mathsf T^{\mathbf
f}_t(\mathbf w,dz_t)\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(dz_{0:t-1}\given\mathbf w)$ under
$\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$ and the same law with $\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t$ in place
of $\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t$ under $\widetilde{\mathbb P}^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$, while under
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}$ the pair $(\mathbf f_t,Z_t)$ has the law
$(\mathbf f_t)_\#\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(d\mathbf w)\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t(\mathbf
w,dz_t)$ by the definition of $\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t$. Part (a) with first coordinate
$(\mathbf f_t,Z_t)$ therefore writes
$\KL(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}\Vert\widetilde{\mathbb P}^{\mathbf f}_{t+1})$ as
the divergence of the $(\mathbf f_t,Z_t)$-marginals plus an average, under the
$(\mathbf f_t,Z_t)$-marginal of $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}$, of the divergence
from the conditional history law of $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}$ to
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(\cdot\given\mathbf w)$; the same application to
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}$ and $\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$, whose
$(\mathbf f_t,Z_t)$-marginals coincide, shows that this average is
$\KL(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}\Vert\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1})$. Part (a) along
$\mathbf f_t$ turns the marginal term into
$\int\KL(\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t(\mathbf w,\cdot)\Vert\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t(\mathbf
w,\cdot))(\mathbf f_t)_\#\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(d\mathbf w)$, the $t$-th row term
of \eqref{eq:rg-memory-borel-identity} after the change of variables
$\mathbf w=\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1})$. Pushing $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}$ and
$\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$ instead through
$(z_{0:t-1},z_t)\mapsto(\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1}),(z_t,z_{0:t-1}))$, inverted in the same
way, and applying (a) along $\mathbf f_t$, whose laws agree, writes
$\KL(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}\Vert\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1})$ as the average
of the divergence between the conditional law of $(Z_t,Z_{0:t-1})$ given $\mathbf f_t$
and the product
$\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t(\mathbf f_t,\cdot)\otimes\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_t(\cdot\given\mathbf
f_t)$ of its conditional marginals, which is
$I(Z_t;Z_{0:t-1}\given\mathbf f_t)$. Summing over $t$ proves
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-borel-identity}, each term being independent of the chosen versions
by (a).

The row terms are nonnegative and vanish for
$\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t=\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t$, which gives the minimum and, when
$D^{\mathbf f}_N$ is finite, the difference. The $t$-th defect term vanishes exactly
when $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{t+1}=\mathbb P^{\mathbf f}_{t+1}$, that is, by
uniqueness of disintegrations, when
$\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t(\mathbf f_t(\cdot),\cdot)$ is a version of the law of $Z_t$
given $Z_{0:t-1}$; conversely, a version that depends on the history only through
$\mathbf f_t$ is a conditional law of $Z_t$ given $\mathbf f_t$ and so agrees with
$\mathsf T^{\mathbf f}_t$ at almost every value. For
$\mathbf f_t(z_{0:t-1})=\mathbf z_t$ the history is the concatenation of
$Z_{0:t-k-1}$ with $\mathbf z_t$, so at almost every value $\mathbf z$ of
$\mathbf z_t$ the two laws whose divergence enters
$I(Z_t;Z_{0:t-1}\given\mathbf z_t)$ are the images of those entering
$I(Z_t;Z_{0:t-k-1}\given\mathbf z_t)$ under
$(z_t,z_{0:t-k-1})\mapsto(z_t,(z_{0:t-k-1},\mathbf z))$, which a coordinate projection
inverts; part (d) twice equates the two divergences, and an empty discarded history
is a point and contributes zero. On a finite $\mathsf Z$ the rows at contexts of
positive mass are those of \eqref{eq:rg-memory-projection-product}, the expectations
are the sums of \eqref{eq:rg-memory-excess-risk}, and
$\widetilde{\mathsf T}_t=\mathsf T^{(k)}_t$ gives
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-information-defect}. Finally, where the competitor row is
$e^{-\psi}$ times the source row, the two rows are the laws $\bar{\mathsf m}_o$ and
$\widehat{\mathsf m}^\psi$ of \Cref{cor:rg-perturbation-divergence-bounds} with
$\bar{\mathsf m}_o$ the source row and $\bar{\mathsf m}_o(e^{-\psi})=1$, and
\eqref{eq:rg-perturbation-two-sided-kl} bounds the row divergence. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{thm:rg-memory-duration}.}
Stationarity and nonnegativity force $\mathsf T_{yy'}=0$ when $\bar p_y>0=\bar
p_{y'}$, so the positive support is closed. For $k\ge1$ the positive $k$-context
chain shifts a context to its last $k$ symbols after appending a symbol, with row
$\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{k+1}(\mathbf zz)/\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_k(\mathbf z)$; its incoming mass at a context
is the corresponding marginal of $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_{k+1}$, which equals $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{path}}_k$ of that
context by stationarity, and this gives stationarity and exact $(k+1)$-windows; for
$k=0$ the projection is the stationary product of the one-symbol marginal.
Conditioning and stationarity give $0\le\mathrm h_{j+1}\le\mathrm h_j$. The chain
rule gives $H(Z_{0:N-1})=\sum_{j<N}\mathrm h_j$, and for $N>k$ the projected
cross-entropy is $H(Z_{0:k-1})+(N-k)\mathrm h_k$, which gives the sum and its rate.
For two adjacent observed blocks of lengths $n_1$ and $n_2$, past and future are
conditionally independent given the intervening hidden state, so their mutual
information is at most $\mathrm c_Y$; expanding it by stationary entropies gives
$H(Z_{0:n_2-1})-\sum_{j=0}^{n_2-1}\mathrm h_{n_1+j}\le\mathrm c_Y$, and letting $n_1$
and then $n_2$ grow gives the series bound, whose monotone terms give
$(k+1)\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_k\le\mathrm c_Y$. Finally, for $N>k$,
$N\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_k-D^{(k)}_N=k\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_k
+\sum_{t=k}^{N-1}\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_t\le\mathrm c_Y$, for $N\le k$ the bound
$N\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_k\le\mathrm c_Y$ holds, and bounding each nonzero summand of
$D^{(k)}_N$ by $\mathrm h^{\mathrm{ex}}_k$ gives the last inequality. $\square$

\noindent\emph{Proof of \Cref{prop:hist-law-fisher-agentization-nogo}.}
Relabelings fix the uniform law and its Fisher metric, so a natural rule would be
permuted by them and would induce a homomorphism from the symmetric group $S_7$ to
the symmetric group $S_3$ of the three blocks. Its restriction to the alternating
group $A_7$, which is perfect, is trivial, because $S_3$ is solvable, so every block
would be $A_7$-invariant. The tangent space is the sum-zero representation of
$A_7$. Since $A_7$ is doubly transitive on the seven outcomes, the commutant of its
permutation representation is spanned by the identity and the all-ones matrix, so
every equivariant endomorphism of the sum-zero space is scalar, the representation
is irreducible, and it has no invariant two-dimensional subspace. $\square$
