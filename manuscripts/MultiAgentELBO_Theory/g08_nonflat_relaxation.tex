\chapter[Coarse Maps II: Nonflat Links, Relaxation, and Quotient Volume]{Coarse Maps II: Nonflat Links,\\ Relaxation, and Quotient Volume}
\label{ch:gaussian-nonflat}

What does hard aggregation do to nonflat internal links, how does a Gaussian
mean relax onto the fixed sector, what is the Gaussian form of the
holonomy-constrained parent, and how does the volume of hard membership enter a
partition function? A twisted internal link adds a positive-semidefinite self
term to its cluster, which only a singular weight can hide, and one effective
quotient for all links exists exactly under the condition of
\Cref{prop:cg-effective-support}. The constant-metric flow relaxes
exponentially onto the fixed sector and is the Fisher natural-gradient flow on
the fixed-covariance mean family (\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}).
The Gaussian forward-KL barycenter is moment matching with a log-determinant
score (\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}), and under compact
holonomy the constrained parent is the Haar-projected moment match
(\Cref{cor:cg-compact-holonomy-barycenter}), while no parameter-independent
Markov kernel realizes the joint barycenter of two normal laws for all
parameters (\Cref{prop:hist-normal-barycenter-kernel}). Hard membership carries the
quotient metric of \Cref{prop:cg-quotient-metric}, whose Jacobian changes raw
normalizers and not normalized laws. These results continue
\Cref{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining} and specialize the structural fixed sectors
(\Cref{sec:cg-partial}) and the holonomy-constrained marginal parents
(\Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal}) of the main manuscript; the relaxation is
the identified natural-gradient example of \Cref{ch:infogeometry}. Notation
follows \Cref{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining}; in addition \(\mathsf G\) denotes a
constant metric, \(g\) a passive linear change of coordinates or an element of a
holonomy group, and \(w_i\) mixture weights.

This chapter does not identify a generic, state-dependent Fisher flow with the
constant projector and rate of the constant-metric relaxation, and it does not
claim that a partition is selected by quotient volume or by transported
relative entropy. A natural partition selector remains open
(\Cref{open:cg-natural-selector}). \status{NOT-CLAIMED} Balanced cut links admit no continuous
extension to all singular cuts (\Cref{open:cg-cut-compression}).

\section{Nonflat links and effective support}
\label{sec:cg-effective-support}

We return to hard identification with twisted internal links. For an edge
\(e=(i,j)\) with link \(\Theta_e\) and weight \(W_e\succeq0\), the twisted pair
energy is
\begin{equation}
\frac12(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j).
\label{eq:cg-twisted-pair}
\end{equation}
With a latent link, \(\Theta_e=e^{\mathring\omega_e}\) in reference coordinates;
at the means of Gaussian laws whose transported source has the receiver's
covariance, and with \(W_e\) the receiver's precision, this energy is the
law-level transported divergence of \Cref{prop:glink-law-level-energy}.

\propositionheading{Nonflat internal links and effective support}{prop:cg-effective-support}
Let the assembled precision \(\Lambda\), with twisted links, be SPD, and let
\(S\) be the aggregation matrix of a hard partition.
(i) The hard trace of \(\Lambda\), with natural parameters
\((S^\top\mathsf h,S^\top\Lambda S)\), is the conditional law of
\Cref{prop:cg-aggregation-conditioning} with \(B=S\); it is in general not the
coordinate marginal of \Cref{thm:cg-nonflat-series-kron}.
(ii) Under hard identification an internal edge contributes to its cluster the
PSD self term
\begin{equation}
(I-\Theta_e)^\top W_e(I-\Theta_e),
\label{eq:cg-internal-residual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes exactly when
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0 ;
\label{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}
\end{equation}
a positive-definite \(W_e\) forces \(\Theta_e=I\), while a singular weight can
hide a nonidentity link.
(iii) With \(\operatorname{pr}_e:\R^K\to\R^K/\ker W_e\) the quotient map,
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{pr}_e\Theta_e=\operatorname{pr}_e .
\label{eq:cg-edge-quotient}
\end{equation}
(iv) For links \(\Theta_e\in\GL(K)\), a single subspace \(\mathscr N\subseteq\R^K\)
such that every pair form descends to \(\R^K/\mathscr N\) with positive-definite
induced weight exists exactly when
\begin{equation}
\ker W_e=\mathscr N,\qquad \Theta_e\mathscr N=\mathscr N
\quad\text{for every relevant edge},
\label{eq:cg-common-quotient}
\end{equation}
and on that quotient group-valued holonomy is defined; when the kernels differ
there is no canonical product of the edge-local quotients.
(v) On the triangle with
\(W_{12}=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), \(\Theta_{12}=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\),
\(W_{23}=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\), \(\Theta_{23}=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), and
\(W_{31}=\Theta_{31}=I\), every edge satisfies \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}
and the loop product is \(2I\), which fixes no nonzero vector; the kernels
differ, so no common quotient exists. Nevertheless the kernel of the cluster's
connection energy, cut out by the section constraints
\eqref{eq:cg-psd-section-constraint}, is the two-dimensional space of constant
sections, so hard fixed-section aggregation of this cluster keeps the full
fiber. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
(i) Precomposing the energy with \(z=Sy\) gives the natural parameters
\((S^\top\mathsf h,S^\top\Lambda S)\), which are those of the conditional law by
\Cref{prop:cg-aggregation-conditioning}; the marginal precision is a Schur
complement, which is dominated by the restricted precision with equality only
in the \(\Lambda\)-orthogonal case
(\Cref{prop:restrict-marginal-vs-restricted-precision}). (ii) Setting
\(z_i=z_j=y\) turns \eqref{eq:cg-twisted-pair} into
\(\frac12y^\top(I-\Theta_e)^\top W_e(I-\Theta_e)y
=\frac12\|W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)y\|^2\) (\Cref{lem:gauss-internal-edge}), which
vanishes for every \(y\) exactly under \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}. If
\(W_e\succ0\) this forces \(I-\Theta_e=0\), while
\(W_e=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\) and \(\Theta_e=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\)
satisfy it. (iii) \(\operatorname{pr}_e\Theta_e=\operatorname{pr}_e\) means
\(\operatorname{range}(I-\Theta_e)\subseteq\ker W_e=\ker W_e^{1/2}\). (iv) The
pair form descends in both endpoint variables exactly when
\(W_e^{1/2}n=0\) and \(W_e^{1/2}\Theta_en'=0\) for all \(n,n'\in\mathscr N\), that
is, \(\mathscr N\subseteq\ker W_e\) and \(\Theta_e\mathscr N\subseteq\ker W_e\).
Positive definiteness of the induced weight forces \(\ker W_e=\mathscr N\), and
invertibility of \(\Theta_e\) turns \(\Theta_e\mathscr N\subseteq\mathscr N\) into
\(\Theta_e\mathscr N=\mathscr N\) and makes the induced link invertible. (v) Each
weighted residual vanishes by direct multiplication, and
\(\Theta_{12}\Theta_{23}\Theta_{31}=2I\). The kernels are
\(\operatorname{span}(e_2)\), \(\operatorname{span}(e_1)\), and \(\{0\}\). The
section constraints \(W_e^{1/2}(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)=0\) give
\(z_1^{(1)}=z_2^{(1)}\), \(z_2^{(2)}=z_3^{(2)}\), and \(z_3=z_1\), whose solutions
are exactly the constant sections. \(\square\)

\section{Constant-metric Gaussian relaxation}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}

\propositionheading{Relaxation onto the fixed sector}{prop:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}
Let \(\mathsf G=\mathsf G^\top\succ0\) and \(L=L^\top\succeq0\) be constant
matrices on \(\R^d\), let the columns of \(B_0\) form a basis of \(\ker L\), and
define the \(\mathsf G\)-orthogonal projector
\begin{equation}
\Pi_0=B_0(B_0^\top\mathsf GB_0)^{-1}B_0^\top\mathsf G,
\label{eq:cg-r-projector}
\end{equation}
with \(\Pi_0=0\) when \(\ker L=\{0\}\). The flow
\begin{equation}
\mathsf G\dot z=-Lz,\qquad z(0)=z_0,
\label{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow}
\end{equation}
converges to \(\Pi_0z_0\). If \(L\neq0\) and
\begin{equation}
r_+=\lambda_{\min}^{+}\bigl(\mathsf G^{-1/2}L\mathsf G^{-1/2}\bigr)
\label{eq:cg-transverse-rate}
\end{equation}
is the smallest positive eigenvalue, then
\begin{equation}
\|z(t)-\Pi_0z_0\|_{\mathsf G}
\leq e^{-r_+t}
\|z_0-\Pi_0z_0\|_{\mathsf G},
\label{eq:cg-transverse-decay}
\end{equation}
and for \(\mathsf E(z)=\frac12z^\top Lz\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf E(z(t))
=-\dot z^\top\mathsf G\dot z
=-z^\top L\mathsf G^{-1}Lz\leq0.
\label{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}
\end{equation}
A constant passive reframing \(z'=gz\), \(g\in\GL(d)\), preserves the equation
exactly under
\begin{equation}
\mathsf G'=g^{-\top}\mathsf Gg^{-1},\qquad
L'=g^{-\top}Lg^{-1},\qquad
\Pi_0'=g\Pi_0g^{-1},
\label{eq:cg-flow-congruence}
\end{equation}
so the choice \(\mathsf G=I\) is equivariant only under orthogonal reframings.
For a time-dependent \(g=g(t)\) with vanishing original temporal connection, the
covariant derivative
\begin{equation}
D_t'z'=\dot z'-\dot gg^{-1}z'=g\dot z,
\qquad \mathsf G'D_t'z'=-L'z',
\label{eq:cg-time-covariant-flow}
\end{equation}
restores the equation. On the fixed-covariance Gaussian mean family
\(q_z=\mathcal N(z,\mathsf G^{-1})\) the Fisher metric of the mean is
\(\mathsf G\) and
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_z\Vert q_w)
=\frac12(z-w)^\top\mathsf G(z-w),
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-kl}
\end{equation}
so \eqref{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow} is exactly the Fisher natural-gradient
flow of \(\mathsf E\) on that family. For a family \((q_\lambda)\) with Fisher
metric \(g^F\) whose log-density is twice continuously differentiable in
\(\lambda\), with the domination of \Cref{hyp:pb-regular-models} permitting
differentiation under the integral through second order, only the local
expansion
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_\lambda\Vert q_{\lambda+d\lambda})
=\frac12d\lambda^\top g^F(\lambda)d\lambda
+o(\|d\lambda\|^2)
\label{eq:cg-local-fisher-kl}
\end{equation}
holds. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Set \(y=\mathsf G^{1/2}z\) and \(\widetilde L=\mathsf G^{-1/2}L\mathsf G^{-1/2}\succeq0\).
Then \(\dot y=-\widetilde Ly\), and the spectral theorem gives convergence to the
Euclidean projection of \(y_0\) onto \(\ker\widetilde L=\mathsf G^{1/2}\ker L\), with
transverse norm decaying at the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(\widetilde L\).
Transforming back gives \eqref{eq:cg-r-projector} and
\eqref{eq:cg-transverse-decay}, and differentiating \(\mathsf E\) along the
flow gives \eqref{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}. Substituting \(z=g^{-1}z'\)
proves \eqref{eq:cg-flow-congruence}, and \(\dot z'=\dot gz+g\dot z\) proves
\eqref{eq:cg-time-covariant-flow}. The Gaussian relative entropy with equal
covariances gives \eqref{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-kl}, whose Hessian is the Fisher
metric \(\mathsf G\), and the natural gradient of \(\mathsf E\) in that metric
is \(\mathsf G^{-1}Lz\). For the local expansion write
\(\mathbf s_j=\partial_j\log q_\lambda\). At \(\lambda'=\lambda\) the first derivative
of \(\KL(q_\lambda\Vert q_{\lambda'})\) in \(\lambda'\) is
\(-\E_\lambda[\mathbf s_j]=0\), and the second is
\(-\E_\lambda[\partial_i\mathbf s_j]=\E_\lambda[\mathbf s_i\mathbf s_j]=g^F_{ij}(\lambda)\), by
differentiating \(\int q_\lambda\mathbf s_j=0\); Taylor's theorem in \(\lambda'\)
gives \eqref{eq:cg-local-fisher-kl}. \(\square\)

Freezing \(g^F(\lambda_0)=\mathsf G\) yields only a local linearization of a
general family. The rate \(r_+\) and the dissipation per unit \(t\) belong to
the flow parameter, not to the unparameterized orbit.

\section{Gaussian marginal-law specialization}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-marginal-law}

Specialize \Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal} to Gaussian laws after
transporting every constituent marginal to one root, with the full nondegenerate
Gaussian family as admissible parent family. With trivial represented holonomy
the holonomy-fixed subfamily is that whole family; the parent family itself is
declared, not selected by the holonomy.

\propositionheading{Full-Gaussian forward-KL barycenter}{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}
Let \(q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)\) with \(\Sigma_i\succ0\), and let
\(w_i>0\) with \(\sum_iw_i=1\). The unique minimizer over nondegenerate
Gaussians of
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I(\mu,\Sigma)
=\sum_i w_i\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)\Vert\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)\bigr)
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective}
\end{equation}
matches the first two moments,
\begin{equation}
\bar\mu=\sum_i w_i\mu_i,\qquad
\bar\Sigma=\sum_i w_i\bigl[\Sigma_i+(\mu_i-\bar\mu)(\mu_i-\bar\mu)^\top\bigr],
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}
\end{equation}
and its minimized score is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I^\star
=\frac12\Bigl[
\log\det\bar\Sigma-\sum_i w_i\log\det\Sigma_i
\Bigr].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}
\end{equation}
If every constituent and the parent are restricted to one covariance
\(\Sigma_0\succ0\), the minimizer is \(\mathcal N(\bar\mu,\Sigma_0)\), and with
the symmetrized convention
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{SKL}}(p,q)
:=\frac12\bigl[\KL(p\Vert q)+\KL(q\Vert p)\bigr]
\label{eq:cg-symmetric-kl-convention}
\end{equation}
its score satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sum_i w_i\KL\bigl(q_i\Vert\mathcal N(\bar\mu,\Sigma_0)\bigr)
=\sum_{i<j}w_iw_jD_{\mathrm{SKL}}(q_i,q_j)
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}w_iw_jD_{\mathrm{SKL}}(q_i,q_j),
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-pairwise-identity}
\end{equation}
which is not the free-covariance optimum, whose covariance includes the
between-mean term of \eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Minimizing \(\sum_iw_i\KL(q_i\Vert q)\) over an exponential family \(q\) is
minimizing \(\KL(\sum_iw_iq_i\Vert q)\) up to a constant, which is moment
matching \citep[Sec.~10.7, Eqs.~(10.184)--(10.187)]{Bishop2006}; we record the
score. The Gaussian relative entropy gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
2\mathcal D_I(\mu,\Sigma)
&=\Tr(\Sigma^{-1}\Xi(\mu))-K+\log\det\Sigma
-\sum_i w_i\log\det\Sigma_i,\\
\Xi(\mu)&=\sum_i w_i\bigl[\Sigma_i+(\mu_i-\mu)(\mu_i-\mu)^\top\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-expansion}
\end{equation}
Completing the weighted square gives
\(\Xi(\mu)=\bar\Sigma+(\mu-\bar\mu)(\mu-\bar\mu)^\top\), so \(\mu=\bar\mu\) is
the unique minimizer in \(\mu\). At that value
\(\Tr(\Sigma^{-1}\bar\Sigma)-\log\det(\Sigma^{-1}\bar\Sigma)\geq K\), with
equality exactly when \(\Sigma=\bar\Sigma\), and substitution gives
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}. With one covariance \(\Sigma_0\),
\(\KL(q_i\Vert\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma_0))=\frac12\|\mu_i-\mu\|^2_{\Sigma_0^{-1}}\)
is minimized in the weighted sum at \(\bar\mu\), and
\(D_{\mathrm{SKL}}(q_i,q_j)=\frac12\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2_{\Sigma_0^{-1}}\); the
weighted variance identity
\(\sum_iw_i\|\mu_i-\bar\mu\|^2=\frac12\sum_{i,j}w_iw_j\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2\) in the
\(\Sigma_0^{-1}\) inner product gives
\eqref{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-pairwise-identity}. \(\square\)

\corollaryheading{Compact-closure holonomy Gaussian barycenter}{cor:cg-compact-holonomy-barycenter}
In the setting of \Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}, let
\(\mathrm{Hol}_I\subset\GL(K)\) be a represented holonomy group whose closure
\(\overline{\mathrm{Hol}}_I\) is compact, with normalized Haar probability
\(dg\). A nondegenerate Gaussian is \(\mathrm{Hol}_I\)-invariant exactly when it
is \(\overline{\mathrm{Hol}}_I\)-invariant. Define
\begin{equation}
\bar\mu=\sum_i w_i\mu_i,\qquad
\Xi=\sum_i w_i(\Sigma_i+\mu_i\mu_i^\top),
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-raw-moments}
\end{equation}
and their Haar projections
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{Hol}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathrm{Hol}}_I}g\bar\mu dg,\qquad
\Xi_{\mathrm{Hol}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathrm{Hol}}_I}g\Xi g^\top dg,\qquad
\Sigma_{\mathrm{Hol}}
=\Xi_{\mathrm{Hol}}-\mu_{\mathrm{Hol}}\mu_{\mathrm{Hol}}^\top .
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-moments}
\end{equation}
Then \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{Hol}}\succ0\), and
\(\mathcal N(\mu_{\mathrm{Hol}},\Sigma_{\mathrm{Hol}})\) is the unique minimizer of
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective} over \(\mathrm{Hol}_I\)-invariant
nondegenerate Gaussians, with score
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_{I,\mathrm{Hol}}^\star
=\frac12\Bigl[
\log\det\Sigma_{\mathrm{Hol}}-\sum_i w_i\log\det\Sigma_i
\Bigr].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}
\end{equation}
The constraint can be active without any disagreement among the constituents:
for \(K=2\), \(\mathrm{Hol}_I=\{I_2,-I_2\}\), and the one-law ensemble
\(\mathcal N(u,I_2)\) with \(u\neq0\), the unconstrained optimizer
\(\mathcal N(u,I_2)\) is not invariant, while the constrained optimizer is
\(\mathcal N(0,I_2+uu^\top)\) with score \(\frac12\log(1+\|u\|^2)>0\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The stabilizer of a Gaussian under the continuous pushforward action of
\(\GL(K)\) is closed, so invariance under \(\mathrm{Hol}_I\) and under its
closure agree. For every invariant candidate \(q\), invariance of relative
entropy under a common invertible pushforward gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_i w_i\KL(q_i\Vert q)
=\int_{\overline{\mathrm{Hol}}_I}\sum_i w_i
\KL(g_\#q_i\Vert q)dg,
\label{eq:cg-haar-objective-average}
\end{equation}
so the objective is that of the orbit-averaged ensemble, the Haar-symmetrized
mixture \eqref{eq:cg-haar-symmetrized-mixture}, whose first and second moments
are \(\mu_{\mathrm{Hol}}\) and \(\Xi_{\mathrm{Hol}}\). Haar invariance makes both
moments invariant, so the moment-matched Gaussian of that ensemble is an
admissible candidate, and \Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}, applied
to the averaged ensemble, makes it the unique minimizer among all nondegenerate
Gaussians and hence among invariant ones; this is the Gaussian clause of
\Cref{prop:cg-holonomy-compact-closed-form} with the moments made explicit. The
covariance \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{Hol}}\) is the covariance of the averaged mixture,
which dominates the Haar average of the positive-definite matrices
\(g\Sigma_ig^\top\), so it is positive definite. Every element of a compact
matrix group has \(|\det g|=1\), so
\(\log\det(g\Sigma_ig^\top)=\log\det\Sigma_i\), which gives
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}. In the witness the Haar average over
\(\{\pm I_2\}\) sends \(\bar\mu=u\) to \(0\) and leaves \(\Xi=I_2+uu^\top\)
unchanged, and \(\det(I_2+uu^\top)=1+\|u\|^2\). \(\square\)

The trivial-holonomy case of the corollary is
\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}. Compactness cannot be dropped: a
holonomy element with an eigenvalue off the unit circle, such as \(2I_K\), fixes
no nondegenerate Gaussian (\Cref{cor:cg-holonomy-noncompact-gaussian}).

The Markov property of a configuration arrow (\Cref{def:hist-coarse-configuration-map}) can fail
even for natural meta-agent maps, as the barycenter of two normal laws shows.

\propositionheading{Normal barycenters and parameter-independent kernels}{prop:hist-normal-barycenter-kernel}
Let $\Sigma_0\succ0$ be an $n\times n$ covariance, $\mathsf H\in\R^{n'\times n}$,
$\mathsf b\in\R^{n'}$, and $\Sigma_1\succeq0$ an $n'\times n'$ covariance, and let $\kappa$ be
a Markov kernel from $\R^n$ to $\R^{n'}$ with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma_0)\kappa=\mathcal N(\mathsf H\mu+\mathsf b,\Sigma_1)
\quad\text{for every }\mu\in\R^n.
\label{eq:hist-gaussian-location-image}
\end{equation}
Then $\Sigma_1\succeq\mathsf H\Sigma_0\mathsf H^\top$ and
$\kappa(y,\cdot)=\mathcal N(\mathsf Hy+\mathsf b,\Sigma_1-\mathsf H\Sigma_0\mathsf H^\top)$ for
Lebesgue-almost every $y$.  Consequently no Markov kernel from $\R^2$ to $\R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\mathcal N(\mu_1,\varsigma^2)\otimes\mathcal N(\mu_2,\varsigma^2)\bigr)\kappa
=\mathcal N\left(\tfrac{\mu_1+\mu_2}{2},\varsigma^2\right)
\quad\text{for all }\mu_1,\mu_2\in\R\text{ and all }\varsigma>0,
\label{eq:hist-common-variance-barycenter}
\end{equation}
and none sends $\mathcal N(\mu_1,\varsigma_1^2)\otimes\mathcal N(\mu_2,\varsigma_2^2)$ to
the joint barycenter $\mathcal N\bigl(\tfrac{\mu_1+\mu_2}{2},\tfrac{\varsigma_1^2+
\varsigma_2^2}{2}\bigr)$ for all parameters.  For each fixed $\varsigma$, the kernel
$y\mapsto\mathcal N\bigl(\tfrac{y_1+y_2}{2},\tfrac{\varsigma^2}{2}\bigr)$ realizes
\eqref{eq:hist-common-variance-barycenter} at that $\varsigma$, and the deterministic
kernel $y\mapsto(y_1+y_2)/\sqrt2$ sends $\mathcal N(0,\varsigma_1^2)\otimes\mathcal
N(0,\varsigma_2^2)$ to $\mathcal N\bigl(0,\tfrac{\varsigma_1^2+\varsigma_2^2}{2}\bigr)$ for
all $\varsigma_1,\varsigma_2>0$.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For $\omega\in\R^{n'}$ put $k_\omega(y)=\int e^{\mathrm i\omega^\top y'}\kappa(y,dy')$, a
measurable function with $|k_\omega|\leq1$, and the bounded function
$h_\omega(y)=\exp\bigl(\mathrm i\omega^\top(\mathsf Hy+\mathsf
b)-\tfrac12\omega^\top(\Sigma_1-\mathsf H\Sigma_0\mathsf H^\top)\omega\bigr)$.  The
characteristic function of $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma_0)$ at $\mathsf H^\top\omega$ gives
$\int h_\omega d\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma_0)=\exp(\mathrm i\omega^\top(\mathsf H\mu+\mathsf
b)-\tfrac12\omega^\top\Sigma_1\omega)$, which by \eqref{eq:hist-gaussian-location-image}
equals $\int k_\omega d\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma_0)$, so the bounded function
$k_\omega-h_\omega$ has zero convolution with the $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_0)$ density.  In
tempered distributions its Fourier transform is annihilated by multiplication with a
nowhere-vanishing Gaussian. The quotient of any compactly supported smooth test function by that
Gaussian is again compactly supported and smooth, so the transform vanishes on all such tests.
It is therefore zero as a distribution, hence as a tempered distribution, and
$k_\omega=h_\omega$ almost everywhere.  Then $|h_\omega|\leq1$ on a nonempty set forces
$\omega^\top(\Sigma_1-\mathsf H\Sigma_0\mathsf H^\top)\omega\geq0$, and for $\omega$ in a
countable dense set the characteristic functions of $\kappa(y,\cdot)$ and of
$\mathcal N(\mathsf Hy+\mathsf b,\Sigma_1-\mathsf H\Sigma_0\mathsf H^\top)$ agree for
almost every $y$, hence everywhere by continuity.  For
\eqref{eq:hist-common-variance-barycenter}, $\Sigma_0=\varsigma^2\mathrm I_2$,
$\mathsf H=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $\mathsf b=0$, and $\Sigma_1=\varsigma^2$ force
$\kappa(y,\cdot)=\mathcal N\bigl(\tfrac{y_1+y_2}{2},\tfrac{\varsigma^2}{2}\bigr)$ almost
everywhere, and two values of $\varsigma$ give incompatible laws on a set of full
measure; the joint barycenter restricted to $\varsigma_1=\varsigma_2$ is
\eqref{eq:hist-common-variance-barycenter}.  For the realizations, the output means are
$\tfrac{\mu_1+\mu_2}{2}$ and $0$, and the output variances are
$\tfrac14(2\varsigma^2)+\tfrac{\varsigma^2}2=\varsigma^2$ and
$\tfrac12(\varsigma_1^2+\varsigma_2^2)$.  $\square$

\section{Quotient volume for hard membership}
\label{sec:cg-quotient-volume}

\propositionheading{Translation-quotient metric of hard membership}{prop:cg-quotient-metric}
Let \(\widehat S\in\{0,1\}^{N\times|\mathcal P|}\) be the membership matrix of a
partition \(\mathcal P\) with nonempty cluster sizes \(n_I\), write
\(\mathbf n=(n_I)_I\), so that \(N=\sum_In_I\), and let \(\operatorname{pdet}\)
denote the product of the positive eigenvalues of a positive-semidefinite
matrix. On the translation quotient, the metric induced from the fine Euclidean
quotient is
\begin{equation}
\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{diag}(n_I)-\frac{\mathbf n\mathbf n^\top}{N},\qquad
\ker\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1),\qquad
\operatorname{pdet}\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}=\frac{|\mathcal P|\prod_In_I}{N}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-metric}
\end{equation}
For \(K\) channels, let \(\nu_{\mathrm{std}}\) be Lebesgue measure on the
representatives in \(\R^{|\mathcal P|K}\) orthogonal to
\(\mathbf1\otimes\R^K\), and let \(\nu_{\mathrm{ind}}\) be the Riemannian volume,
equivalently the Hausdorff measure, of \(\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}\otimes I_K\).
Then
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\mathrm{ind}}=\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathcal P}\nu_{\mathrm{std}},\qquad
\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathcal P}
=\Bigl(\frac{|\mathcal P|\prod_In_I}{N}\Bigr)^{K/2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-jacobian}
\end{equation}
If \(\operatorname{range}(S)\cap\ker\Lambda=\mathbf1_N\otimes\R^K\), the quotient
Gaussian with precision \(\Lambda_{\mathrm c}=S^\top\Lambda S\) is proper, and its
determinant relative to the quotient metric is
\begin{equation}
\det\nolimits_{\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\mathrm c})
:=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\mathrm c}}
{(\operatorname{pdet}\mathsf G_{\mathcal P})^K}
=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\mathrm c}}{\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathcal P}^2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-generalized-determinant}
\end{equation}
For the unnormalized factor \(\exp(-x^\top\Lambda_{\mathrm c}x/2)\) and
\(d_q=(|\mathcal P|-1)K\), the normalizers are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{std}}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
(\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\mathrm c})^{-1/2},\\
Z_{\mathrm{ind}}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
\det\nolimits_{\mathsf G_{\mathcal P}\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\mathrm c})^{-1/2}
=\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathcal P}Z_{\mathrm{std}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-quotient-normalizers}
\end{equation}
The factor cancels from the normalized law, because the measure changes by the
same factor, while raw partition functions differ by
\(\log\operatorname{Jac}_{\mathcal P}\); a partition score therefore needs a
fixed volume convention, an exact correction, or a compensating prior.

\paragraph{Physicist summary.}
Hard aggregation of a Gaussian network is exact Galerkin restriction: internal
springs disappear, cut springs and self terms add, and the coarse law is the
fine law conditioned on zero internal disagreement, equivalently a
precision-dependent linear pushforward. A twisted internal link leaves a
residual self term that only a singular weight can hide, and hard aggregation
of a cluster keeps whatever its connection energy leaves flat. Marginalization
keeps the hidden normalization and closes only in specific regimes. Independent
tests at one fixed anchor force common congruence axes; on a fixed flat graph,
closure for every positive assignment holds exactly when each hidden component
touches at most two terminals, ground included, so that with a prior on every
agent, marginalizing a relay between two retained agents generically leaves the
positive family, while a hidden component hanging on one agent only stiffens
that agent's anchor. Nonflat degree-two paths and cycles close by composing
links and covariances with their loop holonomy retained, and anchored ones close
with new self terms but no canonical link. Branching can produce a proper
Gaussian whose marginal has no positive two-node presentation, although its
action is still of M\"obius order two. Relaxation under a frozen metric is an
exact natural-gradient flow on a fixed-covariance mean family, the
holonomy-constrained Gaussian parent is the Haar-projected moment match, and
quotient volumes change raw normalizers without supplying an intrinsic blocking
scale.
