\chapter{Operator Coarse-Graining}
\label{ch:gaussian-renormalization}

What does a hard aggregation of agents do to the operator parameters of the
interaction family, and when is its output again a normalized Gaussian law? The
chapter answers both questions for one finite step and its finite compositions. On
the parameters \((A_i,W_{ij})\) of \Cref{def:gauss-interaction-family},
aggregation is a positive linear semigroup action that sums self terms and cut
weights and annihilates internal edges (\Cref{prop:grg-aggregation-semigroup}). On
the interior of the interaction cone, with Lebesgue reference measure and a declared
information-vector rule, a step rescaled by \(\zeta_\ell>0\) is a normalized
Gaussian law step, which one parameter-independent kernel realizes on a
fixed-precision mean fiber exactly when \(\zeta_\ell\leq1\)
(\Cref{prop:grg-interior-law-step}). The only positive rules that depend
additively on scalar node masses are the biadditive ones, which are closed under
every hard partition (\Cref{prop:grg-positive-additive-linear}). The operator step
is the Gaussian instance of the exact coarse channels of
\Cref{ch:coarsegraining,ch:agent-network-rg}; it is not their definition, because a
law-level step also needs a coarse reference measure, an information-vector rule,
and a finite normalizer, which the interior supplies and the singular boundary does
not. Hard aggregation is one admissible coarse arrow among several: the
effective-support, sheaf, and Kron reductions of \Cref{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining}
give others on narrower domains. The pencil invariants that type the boundary are
in \Cref{sec:grg-spectra}.

The chapter treats the belief and recognition operator sector at \(c_*\); a
model-channel step is a separate construction with its own dimension, links, and
parameters. It does not assert a statement about infinite hierarchies, limiting
rays, or the behavior of iterated steps as the depth grows, or that a law-level step
exists at the boundary member of the biadditive family without a declared repair.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED} The monotonicity question beyond one fixed precision is
\Cref{open:grg-galerkin-monotonicity}, and a gauge-compatible nondegenerate partition
selector is \Cref{open:cg-natural-selector}. Balanced cut links admit no continuous extension
to all singular cuts (\Cref{open:cg-cut-compression}).

\section{The operator arrow}
\label{sec:grg-operator-arrow}

\definitionheading{Operator arrow}{def:grg-operator-arrow}
At depth \(\ell\), let \(\boldsymbol\Xi_\ell=((A_i^{(\ell)}),(W_{ij}^{(\ell)}))\) with
\(A_i^{(\ell)},W_{ij}^{(\ell)}\in\PSD^K\), and assemble the depth-\(\ell\) interaction
precision of \Cref{def:gauss-interaction-family},
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_\ell)_{ii}
=A_i^{(\ell)}+\sum_{j\ne i}W_{ij}^{(\ell)},
\qquad
(\Lambda_\ell)_{ij}=-W_{ij}^{(\ell)};
\label{eq:grg-family}
\end{equation}
a precision assembled from the directed model enters through
\Cref{hyp:gauss-global-interaction}. For a hard partition of the agents into clusters
\(I\), let \(\widehat S_\ell\) be its \(0/1\) membership matrix and
\(S_\ell=\widehat S_\ell\otimes I_K\). The unrescaled aggregation map acts on precisions
and on parameters by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\Lambda_\ell)
=S_\ell^\top\Lambda_\ell S_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\boldsymbol\Xi_\ell)
=\left(
\left(\sum_{i\in I}A_i^{(\ell)}\right)_I,
\left(\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\in J}}W_{ij}^{(\ell)}\right)_{I\ne J}
\right),
\label{eq:grg-aggregation}
\end{equation}
and a coarse-graining step declares a positive rescaling \(\zeta_\ell>0\) and sets
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\ell+1}
=\zeta_\ell^{-1}\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\Lambda_\ell).
\label{eq:grg-flow}
\end{equation}

\propositionheading{Aggregation is a positive semigroup action}{prop:grg-aggregation-semigroup}
(i) The two maps of \eqref{eq:grg-aggregation} agree: assembling
\(\mathcal A_S(\boldsymbol\Xi)\) by \eqref{eq:grg-family} gives \(S^\top\Lambda S\).
(ii) If \(S_1\) maps agents to intermediate clusters and \(S_2\) maps those clusters to
coarse clusters, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{S_2}\mathcal A_{S_1}
=\mathcal A_{S_1S_2}.
\label{eq:grg-semigroup}
\end{equation}
(iii) Each \(\mathcal A_S\) is linear and positive on the product cone of self terms and
edge weights, and it is not injective when some cluster contains an edge, because
every edge internal to a cluster is annihilated.
(iv) If every cluster has \(b\) agents, then \(S^\top S=bI\) and, for every symmetric
\(\Lambda\),
\begin{equation}
b\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda)
\leq\lambda_{\min}(S^\top\Lambda S),
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(S^\top\Lambda S)
\leq b\lambda_{\max}(\Lambda).
\label{eq:grg-spectral-bounds}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
(i) The \((I,J)\) block of \(S^\top\Lambda S\) is \(\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}\Lambda_{ij}\). For
\(I\neq J\) this is \(-\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}W_{ij}\). For \(I=J\) it is
\(\sum_{i\in I}\bigl(A_i+\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}\bigr)-\sum_{i\neq i'\in I}W_{ii'}
=\sum_{i\in I}A_i+\sum_{i\in I,j\notin I}W_{ij}\), which is the diagonal block that
\eqref{eq:grg-family} assembles from \(\mathcal A_S(\boldsymbol\Xi)\). (ii) Congruence
gives \(S_2^\top(S_1^\top\Lambda S_1)S_2=(S_1S_2)^\top\Lambda(S_1S_2)\), and the parameter
formula follows by associativity of finite sums. (iii) Finite sums preserve positive
semidefiniteness, and changing an internal weight while holding every cut weight and
self term fixed leaves \(\mathcal A_S(\boldsymbol\Xi)\) unchanged. (iv) Congruence by \(S\)
preserves the Loewner inequalities
\(\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda)I\preceq\Lambda\preceq\lambda_{\max}(\Lambda)I\), and
\(S^\top S=bI\). \hfill$\square$

\medskip
Source and target of \eqref{eq:grg-flow} have different dimensions. A finite sequence
of steps is therefore a composition of positive maps between different spaces, one
arrow of the scale diagram of \Cref{sec:rg-scale-category} per step, and not an
autonomous dynamical system on one parameter space. The bounds
\eqref{eq:grg-spectral-bounds} are finite-level statements; divergence or convergence
along a sequence of steps would require an infinite hierarchy and a declared
normalization, which this supplement does not declare. The parameter formula of
\eqref{eq:grg-aggregation} is written in the chart in which
\Cref{def:gauss-interaction-family} is imposed; nonflat data must first satisfy the
effective-support conditions of \Cref{sec:cg-effective-support} or be treated by the
rectangular endpoint maps of \Cref{sec:cg-sheaf-closure}, and the full treatment of
hard aggregation is \Cref{sec:cg-gaussian-aggregation}.

\propositionheading{Interior law-level step}{prop:grg-interior-law-step}
Let \(\Lambda_\ell\succ0\), let \(S_\ell\) be injective, let \(\zeta_\ell>0\), and write
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm c}=S_\ell^\top\Lambda_\ell S_\ell\). Declare Lebesgue reference measure
on each coarse coordinate space and the information-vector rule
\(\mathsf h_{\ell+1}=S_\ell^\top\mathsf h_\ell\). Then
\((\mathsf h_{\ell+1},\Lambda_{\ell+1})=(S_\ell^\top\mathsf h_\ell,\zeta_\ell^{-1}\Lambda_{\mathrm c})\)
is the natural parameter of a normalized Gaussian with finite log normalizer
\(\mathsf A(\mathsf h_{\ell+1},\Lambda_{\ell+1})\), so every iterate of \eqref{eq:grg-flow}
started in the interior stays in the Gaussian natural domain. At \(\zeta_\ell=1\) the step
is the pushforward of the fine law under \(P_{\Lambda_\ell}\) of
\Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}. For general \(\zeta_\ell\), the step sends
\(\mathcal N(\mu,\Lambda_\ell^{-1})\) to
\(\mathcal N(\zeta_\ell P_{\Lambda_\ell}\mu,\zeta_\ell\Lambda_{\mathrm c}^{-1})\), and on the
fixed-precision mean fiber of \(\Lambda_\ell\) it is realized by a parameter-independent
Markov kernel if and only if \(\zeta_\ell\leq1\), for instance by
\(y\mapsto\mathcal N(\zeta_\ell P_{\Lambda_\ell}y,\zeta_\ell(1-\zeta_\ell)\Lambda_{\mathrm c}^{-1})\).
The alternative rule \(\mathsf h_{\ell+1}=\zeta_\ell^{-1/2}S_\ell^\top\mathsf h_\ell\), with the
same coarse precision, is the pushforward under
\(y\mapsto\zeta_\ell^{1/2}P_{\Lambda_\ell}y\) for every \(\zeta_\ell>0\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Injectivity of \(S_\ell\) and \(\Lambda_\ell\succ0\) give \(\Lambda_{\mathrm c}\succ0\), hence
\(\zeta_\ell^{-1}\Lambda_{\mathrm c}\succ0\), and \Cref{prop:gauss-log-normalizer-moments}
gives a finite normalizer; induction gives the statement about iterates. The case
\(\zeta_\ell=1\) is \Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}(i). In general the coarse mean is
\(\zeta_\ell\Lambda_{\mathrm c}^{-1}S_\ell^\top\mathsf h_\ell=\zeta_\ell P_{\Lambda_\ell}\mu\) with
\(\mathsf h_\ell=\Lambda_\ell\mu\). For \(\zeta_\ell\leq1\) the displayed kernel produces the
covariance
\(\zeta_\ell^2P_{\Lambda_\ell}\Lambda_\ell^{-1}P_{\Lambda_\ell}^\top
+\zeta_\ell(1-\zeta_\ell)\Lambda_{\mathrm c}^{-1}=\zeta_\ell\Lambda_{\mathrm c}^{-1}\).
Conversely, if a parameter-independent kernel realizes the step, the data-processing
inequality \Cref{thm:cg-kl-dpi-extended} applied to the fine laws with means \(\mu\) and
\(\mu+S_\ell v\) gives
\(\tfrac12v^\top\Lambda_{\mathrm c}v\geq\tfrac12\zeta_\ell v^\top\Lambda_{\mathrm c}v\), the right
side being the relative entropy of the two coarse laws, so \(\zeta_\ell\leq1\). The last rule
is the image of \Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}(i) under multiplication of the
coarse coordinates by \(\zeta_\ell^{1/2}\). \hfill$\square$

\openproblemheading{Monotonicity beyond one fixed precision}{open:grg-galerkin-monotonicity}
On one fixed-precision mean fiber the unrescaled step is the pushforward of one
parameter-independent kernel (\Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}), and after a
rescaling with \(\zeta_\ell\leq1\) it is still realized by one kernel
(\Cref{prop:grg-interior-law-step}), so the Fisher metric of the mean family contracts
there by \Cref{thm:cg-fisher-contraction}. Three cases are unsettled. First, along a
family of precisions on which \(\operatorname{range}(\Lambda S)\) varies, no single kernel
exists (\Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}(v)); a monotone functional would have to be
supplied and its decrease proved directly, for instance as a Lyapunov functional of the
step. Second, at the boundary member \(A=0\) of the biadditive family
\eqref{eq:grg-biadditive}, \(\Lambda\) is singular and \(P_\Lambda\) need not be defined; a
declared quotient, pin, or mass repair with its own reference measure, normalizer, and
pushforward identity would settle it. Third, under iteration to unbounded depth,
\Cref{prop:ig-galerkin-pushforward}(iii) controls every finite composition, but no limit
object of the composed kernels has been constructed; a projective-limit theorem for the
composed kernels would settle it.

\section{The biadditive closed family}
\label{sec:grg-biadditive}

Let each agent carry a mass \(x_i\geq0\), and fix \(A,M\in\PSD^K\). The biadditive family
is
\begin{equation}
A_i=x_iA,
\qquad
W_{ij}=x_ix_jM,
\label{eq:grg-biadditive}
\end{equation}
and its image under a hard partition, with cluster masses \(x_I=\sum_{i\in I}x_i\), is
\begin{equation}
A_I=x_IA,
\qquad
W_{IJ}=x_Ix_JM.
\label{eq:grg-biadditive-coarse}
\end{equation}

\propositionheading{Positive additive rules are forced to be linear}{prop:grg-positive-additive-linear}
(i) The family \eqref{eq:grg-biadditive} is closed under every hard partition, with
image \eqref{eq:grg-biadditive-coarse}.
(ii) Suppose \(\alpha:\R_+\to\PSD^K\) is additive and \(w:\R_+^2\to\PSD^K\) is symmetric
and separately additive. Then there are \(A,M\in\PSD^K\) with
\begin{equation}
\alpha(x)=xA,
\qquad
w(x,y)=xyM.
\label{eq:grg-additive-classification}
\end{equation}
Hence every positive rule that depends only on additive scalar masses and obeys these
additivity hypotheses has the form \eqref{eq:grg-biadditive}, which is the unique such
family. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
(i) By \Cref{prop:grg-aggregation-semigroup}(i),
\(A_I=\sum_{i\in I}x_iA=x_IA\) and \(W_{IJ}=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}x_ix_jM=x_Ix_JM\).
(ii) For \(v\in\R^K\), the scalar function \(f(x)=v^\top\alpha(x)v\) is additive and
nonnegative, so \(f(0)=0\), \(f(qx)=qf(x)\) for rational \(q\geq0\), and \(f\) is monotone,
since \(f(y)-f(x)=f(y-x)\geq0\) for \(y\geq x\). For \(x\geq0\) and rationals
\(q\leq x\leq q'\), \(qf(1)\leq f(x)\leq q'f(1)\), so \(f(x)=xf(1)\). Polarization of the
symmetric matrices \(\alpha(x)\) gives \(\alpha(x)=x\alpha(1)\). For fixed \(y\), the same
argument gives \(w(x,y)=xw(1,y)\), and applied to the second argument it gives
\(w(1,y)=yw(1,1)\). Take \(A=\alpha(1)\) and \(M=w(1,1)\). \hfill$\square$

\medskip
Closure is not selection: nothing in the proposition makes the family attract other
data. Its boundary member \(A=0\) is a boundary operator of the interaction
representation. There \(\Lambda=L\), every configuration constant across agents lies
in the kernel (\Cref{prop:ig-generalized-spectrum-localization}), the pencil
\((L,\Lambda)\) is irregular (\Cref{prop:grg-pencil-regularity}), and no full-space
normalized Gaussian density exists. Pinning, quotienting the kernel, and retaining an
external positive mass are different declared repairs and define different probability
problems; a pseudoinverse inserted into the original Lebesgue density is none of them.
Under \(\Gamma_b\supseteq\GL^+(K)\) the matrix factor \(M\) carries no continuous label
beyond its rank (\Cref{prop:grg-residual-fiber-label}).

\paragraph{Physicist summary.}
The interaction precision has a clean positive coarse map: cut weights and self terms
add, internal edges drop out, and a declared scalar restores the chosen units. Nested
aggregations compose, so the unrescaled map is a semigroup action, but its source and
target differ in dimension and no autonomous system is defined without further
identifications. On the interior of the interaction cone the unrescaled step is the
pushforward of the fine Gaussian law through the precision-dependent Galerkin map, and
a rescaled step is still a normalized Gaussian law, realized by one kernel on a
fixed-precision fiber exactly when the rescaling does not increase the Fisher
information of the mean family. The
only positive rules additive in scalar node masses are biadditive,
\(W_{ij}=x_ix_jM\) and \(A_i=x_iA\); their matrix factor carries only its rank as a
congruence label under orientation-preserving frames, and their member without self
terms is a boundary operator that carries no normalized law until a repair is declared.
