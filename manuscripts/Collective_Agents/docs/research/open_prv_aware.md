## Target

- Locked statement: From `sections/ext4_prv.tex` (`open:prv-aware`, lines 117–119): "Between the $d$ numbers of \cref{prop:prv-aware} and the $k\rank H_i$ numbers of \cref{thm:prv-window}, determine the least message dimension for exact fusion when encoders know the model but reports are asynchronous or the schedule is random."
- Falsifier: (i) Any exact continuous star protocol (`def:prv-protocol`) for $Q_x \succ 0$ and invertible $A$ whose window message dimension $m$ is strictly less than $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$ (e.g., $m = 1$ in the $d=1, k=2, n=2$ asynchronous example of C3, or $m < \min(s,k)d$ in C4, or $m < d + \sum q_\tau$ in C5, or $m < (p+1)d$ in C6); (ii) any schedule realization where the minimal basis encoder $m_i = B_i \tilde{\ell}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)}$ deviates from the exact Kalman posterior $p(x_t \mid o_{D_z(t)})$.
- May assume: Live linear-Gaussian star model of `sections/ext4_prv.tex` (lines 7–11) and `sections/appext4_prv.tex` (`lem:prv-trajectory`, `thm:prv-window`, `prop:prv-aware`): state $x_{t+1} = A x_t + w_t^x$ with $w_t^x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_x)$ ($Q_x \succ 0$ except in boundary claim C7), $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(m_0, P_0)$ ($P_0 \succ 0$), $n$ members observing $o_{j,t} = H_j x_t + v_{j,t}$ ($v_{j,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_j)$, $R_j \succ 0$), all noises mutually independent; hub $z$ knows the model $(A, Q_x, P_0, m_0, (H_j, R_j)_{j=1}^n)$ and the discrete observation-key set $D_z(t)$ of arrived reports; encoders are model-aware (`def:prv-protocol`) and continuous in the observation data $\varphi_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathbb{R}^m$ for each fixed discrete schedule equivalence class.
- Must not assume: Common synchronous reporting schedules (`prop:prv-aware`), blind encoders (`thm:prv-window`), vanishing process noise $Q_x = 0$ (`thm:prv-tree`(a)), discontinuous space-filling encodings $\mathbb{R}^N \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, or generic-parameter assumptions when unconditional algebraic proofs exist.
- Alternative forms: Complete exact characterization $m_i^* = r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$ for arbitrary deterministic or random schedule families (C1), universal closed-form upper bounds in terms of future retroactive stages $|\mathcal{U}_i|$, late-arriving sensing subspaces $\tilde{q}_\tau$, and delivery delay $\Delta_{\max}$ (C2), together with matching exact closed-form evaluations across all canonical off-schedule regimes: deterministic staggered schedules (C4), steady-state round-robin (C6), decentralized random reporting and random delivery delays vs causal schedule broadcast (C4, C5), explicit counterexamples refuting $d$-dimensional encodings off schedule (C3), and boundary collapse at $Q_x = 0$ and $A = 0$ (C7).
- Test instance: Minimal asynchronous counterexample ($d=1, A=1, Q_x=1, P_0=1, H_1=H_2=1, R_1=R_2=1$, $n=2$): member $1$ reports $W_1 = \{1,2\}$ at $t=2$, member $2$ reports $W_2 = \{1,2,3\}$ at $t=3$. Registered values: at $t=2$, $\mathbb{E}[x_2 \mid o_{1,1}, o_{1,2}] = \tfrac{1}{4} o_{1,1} + \tfrac{5}{8} o_{1,2}$ (ratio $2:5$, variance $\tfrac{5}{8}$); at $t=3$, $(o_{1,1}, o_{1,2})$ weights in $\mathbb{E}[x_3 \mid o_{D_z(3)}]$ are $(\tfrac{2}{45}, \tfrac{7}{45})$ (ratio $2:7$, variance $\tfrac{26}{45}$); $2\times 2$ determinant $= \tfrac{1}{90} \ne 0$, forcing $m_1^* = 2 > d = 1$.
- Out of scope: General graphs with cycles (`open:prv-minimal`), hierarchies with distinct node variables (`open:prv-hierarchy`), unknown or uncertain sensing models $(H_j, R_j)$, and non-Gaussian / nonlinear state-space models.
- Reading chosen: We measure message dimension as the Euclidean dimension $m$ of continuous encoders $\varphi_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathbb{R}^m$ per reported window $W_i = \{a+1, \dots, b\}$ of length $k = b - a$ (separating continuous message reals from discrete arrival/window key metadata, matching `thm:prv-window`, `prop:prv-aware`, and `rem:prv-cover-scope`). We treat both deterministic known asynchronous schedules and random schedules (distinguishing decentralized encoders that do not observe other members' past report times from encoders with causal hub schedule broadcast, and distinguishing in-order batch reporting from per-observation random delivery delays). All lower bounds apply to arbitrary continuous nonlinear protocols even with arbitrary hub-to-member feedback; for general schedules $\mathcal{E}_i$ consists of hub evaluations prior to any subsequent re-transmission of $W_i$ by member $i$, and in our concrete theorems (C3, C4, C5, C6) every required configuration in $\mathcal{E}_i$ occurs either at $t = b$ itself or strictly before member $i$'s next report, so no single-transmission restriction is needed there.

## Claims

### C1: Master Characterization and Markov Factorization Theorem

- Statement: Let $Q_x \succ 0$ and $P_0 \succ 0$ in the linear-Gaussian star model (`ext4_prv.tex`, line 7). Consider member $i$'s window $W_i = \{a+1, \dots, b\}$ of length $k = b - a \ge 1$ reported at time $t_{\text{rep}} \ge b$, let $\mathcal{I}$ be an encoder information equivalence class of schedules indistinguishable to member $i$ at $t_{\text{rep}}$, let $\mathcal{E}_i$ be the set of reachable hub evaluation pairs $(D, t)$ ($W_i \subseteq D$, $t \ge t_{\text{rep}}$, prior to any subsequent re-transmission of $W_i$ by member $i$) across schedules in $\mathcal{I}$, and let $\mathcal{U}_i = \{D_{<b} : (D,t) \in \mathcal{E}_i\}$ where $D_{<b} = \{(j,\tau) \in D : \tau < b\}$. (a) *(Markov Factorization)* For every $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$, $M_{D,t} = \Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D)\,\Phi(D_{<b})$, where $\Phi(D_{<b})$ depends only on $D_{<b}$ and is invariant to all observations at times $\ge b$; when $A$ is invertible, $\Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D)$ is invertible for every $t \ge b$, so $\operatorname{row}(M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) = \operatorname{row}(\Phi(D_{<b})|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k})$. (b) *(Exact Least Message Dimension)* Let $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) := \dim\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{row}(M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) : (D,t) \in \mathcal{E}_i\}$ (equal to $\dim\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{row}(\Phi(U)|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}$ when $A$ is invertible or when $t=b$ occurs for every $U \in \mathcal{U}_i$). Every exact star protocol (`def:prv-protocol`, even nonlinear and with arbitrary hub-to-member feedback) whose window encoder $\varphi_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is continuous in the data satisfies $m \ge r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$ (and any linear encoder $\Phi_i O_i$ satisfies $\rank \Phi_i \ge r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$). Conversely, the linear model-aware encoder sending $m_i = B_i \tilde{\ell}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)}$ (rows of $B_i$ spanning $\operatorname{row}([M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}])$) achieves exact fusion at every $(D,t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$.

### C2: Universal Four-Way Upper Bound via Resolvent Perturbation

- Statement: In the setting of C1 with arbitrary $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, $Q_x \succ 0$, $P_0 \succ 0$, and $k = b - a \ge 2$, fix any reference $U_0 \in \mathcal{U}_i$. Let $s_{\Phi} := |\{\Phi(U) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}| \le |\mathcal{U}_i|$ be the number of distinct filtered window operators realized over $\mathcal{U}_i$. For each $\tau \in \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$, let $\mathcal{V}_\tau = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_\tau^{\text{var}}} \operatorname{im}(H_j^\top)$ be the sum of sensing subspaces of members whose observation multiplicity at time $\tau$ is not constant over $U \in \mathcal{U}_i$; define $\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_\tau = \mathcal{V}_\tau$ for $\tau > a+1$, and $\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{a+1} = \mathcal{V}_{a+1} + \operatorname{im}(Q_x^{-1} A)$ if $P_{a \mid U}$ varies over $U \in \mathcal{U}_i$ (else $\mathcal{V}_{a+1}$), with $\tilde{q}_\tau = \dim \widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_\tau \le d$. Then
  $$r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le \min\Bigl(\, s_{\Phi}\,d,\;\; d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} \tilde{q}_\tau,\;\; r_i + d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-2} \tilde{q}_\tau,\;\; k\,r_i \,\Bigr)$$
  (with $s_{\Phi} \le |\mathcal{U}_i|$; for $k=1$, $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le r_i \le d$). Consequently: (i) if $|\mathcal{U}_i| = 1$ (synchronized or last-to-report on $< b$), $r^* \le \min(d, k r_i)$ (recovering `prop:prv-aware`); (ii) under bounded delivery delay $\Delta_{\max} < k-1$ where all observations at $\tau \le b-1-\Delta_{\max}$ have arrived by $t_{\text{rep}}$, $r^* \le \min(d + \sum_{\tau=b-\Delta_{\max}}^{b-1} q_\tau,\, (\Delta_{\max}+1)d,\, k r_i)$; (iii) under causal hub schedule broadcast with $m \le n-1$ pending in-order members, $|\mathcal{U}_i| \le 2^m$ (and $s_{\Phi} = |\{\Phi(U) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}| \le m+1$ if the pending members have identical sensing and equal last-report times, giving $r^* \le \min((m+1)d, k r_i)$), so for $m=1$ ($n=2$), $r^* \le \min(2d, k r_i)$ independent of $k$; (iv) if late observations occur only at times $\le a$, $r^* \le \min(d + \rank A,\, k r_i) \le 2d$ independent of $k$.

### C5: Exact Dimension Under Decentralized Random Schedules (Subspace-Coupled Theorem)

- Statement: Let $Q_x \succ 0$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ invertible, and $P_0 \succ 0$, and consider member $i$'s window $W_i = \{a+1, \dots, b\}$ ($k = b - a \ge 1$) reported at $t_{\text{rep}} = b$. Suppose for each $\tau \in \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$ a set $\mathcal{J}_\tau \subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}$ of other members has random reporting or delivery schedules (indistinguishable to member $i$ at encoding time $b$) such that $\mathcal{U}_i$ contains a chain $U^{(0)} \subsetneq U^{(1)} \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq U^{(L)}$ sharing one pre-window set $U^{(0)}_{\le a}$ and adding the individual observations $\{(j, \tau) : \tau \in \{a+1,\dots,b-1\},\, j \in \mathcal{J}_\tau\}$ one by one (e.g., independent Bernoulli($p_j$) reporting with $p_j \in (0,1)$, or per-observation random delays spanning $[0, k-1]$). Let $\mathcal{V}_\tau = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_\tau} \operatorname{im}(H_j^\top)$ and $q_\tau = \dim \mathcal{V}_\tau$. Then: (a) if $\rank H_i = d$, $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \ge d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} q_\tau$, with exact equality whenever no other observations on $\{1,\dots,b-1\}$ vary over $\mathcal{U}_i$ (in particular, for $n=2$ with Bernoulli member $2$ and no pre-window variation, $m_1^* = d + (k-1)\rank H_2$); (b) if $\mathcal{V}_\tau = \mathbb{R}^d$ for every $\tau \in \{a+1,\dots,b-1\}$, then for arbitrary $\rank H_i = r_i \le d$, $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) = k\,r_i = k\rank H_i$ unconditionally (model-awareness yields zero continuous dimension savings over the blind bound of `thm:prv-window`); (c) when $r_i < d$ and $\mathcal{V}_\tau \subsetneq \mathbb{R}^d$, strict inequality $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) < \min(d + \sum q_\tau, k r_i)$ can occur (e.g., decoupled $d=2, A=Q_x=P_0=I_2, H_1=(1,0), H_2=(0,1)$ has $r^* = 1 < \min(2+(k-1), k) = k$).

### C6: Exact Steady-State Round-Robin Theorem

- Statement: Let $Q_x \succ 0$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ invertible, and $P_0 \succ 0$, and let $n \ge 2$ members with arbitrary heterogeneous full-rank sensing matrices $\Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} \succ 0$ ($j = 1, \dots, n$) report periodically with common period $k \ge 2$ at distinct phases $0 < \phi_1 < \phi_2 < \dots < \phi_{n-1} < k$ and $\phi_n = k$ (steady-state round-robin: for window $W_n = \{a+1, \dots, a+k\}$ with $a \equiv 0 \pmod k$, at report time $b = a+k$ member $j$ has already delivered $\{1,\dots,a+\phi_j\}$ and delivers $\{a+\phi_j+1,\dots,a+k-1\}$ at time $b+\phi_j$). Let $p := |\{j \in \{1,\dots,n-1\} : \phi_j \le k - 2\}|$. Then the exact least continuous message dimension for member $n$'s report at $t = b$ is $m_n^* = (p + 1)\,d$. In particular, for $n = k$ members reporting one per step ($\phi_j = j$ for $j = 1, \dots, k$), every member's exact least continuous message dimension is $(k - 1)\,d$ (strictly below the blind bound $kd$).

### C7: Degenerate Boundary Regimes ($Q_x = 0$ and $A = 0$)

### Proof of C1 (Master Characterization and Markov Factorization)

**Part (a) — Markov Factorization.** Fix $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$ with $W_i = \{a+1, \dots, b\} \subseteq D$ and $t \ge t_{\text{rep}} \ge b$. Let $T = \max(t, \max_{(j,\tau) \in D} \tau)$ and write $X = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$ partitioned into $X_{<b} = (x_1, \dots, x_{b-1})$, $x_b$, and $X_{>b} = (x_{b+1}, \dots, x_T)$ (empty when $b=1$ or $T=b$, respectively). Because $Q_x \succ 0$ and $P_0 \succ 0$, `lem:prv-trajectory`(a)–(b) gives $J_D = J_0^{(T)} + \sum_{(j,\tau)\in D} E_\tau \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} E_\tau^\top \succ 0$, and the posterior mean of $X$ given $o_D$ is $J_D^{-1}(j_0 + \sum_{(j,\tau)\in D} E_\tau h_{j,\tau}^{\mathrm o})$. Thus the sensitivity of $\mathbb{E}[x_t \mid o_D]$ to member $i$'s window ledger $\ell_i = (\ell_{i,<b}, h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o})$ is $M_{D,t} \ell_i = E_t^\top y$, where $y = (y_{<b}, y_b, y_{>b})$ solves
$$J_D \begin{pmatrix} y_{<b} \\ y_b \\ y_{>b} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} u_{<b} \\ u_b \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad u_{<b} = \sum_{\tau = a+1}^{b-1} E_\tau^{(<b)} h_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm o}, \quad u_b = h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o}.$$

If $k = 1$ (so $a = b-1$ and $u_{<b} = 0$), then $y = J_D^{-1} E_b h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o}$, hence $E_t^\top y = (E_t^\top J_D^{-1} E_b) h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o} = \Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D) \Phi(D_{<b}) \ell_i$ with $\Phi(D_{<b})\ell_i = h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o}$.

Now assume $k \ge 2$ (so $b \ge 2$). Because the state trajectory is Markov ($p(X) = p(x_1) \prod_{\tau=1}^{T-1} p(x_{\tau+1} \mid x_\tau)$) and observations are block-diagonal in time, the $(<b, >b)$ block of $J_D$ is zero, the $(<b, <b)$ block is $K(D_{<b}) := J_{D_{<b}}^{(b-1)} + E_{b-1} (A^\top Q_x^{-1} A) E_{b-1}^\top \succ 0$ (which depends only on $D_{<b}$, with the $A^\top Q_x^{-1} A$ term coming from $p(x_b \mid x_{b-1})$), and the $(b, <b)$ block is $J_{b, <b} = [0, \dots, 0, -Q_x^{-1} A]$. Write the block partition of $J_D$ as
$$J_D = \begin{pmatrix} K(D_{<b}) & J_{<b, b} & 0 \\ J_{b, <b} & J_{b, b} & J_{b, >b} \\ 0 & J_{>b, b} & J_{>b, >b} \end{pmatrix}.$$
Eliminating $y_{<b} = K(D_{<b})^{-1}(u_{<b} - J_{<b, b} y_b)$ from the first block row and substituting into the remaining two block rows yields
$$\begin{pmatrix} S_{b, b} & J_{b, >b} \\ J_{>b, b} & J_{>b, >b} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} y_b \\ y_{>b} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} u_b - J_{b, <b} K(D_{<b})^{-1} u_{<b} \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
where $S_{b,b} = J_{b,b} - J_{b,<b} K(D_{<b})^{-1} J_{<b,b}$. By the block-matrix inverse formula for Schur complements, the inverse of the coefficient matrix $\widetilde{J} := \begin{pmatrix} S_{b,b} & J_{b,>b} \\ J_{>b,b} & J_{>b,>b} \end{pmatrix}$ is identically the $([b..T], [b..T])$ principal submatrix of $J_D^{-1}$, i.e., the posterior covariance $\Cov(X_{\ge b} \mid o_D)$. Hence its $(t, b)$ block is $\Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D)$, giving
$$M_{D,t} \ell_i = y_t = \Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D) \Bigl( h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o} + Q_x^{-1} A E_{b-1}^\top K(D_{<b})^{-1} u_{<b} \Bigr).$$

Finally, we reduce $K(D_{<b})$ to the window block $\{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$. If $a = 0$, $u_{<b} = \ell_{i,<b}$ and $K_{\text{win}}(D_{<b}) = K(D_{<b})$. If $a \ge 1$, partition $\{1, \dots, b-1\} = \{1, \dots, a\} \sqcup \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$; since $u_{<b}$ vanishes on $\{1, \dots, a\}$ and equals $\ell_{i,<b}$ on $\{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$, eliminating the $\{1, \dots, a\}$ coordinates of $K(D_{<b})^{-1} u_{<b}$ gives
$$E_{b-1}^\top K(D_{<b})^{-1} u_{<b} = (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(D_{<b})^{-1} \ell_{i,<b},$$
where $K_{\text{win}}(D_{<b})$ is the Schur complement of the $\{1, \dots, a\}$ block of $K(D_{<b})$. Explicitly, the $\{1, \dots, a\}$ block of $K(D_{<b})$ is $J_{D_{\le a}}^{(a)} + E_a (A^\top Q_x^{-1} A) E_a^\top$, which couples to $\{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$ solely through the $(a, a+1)$ block $-A^\top Q_x^{-1}$. Taking the Schur complement of $\{1, \dots, a-1\}$ inside $J_{D_{\le a}}^{(a)} + E_a A^\top Q_x^{-1} A E_a^\top$ shows that the $(a,a)$ block of its inverse is $(\Lambda_{a \mid D_{<b}} + A^\top Q_x^{-1} A)^{-1}$, where $\Lambda_{a \mid D_{<b}} = P_{a \mid D_{<b}}^{-1} \succ 0$ is the posterior precision of $x_a$ given $D_{\le a}$. By the Woodbury identity (verified directly by multiplying by $Q_x + A P_{a \mid D_{<b}} A^\top$),
$$Q_x^{-1} - Q_x^{-1} A (\Lambda_{a \mid D_{<b}} + A^\top Q_x^{-1} A)^{-1} A^\top Q_x^{-1} = (A P_{a \mid D_{<b}} A^\top + Q_x)^{-1}.$$
Thus $K_{\text{win}}(D_{<b})$ is precisely the precision matrix of $(x_{a+1}, \dots, x_{b-1})$ initialized with $x_a \sim \mathcal{N}(0, P_{a \mid D_{<b}})$, updated with the observations in $D_{<b}$ at times $\{a+1,\dots,b-1\}$, plus $E_{b-1}^{\text{win}} (A^\top Q_x^{-1} A) (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top$. Setting $\Phi(U) \ell_i := h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o} + Q_x^{-1} A (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U)^{-1} \ell_{i,<b}$ gives $M_{D,t} = \Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D)\,\Phi(D_{<b})$. When $A$ is invertible, `lem:prv-trajectory`(b) (applied to the chain up to $T$ with step evidence $D_\tau \succeq 0$) establishes that $\Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D) = \Cov(x_b, x_t \mid o_D)^\top$ is invertible for every $t \ge b$ (for $t = b$, $\Cov(x_b \mid o_D) \succ 0$ is always invertible even for singular $A$). Hence left-multiplication by $\Cov(x_t, x_b \mid o_D)$ preserves the row space: $\operatorname{row}(M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) = \operatorname{row}(\Phi(D_{<b})|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k})$.

*Checkpoint (C1a)*: Signs: $-J_{b,<b} = +Q_x^{-1} A$ (positive in $\Phi$); matches test value $\Phi = (\tfrac25, 1) > 0$. Factors: no factors of $2$ or $\pi$ dropped. Conventions: $J_D$ matches `lem:prv-trajectory`(b). Types: $\Cov(x_t,x_b|o_D) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, $\Phi(U) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times kd}$, $M_{D,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times kd}$.

**Part (b) — Exact Least Message Dimension.**
*Lower bound.* Let $L_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathcal{R}_i^k$ be the linear surjection $O_i \mapsto (H_i^\top R_i^{-1} o_{i,\tau})_{\tau \in W_i}$, and let $\mathcal{M}_i : \mathcal{R}_i^k \to \prod_{(D,t)\in\mathcal{E}_i} \mathbb{R}^d$ be $\ell_i \mapsto (M_{D,t} \ell_i)_{(D,t)\in\mathcal{E}_i}$. Since $\dim \mathcal{R}_i^k = k r_i < \infty$, $\operatorname{im}(\mathcal{M}_i)$ is finite-dimensional of dimension $r^* := r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$. Fix all observations outside $W_i$ to zero ($o_{-W_i} = 0$). For a schedule equivalence class $\mathcal{I}$, member $i$'s encoder at $t_{\text{rep}}$ is a single continuous map $\varphi_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathbb{R}^m$ (holding $o_{-W_i} = 0$ fixed fixes every prior observation of $i$ and every hub-to-$i$ message before $t_{\text{rep}}$). Suppose $O_i, O_i' \in \mathcal{O}_i$ satisfy $\varphi_i(O_i) = \varphi_i(O_i')$. For any $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$, since $o_{-W_i} = 0$ is fixed and member $i$ does not re-transmit $W_i$ prior to $t$, every message sent by every other member (even if influenced by hub-to-member feedback derived from $m_i = \varphi_i(O_i)$) is identical under $O_i$ and $O_i'$. Thus the hub receives identical transcripts under $O_i$ and $O_i'$ at $(D, t)$, so exactness requires $\mathbb{E}[x_t \mid o_D]$ to coincide under $O_i$ and $O_i'$, i.e., $M_{D,t} L_i (O_i - O_i') = 0$ for all $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$. Hence $\varphi_i(O_i) = \varphi_i(O_i') \implies (\mathcal{M}_i \circ L_i)(O_i) = (\mathcal{M}_i \circ L_i)(O_i')$.
- For a linear encoder $\varphi_i(O_i) = \Phi_i O_i$, $\ker \Phi_i \subseteq \ker(\mathcal{M}_i \circ L_i)$, so $\rank \Phi_i \ge \rank(\mathcal{M}_i \circ L_i) = r^*$.
- For a continuous encoder $\varphi_i : \mathcal{O}_i \to \mathbb{R}^m$, choose a linear subspace $V \subseteq \mathcal{O}_i$ of dimension $r^*$ such that $(\mathcal{M}_i \circ L_i)|_V : V \to \operatorname{im}(\mathcal{M}_i)$ is bijective. Then $\varphi_i|_V : V \cong \mathbb{R}^{r^*} \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is a continuous injection. If $m < r^*$, composing with the canonical inclusion $\iota : \mathbb{R}^m \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{r^*}$ produces a continuous injection $\mathbb{R}^{r^*} \to \mathbb{R}^{r^*}$ whose image lies in $\mathbb{R}^m \times \{0\}^{r^*-m}$, which has empty interior in $\mathbb{R}^{r^*}$, contradicting Brouwer's Invariance of Domain. Therefore $m \ge r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)$.

*Matching upper bound.* Express $\ell_i \in \mathcal{R}_i^k$ in coordinates $\tilde{\ell}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{k r_i}$ via a basis $B_i^{\text{sens}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r_i}$ of $\mathcal{R}_i$. Choose a matrix $B_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r^* \times k r_i}$ whose rows form a basis of $\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{row}(M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) : (D,t) \in \mathcal{E}_i\}$ (computable from the model and $\mathcal{E}_i$ alone). Member $i$ sends $m_i = B_i \tilde{\ell}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r^*(\mathcal{E}_i)}$. For every $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$, each row of $M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}$ is a unique linear combination of the rows of $B_i$, yielding a deterministic matrix $C_{D,t,W_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r^*}$ (computable by the hub from the model and $(D, t)$) such that $M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k} = C_{D,t,W_i} B_i$, hence $M_{D,t} \ell_i = C_{D,t,W_i} m_i$. Summing over all received windows $W_\nu$ partitioning $D = D_z(t)$ gives the exact posterior mean $\mathbb{E}[x_t \mid o_D] = E_t^\top J_D^{-1} j_0 + \sum_{W_\nu \subseteq D} C_{D,t,W_\nu} m_{W_\nu}$, while the exact posterior covariance $E_t^\top J_D^{-1} E_t$ depends only on the model and $D$. $\square$

### Proof of C2 (Universal Four-Way Upper Bound via Resolvent Perturbation)

For $k = 1$, $\mathcal{R}_i^1$ has dimension $r_i \le d$, so $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le r_i$. Now assume $k \ge 2$ and fix any $U_0 \in \mathcal{U}_i$. By C1(a), $\operatorname{row}(M_{D,t}|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) \subseteq \operatorname{row}(\Phi(D_{<b})|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k})$ for every $(D, t) \in \mathcal{E}_i$ (with no invertibility assumption on $A$). Hence $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le \dim\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{row}(\Phi(U)|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}$.
1. *First and fourth bounds:* Each distinct operator $\Phi(U)$ ($U \in \mathcal{U}_i$) has $d$ rows, so the stacked matrix over $\{\Phi(U) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}$ has $s_\Phi d \le |\mathcal{U}_i|\,d$ rows, giving $r^* \le s_\Phi d \le |\mathcal{U}_i|\,d$; its domain $\mathcal{R}_i^k$ has dimension $k r_i$, giving $r^* \le k r_i$.
2. *Resolvent decomposition:* For any $U \in \mathcal{U}_i$, compare $K_{\text{win}}(U)$ with $K_{\text{win}}(U_0)$:
   $$K_{\text{win}}(U) - K_{\text{win}}(U_0) = E_{a+1}^{\text{win}} \Delta_{\text{pre}}(U) (E_{a+1}^{\text{win}})^\top + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} E_\tau^{\text{win}} \Delta_\tau(U) (E_\tau^{\text{win}})^\top,$$
   where $\Delta_\tau(U) = \sum_{(j,\tau) \in U} \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} - \sum_{(j,\tau) \in U_0} \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o}$ (whose column and row spaces lie in $\mathcal{V}_\tau = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_\tau^{\text{var}}} \operatorname{im}(H_j^\top)$) and
   $$\Delta_{\text{pre}}(U) = (A P_{a \mid U} A^\top + Q_x)^{-1} - (A P_{a \mid U_0} A^\top + Q_x)^{-1}.$$
   By the Woodbury identity $(A P_a A^\top + Q_x)^{-1} = Q_x^{-1} - Q_x^{-1} A (\Lambda_a + A^\top Q_x^{-1} A)^{-1} A^\top Q_x^{-1}$, the $Q_x^{-1}$ terms cancel:
   $$\Delta_{\text{pre}}(U) = Q_x^{-1} A \Bigl[ (\Lambda_{a \mid U_0} + A^\top Q_x^{-1} A)^{-1} - (\Lambda_{a \mid U} + A^\top Q_x^{-1} A)^{-1} \Bigr] A^\top Q_x^{-1}.$$
   Hence $\operatorname{im}(\Delta_{\text{pre}}(U)) \subseteq \operatorname{im}(Q_x^{-1} A)$ (of dimension $\rank A$) whenever $P_{a \mid U}$ varies with $U$, and $\Delta_{\text{pre}}(U) = 0$ otherwise. Choosing a basis $V_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times \tilde{q}_\tau}$ of $\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_\tau$ for each $\tau \in \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$ and setting $W_{\text{pert}} := \diag(V_{a+1}^\top, \dots, V_{b-1}^\top) \in \mathbb{R}^{q_{\text{tot}} \times (k-1)d}$ ($q_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} \tilde{q}_\tau$), there exists a symmetric matrix $\Sigma(U) \in \mathbb{R}^{q_{\text{tot}} \times q_{\text{tot}}}$ such that $K_{\text{win}}(U) - K_{\text{win}}(U_0) = W_{\text{pert}}^\top \Sigma(U) W_{\text{pert}}$.
3. *Second bound:* Applying the exact resolvent identity $K_{\text{win}}(U)^{-1} - K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} = -K_{\text{win}}(U)^{-1}(K_{\text{win}}(U) - K_{\text{win}}(U_0))K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1}$ (which requires no invertibility or definiteness of $\Sigma(U)$) gives
   $$\Phi(U) - \Phi(U_0) = \Bigl[\, - Q_x^{-1} A (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U)^{-1} W_{\text{pert}}^\top \Sigma(U) \cdot W_{\text{pert}} K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1}, \;\; 0 \,\Bigr].$$
   Therefore every row of $(\Phi(U) - \Phi(U_0))|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}$ lies in the $q_{\text{tot}}$-dimensional row space of $[W_{\text{pert}} K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i, \, 0]$. Combined with $\operatorname{row}(\Phi(U_0)|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k})$ (dimension $\le d$), we obtain $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} \tilde{q}_\tau$.
4. *Third bound:* Split $\Phi(U_0)|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k} = [Q_x^{-1} A (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i, \, B_i]$ into $[0, \, B_i]$ (rank $r_i$) plus a term inside $\operatorname{row}((E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i) \times \{0\}$ (dimension $\le d$). Likewise, the $\tau = b-1$ block of $W_{\text{pert}} K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i$ is $V_{b-1}^\top (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i$, whose rows already lie in $\operatorname{row}((E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_0)^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i)$. The remaining blocks $\tau \in \{a+1, \dots, b-2\}$ contribute at most $\sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-2} \tilde{q}_\tau$ rows. Summing the three subspaces gives $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le r_i + d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-2} \tilde{q}_\tau$.
5. *Corollaries (i)–(iv):* (i) $|\mathcal{U}_i| = 1 \implies s_\Phi = 1 \implies r^* \le \min(d, k r_i)$. (ii) Guaranteed arrival for $\tau \le b-1-\Delta_{\max}$ makes $P_{a \mid U}$ constant (since $a \le b-1-\Delta_{\max}$) and $\tilde{q}_\tau = 0$ for $\tau < b-\Delta_{\max}$, leaving at most $\Delta_{\max}$ active timestamps. (iii) With causal broadcast, each of the $m$ pending in-order members delivers its entire pending $< b$-backlog atomically on its first report after $t_{\text{rep}}$, so $D_{<b}$ is determined by the subset $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,m\}$ of pending members that have reported ($|\mathcal{U}_i| \le 2^m$); when those $m$ members have identical sensing and equal last-report times, $K_{\text{win}}(U)$ and $\Phi(U)$ depend only on $|S| \in \{0,\dots,m\}$, so $s_\Phi = |\{\Phi(U) : U \in \mathcal{U}_i\}| \le m+1$ and $r^* \le \min((m+1)d, k r_i)$. (iv) When $\mathcal{J}_\tau^{\text{var}} = \emptyset$ for all $\tau \in \{a+1,\dots,b-1\}$, only $\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{a+1} = \operatorname{im}(Q_x^{-1} A)$ is nonzero, giving $\sum \tilde{q}_\tau = \rank A$. $\square$

### Proof of C3 (Explicit Counterexample Refuting $d$-Dimensional Encoding Off Schedule)

Take $d = 1, A = 1, Q_x = 1, P_0 = 1, m_0 = 0, n = 2, H_1 = H_2 = 1, R_1 = R_2 = 1$. Schedule (deterministic and known to both members and the hub): member $1$ observes at $t = 1, 2$ and reports $W_1 = \{1, 2\}$ at $t = 2$; member $2$ observes at $t = 1, 2, 3$ and reports $W_2 = \{1, 2, 3\}$ at $t = 3$.
- At $t = 2$, $D_z(2) = \{(1,1), (1,2)\}$. Since $x_1 = x_0 + w_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2)$ and $x_2 = x_1 + w_1$, the prior precision of $(x_1, x_2)$ is $J_0^{(2)} = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 3/2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Adding member $1$'s two unit observations gives $J_{D_z(2)} = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 5/2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, with $\det = 4$ and $J_{D_z(2)}^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 5/2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Thus $M_{D_z(2), 2} = (\tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{5}{8})$.
- At $t = 3$, $D_z(3) = \{(1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), (2,3)\}$. Adding the observations to $J_0^{(3)} = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 3/2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ gives $J_{D_z(3)} = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 7/2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 4 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Solving $J_{D_z(3)} u = e_3$ yields $u_2 = \tfrac{7}{2} u_1$, $u_3 = 13 u_1$, $\tfrac{45}{2} u_1 = 1$, hence $u = (\tfrac{2}{45}, \tfrac{7}{45}, \tfrac{26}{45})^\top$. Thus $M_{D_z(3), 3} = (\tfrac{2}{45}, \tfrac{7}{45})$.
- Stacking $M_{D_z(2), 2}$ and $M_{D_z(3), 3}$ yields $M = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1/4 & 5/8 \\ 2/45 & 7/45 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $\det M = \tfrac{7}{180} - \tfrac{10}{360} = \tfrac{1}{90} \ne 0$, so $r^*(\mathcal{E}_1) = 2$. By C1(b), every exact continuous encoder for member $1$ requires $m \ge 2 > d = 1$. $\square$

### Proof of C4 (Block-Krylov Theorem)

For $k = 1$, $\Phi(U_r) = I_d$ has rank $d = \min(s, 1)\,d$. For $s = 1$, $\Phi(U_1) = [\dots, I_d]$ has rank $d = \min(1, k)\,d$. Now assume $k \ge 2$ and $s \ge 2$. Since $\rank(\mathcal{M}_1) \le k d$ always, replacing $s$ by $\min(s, k)$ (taking the first $\min(s, k)$ configurations) reduces the proof to showing that for $2 \le s \le k$, the $sd \times kd$ matrix
$$\Psi := \begin{pmatrix} Q_x^{-1} A (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_1)^{-1} & I_d \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ Q_x^{-1} A (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{\text{win}}(U_s)^{-1} & I_d \end{pmatrix}$$
has full row rank $sd$. (Since $\rank H_1 = d$, $\mathcal{R}_1^k = \mathbb{R}^{kd}$, and since $A$ is invertible, C1(a) gives $r^*(\mathcal{E}_1) = \rank \Psi$.)

Suppose $\beta_1^\top, \dots, \beta_s^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$ satisfy $\sum_{r=1}^s \beta_r^\top \Phi(U_r) = 0$. From the last block column ($I_d$),
$$\sum_{r=1}^s \beta_r^\top = 0.$$
Let $T := (\Lambda^{\mathrm o})^{-1/2} \succ 0$ and conjugate $K_{\text{win}}(U_r) = K_0 + c_r (I_{k-1} \otimes \Lambda^{\mathrm o})$ by $I_{k-1} \otimes T$:
$$\widetilde{K}_r := (I_{k-1} \otimes T) K_{\text{win}}(U_r) (I_{k-1} \otimes T) = \widetilde{K}_0 + c_r I_{(k-1)d}, \qquad \widetilde{K}_0 := (I_{k-1} \otimes T) K_0 (I_{k-1} \otimes T).$$
Notice that $K_{\text{win}}(U_r)^{-1} = (I_{k-1} \otimes T)(\widetilde{K}_0 + c_r I)^{-1}(I_{k-1} \otimes T)$ and $(E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top (I_{k-1} \otimes T) = T (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top$. Setting $w_r^\top := \beta_r^\top Q_x^{-1} A T \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$ (invertible map since $Q_x, A, T$ are invertible), we have $\sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top = 0$, and right-multiplying the first $k-1$ block columns of $\sum_r \beta_r^\top \Phi(U_r) = 0$ by $(I_{k-1} \otimes T^{-1})$ yields
$$\sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top (\widetilde{K}_0 + c_r I)^{-1} = 0.$$
Because the matrices $\widetilde{K}_0 + c_r I$ pairwise commute, multiplying on the right by $\prod_{m=1}^s (\widetilde{K}_0 + c_m I)$ gives
$$\sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top \prod_{m \ne r} (\widetilde{K}_0 + c_m I) = 0.$$
Write $\prod_{m \ne r} (z + c_m) = \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} e_{s-1-j}^{(r)} z^j$, where $e_0^{(r)} = 1$. Since $\sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top e_0^{(r)} = \sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top = 0$, the $j = s-1$ term vanishes, leaving
$$\sum_{j=0}^{s-2} v_j^\top (E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top \widetilde{K}_0^j = 0, \qquad v_j^\top := \sum_{r=1}^s e_{s-1-j}^{(r)} w_r^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}.$$

Now exploit the block-tridiagonal structure of $\widetilde{K}_0$: its subdiagonal $(t+1, t)$ block (for $1 \le t \le k-2$) is $B_{\text{sub}} := -T Q_x^{-1} A T$, which is invertible because $T, Q_x, A$ are invertible, and all blocks below the first subdiagonal vanish. By induction on $j \in \{0, 1, \dots, k-2\}$, the row-block vector $(E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top \widetilde{K}_0^j$ is supported exclusively on blocks $\{k-1-j, \dots, k-1\}$, and its leftmost block (block $k-1-j$) equals $B_{\text{sub}}^j$ (invertible). Since $s \le k$, we have $s - 2 \le k - 2$, so block $k - 1 - (s - 2) \ge 1$ exists inside $\{1, \dots, k-1\}$ and receives a contribution ONLY from $j = s-2$, forcing $v_{s-2}^\top B_{\text{sub}}^{s-2} = 0 \implies v_{s-2} = 0$. Descending induction on $j = s-2, s-3, \dots, 0$ gives $v_j = 0$ for all $0 \le j \le s-2$. Therefore the vector polynomial $P(z) := \sum_{r=1}^s w_r^\top \prod_{m \ne r} (z + c_m)$ has every coefficient equal to zero ($z^{s-1}$ coefficient $\sum w_r^\top = 0$, and $z^j$ coefficient $v_j^\top = 0$ for $j \le s-2$), so $P(z) \equiv 0$. Evaluating at $z = -c_r$ gives $w_r^\top \prod_{m \ne r} (c_m - c_r) = 0$; since the $c_r$ are pairwise distinct, $w_r = 0$ and hence $\beta_r = 0$ for every $r = 1, \dots, s$. Thus $\rank \Psi = sd$.

Specializations (i) and (ii) follow directly because in (i), $c_r = \alpha_1 + \sum_{j \text{ reported by } t_r} \alpha_j$ takes $s$ strictly increasing values, and in (ii), as $S$ ranges over subsets of $\{2, \dots, n\}$ whose packets arrive before member $1$'s packet, $c(S) = \alpha_1 + \sum_{j \in S} \alpha_j$ takes $n$ distinct values for identical $\alpha_j$ and $2^{n-1}$ distinct values when subset sums are distinct. $\square$

*Checkpoint (C4)*: Signs: $B_{\text{sub}} = -T Q_x^{-1} A T$ (invertible regardless of sign). Factors: $e_0^{(r)} = 1$ cancels $z^{s-1}$ using $\sum \beta_r^\top = 0$. Conventions: matches exact SymPy test $d=2, k=3, s=1..4 \implies [2,4,6,6]$. Types: $v_j^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$, $\widetilde{K}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{(k-1)d \times (k-1)d}$.

### Proof of C5 (Subspace-Coupled Theorem Under Decentralized Random Schedules)

For $k = 1$, all statements hold trivially ($r^* = r_i$). Assume $k \ge 2$. Let $U^{(0)} \subsetneq U^{(1)} \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq U^{(L)}$ be the hypothesized chain in $\mathcal{U}_i$, where $U^{(m)} = U^{(m-1)} \cup \{(j_m, \tau_m)\}$ with $\tau_m \in \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$ and $j_m \in \mathcal{J}_{\tau_m}$. Since all $U^{(m)}$ share the same pre-window set $U^{(0)}_{\le a}$, they share the same $P_{a \mid U^{(0)}} \succ 0$. For each $j$ with $r_j = \rank H_j \ge 1$, factor $H_j = F_j \tilde{H}_j$ with $F_j \in \mathbb{R}^{p_j \times r_j}$ full column rank and $\tilde{H}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{r_j \times d}$ full row rank; then $\Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} = \tilde{H}_j^\top \tilde{R}_j^{-1} \tilde{H}_j$ with $\tilde{R}_j := (F_j^\top R_j^{-1} F_j)^{-1} \succ 0$ ($r_j \times r_j$) and $\operatorname{row}(\tilde{H}_j) = \operatorname{im}(H_j^\top)$.

Write $K_m := K_{\text{win}}(U^{(m)}) \succ 0$. For each $m = 1, \dots, L$, $K_m = K_{m-1} + E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}} \tilde{H}_{j_m}^\top \tilde{R}_{j_m}^{-1} \tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top$. By the Woodbury identity,
$$K_m^{-1} - K_{m-1}^{-1} = - K_{m-1}^{-1} E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}} \tilde{H}_{j_m}^\top S_m^{-1} \tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1},$$
where $S_m := \tilde{R}_{j_m} + \tilde{H}_{j_m} ((E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1} E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}}) \tilde{H}_{j_m}^\top \succ 0$ is invertible. Hence
$$\Phi(U^{(m)}) - \Phi(U^{(m-1)}) = \bigl[\, - L_m \cdot \tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1}, \;\; 0 \,\bigr],$$
with left multiplier $L_m := Q_x^{-1} A \bigl((E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1} E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}}\bigr) \tilde{H}_{j_m}^\top S_m^{-1} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r_{j_m}}$.
- $Q_x^{-1} A$ is invertible ($Q_x \succ 0$, $A$ invertible).
- $(E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1} E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}}$ is $\Cov(x_{b-1}, x_{\tau_m})$ for the $(k-1)$-step chain initialized at $P_{a \mid U^{(0)}} \succ 0$ with PSD step evidence (including $A^\top Q_x^{-1} A \succeq 0$ at $b-1$), hence invertible by `lem:prv-trajectory`(b).
- $\tilde{H}_{j_m}^\top$ has full column rank $r_{j_m}$ and $S_m^{-1}$ is invertible.
Therefore $L_m$ has full column rank $r_{j_m}$, so left-multiplication by $L_m$ preserves row spaces: restricting to $\mathcal{R}_i^k$ (via $\mathcal{B}_i = I_{k-1} \otimes B_i^{\text{sens}}$),
$$\operatorname{row}\bigl((\Phi(U^{(m)}) - \Phi(U^{(m-1)}))|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}\bigr) = \operatorname{row}\bigl(\tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i\bigr) \times \{0_{r_i}\}.$$

We claim by induction on $m = 0, 1, \dots, L$ that
$$V_m := \operatorname{span}\bigl\{\operatorname{row}(\tilde{H}_{j_s} (E_{\tau_s}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{s-1}^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i) : 1 \le s \le m\bigr\} = \operatorname{span}\bigl\{\operatorname{row}(\tilde{H}_{j_s} (E_{\tau_s}^{\text{win}})^\top K_0^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i) : 1 \le s \le m\bigr\} =: W_m.$$
Base $m=0$: both are $\{0\}$. Step $m-1 \to m$: by the resolvent identity,
$$K_{m-1}^{-1} - K_0^{-1} = -K_{m-1}^{-1}(K_{m-1} - K_0)K_0^{-1} = -\sum_{s=1}^{m-1} K_{m-1}^{-1} E_{\tau_s}^{\text{win}} \tilde{H}_{j_s}^\top \tilde{R}_{j_s}^{-1} \bigl(\tilde{H}_{j_s} (E_{\tau_s}^{\text{win}})^\top K_0^{-1}\bigr).$$
Left-multiplying by $\tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top$ and right-multiplying by $\mathcal{B}_i$ shows that $\tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_{m-1}^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i$ differs from $\tilde{H}_{j_m} (E_{\tau_m}^{\text{win}})^\top K_0^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i$ by a linear combination of rows in $W_{m-1} = V_{m-1}$, so $V_m = W_m$.

At $m = L$, since $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_\tau} \operatorname{row}(\tilde{H}_j) = \mathcal{V}_\tau$, choosing a basis $V_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times q_\tau}$ of $\mathcal{V}_\tau$ and letting $\mathcal{H} := \diag(V_{a+1}^\top, \dots, V_{b-1}^\top) \in \mathbb{R}^{(\sum q_\tau) \times (k-1)d}$ (full row rank $\sum q_\tau$) gives $V_L = W_L = \operatorname{row}(\mathcal{H} K_0^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i)$.
- *(Part a)* When $\rank H_i = d$, $\mathcal{B}_i$ is invertible ($(k-1)d \times (k-1)d$) and the last block column of $\Phi(U^{(0)})|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}$ is $B_i^{\text{sens}}$ (invertible $d \times d$), whereas every difference $\Phi(U^{(m)}) - \Phi(U^{(m-1)})$ vanishes in the last block column. Thus $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \ge d + \rank(\mathcal{H} K_0^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i) = d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} q_\tau$. If no other observations on $\{1, \dots, b-1\}$ vary over $\mathcal{U}_i$, C2 gives the matching upper bound $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le d + \sum_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1} q_\tau$. (For independent Bernoulli($p_j$) reporting with $p_j \in (0,1)$, conditioning on each $j \in \mathcal{J}$ reporting at $a$ — probability $\prod p_j > 0$ — gives positive probability to every tuple of last-report times $(u_j) \in \{a, \dots, b-1\}^{\mathcal{J}}$, realizing the chain chronologically.)
- *(Part b)* When $\mathcal{V}_\tau = \mathbb{R}^d$ for all $\tau \in \{a+1, \dots, b-1\}$, $\mathcal{H}$ is invertible ($(k-1)d \times (k-1)d$), so $\mathcal{H} K_0^{-1}$ is invertible and $\dim V_L = \rank(\mathcal{H} K_0^{-1} \mathcal{B}_i) = \rank \mathcal{B}_i = (k-1) r_i$. Because the last block column of $\Phi(U^{(0)})|_{\mathcal{R}_i^k}$ has rank $r_i$ and every difference vanishes there, $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \ge r_i + (k-1) r_i = k r_i$. C2 gives $r^*(\mathcal{E}_i) \le k r_i$, hence equality.
- *(Part c)* For $d=2, A=Q_x=P_0=I_2, H_1=(1,0), H_2=(0,1), R_1=R_2=1$, the precision matrix $K_{\text{win}}(U)$ is diagonal in the two state coordinates for every $U$, and $\Lambda_2^{\mathrm o} = \diag(0, 1)$ perturbs only coordinate $2$ while $\mathcal{R}_1 = \operatorname{span}(e_1)$ sees only coordinate $1$. Thus $\Phi(U)|_{\mathcal{R}_1^k}$ is constant in $U$ and has rank $1$ for every $k \ge 1$, strictly below $\min(d + (k-1) r_2, k r_1) = k$ for $k \ge 2$. $\square$

### Proof of C6 (Exact Steady-State Round-Robin Theorem)

For $p = 0$ (no member has $\phi_j \le k-2$), every other member already delivered $\{1, \dots, b-1\}$ by time $b$, so $|\mathcal{U}_n| = 1$ and C1–C2 give $m_n^* = d = (0+1)d$. Now assume $p \ge 1$, so the pending members are $1, \dots, p$ with $0 < \phi_1 < \dots < \phi_p \le k-2$ (and possibly member $p+1$ at $\phi_{p+1} = k-1$ with empty pending set). Because every member $j \in \{1,\dots,n-1\}$ reported at $a + \phi_j \ge a + 1$ prior to $b = a+k$, all observations at timestamps $\le a$ are already in $D_z(b)$; hence $P_{a \mid U}$ is constant across all $t \ge b$. As members $1, \dots, p$ report sequentially at $b + \phi_1 < \dots < b + \phi_p < b + k$ (all strictly before member $n$'s next report at $b + k$), $D_z(t)_{<b}$ visits exactly $p + 1$ distinct sets $U_1 \subsetneq U_2 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq U_{p+1}$, where $U_{j+1} \setminus U_j = \{(j, \tau) : \tau \in \{a+\phi_j+1, \dots, b-1\}\}$ for $j = 1, \dots, p$.

Upper bound: $|\mathcal{U}_n| = p + 1$, so C2 gives $m_n^* \le (p+1)d$.

Lower bound: Write $K_r := K_{\text{win}}(U_r) \succ 0$ for $r = 1, \dots, p+1$. Since $\Lambda_n^{\mathrm o} \succ 0$ ($\mathcal{R}_n^k = \mathbb{R}^{kd}$) and $A$ is invertible, C1(a) gives $m_n^* = \rank([\Phi(U_r)]_{r=1}^{p+1})$. For each $j \in \{1, \dots, p\}$, apply the resolvent identity to $K_j^{-1} - K_{j+1}^{-1}$:
$$K_j^{-1} - K_{j+1}^{-1} = K_j^{-1}(K_{j+1} - K_j)K_{j+1}^{-1} = \sum_{\tau = a+\phi_j+1}^{b-1} K_j^{-1} E_\tau^{\text{win}} \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} (E_\tau^{\text{win}})^\top K_{j+1}^{-1}.$$
Now express $K_{j+1}^{-1}$ in terms of the terminal matrix $K_{p+1}^{-1}$ via $K_{j+1}^{-1} = K_{p+1}^{-1} + K_{j+1}^{-1}(K_{p+1} - K_{j+1})K_{p+1}^{-1}$. Crucially, $U_{p+1} \setminus U_{j+1}$ contains observations ONLY from members $j+1, \dots, p$, whose timestamps all satisfy $m \ge a + \phi_{j+1} + 1 > a + \phi_j + 1$ (with the empty sum for $j = p$). Therefore $K_{p+1} - K_{j+1} = \sum_{m = a+\phi_{j+1}+1}^{b-1} E_m^{\text{win}} \Delta_m^{(j)} (E_m^{\text{win}})^\top$, and substituting into the previous display yields
$$\Phi(U_j) - \Phi(U_{j+1}) = \Bigl[\, \sum_{\tau = a+\phi_j+1}^{b-1} C_{j, \tau}\, (E_\tau^{\text{win}})^\top K_{p+1}^{-1}, \;\; 0 \,\Bigr],$$
where: (i) $C_{j,\tau} = 0$ for all $\tau < a + \phi_j + 1$, and (ii) for $\tau = a + \phi_j + 1$ (strictly less than $a + \phi_{j+1} + 1$, so untouched by the $K_{p+1} - K_{j+1}$ term),
$$C_{j, \, a+\phi_j+1} = Q_x^{-1} A \bigl((E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_j^{-1} E_{a+\phi_j+1}^{\text{win}}\bigr) \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o}.$$
By hypothesis $Q_x \succ 0$, $A$ is invertible, $\Lambda_j^{\mathrm o} \succ 0$, and $(E_{b-1}^{\text{win}})^\top K_j^{-1} E_{a+\phi_j+1}^{\text{win}}$ is invertible by `lem:prv-trajectory`(b), so every pivot block $C_{j, a+\phi_j+1}$ is invertible ($d \times d$). Since the rows of $\{ (E_\tau^{\text{win}})^\top K_{p+1}^{-1} \}_{\tau=a+1}^{b-1}$ form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^{(k-1)d}$ and $a+\phi_1+1 < \dots < a+\phi_p+1$, the $p$ row blocks $\Phi(U_j) - \Phi(U_{j+1})$ ($j=1,\dots,p$) are in strict block-row-echelon form with invertible $d \times d$ pivots, hence have full row rank $pd$. Because $\Phi(U_{p+1}) = [\dots, I_d]$ has $I_d$ in the last block column where all $p$ differences vanish, $\rank([\Phi(U_r)]_{r=1}^{p+1}) = d + pd = (p+1)d$. When $n = k$ and $\phi_j = j$, member $k-1$ has $\phi_{k-1} = k-1$, so $p = k - 2$ and $m_k^* = (k-1)d$. $\square$

### Proof of C7 (Boundary Regimes $Q_x = 0$ and $A = 0$)

(a) When $Q_x = 0$ and $A$ is invertible, $x_\tau = A^{\tau - b} x_b$ deterministically for every $\tau \in W_i$. The likelihood of $O_i$ given the entire state trajectory $X$ therefore equals
$$p(O_i \mid X) = p(O_i \mid x_b) \propto \exp\left( \Bigl(\sum_{\tau \in W_i} (A^{\tau - b})^\top h_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm o}\Bigr)^\top x_b - \frac{1}{2} x_b^\top \Bigl(\sum_{\tau \in W_i} (A^{\tau - b})^\top \Lambda_i^{\mathrm o} A^{\tau - b}\Bigr) x_b \right).$$
The linear data-dependent statistic $z_i := \sum_{\tau \in W_i} (A^{\tau - b})^\top h_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm o} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (at most $\min(d, k r_i)$ independent coordinates) is thus sufficient for $O_i$ given any other observations $o_{D \setminus W_i}$ at any query time $t$.

(b) When $A = 0$ and $Q_x \succ 0$, the states $x_1, x_2, \dots$ are mutually independent ($x_\tau = w_{\tau-1}^x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_x)$), so $J_D$ is block-diagonal. For any $(D, t)$ with $W_i \subseteq D$ and $t \ge b$: if $t > b$, $M_{D,t} = 0$; if $t = b$, $M_{D,b} \ell_i = (Q_x^{-1} + \sum_{(j,b)\in D} \Lambda_j^{\mathrm o})^{-1} h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o}$, which depends on $O_i$ solely through $h_{i,b}^{\mathrm o} \in \mathcal{R}_i$ ($\dim = r_i = \rank H_i \le d$). $\square$
