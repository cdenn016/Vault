- **Verdict:**

Moreover, an elementary closed-form 3-member, 3-policy cyclic collective reproduces an exact reversed-cycle redundant linear-Gaussian group posterior for every $\gamma > 0$ (`C4`).

## Conventions & Standing Assumptions

- **Standing probability convention:** Every group model $(q, \psi, \mathfrak{B})$ considered below is defined on a standard Borel probability space (so regular conditional probabilities and the mutual-information chain rule hold whenever all mutual informations are finite), with natural logarithm $\ln$ (nats). When $n = 2$, we use the standard empty-product convention $\mathcal{A}_{-ij} = \{()\}$, $A_{-ij}(()) = \varnothing$.
- **Collective and policy space (`def:agy-group-model`):** $C = \{1, \dots, n\}$ with $n \ge 2$ members (indexed $0, 1, 2$ in cyclic 3-member constructions). Each member $i$ has a finite policy set $\mathcal{A}_i$ containing a distinguished null policy $0$, and $\mathcal{A} = \prod_{i=1}^n \mathcal{A}_i$. Policy $\pi_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$ opens a slot set $\mathfrak{B}_i(\pi_i)$, disjoint across members, with $\mathfrak{B}_i(0) = \varnothing$. Write $A_{-i}(\pi_{-i}) = \bigcup_{k \ne i} \mathfrak{B}_k(\pi_k)$, $A_{-ij}(c) = \bigcup_{k \notin \{i, j\}} \mathfrak{B}_k(c_k)$, and $\mathfrak{B}(\pi) = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \mathfrak{B}_i(\pi_i)$.
- **Slot model and finite expected free energies:** Slot outcomes $o_A$ are conditionally independent across disjoint slot sets given $\eta_C$ under the shared belief $q_C$, with all mutual informations $I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}) < \infty$. Member $i$'s slot-product preference satisfies $\psi^{(i)}_{A \cup B}(q_C) = \psi^{(i)}_A(q_C) + \psi^{(i)}_B(q_C)$ for disjoint slot sets $A, B$, with $\psi^{(i)}_\varnothing(q_C) = 0$ and all $\psi^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{B}_j(\pi_j)}(q_C) \in \mathbb{R}$ finite.
- **Individual and group EFEs:** Under `(P0)`–`(P2)`,
  $$G_i(\pi) = -I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}) + \psi^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}(q_C), \qquad G_C(\pi) = -I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}) + \sum_{j=1}^n \psi^{(j)}_{\mathfrak{B}_j(\pi_j)}(q_C).$$
  Because $\psi^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}(q_C) - \sum_j \psi^{(j)}_{\mathfrak{B}_j(\pi_j)}(q_C) = \sum_{j \ne i} [\psi^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{B}_j(\pi_j)}(q_C) - \psi^{(j)}_{\mathfrak{B}_j(\pi_j)}(q_C)]$ depends only on $\pi_{-i}$, we have $G_i(\pi) - G_C(\pi) = f_i(\pi_{-i})$ for every $i$.
- **Group posterior and synchronous kernel:** Under `(P3)`, $\gamma > 0$ and $E_i(\pi_i) > 0$ for all $\pi_i \in \mathcal{A}_i$, with $\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} E_i(a) = 1$. The group posterior is
  $$\mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)(\pi) = \frac{1}{\mathsf{Z}(\gamma)} \left(\prod_{i=1}^n E_i(\pi_i)\right) e^{-\gamma G_C(\pi)}.$$
  Member $i$'s resampling conditional given $\pi_{-i}$ is
  $$K_i(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i}) = \frac{E_i(\pi'_i) e^{-\gamma G_i(\pi'_i, \pi_{-i})}}{\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} E_i(a) e^{-\gamma G_i(a, \pi_{-i})}} = \frac{E_i(\pi'_i) e^{-\gamma G_C(\pi'_i, \pi_{-i})}}{\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} E_i(a) e^{-\gamma G_C(a, \pi_{-i})}} = \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i}).$$
  The synchronous deliberation Markov kernel on $\mathcal{A}$ is
  $$T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') = \prod_{i=1}^n K_i(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i}) > 0 \qquad \forall \pi, \pi' \in \mathcal{A}.$$
  Because $T_{\mathrm{sync}}$ is entrywise strictly positive on the finite state space $\mathcal{A}$, by the Perron–Frobenius / finite ergodic Markov chain theorem it is irreducible and aperiodic, has a unique stationary distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma; E, G_C)$ (denoted $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty$) with full support $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\pi) > 0$ for all $\pi \in \mathcal{A}$, and satisfies $\mu_0 T_{\mathrm{sync}}^t \to \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty$ entrywise for every initial law $\mu_0$.
- **Discriminating conventions with test values:**
  - Total variation distance: $\mathrm{TV}(P, Q) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{A}} |P(\pi) - Q(\pi)|$; on $\{0, 1\}$, $\mathrm{TV}((0.8, 0.2), (0.3, 0.7)) = 0.5$.
  - Pearson $\chi^2$-divergence: $\chi^2(p \,\|\, r \otimes s) = \sum_{a, b} \frac{(p(a,b) - r(a)s(b))^2}{r(a)s(b)} = \sum_{a, b} \frac{p(a,b)^2}{r(a)s(b)} - 1 \ge 0$; for $p = \operatorname{diag}(1/2, 1/2)$ on $\{0, 1\}^2$, $\chi^2(p \,\|\, r \otimes s) = 1$.
  - Pairwise Gaussian slot redundancy: for $\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ observed independently by two slots of SNR $S > 0$, $I_{q_C}(o_1; o_2) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{(1+S)^2}{1+2S}$; at $S = 1$, $I_{q_C}(o_1; o_2) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(4/3) \approx 0.1438410362$.
  - Log odds ratio sign (`prop:agy-performative-sign`): for epistemic slots from the null policies in context $c \in \mathcal{A}_{-ij}$,
    $$\ln \frac{\mathbb{P}_C(a, b, c)\,\mathbb{P}_C(0, 0, c)}{\mathbb{P}_C(a, 0, c)\,\mathbb{P}_C(0, b, c)} = -\gamma \partial^2_{(0,a),(0,b)} G_C(\cdot, \cdot, c) = -\gamma I_{q_C}\!\big(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)}\big) \le 0.$$

### C1: Exact One-Step Residual Identity and Same-Precision Resolution of `conj:agy-synchronous`
- Statement: Let $n \ge 2$ members satisfy `(P0)`–`(P3)` with finite expected free energies, common precision $\gamma > 0$, and full-support product habits $\prod_{i=1}^n E_i$. Write $\mathbb{P}_C = \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)$, let $X \sim \mathbb{P}_C$, and draw $Y \mid X \sim T_{\mathrm{sync}}(X, \cdot)$. Then for every distinct pair $i \ne j$, writing $p_c(a, b) = \mathbb{P}_C(X_i = a, X_j = b \mid X_{-ij} = c)$ with marginals $r_c(a) = \sum_b p_c(a, b)$ and $s_c(b) = \sum_a p_c(a, b)$,
  $$\mathbb{P}(Y_i = 0, Y_j = 0) - \mathbb{P}(X_i = 0, X_j = 0) = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{A}_{-ij}} \mathbb{P}_C(X_{-ij} = c)\, p_c(0, 0) \left[ \chi^2(p_c \,\|\, r_c \otimes s_c) + \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} \sum_{b \in \mathcal{A}_j} \frac{p_c(a, b)^2}{r_c(a) s_c(b)} \!\left(e^{\gamma I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)};\, o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)})} - 1\right) \right] \ge 0.$$
  Consequently:
  1. $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma; E, G_C) = \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)$ if and only if $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)}) = 0$ for all $i \ne j$, $a \in \mathcal{A}_i$, $b \in \mathcal{A}_j$, and $c \in \mathcal{A}_{-ij}$ — equivalently, if and only if $\mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)$ is a product law.
  2. Whenever some pair $(i, j)$ carries unconditional redundant information $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)}) > 0$ for some $a \in \mathcal{A}_i, b \in \mathcal{A}_j$, the $c = 0_{-ij}$ term is strictly positive, $\mathrm{TV}(\mathbb{P}_C T_{\mathrm{sync}}, \mathbb{P}_C) \ge \mathbb{P}(Y_i = 0, Y_j = 0) - \mathbb{P}(X_i = 0, X_j = 0) > 0$, and therefore $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma; E, G_C) \ne \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)$ for every $\gamma > 0$.

### Proof of C1 (Exact One-Step Residual Identity and Same-Precision Theorem)

**Step 1 (Mutual-information second-difference identity).** Fix distinct members $i \ne j$, policies $a \in \mathcal{A}_i$, $b \in \mathcal{A}_j$, and a context $c \in \mathcal{A}_{-ij}$. Set $A = \mathfrak{B}_i(a)$, $B = \mathfrak{B}_j(b)$, and $O = A_{-ij}(c)$, which are pairwise disjoint slot sets with $\mathfrak{B}_i(0) = \mathfrak{B}_j(0) = \varnothing$. Because the pragmatic sum $\sum_k \psi^{(k)}_{\mathfrak{B}_k(\pi_k)}(q_C)$ is additive across members, its mixed second difference in $(\pi_i, \pi_j)$ vanishes, leaving
$$\partial^2_{(0,a),(0,b)} G_C(\cdot, \cdot, c) = -I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{A \cup B \cup O}) + I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{A \cup O}) + I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{B \cup O}) - I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_O).$$
By the chain rule for mutual information (all terms being finite by hypothesis),
$$I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{A \cup B \cup O}) - I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{B \cup O}) = I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_A \mid o_{B \cup O}), \qquad I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{A \cup O}) - I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_O) = I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_A \mid o_O).$$
Since slot outcomes are conditionally independent across disjoint slot sets given $\eta_C$, weak union gives $o_A \perp o_B \mid (\eta_C, o_O)$, so $I_{q_C}(o_A; o_B \mid \eta_C, o_O) = 0$. Expanding $I_{q_C}(o_A; (\eta_C, o_B) \mid o_O)$ in both orders (all terms finite since $I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{A \cup B \cup O}) < \infty$) gives
$$I_{q_C}(o_A; \eta_C \mid o_O) + 0 = I_{q_C}(o_A; o_B \mid o_O) + I_{q_C}(o_A; \eta_C \mid o_B, o_O).$$
Hence $\partial^2_{(0,a),(0,b)} G_C(\cdot, \cdot, c) = I_{q_C}(o_A; o_B \mid o_O) \ge 0$, and because the product habits cancel in the cross-ratio, the conditional law $p_c(a, b) = \mathbb{P}_C(X_i = a, X_j = b \mid X_{-ij} = c)$ satisfies
$$\frac{p_c(a, 0)\, p_c(0, b)}{p_c(0, 0)} = p_c(a, b)\, e^{\gamma I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)};\, o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)})} \qquad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}_i,\; \forall b \in \mathcal{A}_j.$$

**Step 2 (General one-step residual covariance identity).** Let $X \sim \mathbb{P}_C$ and draw $Y \mid X \sim T_{\mathrm{sync}}(X, \cdot) = \prod_{k=1}^n \mathbb{P}_C(\cdot \mid X_{-k})$. Fix $i \ne j$ and any functions $g_i : \mathcal{A}_i \to \mathbb{R}$, $g_j : \mathcal{A}_j \to \mathbb{R}$. Define the conditional projections $\Pi_i g_i(X) = \mathbb{E}[g_i(X_i) \mid X_{-i}]$ and $\Pi_j g_j(X) = \mathbb{E}[g_j(X_j) \mid X_{-j}]$, and the residuals $R_i = g_i(X_i) - \Pi_i g_i(X)$ and $R_j = g_j(X_j) - \Pi_j g_j(X)$.

Since $Y_i$ and $Y_j$ are conditionally independent given $X$ with $\mathbb{E}[g_i(Y_i) \mid X] = \Pi_i g_i(X)$ and $\mathbb{E}[g_j(Y_j) \mid X] = \Pi_j g_j(X)$,
$$\mathbb{E}[g_i(Y_i) g_j(Y_j)] = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[g_i(Y_i) \mid X]\,\mathbb{E}[g_j(Y_j) \mid X]\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[(\Pi_i g_i)(X)\, (\Pi_j g_j)(X)\big].$$
Because $i \ne j$, $g_i(X_i)$ is $\sigma(X_{-j})$-measurable and $g_j(X_j)$ is $\sigma(X_{-i})$-measurable, and moreover $\sigma(X_{-ij}) \subseteq \sigma(X_{-i}) \cap \sigma(X_{-j})$. Hence conditioning on $X_{-ij}$ yields $\mathbb{E}[g_i(X_i)\,\Pi_j g_j(X) \mid X_{-ij}] = \mathbb{E}[g_i(X_i) g_j(X_j) \mid X_{-ij}]$ and $\mathbb{E}[(\Pi_i g_i)(X)\, g_j(X_j) \mid X_{-ij}] = \mathbb{E}[g_i(X_i) g_j(X_j) \mid X_{-ij}]$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[R_i R_j \mid X_{-ij}] = \mathbb{E}\big[(\Pi_i g_i)(\Pi_j g_j) \mid X_{-ij}\big] - \mathbb{E}\big[g_i(X_i) g_j(X_j) \mid X_{-ij}\big],$$
and taking expectation over $X_{-ij}$ gives $\mathbb{E}[g_i(Y_i) g_j(Y_j)] - \mathbb{E}[g_i(X_i) g_j(X_j)] = \mathbb{E}[R_i R_j] = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{A}_{-ij}} \mathbb{P}_C(X_{-ij} = c)\,\mathbb{E}[R_i R_j \mid X_{-ij} = c]$.

**Step 3 (Specialization to null-policy indicators).** Choose $g_i(\pi_i) = \mathbf{1}[\pi_i = 0]$ and $g_j(\pi_j) = \mathbf{1}[\pi_j = 0]$. Conditioning on $X_{-ij} = c$, we have $(\Pi_i g_i)(a, b, c) = p_c(0, b) / s_c(b)$ and $(\Pi_j g_j)(a, b, c) = p_c(a, 0) / r_c(a)$ (denominators strictly positive by full support of $\mathbb{P}_C$). By Step 2,
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[R_i R_j \mid X_{-ij} = c] &= \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} \sum_{b \in \mathcal{A}_j} p_c(a, b) \frac{p_c(a, 0)}{r_c(a)} \frac{p_c(0, b)}{s_c(b)} - p_c(0, 0) \\
&= p_c(0, 0) \left[ \sum_{a, b} \frac{p_c(a, b)}{r_c(a) s_c(b)} \cdot \frac{p_c(a, 0) p_c(0, b)}{p_c(0, 0)} - 1 \right].
\end{aligned}$$
Substituting Step 1 and using $\sum_{a, b} \frac{p_c(a, b)^2}{r_c(a) s_c(b)} - 1 = \sum_{a, b} \frac{(p_c(a, b) - r_c(a) s_c(b))^2}{r_c(a) s_c(b)} = \chi^2(p_c \,\|\, r_c \otimes s_c) \ge 0$ gives
$$\mathbb{E}[R_i R_j \mid X_{-ij} = c] = p_c(0, 0) \left[ \chi^2(p_c \,\|\, r_c \otimes s_c) + \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} \sum_{b \in \mathcal{A}_j} \frac{p_c(a, b)^2}{r_c(a) s_c(b)} \!\left(e^{\gamma I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)};\, o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)})} - 1\right) \right] \ge 0.$$
Taking expectation over $c = X_{-ij}$ gives the stated exact identity for $\mathbb{P}(Y_i = 0, Y_j = 0) - \mathbb{P}(X_i = 0, X_j = 0)$.

**Step 4 (Necessary and sufficient condition and conclusion).** Because $\mathbb{P}_C$ has full support on $\mathcal{A}$, every weight $\mathbb{P}_C(X_{-ij} = c) > 0$, $p_c(a, b) > 0$, $r_c(a) > 0$, and $s_c(b) > 0$, and every term inside the brackets is nonnegative. If $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty = \mathbb{P}_C$, then $Y \sim X$, so $\mathbb{P}(Y_i = 0, Y_j = 0) - \mathbb{P}(X_i = 0, X_j = 0) = 0$ for all $i \ne j$, forcing $e^{\gamma I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)})} - 1 = 0$ and hence $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)}) = 0$ for all $i \ne j$, $a, b, c$. Writing $O_k = o_{\mathfrak{B}_k(\pi_k)}$, conditional independence $O_k \perp O_{1:k-1} \mid \eta_C$ yields $I_{q_C}(\eta_C; O_k \mid O_{1:k-1}) = I_{q_C}(\eta_C; O_k) - I_{q_C}(O_k; O_{1:k-1})$; summing over $k = 1, \dots, n$ and applying the chain rule to $I_{q_C}(O_k; O_{1:k-1})$ gives
$$I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}(\pi)}) = \sum_{k=1}^n I_{q_C}(\eta_C; O_k) - \sum_{k=2}^n \sum_{l=1}^{k-1} I_{q_C}(O_k; O_l \mid O_1, \dots, O_{l-1}).$$
Because $\mathfrak{B}_m(0) = \varnothing$, each term $I_{q_C}(O_k; O_l \mid O_1, \dots, O_{l-1})$ equals $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_k(\pi_k)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_l(\pi_l)} \mid o_{A_{-kl}(c)})$ for the context $c$ setting $c_m = \pi_m$ ($m < l$) and $c_m = 0$ ($m > l, m \ne k$). Hence vanishing of all conditional redundancies implies $G_C(\pi)$ is additive across members and $\mathbb{P}_C$ is a product law. Conversely, if $\mathbb{P}_C$ is a product law then $K_i(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i}) = \mathbb{P}_C(\pi'_i)$ is independent of $\pi_{-i}$, so $T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') = \mathbb{P}_C(\pi')$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty = \mathbb{P}_C$. Finally, if some pair $(i, j)$ satisfies $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)}) > 0$, then at the null context $c = 0_{-ij}$ we have $o_{A_{-ij}(0_{-ij})} = \varnothing$, so the $(a, b, 0_{-ij})$ term is strictly positive, yielding $\mathrm{TV}(\mathbb{P}_C T_{\mathrm{sync}}, \mathbb{P}_C) \ge \mathbb{P}(Y_i = 0, Y_j = 0) - \mathbb{P}(X_i = 0, X_j = 0) > 0$ and hence $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty \ne \mathbb{P}_C$. $\square$

### Proof of C2 (Binary-Policy Positive Association, Universal Impossibility, and Bipartite Cut)

**Lemma (Harris's inequality for product laws on $\{0, 1\}^n$).** If $\nu = \bigotimes_{i=1}^n \nu_i$ is a product probability law on $\{0, 1\}^n$ and $f, g : \{0, 1\}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ are coordinatewise nondecreasing, then $\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(f, g) \ge 0$.

*Proof.* Induction on $n$. For $n = 1$, let $X'_1$ be an independent copy of $X_1 \sim \nu_1$; then $\operatorname{Cov}(f(X_1), g(X_1)) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[(f(X_1) - f(X'_1))(g(X_1) - g(X'_1))] \ge 0$ since both factors have the same sign. For $n \ge 2$, write $X = (X_1, X_{-1})$ with $X_1 \perp X_{-1}$. By the law of total covariance,
$$\operatorname{Cov}(f(X), g(X)) = \mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Cov}(f(X_1, X_{-1}), g(X_1, X_{-1}) \mid X_{-1})\big] + \operatorname{Cov}\!\big(\mathbb{E}[f(X_1, X_{-1}) \mid X_{-1}],\, \mathbb{E}[g(X_1, X_{-1}) \mid X_{-1}]\big).$$
The first term is nonnegative by the $n = 1$ case applied conditionally for each $x_{-1}$. By independence of $X_1$ and $X_{-1}$, $x_{-1} \mapsto \mathbb{E}[f(X_1, x_{-1})]$ and $x_{-1} \mapsto \mathbb{E}[g(X_1, x_{-1})]$ are nondecreasing on $\{0, 1\}^{n-1}$, so the second term is nonnegative by the induction hypothesis. $\square$

**Proof of C2(1).** Equip $\{0, 1\}^n$ with the coordinatewise order $x \le y \iff x_k \le y_k$ for all $k$. Fix $x \le y$ and member $i$. Set $S = \{k \ne i : x_k = 1\} \subseteq T = \{k \ne i : y_k = 1\}$ and $D = T \setminus S$, with $A_S = \bigcup_{k \in S} \mathfrak{B}_k(1)$, $A_T = \bigcup_{k \in T} \mathfrak{B}_k(1)$, and $A_D = \bigcup_{k \in D} \mathfrak{B}_k(1)$ (pairwise disjoint from $\mathfrak{B}_i(1)$). By the same mutual-information chain-rule and conditional-independence identity as Step 1 of `C1` (using $o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(1)} \perp o_{A_D} \mid (\eta_C, o_{A_S})$ and finiteness of all mutual informations),
$$\ln \frac{K_i(1 \mid y_{-i})}{K_i(0 \mid y_{-i})} - \ln \frac{K_i(1 \mid x_{-i})}{K_i(0 \mid x_{-i})} = -\gamma \Big[-I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(1)} \mid o_{A_T}) + I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(1)} \mid o_{A_S})\Big] = -\gamma I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(1)}; o_{A_D} \mid o_{A_S}) \le 0.$$
Since $t \mapsto e^t / (1 + e^t)$ is strictly increasing, $K_i(1 \mid y_{-i}) \le K_i(1 \mid x_{-i})$ for every $i$. Coupling $Y^{(x)} \sim T_{\mathrm{sync}}(x, \cdot)$ and $Y^{(y)} \sim T_{\mathrm{sync}}(y, \cdot)$ via i.i.d. $U_1, \dots, U_n \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0, 1]$ by $Y^{(x)}_i = \mathbf{1}[U_i \le K_i(1 \mid x_{-i})]$ and $Y^{(y)}_i = \mathbf{1}[U_i \le K_i(1 \mid y_{-i})]$ gives $Y^{(y)} \le Y^{(x)}$ surely. Hence for every nondecreasing $f : \{0, 1\}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, $(T_{\mathrm{sync}} f)(y) \le (T_{\mathrm{sync}} f)(x)$, i.e., $T_{\mathrm{sync}} f$ is nonincreasing.

Now let $\mu$ be any positively associated law on $\{0, 1\}^n$, let $X \sim \mu$, $Y \mid X \sim T_{\mathrm{sync}}(X, \cdot)$, and let $f, g : \{0, 1\}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be nondecreasing. By the law of total covariance,
$$\operatorname{Cov}(f(Y), g(Y)) = \mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Cov}(f(Y), g(Y) \mid X)\big] + \operatorname{Cov}\!\big((T_{\mathrm{sync}} f)(X),\, (T_{\mathrm{sync}} g)(X)\big).$$
Given $X = x$, $Y$ has the product distribution $\prod_i K_i(\cdot \mid x_{-i})$, so $\operatorname{Cov}(f(Y), g(Y) \mid X = x) \ge 0$ by the Lemma. In the second term, $-T_{\mathrm{sync}} f$ and $-T_{\mathrm{sync}} g$ are both nondecreasing, so $\operatorname{Cov}(T_{\mathrm{sync}} f(X), T_{\mathrm{sync}} g(X)) = \operatorname{Cov}(-T_{\mathrm{sync}} f(X), -T_{\mathrm{sync}} g(X)) \ge 0$ by positive association of $\mu$. Thus $\mu T_{\mathrm{sync}}$ is positively associated. Starting from any product law $\mu_0$ (positively associated by the Lemma), induction gives that $\mu_t = \mu_0 T_{\mathrm{sync}}^t$ is positively associated for all $t \ge 0$. Because $T_{\mathrm{sync}} > 0$ entrywise on finite $\{0, 1\}^n$, $\mu_t \to \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty$ entrywise as $t \to \infty$, and continuity of covariance on the finite simplex yields positive association of $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty$. $\square$

**Proof of C2(2).** Let $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C \propto \prod_{k=1}^n \tilde{E}_k e^{-\gamma_C \tilde{G}_C}$ be any binary epistemic group posterior satisfying the stated hypotheses, with distinct redundant pair $i \ne j$ ($I_{\tilde{q}_C}(o_{\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_i(1)}; o_{\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_j(1)}) > 0$).

- *Case 1 (full-support product habits $\tilde{E}_k(a) > 0$ for all $k, a$):* Because $\tilde{G}_C$ is finite and $\tilde{E}_k$ has full support, $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(\pi) > 0$ for all $\pi \in \{0, 1\}^n$. Applying the same mutual-information chain-rule and conditional-independence identity under $\tilde{q}_C$ (valid by the standing standard Borel convention and finiteness of mutual informations) with $S = \varnothing$ and $T = \{k \ne i : c_k = 1\}$ gives, for every $c \in \{0, 1\}^{n-1}$,
  $$\ln \frac{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} = c)}{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 0 \mid X_{-i} = c)} - \ln \frac{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} = 0_{-i})}{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 0 \mid X_{-i} = 0_{-i})} = -\gamma_C I_{\tilde{q}_C}(o_{\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_i(1)}; o_{\tilde{A}_{-i}(c)}) \le 0,$$
  with strict inequality at $c = e_j$ (where only member $j \ne i$ is active, so $\tilde{A}_{-i}(e_j) = \tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_j(1)$). Thus $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} = c) \le \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} = 0_{-i})$ for all $c$, strictly at $c = e_j$. Because $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C$ has full support, $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_{-i} = e_j \mid X_{-i} \ne 0_{-i}) > 0$, so averaging over $c \ne 0_{-i}$ gives $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} \ne 0_{-i}) < \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid X_{-i} = 0_{-i})$. With $A = X_i$ and $B = \mathbf{1}[X_{-i} \ne 0_{-i}]$, both nondecreasing on $\{0, 1\}^n$ and $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(B = 0), \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(B = 1) > 0$,
  $$\operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C}(X_i, \mathbf{1}[X_{-i} \ne 0_{-i}]) = \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(B = 1)\,\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(B = 0)\Big[\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid B = 1) - \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(X_i = 1 \mid B = 0)\Big] < 0,$$
  contradicting C2(1), so $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma) \ne \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C$.
- *Case 2 (non-full-support product habits, $\tilde{E}_k(a) = 0$ for some $k, a$):* Then $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C(\pi) = 0$ for every profile $\pi$ with $\pi_k = a$. In contrast, because $T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') > 0$ for all $\pi, \pi' \in \{0, 1\}^n$, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(\pi') = \sum_\pi \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(\pi) T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') > 0$ for every $\pi' \in \{0, 1\}^n$. Hence $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma) \ne \tilde{\mathbb{P}}_C$ by support mismatch. $\square$

**Proof of C2(3).** If $U = \varnothing$ or $W = \varnothing$, factorization is trivial; assume $U, W \ne \varnothing$. Fix $i \in U$ and any $a \in \mathcal{A}_i$. By Step 1 of `C1`, changing any $j \in U \setminus \{i\}$ from $0$ to $b$ while holding all other policies fixed changes $G_C(a, \pi_{-i}) - G_C(0, \pi_{-i})$ by $I_{q_C}(o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}; o_{\mathfrak{B}_j(b)} \mid o_{A_{-ij}(c)}) = 0$. The identical argument with $U$ and $W$ exchanged gives $K_j(\pi'_j \mid \pi_{-j}) = K_j(\pi'_j \mid \pi_U)$ for every $j \in W$.

Define the block kernels $K_U(\pi'_U \mid \pi_W) := \prod_{i \in U} K_i(\pi'_i \mid \pi_W)$ and $K_W(\pi'_W \mid \pi_U) := \prod_{j \in W} K_j(\pi'_j \mid \pi_U)$, so that $T_{\mathrm{sync}}((\pi_U, \pi_W), (\pi'_U, \pi'_W)) = K_U(\pi'_U \mid \pi_W)\, K_W(\pi'_W \mid \pi_U)$. For any $(\mu_U, \mu_W) \in \Delta(\mathcal{A}_U) \times \Delta(\mathcal{A}_W)$,
$$(\mu_U \otimes \mu_W) T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi'_U, \pi'_W) = (\mu_W K_U)(\pi'_U)\, (\mu_U K_W)(\pi'_W).$$
Thus the continuous (affine) self-map $F : \Delta(\mathcal{A}_U) \times \Delta(\mathcal{A}_W) \to \Delta(\mathcal{A}_U) \times \Delta(\mathcal{A}_W)$ defined by $F(\mu_U, \mu_W) = (\mu_W K_U, \mu_U K_W)$ intertwines with $T_{\mathrm{sync}}$ via $\Phi(\mu_U, \mu_W) = \mu_U \otimes \mu_W$. Because $\mathcal{A}_U$ and $\mathcal{A}_W$ are finite and nonempty, $\Delta(\mathcal{A}_U) \times \Delta(\mathcal{A}_W)$ is a nonempty compact convex subset of Euclidean space, so Brouwer's fixed-point theorem yields a fixed point $(\mu_U^\ast, \mu_W^\ast)$ of $F$. Then $\mu_U^\ast \otimes \mu_W^\ast = \Phi(\mu_U^\ast, \mu_W^\ast)$ is stationary for $T_{\mathrm{sync}}$, and therefore equals $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)$ by uniqueness. Because neither $K_i(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i}) = \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma; E, G_C)(\pi'_i \mid \pi_{-i})$ nor $T_{\mathrm{sync}} > 0$ nor the vanishing of $\gamma I_{q_C}$ uses $\gamma > 0$, this proof holds verbatim for any shared $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$. $\square$

### Proof of C3 (Self-Reproducing 3-Member, 3-Policy Linear-Gaussian Collective)

**Lemma (Peretto detailed balance for pairwise potentials; cf. Peretto 1984).** On a finite member set $C$ ($|C| = n \ge 2$), suppose $G_C(\pi) = \sum_{i \in C} g_i(\pi_i) + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \ne j \in C} V_{ij}(\pi_i, \pi_j)$ with symmetric $V_{ji}(\pi_j, \pi_i) = V_{ij}(\pi_i, \pi_j)$. Write $w_i(\pi_i) = E_i(\pi_i) e^{-\gamma g_i(\pi_i)} > 0$ and $Z_i(\pi_{-i}) = \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_i} w_i(a) \exp(-\gamma \sum_{j \ne i} V_{ij}(a, \pi_j))$. Then $T_{\mathrm{sync}}$ is reversible with respect to, and has unique stationary distribution,
$$\mu_{\mathrm{P}}(\pi) \propto \left(\prod_{i \in C} w_i(\pi_i)\right) \left(\prod_{i \in C} Z_i(\pi_{-i})\right).$$

*Proof.* By definition, $T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') = \frac{\prod_{i \in C} w_i(\pi'_i)}{\prod_{i \in C} Z_i(\pi_{-i})} \exp\!\big(-\gamma \sum_{i \ne j} V_{ij}(\pi'_i, \pi_j)\big)$. Multiplying by $\mu_{\mathrm{P}}(\pi)$ cancels $\prod_i Z_i(\pi_{-i})$, leaving $\mu_{\mathrm{P}}(\pi) T_{\mathrm{sync}}(\pi, \pi') \propto \big(\prod_i w_i(\pi_i) w_i(\pi'_i)\big) \exp\!\big(-\gamma \sum_{i \ne j} V_{ij}(\pi'_i, \pi_j)\big)$. Relabeling $(i, j) \leftrightarrow (j, i)$ and using $V_{ji}(\pi_j, \pi'_i) = V_{ij}(\pi'_i, \pi_j)$ shows the right-hand side is symmetric in $(\pi, \pi')$. Since $T_{\mathrm{sync}} > 0$ entrywise on finite $\mathcal{A}$, its stationary law is unique and equals $\mu_{\mathrm{P}}$. $\square$

**Step 1 (Cyclic 3-member, 3-policy pairwise reduction).** Take $C = \{0, 1, 2\}$, $\mathcal{A}_i = \{0, 1, 2\}$, $\gamma = 1$, and pairwise interaction $V_{01}(u, v) = V_{12}(u, v) = V_{20}(u, v) = B_{u, v}$ (with $V_{ji}(v, u) := V_{ij}(u, v)$, $B_{0, \cdot} = B_{\cdot, 0} = 0$, and on $\{1, 2\}^2$,

\end{aligned}$$
Factoring $Z(u, v) = Z(u, 0) Z(0, v) \cdot \frac{Z(u, v)}{Z(u, 0) Z(0, v)}$ (using $Z(0, 0) = 1$) decomposes the Peretto stationary law into single-member factors $w(u) Z(u, 0) Z(0, u)$ and purely pairwise interactions $\frac{Z(u, v)}{Z(u, 0) Z(0, v)}$ on each directed edge $(i, (i+1)\bmod 3)$ (equal to $1$ whenever $u = 0$ or $v = 0$). **Step 2 (Exact Implicit Function Theorem existence proof).** Fix any $\epsilon \in (0, 7 - 4\sqrt{3})$ and set $a = \delta$, $b = \beta \delta$, $c = \kappa \delta$, $\gamma_C = \mu \delta$. For any random variables $(U, V)$ taking values $(0, 0)$ with probability $\epsilon$, $(u_1, v_1)$ with probability $p$, and $(u_2, v_2)$ with probability $p$, the cumulant expansion of $\ln \mathbb{E}_w[e^{-\delta(U + V)}]$ around $\delta = 0$ gives
$$-\ln \frac{\mathbb{E}_w[e^{-\delta(U+V)}]}{\mathbb{E}_w[e^{-\delta U}]\,\mathbb{E}_w[e^{-\delta V}]} = -\delta^2 \operatorname{Cov}_w(U, V) + O(\delta^3),$$
and the left-hand side divided by $\delta^2$ extends real-analytically across $\delta = 0$ to $-\operatorname{Cov}_w(U, V)$ (since $\mathbb{E}_w[e^{-\delta X}]$ is entire and equals $1 > 0$ at $\delta = 0$, and both the $\delta^0$ and $\delta^1$ coefficients vanish identically in $(u_1, u_2, v_1, v_2)$). $$\begin{aligned}
F_1(\beta, \kappa, \mu; 0) &= p\big[p(1 + \beta)(1 + \kappa) - (1 + \beta\kappa)\big] - \mu = 0, \\
F_2(\beta, \kappa, \mu; 0) &= p\big[p(1 + \kappa)^2 - 2\kappa\big] - \mu \beta = 0, \\
F_3(\beta, \kappa, \mu; 0) &= p\big[p(1 + \beta)^2 - 2\beta\big] - \mu \kappa = 0.
\end{aligned}$$
Explicitly (corresponding to $C^{1/2} B_0 C^{1/2} = \mu_0 R_{60^\circ}$ solving $B_0 C B_0 = -\mu_0 B_0^\top$ for $C = \operatorname{diag}(p, p) - p^2\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$),

$$\det J_0(\epsilon) = -\frac{24\,\epsilon^2 (1 - \epsilon)^2}{(1 + \epsilon)^2} \ne 0 \qquad \forall \epsilon \in (0, 1).$$

### Proof of C4 (Elementary Closed-Form Reversed-Cycle 3-Member Collective)

In the 3D clockwise model, each coordinate $\eta_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ ($k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$) is observed only by member $k$ (policy $1$, SNR $h_k^2$) and member $(k-1)\bmod 3$ (policy $2$, SNR $h_k^2$), with pairwise redundancy $I_k = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{(1+h_k^2)^2}{1+2h_k^2} > 0$. Thus $G_C$ is purely pairwise:
$$G_C(\pi) = \sum_{i=0}^2 g_i(\pi_i) + I_0 \mathbf{1}[\pi_0=1, \pi_2=2] + I_1 \mathbf{1}[\pi_1=1, \pi_0=2] + I_2 \mathbf{1}[\pi_2=1, \pi_1=2],$$
where $g_i(0) = 0$ and $g_i(a) = G_C(a_i, 0_{-i}) = -I_{q_C}(\eta_C; o_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}) + \psi^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{B}_i(a)}(q_C)$. By the Peretto Lemma of `C3`, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(\pi) \propto (\prod_{i=0}^2 w_i(\pi_i)) Z_0(\pi_1, \pi_2) Z_1(\pi_2, \pi_0) Z_2(\pi_0, \pi_1)$, where for each $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $k = (i+2)\bmod 3$, member $k$'s policy $1$ interacts with $(\pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3} = 2)$ via $\eta_k$ ($I_k$) and policy $2$ interacts with $(\pi_i = 1)$ via $\eta_i$ ($I_i$), giving
$$Z_k(\pi_i, \pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3}) = w_k(0) + w_k(1) \alpha_k^{\mathbf{1}[\pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3}=2]} + w_k(2) \alpha_i^{\mathbf{1}[\pi_i=1]}.$$
Notice that $Z_k(\pi_i, \pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3})$ depends on $(\pi_i, \pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3})$ solely through the two binary indicators $\mathbf{1}[\pi_i = 1]$ and $\mathbf{1}[\pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3} = 2]$; its four distinct values are $Z_k(0, 0)$, $Z_k(1, 0)$, $Z_k(0, 2)$, and $Z_k(1, 2)$, satisfying
$$Z_k(1, 2) Z_k(0, 0) - Z_k(1, 0) Z_k(0, 2) = -(1 - \alpha_i)(1 - \alpha_k) w_k(1) w_k(2) < 0$$
(since $\alpha_i, \alpha_k \in (0, 1)$ and $w_k(1), w_k(2) > 0$). Hence $\tilde{J}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3} := -\ln\frac{Z_k(1, 2) Z_k(0, 0)}{Z_k(1, 0) Z_k(0, 2)} > 0$ for each $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, and we have the exact identity on $\{0, 1, 2\}^2$:
$$Z_k(\pi_i, \pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3}) = Z_k(0, 0) \left(\frac{Z_k(1, 0)}{Z_k(0, 0)}\right)^{\mathbf{1}[\pi_i = 1]} \left(\frac{Z_k(0, 2)}{Z_k(0, 0)}\right)^{\mathbf{1}[\pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3} = 2]} \exp\!\Big(-\tilde{J}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3}\,\mathbf{1}[\pi_i = 1,\, \pi_{(i+1)\bmod 3} = 2]\Big).$$
Multiplying over $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ yields
$$\ln \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(\pi) = \mathrm{const} + \sum_{i=0}^2 \tilde{h}_i(\pi_i) - \tilde{J}_{01}\mathbf{1}[\pi_0=1, \pi_1=2] - \tilde{J}_{12}\mathbf{1}[\pi_1=1, \pi_2=2] - \tilde{J}_{20}\mathbf{1}[\pi_2=1, \pi_0=2],$$
with explicit single-member log-weights (for each $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$):
$$\tilde{h}_i(0) = \ln w_i(0), \qquad \tilde{h}_i(1) = \ln w_i(1) + \ln \frac{Z_{(i+2)\bmod 3}(1, 0)}{Z_{(i+2)\bmod 3}(0, 0)}, \qquad \tilde{h}_i(2) = \ln w_i(2) + \ln \frac{Z_{(i+1)\bmod 3}(0, 2)}{Z_{(i+1)\bmod 3}(0, 0)}.$$
Now fix any $\gamma_C > 0$. On three independent coordinates $\tilde{\eta}_{01}, \tilde{\eta}_{12}, \tilde{\eta}_{20} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, let member $i$'s policy $1$ observe $\tilde{\eta}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3}$ with SNR $\tilde{s}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3} > 0$ and policy $2$ observe $\tilde{\eta}_{(i-1)\bmod 3, i}$ with SNR $\tilde{s}_{(i-1)\bmod 3, i} > 0$, where $\tilde{s}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3}$ is the unique positive root of $\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{(1+s)^2}{1+2s} = \tilde{J}_{i, (i+1)\bmod 3} / \gamma_C$ (explicitly $(e^{2\tilde{J}/\gamma_C} - 1) + \sqrt{(e^{2\tilde{J}/\gamma_C} - 1)^2 + (e^{2\tilde{J}/\gamma_C} - 1)}$), with unit slot noise $\tilde{R}_s = 1$ and normalized Gaussian slot-product preferences $\tilde{p}_s = \mathcal{N}(0, 1 + \tilde{s}_s)$ (finite pragmatic term $\tilde{\psi}_s = \frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e(1 + \tilde{s}_s))$). Then
$$\tilde{G}_C(\pi) = \sum_{i=0}^2 \tilde{G}_C((\pi_i)_i, 0_{-i}) + \frac{1}{\gamma_C}\Big(\tilde{J}_{01}\mathbf{1}[\pi_0=1, \pi_1=2] + \tilde{J}_{12}\mathbf{1}[\pi_1=1, \pi_2=2] + \tilde{J}_{20}\mathbf{1}[\pi_2=1, \pi_0=2]\Big).$$
Defining the explicit full-support product habits $\tilde{E}_i(a) \propto \exp\big(\tilde{h}_i(a) + \gamma_C \tilde{G}_C(a_i, 0_{-i})\big) > 0$ ($a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$) cancels the single-member terms of $-\gamma_C \tilde{G}_C$ and reproduces $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma) = \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma_C; \tilde{E}, \tilde{G}_C)$ identically.

Finally, on the original clockwise pair $(\pi_0 = 1, \pi_2 = 2)$ conditioned on $\pi_1 = 0$, the conditional log odds ratio of $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)$ is
$$\ln \frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(1, 0, 2)\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(0, 0, 0)}{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(1, 0, 0)\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)(0, 0, 2)} = 0$$
(because $\tilde{h}_0, \tilde{h}_2$ cancel and all three reversed-cycle indicators vanish on $(1, 0, 2), (1, 0, 0), (0, 0, 2), (0, 0, 0)$), whereas under $\mathbb{P}_C(\gamma'; E, G_C)$ the same conditional log odds ratio equals $-\gamma' I_0 < 0$ for every $\gamma' > 0$. Hence $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma) \ne \mathbb{P}_C(\gamma'; E, G_C)$ for every $\gamma' > 0$. $\square$

## Scope & Open Questions

- **Exact scope of `conj:agy-synchronous`:**
  - Under the **same-precision reading** ($\gamma_C = \gamma$, matching `(P3)` literally), `conj:agy-synchronous` is completely proved for all $n \ge 2$ and all finite policy sets (`C1`).
  - Under the **free-precision / as-if-agency reading** (whether $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sync}}^\infty(\gamma)$ reproduces *some* redundant group posterior $\prod_i \tilde{E}_i e^{-\gamma_C \tilde{G}_C}$ with $\gamma_C > 0$, mirroring the second sentence of `prop:agy-synchronous`), the statement holds for all binary-policy collectives (`C2`) and fails as soon as $|\mathcal{A}_i| \ge 3$, even when $(q_C, \mathfrak{B}, \psi, E)$ are all held fixed and only $\gamma_C > 0$ varies (`C3`), and in elementary closed form for all $\gamma > 0$ when $\tilde{G}_C$ is allowed to vary (`C4`).
- **Remaining open question (general finite-state fixed points of parallel Gibbs):** Outside the epistemic sign structure of `C1` (which uses $p_c(a, 0) p_c(0, b) \ge p_c(0, 0) p_c(a, b)$), it remains open whether *any* strictly positive non-product joint distribution $P$ on a finite product space $\prod_{i=1}^n \mathcal{A}_i$ ($n \ge 3$) with mixed-sign interactions can satisfy $P T_{\mathrm{sync}}(P) = P$. (For multivariate Gaussian $X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Lambda^{-1})$, the residual covariance identity $\operatorname{Cov}(Y) - \operatorname{Cov}(X) = D_\Lambda^{-1}(\Lambda - D_\Lambda) D_\Lambda^{-1}$ settles this immediately in the negative; numerical searches on $\{0, 1\}^3$, $\{0, 1\}^2 \times \{0, 1, 2\}$, and $\{0, 1\}^4$ found no strictly positive non-product fixed point.)
