## Failed routes

### R1: Assuming pure Crawford--Sobel additive bias ($W = I, b \neq 0$) induces babbling in Stackelberg commitment

- Tried: Checking whether additive bias $b \neq 0$ drives the sender toward silencing $D = -H$ when the receiver learns with an intercept.
- Failed because: Under with-intercept absorption (`prop:lrn-absorption`(a,c)), $\mathbb{E}[\hat m] = m_i$ is invariant to $(D, d, \Sigma_\zeta)$, so $b$ enters only through the constant $J_S^{\mathrm{silence}} = \|b\|_{M_S}^2 + \operatorname{tr}(M_S P)$, while $\Gamma_S = -M_S \preceq 0$ makes the sender strictly prefer infinite amplification $\|H+D\| \to \infty$ (full revelation) rather than babbling. Babbling arises from $b \neq 0$ only in simultaneous-move Nash equilibrium (C3), or in Stackelberg when $W$ has $\Gamma_S \succeq 0$ (e.g. $w \le 1/2$).
- Reopen if: The receiver uses a non-absorbing rule (see C5 for no-intercept) or nonlinear strategies outside `prop:lrn-absorption`.

### R2: Assuming $\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$ is unique in every linear-Gaussian Nash equilibrium

## Appendix A: Complete Self-Contained Derivations (Claims C1--C6)

### Proof of C1
1. **Loss identity and image $\mathcal{X}_{r_c, K}$.** Set $\tilde\eta := \eta - m_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, P)$ and $\tilde m := \hat m - m_i = \mathsf{K}_{A,\Sigma_\zeta}(A\tilde\eta + \zeta + \nu)$. Because $y - d - A m_i = A\tilde\eta + \zeta + \nu$ does not involve $d$, $J_S$ is independent of $d$. Decomposing $\hat m - (W\eta + b) = ((I-W)m_i - b) + (\tilde m - W\tilde\eta)$ into deterministic mean and centered fluctuation gives
   $$J_S = \|(I-W)m_i - b\|_{M_S}^2 + \operatorname{tr}(M_S \operatorname{Cov}(\tilde m)) - 2\operatorname{tr}(M_S \operatorname{Cov}(\tilde m, \tilde\eta) W^\top) + \operatorname{tr}(M_S W P W^\top).$$
   With $S = A P A^\top + R + \Sigma_\zeta \succ 0$, direct expansion gives $\operatorname{Cov}(\tilde m) = \mathsf{K}_{A,\Sigma_\zeta} S \mathsf{K}_{A,\Sigma_\zeta}^\top = P A^\top S^{-1} A P = \Sigma_{\hat m}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(\tilde m, \tilde\eta) = \mathsf{K}_{A,\Sigma_\zeta} A P = \Sigma_{\hat m}$. By symmetry of $\Sigma_{\hat m}$, $2\operatorname{tr}(M_S \Sigma_{\hat m} W^\top) = \operatorname{tr}((M_S W + W^\top M_S)\Sigma_{\hat m})$, so $J_S = J_S^{\mathrm{silence}} + \operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_S \Sigma_{\hat m}) = J_S^{\mathrm{silence}} + \operatorname{tr}(\tilde\Gamma_S X)$. Writing $R_\zeta = R + \Sigma_\zeta \succ 0$ and $\tilde A = R_\zeta^{-1/2} A P^{1/2}$ with SVD $\tilde A = U \Sigma V^\top$ ($s_1 \ge \dots \ge s_r \ge 0$), $X = \tilde A^\top(\tilde A\tilde A^\top + I_{r_c})^{-1}\tilde A = V \operatorname{diag}(\frac{s_1^2}{1+s_1^2}, \dots, \frac{s_r^2}{1+s_r^2}, 0, \dots, 0) V^\top \in \mathcal{X}_{r_c, K}$. Conversely, any $X = V\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\dots,t_r,0,\dots,0)V^\top \in \mathcal{X}_{r_c, K}$ is realized by $s_j = \sqrt{t_j/(1-t_j)}$, $\Sigma_\zeta = 0$, $A = R^{1/2} U \Sigma V^\top P^{-1/2}$.
2. **Ky Fan infimum and attainability.** For $X \in \mathcal{X}_{r_c, K}$ with eigenvalues $1 > t_1 \ge \dots \ge t_r \ge 0$, the von Neumann / Ky Fan trace inequality gives $\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\Gamma_S X) \ge \sum_{k=1}^r \lambda_k(\tilde\Gamma_S) t_k \ge \sum_{k=1}^{\min(r, n_-)} \lambda_k(\tilde\Gamma_S)$, strictly when $n_- \ge 1$ because $t_1 < 1$. Setting $q = \min(r, n_-)$, $A_a = a\sum_{k=1}^q u_k v_k^\top P^{-1/2}$, $\Sigma_\zeta = 0$ gives $X(A_a, 0) = \sum_{k=1}^q \frac{a^2}{a^2 + \rho_k} v_k v_k^\top$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\Gamma_S X(A_a, 0)) - \sum_{k=1}^q \lambda_k(\tilde\Gamma_S) = \sum_{k=1}^q (-\lambda_k(\tilde\Gamma_S))\frac{\rho_k}{a^2+\rho_k} = O(a^{-2}) \to 0$.
3. **Dichotomy and selective disclosure.** If $n_- = 0$, $\tilde\Gamma_S \succeq 0$, so $\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\Gamma_S X) \ge 0$ with equality iff $\operatorname{col}(X) \subseteq \ker(\tilde\Gamma_S) \iff \operatorname{row}(A P^{1/2}) \subseteq \ker(\tilde\Gamma_S) \iff \Gamma_S P A^\top = 0$ (attained by $A = 0$, i.e. $D = -H$). If $0 < n_- < r$, any minimizing sequence $X_m$ satisfies $\sum_{\lambda_k < 0}(-\lambda_k)(1 - v_k^\top X_m v_k) + \sum_{\lambda_k > 0} \lambda_k (v_k^\top X_m v_k) \to 0$, forcing $v_k^\top X_m v_k \to 1$ on $V_-$ and $\to 0$ on $V_+$, so $X_m \to \Pi_{V_-}$ on $V_- \oplus V_+$.
4. **Gradient at honesty.** By Woodbury, $\Sigma_{\hat m}(A, 0) = P - (P^{-1} + A^\top R^{-1} A)^{-1}$, so $\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{\hat m}[\delta A] = P^+_A(\delta A^\top R^{-1} A + A^\top R^{-1}\delta A) P^+_A$ and $\mathrm{d}J_S[\delta A] = 2\operatorname{tr}(P^+_A \Gamma_S P^+_A A^\top R^{-1}\delta A)$. Evaluating at $A = H$ gives $\nabla_D J_S|_{D=0,\Sigma_\zeta=0} = 2 R^{-1} H P^+ \Gamma_S P^+$. Since $\nabla_D(\lambda_D\|D\|_F^2)|_{D=0} = 0$, stationarity at $D = 0$ holds iff $H P^+ \Gamma_S P^+ = 0$. $\square$

### Proof of C2
Put $R_\zeta = R + \Sigma_\zeta \succeq R \succ 0$ and $\tilde A_\zeta = R_\zeta^{-1/2} A P^{1/2}$ with singular values $s_1 \ge \dots \ge s_r \ge 0$. By von Neumann's trace inequality on $\tilde\Gamma_S$ and $X(A, \Sigma_\zeta)$ and on $R_\zeta$ and $\tilde A_\zeta \tilde A_\zeta^\top$, together with Weyl's monotonicity $\rho_j(R_\zeta) \ge \rho_j(R)$,
$$J_S^{\mathrm{pow}}(A - H, d, \Sigma_\zeta) \ge J_S^{\mathrm{silence}} + \lambda\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\zeta) + \sum_{j=1}^r \Bigl(\lambda_j(\tilde\Gamma_S)\frac{s_j^2}{1+s_j^2} + \lambda\rho_j(R) s_j^2\Bigr).$$
Each scalar summand $g_j(x) = \lambda_j x/(1+x) + \lambda\rho_j x$ on $x \ge 0$ is minimized uniquely at $x^\ast = s_j^{2\ast} = [\sqrt{\max(-\lambda_j, 0)/(\lambda\rho_j)} - 1]_+$ (strictly convex when $\lambda_j < 0$; strictly increasing when $\lambda_j \ge 0$), with minimum value $-([\sqrt{\max(-\lambda_j, 0)} - \sqrt{\lambda\rho_j}]_+)^2$. Because $\lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_r$ and $0 < \rho_1 \le \dots \le \rho_r$, $s_1^{2\ast} \ge \dots \ge s_r^{2\ast} \ge 0$ automatically obeys the singular-value ordering. Taking $\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$ and $A^\ast = \sum_{j=1}^r \sqrt{\rho_j s_j^{2\ast}}\, u_j v_j^\top P^{-1/2}$ simultaneously attains equality in both trace inequalities and in $\lambda\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\zeta) \ge 0$. Any global minimizer must satisfy $\lambda\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\zeta^\ast) = 0 \implies \Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$, and when $k^\ast = 0$ all $s_j^\ast = 0 \implies A^\ast = 0$ uniquely. $\square$

### Proof of C3
1. **One-shot BR.** Expanding $\hat m(y) - (W\eta + b) = \mu_e + (\hat{\mathsf{K}} A - W)(\eta - m_i) + \hat{\mathsf{K}}(\zeta + \nu)$ gives $J_S^{\mathrm{fix}} = \|\mu_e\|_{M_S}^2 + \operatorname{tr}(M_S(\hat{\mathsf{K}} A - W)P(\hat{\mathsf{K}} A - W)^\top) + \operatorname{tr}(M_S\hat{\mathsf{K}}(\Sigma_\zeta + R)\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\top)$, jointly convex in $(A, d, \Sigma_\zeta)$. When $\operatorname{rank}(M_S^{1/2}\hat{\mathsf{K}}) = r_c$, $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\top M_S \hat{\mathsf{K}} \succ 0$ forces $\Sigma_\zeta^{\mathrm{BR}} = 0$, and the FOCs $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\top M_S \mu_e = 0$ and $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\top M_S(\hat{\mathsf{K}} A - W)P = 0$ solve uniquely for $(A^{\mathrm{BR}}, d^{\mathrm{BR}})$. In the scalar aligned case against honest $\mathsf{K}^\ast = HP/(H^2P+R)$, $A^{\mathrm{BR}} = 1/\mathsf{K}^\ast = H + R/(HP)$ gives $D^{\mathrm{BR}} = R/(HP)$ and $J_S^{\mathrm{fix}} = M_S(\mathsf{K}^\ast)^2 R < M_S P^+$.
2. **Vector Nash characterization ($M_S \succ 0$).** By joint convexity, $(A^\ast, d^\ast, \Sigma_\zeta^\ast)$ minimizes $J_S^{\mathrm{fix}}(\cdot \mid A^\ast, d^\ast, \hat{\mathsf{K}}^\ast)$ iff (N1) $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^{\ast\top} M_S((I-W)m_i - b) = 0$, (N2) $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^{\ast\top} M_S(\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\ast A^\ast - W) = 0$, and (N3) $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\ast \Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$.
   - *(N3) and invariance:* Put $M_0 = A^\ast P A^{\ast\top} + R \succ 0$, $S^\ast = M_0 + \Sigma_\zeta^\ast \succ 0$. If $A^{\ast\top} S^{\ast -1}\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$, set $Z = S^{\ast -1}\Sigma_\zeta^\ast$; then $A^{\ast\top} Z = 0 \implies M_0 Z = R Z$, so $\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = (R + \Sigma_\zeta^\ast)Z \implies \Sigma_\zeta^\ast(I-Z) = R Z$. Since $I - Z = S^{\ast -1} M_0$ is invertible, $\operatorname{col}(\Sigma_\zeta^\ast) = R\operatorname{col}(Z) \subseteq R\ker(A^{\ast\top})$. Conversely, if $\operatorname{col}(\Sigma_\zeta^\ast) \subseteq R\ker(A^{\ast\top})$, then $S^\ast(\ker(A^{\ast\top})) \subseteq R\ker(A^{\ast\top})$; equality of dimensions gives $S^{\ast -1}(R\ker(A^{\ast\top})) = \ker(A^{\ast\top})$, hence $A^{\ast\top} S^{\ast -1}\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$. Moreover, for any $y \in \mathbb{R}^{r_c}$, $\Sigma_\zeta^\ast y = R v = M_0 v$ ($v \in \ker(A^{\ast\top})$) gives $y^\top \Sigma_\zeta^\ast M_0^{-1} A^\ast = v^\top A^\ast = 0$, so $S^\ast M_0^{-1} A^\ast = A^\ast \implies S^{\ast -1} A^\ast = M_0^{-1} A^\ast$ and $\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\ast = \mathsf{K}_{A^\ast, 0}$.
   - *(N1)--(N2):* With $\mathcal{U} = \operatorname{col}(\hat{\mathsf{K}}^\ast) = \operatorname{col}(\Sigma_{\hat m})$ of dimension $r' = \operatorname{rank}(A^\ast) \ge 1$, pick $U_b \in \mathbb{R}^{K\times r'}$ with $U_b^\top P^{-1} U_b = I_{r'}$, $V_1 = P^{-1/2} U_b$, $G_S = U_b^\top M_S U_b \succ 0$. Then $\Sigma_{\hat m} = U_b \Omega U_b^\top$ ($0 \prec \Omega \prec I_{r'}$ symmetric). (N2) is $G_S \Omega U_b^\top = U_b^\top M_S W P$, which holds iff $P W^\top M_S \mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$ and $\Omega = G_S^{-1}(U_b^\top M_S W U_b)$ (multiply right by $P^{-1} U_b$; conversely, $P W^\top M_S U_b = U_b B \implies U_b^\top M_S W P = B^\top U_b^\top$ and $B^\top = U_b^\top M_S W U_b = G_S \Omega$). The SVD reconstruction of $A^\ast = R^{1/2} Q_L \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{t_j/(1-t_j)}) Q_R^\top U_b^\top P^{-1}$ matches $X = V_1 \Omega V_1^\top$ by direct multiplication.
3. **Scalar case.** Specializing $K = r_c = 1$ gives $U_b = \sqrt{P}, \Omega = w$, yielding (i) $(1-w)m_i = b$, (ii) $w \in (0, 1)$, $A^\ast = \pm\sqrt{\frac{w}{1-w}\frac{R}{P}}$, (iii) $\Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$, and $J_S^{\mathrm{Nash}} = J_S^{\mathrm{silence}} + M_S w(1-2w)P$. $\square$

### Proof of C4
By C3(1), $A_{n+1} = w/\hat{\mathsf{K}}_n = w(A_n + R/(A_n P))$ and $d_{n+1} - d_n = \delta_b/\hat{\mathsf{K}}_n - (A_{n+1} - A_n)m_i$, which telescopes to $d_n = d_0 - (A_n - A_0)m_i + \delta_b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\hat{\mathsf{K}}_k^{-1}$.
- $w = 0$: $A_1 = 0$.
- $0 < w < 1$: $\operatorname{sgn}(A_n) = \operatorname{sgn}(A_0)$, and $u_n := A_n/A^\ast > 0$ obeys $u_{n+1} = w u_n + (1-w)u_n^{-1}$. For $u \neq 1$, $\min(u, u^{-1}) < w u + (1-w)u^{-1} < \max(u, u^{-1})$, so $|\ln u_{n+1}| < |\ln u_n|$ strictly. By compactness of $[e^{-|\ln u_0|}, e^{|\ln u_0|}]$ and continuity, $|\ln u_n| \to 0$, so $A_n \to A^\ast$; $f'(A^\ast) = w(1 - (1-w)/w) = 2w - 1$ (and $f''(A^\ast) \neq 0$ at $w=1/2$). Since $\hat{\mathsf{K}}_n \to \mathsf{K}^\ast \neq 0$, $d_{n+1} - d_n \to \delta_b/\mathsf{K}^\ast$.
- $-1 < w < 0$: $\operatorname{sgn}(A_{n+1}) = -\operatorname{sgn}(A_n)$ while $|A_n|$ obeys the $0 < |w| < 1$ recursion, converging to $A_\#$.
- $w = 1$: $A_{k+1}^2 = A_k^2 + \frac{2R}{P} + \frac{R^2}{A_k^2 P^2} \ge A_0^2 + \frac{2R}{P}(k+1)$, so $\frac{R^2}{A_k^2 P^2} \le \frac{R}{2Pk}$ ($k \ge 1$), giving $A_n^2 = \frac{2R}{P} n + O(\ln n)$ and $|A_n| = \sqrt{\frac{2R}{P} n} + O(\frac{\ln n}{\sqrt{n}})$. Since $\hat{\mathsf{K}}_k^{-1} = A_{k+1}$, summing gives $d_n = d_0 - (A_n - A_0)m_i + b\sum_{k=1}^n A_k = \operatorname{sgn}(A_0)\frac{2}{3} b\sqrt{\frac{2R}{P}} n^{3/2} + O(n^{1/2}\ln n)$ for $b \neq 0$.

- $w \le -1$: alternating signs with magnitudes obeying $|w| \ge 1$. $\square$

### Proof of C5
1. **Mean bias & residual moments.** Sherman--Morrison on $M_2 = C_\phi + \bar m\bar m^\top$ gives $\alpha_0 = 1 - \bar m^\top M_2^{-1}\bar m = (1 + \bar m^\top C_\phi^{-1}\bar m)^{-1} \in (0, 1]$. On a test round, $y - \hat H_{\mathrm{ni}} m_i = A(\eta - m_i) + \beta_i d + \zeta + \nu$, so $\mathbb{E}[\hat m_{\mathrm{ni}}] = m_i + \beta_i \hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\mathrm{ni}} d$. On grounded rounds, $e_t = d(1 - \bar m^\top M_2^{-1}\phi_t) + \zeta_t + \nu_t$ has $\mathbb{E}[e_t] = \alpha_0 d$ and $\mathbb{E}[(1 - \bar m^\top M_2^{-1}\phi_t)^2] = 1 - 2\bar m^\top M_2^{-1}\bar m + \bar m^\top M_2^{-1} M_2 M_2^{-1}\bar m = \alpha_0$, hence $\mathbb{E}[e_t e_t^\top] = R + \Sigma_\zeta + \alpha_0 d d^\top$.
2. **Known-$R$ breakdown.** At $\bar m = 0$, $\hat H_{\mathrm{ni}} = A$, $\beta_i = 1$, $\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\mathrm{ni}} = \mathsf{K}_{A,0}$. In scalar Nash ($\bar m = m_i = 0, w \in (0, 1)$), $\mathsf{K}_{A^\ast,0} A^\ast = w$ and $\mathsf{K}_{A^\ast,0} d^\ast = b$ satisfy C3(1) while $\hat H_{\mathrm{ni}} = A^\ast$; the receiver's error $(w-1)\eta + \mathsf{K}_{A^\ast,0}\nu + b$ has MSE $(1-w)^2 P + w(1-w)P + b^2 = (1-w)P + b^2 > P \iff b^2 > w P$.
3. **Empirical-$R$ half-sigma cap and sharpness.** Set $B = \hat H_{\mathrm{ni}} P^{1/2}$, $\tilde d = \sqrt{\alpha_0}\, d$, $S_0 = B B^\top + R + \Sigma_\zeta \succ B B^\top$. By Sherman--Morrison, $(S_0 + \tilde d\tilde d^\top)^{-1}\tilde d = S_0^{-1}\tilde d/(1 + t)$ ($t := \tilde d^\top S_0^{-1}\tilde d \ge 0$), so $\|\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\mathrm{ni}}\tilde d\|_{P^{-1}}^2 = \frac{\tilde d^\top S_0^{-1} B B^\top S_0^{-1}\tilde d}{(1+t)^2} < \frac{t}{(1+t)^2} \le \frac{1}{4}$ (strict since $B B^\top \prec S_0$ for $\tilde d \neq 0$, and $0 < 1/4$ for $\tilde d = 0$). Scaling by $|\beta_i|/\sqrt{\alpha_0}$ gives the bound. For sharpness, $B_a = a u v^\top$, $\tilde d_a = a u$ (unit $u, v$, $A_a = \hat H_a - d_a\bar m^\top M_2^{-1}$) gives $t_a = \frac{a^2 u^\top R^{-1} u}{1 + a^2 u^\top R^{-1} u} \to 1$ and $\|\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\mathrm{ni}}\tilde d_a\|_{P^{-1}} = \frac{t_a}{1 + t_a} \to \frac{1}{2}$. $\square$

### Proof of C6
1. **Observational equivalence.** Conditioned on $\phi_t = \bar m + \tilde\phi_t$ ($\tilde\phi_t = \mathcal{P}_C\tilde\phi_t$ a.s.), $y_t \sim \mathcal{N}((H+D)\mathcal{P}_C\tilde\phi_t + (H+D)\bar m + d,\, R + \Sigma_\zeta)$. Matching conditional means a.s. and conditional covariances yields $(H+D)\bar m + d = (H'+D')\bar m + d'$ (by taking $\mathbb{E}$) and $((H+D) - (H'+D'))\mathcal{P}_C = 0$ (by taking covariance), plus $R + \Sigma_\zeta = R' + \Sigma_\zeta'$.
2. **Minimal references.** Immediate from Part 1: $d = ((H+D)\bar m + d) - (H+D)\mathcal{P}_C\bar m$ when $\bar m \in \operatorname{col}(C_\phi)$; $(H, 0, 0, R+\Sigma_\zeta, 0)$ and $(H+D, 0, 0, R, 0)$ witness non-identifiability without references on $R$ and $H$; $D M_2 D^\top = 0 \implies D\mathcal{P}_C = 0, D\bar m = 0$.
3. **Exact KL divergence.** By the chain rule (same marginal $\phi_t$) and the Gaussian KL formula, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{D,d,\Sigma_\zeta}\Vert P_{0,0,0}) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl[\mathbb{E}_\phi[(D\phi + d)^\top R^{-1}(D\phi + d)] + \operatorname{tr}(R^{-1}\Sigma_\zeta) - \ln\det(I_{r_c} + R^{-1}\Sigma_\zeta)\bigr]$, which expands to the displayed sum of three nonnegative terms ($\sigma - \ln(1+\sigma) \ge 0$ on $[0, \infty)$ with equality iff $\sigma = 0$).
4. **KL-constrained scalar tradeoff.** For $w > 1/2$, $d^\ast = 0, \Sigma_\zeta^\ast = 0$ strictly maximize $|H+D|^2 P^2/((H+D)^2 P + R + \Sigma_\zeta)$ under $D_{\mathrm{KL}} \le \delta$, leaving $|D| \le \sqrt{2R\delta/P}$ uniquely maximized at $D^\ast = \operatorname{sgn}(H)\sqrt{2R\delta/P}$. For $w < 1/2$ (WLOG $H > 0$), $h(s) = s - \ln(1+s) - \frac{s^2}{2(1+s)} \ge 0$ on $s \ge 0$ bounds $s = \Sigma_\zeta/R = O(\sqrt{\delta})$ along with $D = O(\sqrt{\delta})$. Putting $x = -D\sqrt{P/(2R)}$, $y = \Sigma_\zeta/(2R) \ge 0$, we have $g(x, y) := x^2 + \frac{1}{2}(2y - \ln(1+2y)) = x^2 + y^2 + O(\delta^{3/2})$ and $f(0,0) - f(D, \Sigma_\zeta) = c_x x + c_y y + O(\delta)$ with $c_x = \frac{2P\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{SNR}_0}}{(1+\mathrm{SNR}_0)^2}$, $c_y = \frac{2P\,\mathrm{SNR}_0}{(1+\mathrm{SNR}_0)^2}$. For small $\delta$, the constraint is active with $d^\ast = 0$ and $(x^\ast, y^\ast)/\sqrt{\delta} \to (c_x, c_y)/\sqrt{c_x^2 + c_y^2}$ in the open quadrant $x^\ast, y^\ast > 0$; KKT $\nabla(-f) = \mu\nabla g$ gives $c_x + O(\sqrt{\delta}) = 2\mu x^\ast$ and $c_y + O(\sqrt{\delta}) = 2\mu y^\ast/(1+2y^\ast)$, whence $y^\ast/x^\ast = \sqrt{\mathrm{SNR}_0/2} + O(\sqrt{\delta})$, yielding the loss reduction $(1-2w)\sqrt{c_x^2 + c_y^2}\sqrt{\delta} + O(\delta)$, relative-perturbation ratio $\to \mathrm{SNR}_0$, and KL-share ratio $\to \mathrm{SNR}_0/2$. $\square$
