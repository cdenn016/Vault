## Claims

### C1: Exact Existence Threshold, Uniqueness, Global Weak Convergence, and Gaussian-Mixture Stationary Law for General $n \ge 2$ and Arbitrary $d_i \ge 1$

- Statement: In the setting of `def:obj-quadratic` ($n \ge 2$ members, arbitrary act dimensions $d_1, \dots, d_n \ge 1$, $d_u = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i$, $K_{ii} = K_{ii}^\top \succ 0$ for all $i$, arbitrary $K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_u}$ without assuming symmetry or invertibility, arbitrary $\mathsf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, and $\gamma > 0$), let $\mathsf{A}_i = I - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K$, $\omega = (I_0, I_1, \dots) \sim \operatorname{Unif}\{1, \dots, n\}^{\otimes \mathbb{N}_0}$, $P_0(\omega) = I$, $P_k(\omega) = \mathsf{A}_{I_0} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{I_{k-1}}$ ($k \ge 1$), and define the top Lyapunov exponent
  $$\lambda_1 := \inf_{t \ge 1} \frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln \|P_t(\omega)\|\bigr] = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln \|P_t(\omega)\|\bigr] \in [-\infty, \infty).$$
  Then $\lambda_1$ is well-defined, independent of the choice of norm on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_u}$, depends on $K$ only through $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$, and is independent of $\mathsf{h}$ and $\gamma$. Moreover:
  1. Heat-bath deliberation admits a stationary probability law (with no moment assumption) **if and only if** $\lambda_1 < 0$. In particular, $\lambda_1 < 0$ implies $K$ is invertible (so $u^{\mathrm{N}} = -K^{-1}\mathsf{h}$ exists), and existence depends on $K$ only through $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$ — equivalently, it is invariant under replacing $K$ by any admissible $K' = K'_{\mathrm{d}} K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$ with $K'_{\mathrm{d}} = \operatorname{blockdiag}(K'_{ii})$, $K'_{ii} = (K'_{ii})^\top \succ 0$ (i.e., $K' = D K$ with $D = \operatorname{blockdiag}(D_{ii})$ satisfying $D_{ii} K_{ii} = (D_{ii} K_{ii})^\top \succ 0$, including $D = \operatorname{blockdiag}(c_i I_{d_i})$ for $c_i > 0$) — and under arbitrary changes of $\mathsf{h}$ and $\gamma > 0$.
  2. Whenever $\lambda_1 < 0$, the stationary probability law $\mu$ is **unique**, every initial law $\nu_0$ on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ satisfies $\nu_t \Rightarrow \mu$ weakly as $t \to \infty$, and $\mu$ is the exact centered-at-$u^{\mathrm{N}}$ Gaussian scale mixture
     $$\mu = \mathbb{E}_\omega\bigl[\mathcal{N}\bigl(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \, \Sigma(\omega)\bigr)\bigr], \qquad \Sigma(\omega) := \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^\infty P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} K_{I_k I_k}^{-1} E_{I_k}^\top P_k(\omega)^\top,$$
     where the series converges absolutely in operator norm almost surely, $\Sigma(\omega) \succ 0$ almost surely, $\mu$ is centrally symmetric about $u^{\mathrm{N}}$, and $\mu$ is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ (admitting an everywhere strictly positive Radon–Nikodym density).

### C2: Universal Breaking of Gaussianity for Nonsymmetric $K$ and Identifiability Against Finite Gaussian Mixtures

- Statement:
  1. In the setting of `def:obj-quadratic` for any $n \ge 2$ and any $d_1, \dots, d_n \ge 1$ (without assuming $K$ invertible), heat-bath deliberation admits a Gaussian stationary probability law **if and only if** $K = K^\top \succ 0$, in which case $\mu = \mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, (\gamma K)^{-1})$ is the unique stationary law and is reversible. Consequently, for every nonsymmetric interaction matrix ($K_{\mathrm{a}} \ne 0$), whenever a stationary law exists, Gaussianity strictly fails.
  2. **(Identifiability Lemma.)** Let $V$ be a $(0, \infty)$-valued random variable (with no moment or boundedness assumption). If the centered Gaussian scale mixture $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}(0, V)]$ equals a finite Gaussian mixture $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j \mathcal{N}(m_j, \sigma_j^2)$ ($M \ge 1$, $w_j > 0$, $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j = 1$, $m_j \in \mathbb{R}$, $\sigma_j^2 \ge 0$), then $m_j = 0$ for every $j$ and $\operatorname{law}(V) = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j \delta_{\sigma_j^2}$; in particular, $\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{law}(V))$ has cardinality at most $M$. Consequently, when $\lambda_1 < 0$, if there exists $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ such that $v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v$ has infinite support (e.g. takes infinitely many distinct values with positive probability), then $\mu = \mathbb{E}_\omega[\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))]$ is **not** a finite Gaussian mixture of any order.

### C6: Exact Directed $n$-Cycle Characterization and Infinite-Gaussian-Mixture Structure Even Under $\rho(\mathcal{T}) < 1$ with Exact Gaussian 1D Marginals

## Derivations

### Standing Setup and Notation

Throughout, we work in the setting of `def:obj-quadratic` (`ext3_obj.tex`, lines 7–13) and `eq:obj-update` (`appext3_obj.tex`, lines 3–10): $n \ge 2$ members have acts $u_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_i}$ ($d_i \ge 1$, $d_u = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i$), $E_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_i}$ is the coordinate inclusion ($u_i = E_i^\top u$, $\sum_{i=1}^n E_i E_i^\top = I$), $K = (K_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_u}$ has symmetric positive definite diagonal blocks $K_{ii} = K_{ii}^\top \succ 0$, $K_{\mathrm{d}} = \operatorname{blockdiag}(K_{11}, \dots, K_{nn}) \succ 0$, $\mathsf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, and $\gamma > 0$. By `appext3_obj.tex` (line 121, proof of `prop:obj-identification`(a)), every such pair $(K, \mathsf{h})$ is realized by a quadratic deliberation.

The heat-bath kernel is $T = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n T_i$, where $T_i$ updates $u$ via
$$u^+ = \mathsf{A}_i u + \mathsf{b}_i + E_i \xi_i, \qquad \mathsf{A}_i := I - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K, \qquad \mathsf{b}_i := -E_i K_{ii}^{-1} \mathsf{h}_i, \qquad \xi_i \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(0, (\gamma K_{ii})^{-1}\bigr)$$
independently of $u$. Note two elementary algebraic identities (derived here):
1. $\mathsf{A}_i E_i = E_i - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} (E_i^\top K E_i) = E_i - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} K_{ii} = 0$.
2. $\mathsf{A}_i^2 = (I - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K) \mathsf{A}_i = \mathsf{A}_i - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K + E_i K_{ii}^{-1} (E_i^\top K E_i) K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K = \mathsf{A}_i$.
3. Whenever $K$ is invertible and $u^{\mathrm{N}} = -K^{-1}\mathsf{h}$, $\mathsf{A}_i u^{\mathrm{N}} + \mathsf{b}_i = u^{\mathrm{N}} - E_i K_{ii}^{-1}(E_i^\top K u^{\mathrm{N}} + \mathsf{h}_i) = u^{\mathrm{N}}$, so $u^+ - u^{\mathrm{N}} = \mathsf{A}_i(u - u^{\mathrm{N}}) + E_i \xi_i$.

### Proof of C1

**Step 1 (Well-definedness, norm-independence, invariance, and $K$-invertibility).**
Let $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ be the spectral norm on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_u}$, $M_{\max} := \max_{1 \le i \le n} \|\mathsf{A}_i\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, and $\tilde M := \max(1, M_{\max})$. Since $\omega = (I_0, I_1, \dots) \sim \operatorname{Unif}\{1, \dots, n\}^{\otimes \mathbb{N}_0}$ is i.i.d., for every $t \ge 1$ we have $\|P_t(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \tilde M^t$, so $a_t := \mathbb{E}[\ln \|P_t(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}}] \in [-\infty, t \ln \tilde M]$ is well-defined (with $\ln 0 = -\infty$). For all $s, t \ge 1$, submultiplicativity gives $\|P_{s+t}(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|P_s(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \|P_t(\theta^s\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ where $(\theta^s\omega)_k = I_{s+k}$ has the same distribution as $\omega$. Taking logarithms and expectations (noting $\ln \|P_r\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le r \ln \tilde M < \infty$, so $+\infty - \infty$ never occurs) yields $a_{s+t} \le a_s + a_t$. By Fekete's subadditive lemma (if $a_{t_0} = -\infty$ for some $t_0$ then $a_t = -\infty$ for all $t \ge t_0$ and $\lim a_t/t = \inf a_t/t = -\infty$; otherwise $a_t \in \mathbb{R}$ for all $t$ and the standard Fekete argument gives $\lim_{t\to\infty} a_t/t = \inf_{t\ge 1} a_t/t \in [-\infty, \infty)$), the limit $\lambda_1 := \lim_{t\to\infty} a_t/t = \inf_{t\ge 1} a_t/t$ exists in $[-\infty, \infty)$. Since any norm $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_u}$ satisfies $c_1 \|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|\cdot\| \le c_2 \|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for constants $0 < c_1 \le c_2 < \infty$, $\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\ln \|P_t\|] = \frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\ln\|P_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}] + O(1/t)$ converges to the same $\lambda_1$. Because $\mathsf{A}_i = I - E_i E_i^\top (K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K)$ depends on $K$ only through $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$ and does not depend on $\mathsf{h}$ or $\gamma$, $\lambda_1$ has the same invariances; in particular, if $K' = K'_{\mathrm{d}} K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$ with $K'_{\mathrm{d}} = \operatorname{blockdiag}(K'_{ii})$ and $K'_{ii} = (K'_{ii})^\top \succ 0$ (equivalently $K' = D K$ with $D = \operatorname{blockdiag}(D_{ii})$ and $D_{ii} K_{ii} = (D_{ii} K_{ii})^\top \succ 0$), then $K'$ has diagonal blocks $(K')_{ii} = K'_{ii} = (K'_{ii})^\top \succ 0$ (hence is admissible) and $(K'_{\mathrm{d}})^{-1} K' = K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$, leaving every $\mathsf{A}_i$ and $\lambda_1$ unchanged.

If $v \in \ker K$ with $\|v\|_2 = 1$, then $\mathsf{A}_i v = (I - E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K) v = v$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$, so $P_t(\omega) v = v$ and $\|P_t(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \ge 1$ everywhere for every $t \ge 1$, giving $\lambda_1 \ge 0$. Contrapositively, $\lambda_1 < 0$ implies $\ker K = \{0\}$, so $K$ is invertible and $u^{\mathrm{N}} = -K^{-1}\mathsf{h}$ exists.

**Step 2 (Sufficiency: $\lambda_1 < 0 \implies$ a.s. exponential decay, explicit stationary mixture $\mu$, uniqueness, global weak convergence).**
Assume $\lambda_1 < 0$. By definition of $\lambda_1 = \inf_{t \ge 1} a_t/t$, choose $m \ge 1$ such that $\mathbb{E}[\ln \|P_m(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}}] < 0$. Partition $\omega$ into i.i.d. length-$m$ blocks $B_r := \mathsf{A}_{I_{rm}} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{I_{(r+1)m-1}}$ ($r = 0, 1, \dots$).
- *Case 2a:* $p_0 := \mathbb{P}(B_0 = 0) > 0$. Then $\mathbb{P}(\forall r < R,\, B_r \ne 0) = (1 - p_0)^R \to 0$, so a.s. there exists a finite $r_0(\omega)$ with $B_{r_0} = 0$, whence $P_k(\omega) = 0$ for all $k \ge (r_0(\omega) + 1)m$.
- *Case 2b:* $\mathbb{P}(B_0 = 0) = 0$. For $L > 0$, set $Y_r^{(L)} := \max(\ln \|B_r\|_{\mathrm{op}}, -L)$. Since $Y_0^{(L)} \le m \ln \tilde M$ and $Y_0^{(L)} \downarrow \ln\|B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ as $L \uparrow \infty$, Monotone Convergence applied to $m \ln \tilde M - Y_0^{(L)} \ge 0$ yields $\lim_{L\to\infty} \mathbb{E}[Y_0^{(L)}] = \mathbb{E}[\ln\|B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}] < 0$. Fix $L > 0$ so that $-\delta_0 := \mathbb{E}[Y_0^{(L)}] < 0$. Because $\{Y_r^{(L)}\}_{r \ge 0}$ are i.i.d. and bounded (hence integrable), by the Strong Law of Large Numbers $\frac{1}{q}\sum_{r=0}^{q-1} Y_r^{(L)} \to -\delta_0$ a.s. Thus for a.e. $\omega$ there exists $q_0(\omega)$ such that for all $q \ge q_0(\omega)$, $\sum_{r=0}^{q-1} \ln \|B_r\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \sum_{r=0}^{q-1} Y_r^{(L)} \le -\frac{1}{2} q \delta_0$. Writing $k = qm + s$ ($0 \le s < m$), submultiplicativity gives $\|P_k(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \tilde M^m e^{-\frac{\delta_0}{2m}(k - m)}$ for all $k \ge q_0(\omega) m$.

In both cases, there exist a deterministic $\eta > 0$ and an a.s. finite random constant $C(\omega) < \infty$ such that $\|P_k(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C(\omega) e^{-\eta k}$ for all $k \ge 0$ a.s.

Let $K_{\max}^{-1} := \max_{1 \le i \le n} \|K_{ii}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. For every $k \ge 0$, $\|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} K_{I_k I_k}^{-1} E_{I_k}^\top P_k(\omega)^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C(\omega)^2 K_{\max}^{-1} e^{-2\eta k}$, so the PSD series
$$\Sigma(\omega) := \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^\infty P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} K_{I_k I_k}^{-1} E_{I_k}^\top P_k(\omega)^\top$$
converges absolutely in operator norm a.s. Let $\xi = (\xi_0, \xi_1, \dots)$ be conditionally independent given $\omega$ with $\xi_k \mid \omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (\gamma K_{I_k I_k})^{-1})$. Conditional on $\omega$, the partial sums $\tilde Z_t(\omega) := \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} \xi_k$ form a centered Gaussian martingale in $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ with $\sup_{t \ge 1} \mathbb{E}[\|\tilde Z_t(\omega)\|_2^2 \mid \omega] = \operatorname{tr}\Sigma(\omega) < \infty$ a.s., so by $L^2$-martingale convergence (equivalently, Kolmogorov's two-series theorem) $\tilde Z_t(\omega) \to \tilde Z_\infty(\omega)$ a.s. (and in $L^2$ given $\omega$), where $\tilde Z_\infty(\omega) \mid \omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma(\omega))$. Define $\mu := \operatorname{law}(u^{\mathrm{N}} + \tilde Z_\infty(\omega)) = \mathbb{E}_\omega[\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))]$.

For any initial law $\nu_0$ on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, let $U_0 \sim \nu_0$ be independent of $(\omega, \xi)$. The forward chain $u_t$ started from $U_0$ satisfies $u_t - u^{\mathrm{N}} = \mathsf{A}_{I_{t-1}} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{I_0}(U_0 - u^{\mathrm{N}}) + \sum_{s=0}^{t-1} \mathsf{A}_{I_{t-1}} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{I_{s+1}} E_{I_s} \xi_s$. Because $((I_0, \xi_0), \dots, (I_{t-1}, \xi_{t-1}))$ is i.i.d. and independent of $U_0$, reversing indices $s \mapsto t - 1 - s$ preserves the joint law and shows that $\operatorname{law}(u_t) = \operatorname{law}(U_t)$, where
$$U_t := u^{\mathrm{N}} + P_t(\omega)(U_0 - u^{\mathrm{N}}) + \tilde Z_t(\omega).$$
As $t \to \infty$, $P_t(\omega)(U_0 - u^{\mathrm{N}}) \to 0$ a.s. and $\tilde Z_t(\omega) \to \tilde Z_\infty(\omega)$ a.s., so $U_t \to u^{\mathrm{N}} + \tilde Z_\infty(\omega)$ a.s., hence $\nu_t = \operatorname{law}(u_t) \Rightarrow \mu$ weakly.

To verify stationarity of $\mu$, observe from the definition of $\Sigma(\omega)$ that
$$\Sigma(\omega) = \frac{1}{\gamma} E_{I_0} K_{I_0 I_0}^{-1} E_{I_0}^\top + \mathsf{A}_{I_0} \Sigma(\theta\omega) \mathsf{A}_{I_0}^\top,$$
where $I_0 \sim \operatorname{Unif}\{1, \dots, n\}$ is independent of $\theta\omega$. If $U \sim \mu$ is independent of $(I_0, \xi_0)$, then conditional on $(I_0, \theta\omega)$, $u^{\mathrm{N}} + \mathsf{A}_{I_0}(U - u^{\mathrm{N}}) + E_{I_0} \xi_0$ has law $\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \mathsf{A}_{I_0}\Sigma(\theta\omega)\mathsf{A}_{I_0}^\top + \gamma^{-1} E_{I_0} K_{I_0 I_0}^{-1} E_{I_0}^\top) = \mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))$. Averaging over $(I_0, \theta\omega)$ gives $\mu T = \mu$. If $\nu$ is any stationary law, starting from $\nu_0 = \nu$ gives $\nu = \nu_t \Rightarrow \mu$, so $\nu = \mu$ (uniqueness).

**Step 3 (Strict positive definiteness $\Sigma(\omega) \succ 0$ a.s., Lebesgue equivalence, central symmetry).**
Let $\Omega_{\mathrm{all}} := \{\omega : \{I_k : k \ge 0\} = \{1, \dots, n\}\}$. For each $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, $\mathbb{P}(\forall k \ge 0,\, I_k \ne j) = \lim_{t\to\infty}(1 - 1/n)^t = 0$, so $\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\mathrm{all}}) = 1$. Fix $\omega \in \Omega_{\mathrm{all}}$ with $\Sigma(\omega)$ convergent, and let $0 = \tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots < \tau_n < \infty$ be the first hitting times of the $n$ distinct members $j_r := I_{\tau_r}$ ($r = 1, \dots, n$). Suppose $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ satisfies $v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v = 0$. Since every summand in $\Sigma(\omega)$ is PSD and $K_{ii}^{-1} \succ 0$, $v^\top P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} = 0$ for every $k \ge 0$. We prove $v^\top E_{j_r} = 0$ for all $r = 1, \dots, n$ by induction on $r$:
- *Base ($r = 1$):* At $k = \tau_1 = 0$, $P_0(\omega) = I$, so $v^\top E_{j_1} = 0$.
- *Inductive step:* Assume $v^\top E_{j_1} = \dots = v^\top E_{j_r} = 0$. Note that whenever $v^\top E_i = 0$, we have $v^\top \mathsf{A}_i = v^\top - (v^\top E_i) K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top K = v^\top$. For every $0 \le s < \tau_{r+1}$, $I_s \in \{j_1, \dots, j_r\}$, so $v^\top \mathsf{A}_{I_s} = v^\top$; hence $v^\top P_{\tau_{r+1}}(\omega) = v^\top$, and the $k = \tau_{r+1}$ condition gives $0 = v^\top P_{\tau_{r+1}}(\omega) E_{j_{r+1}} = v^\top E_{j_{r+1}}$.
Thus $v^\top E_j = 0$ for all $j = 1, \dots, n$, forcing $v = 0$. Hence $\Sigma(\omega) \succ 0$ a.s. For every $\omega$ with $\Sigma(\omega) \succ 0$, $\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))$ has everywhere strictly positive Lebesgue density $g_\omega(u) > 0$. By Tonelli's theorem, for every Borel set $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, $\mu(B) = \mathbb{E}_\omega[\int_B g_\omega(u)\,\mathrm{d}u] = \int_B \mathbb{E}_\omega[g_\omega(u)]\,\mathrm{d}u$, so $\mu$ is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ with everywhere strictly positive density $m(u) = \mathbb{E}_\omega[g_\omega(u)]$. Central symmetry $\mu(u^{\mathrm{N}} - B) = \mu(u^{\mathrm{N}} + B)$ follows immediately from symmetry of each component $\mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma(\omega))$.

**Step 4 (Necessity: $\exists$ stationary law $\mu \implies \lambda_1 < 0$, with no moment or invertibility assumption).**
Suppose $\mu$ is a stationary probability law for $T$. Let $U_0 \sim \mu$ be independent of $(\omega, \xi)$. Without assuming $K$ invertible, reversing indices in the $t$-step forward recursion shows that for every $t \ge 1$, the backward iterate
$$U_t := P_t(\omega) U_0 + m_t(\omega) + \tilde Z_t(\omega), \qquad m_t(\omega) := \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} P_k(\omega) \mathsf{b}_{I_k}, \qquad \tilde Z_t(\omega) := \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} \xi_k$$
has exact law $\mu$. Conditional on $(\omega, U_0)$, $\tilde Z_t(\omega) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_t(\omega))$ with $\Sigma_t(\omega) := \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1} P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} K_{I_k I_k}^{-1} E_{I_k}^\top P_k(\omega)^\top$, while $P_t(\omega) U_0 + m_t(\omega)$ is fixed. For any $R > 0$ and any standard coordinate $a \in \{1, \dots, d_u\}$, $\{u : \|u\|_2 \le R\} \subseteq \{u : |u_a| \le R\}$, and for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ ($\sigma \ge 0$), $\mathbb{P}(|x + Y| \le R) \le \min(1, 2R/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}))$ (with $1/0 = +\infty$). Taking the minimum over $a \in \{1, \dots, d_u\}$ and using $\max_a (\Sigma_t(\omega))_{aa} \ge \frac{1}{d_u}\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_t(\omega)$ gives
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|U_t\|_2 \le R \;\big|\; \omega, U_0\bigr) \le \min\Biggl(1, \, \frac{2R\sqrt{d_u}}{\sqrt{2\pi \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_t(\omega)}}\Biggr).$$
Taking expectations and using $\operatorname{law}(U_t) = \mu$,
$$\mu\bigl(\{u : \|u\|_2 \le R\}\bigr) \le \mathbb{E}_\omega\Biggl[\min\Biggl(1, \, \frac{2R\sqrt{d_u}}{\sqrt{2\pi \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_t(\omega)}}\Biggr)\Biggr] \qquad \forall t \ge 1, \, \forall R > 0.$$
Since $\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_t(\omega) \uparrow \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\infty(\omega) \in [0, \infty]$ monotonically as $t \to \infty$ and the integrand is bounded by $1$, Dominated Convergence as $t \to \infty$ gives $\mu(\{\|u\|_2 \le R\}) \le \mathbb{E}_\omega[\min(1, 2R\sqrt{d_u}/\sqrt{2\pi \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\infty(\omega)})]$ for all $R > 0$. Letting $R \to \infty$ and applying Dominated Convergence again yields $1 \le \mathbb{P}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\infty(\omega) < \infty)$, so $\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\infty(\omega) < \infty$ a.s. With $c_0 := \min_{1 \le i \le n} \lambda_{\min}(K_{ii}^{-1}) > 0$,
$$\frac{c_0}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k}\|_F^2 \le \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \bigl\|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} K_{I_k I_k}^{-1/2}\bigr\|_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\infty(\omega) < \infty \qquad \text{a.s.},$$
so $\|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k}\|_F \to 0$ a.s.

*Substep 4a ($P_k(\omega) \to 0$ a.s. via elementary stopping-time Borel–Cantelli).*
Fix $\epsilon > 0$. Let $\mathcal{F}_k := \sigma(I_0, \dots, I_k)$ ($\mathcal{F}_{-1}$ trivial), so $P_k(\omega)$ is $\mathcal{F}_{k-1}$-measurable and $I_k$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_{k-1}$. Set $B_k := \{\|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k}\|_F \ge \epsilon/\sqrt{n}\} \in \mathcal{F}_k$ and $p_k := \mathbb{P}(B_k \mid \mathcal{F}_{k-1})$. Since $\|P_k(\omega) E_{I_k}\|_F \to 0$ a.s., $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{B_k} < \infty$ a.s. For any integer $L \ge 1$, define $\sigma_L := \inf\{m \ge 0 : \sum_{j=0}^m \mathbf{1}_{B_j} \ge L\}$ ($\inf \varnothing = \infty$). For every $k \ge 0$, $\{k \le \sigma_L\} = \{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \mathbf{1}_{B_j} \le L - 1\} \in \mathcal{F}_{k-1}$ (with empty sum $0 \le L - 1$ for $k = 0$), and $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} \mathbf{1}_{B_k} \le L$ identically. By Tonelli's theorem and the tower property,
$$\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} p_k\Biggr] = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} p_k\bigr] = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} \mathbf{1}_{B_k}\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} \mathbf{1}_{B_k}\Biggr] \le L < \infty.$$
Hence $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{k \le \sigma_L\}} p_k < \infty$ a.s.; on $\{\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{B_k} < L\} \subseteq \{\sigma_L = \infty\}$, this gives $\sum_{k=0}^\infty p_k < \infty$ a.s. Taking the countable union over $L \ge 1$ shows $\sum_{k=0}^\infty p_k < \infty$ a.s. Now whenever $\|P_k(\omega)\|_F \ge \epsilon$, the identity $\sum_{i=1}^n \|P_k(\omega) E_i\|_F^2 = \|P_k(\omega)\|_F^2 \ge \epsilon^2$ ensures some $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ satisfies $\|P_k(\omega) E_i\|_F \ge \epsilon/\sqrt{n}$, whence $p_k \ge \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|P_k(\omega)\|_F \ge \epsilon\}}$. Therefore $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{\|P_k(\omega)\|_F \ge \epsilon\}} \le n \sum_{k=0}^\infty p_k < \infty$ a.s., so $\|P_k(\omega)\|_F < \epsilon$ for all sufficiently large $k$ a.s. Taking $\epsilon = 1/m$ ($m \in \mathbb{N}$) gives $P_k(\omega) \to 0$ a.s.

*Substep 4b ($P_k(\omega) \to 0$ a.s. $\implies \lambda_1 < 0$ via greedy covering + Birkhoff + Fatou).*
For $N \ge 1$, define $E_N := \{\sup_{k \ge N} \|P_k(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1/2\}$. Since $P_k(\omega) \to 0$ a.s., $E_1 \subseteq E_2 \subseteq \dots$ and $\mathbb{P}(\bigcup_{N=1}^\infty E_N) = 1$, so we may fix $N \ge 1$ such that $p := \mathbb{P}(E_N) \ge 1/2 > 0$. Define $Z_j(\omega) := \mathbf{1}_{E_N}(\theta^j \omega)$ for $j \ge 0$. Because the one-sided i.i.d. product shift $\theta$ is ergodic (by Kolmogorov's 0–1 law, every $\theta$-invariant event lies in the tail $\sigma$-algebra and has probability $0$ or $1$), Birkhoff's ergodic theorem yields $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-N} Z_j(\omega) \to p$ a.s. as $T \to \infty$. In particular, $t_1(\omega) := \inf\{j \ge 0 : Z_j(\omega) = 1\} < \infty$ a.s. For any sample path $\omega$ and any $T > t_1(\omega) + N$, inductively select $0 \le t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_r \le T - N$ ($r = r(T, \omega) \ge 1$) by $t_{s+1} := \min\{j \in [t_s + N, T - N] : Z_j(\omega) = 1\}$ while this set is nonempty. Then:
1. Every $j \in \{0, \dots, T - N\}$ with $Z_j(\omega) = 1$ satisfies $t_s \le j \le t_s + N - 1$ for the largest $s$ with $t_s \le j$, so $\sum_{j=0}^{T-N} Z_j(\omega) \le N r(T, \omega)$.
2. Factoring $P_T(\omega) = P_{t_1}(\omega) \cdot P_{t_2 - t_1}(\theta^{t_1}\omega) \cdots P_{t_r - t_{r-1}}(\theta^{t_{r-1}}\omega) \cdot P_{T - t_r}(\theta^{t_r}\omega)$, each of the $r$ blocks after $P_{t_1}(\omega)$ has length $\ge N$ and starts at an index $t_s$ with $Z_{t_s}(\omega) = 1$, hence has operator norm $\le 1/2$, while $\|P_{t_1}(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \tilde M^{t_1(\omega)}$.
Thus $\|P_T(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \tilde M^{t_1(\omega)} 2^{-r(T, \omega)}$ for all $T > t_1(\omega) + N$, so almost surely
$$\limsup_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \ln \|P_T(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \limsup_{T \to \infty} \Biggl(\frac{t_1(\omega)}{T}\ln \tilde M - \frac{\ln 2}{N} \cdot \frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-N} Z_j(\omega)\Biggr) = -\frac{p \ln 2}{N} < 0.$$
Finally, $f_T(\omega) := \ln \tilde M - \frac{1}{T}\ln\|P_T(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \in [0, +\infty]$ is nonnegative, so Fatou's lemma gives
$$\ln \tilde M - \lambda_1 = \liminf_{T \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[f_T] \ge \mathbb{E}\bigl[\liminf_{T \to \infty} f_T\bigr] \ge \ln \tilde M + \frac{p \ln 2}{N},$$
whence $\lambda_1 \le -\frac{p \ln 2}{N} < 0$. $\square$

### Proof of C2

**Part 1 (Gaussianity iff $K = K^\top \succ 0$).**
If $K = K^\top \succ 0$, `thm:obj-rotation`(b) (`ext3_obj.tex`) gives the unique reversible Gaussian stationary law $\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, (\gamma K)^{-1})$. Conversely, suppose $\mu = \mathcal{N}(m, \Sigma)$ ($\Sigma \succeq 0$, not assumed positive definite) satisfies $\mu T = \mu$. Because each $T_i$ applies the affine map $u \mapsto \mathsf{A}_i u + \mathsf{b}_i$ plus independent Gaussian noise $E_i \xi_i$, each $\mu T_i = \mathcal{N}(m^{(i)}, \Sigma^{(i)})$ is Gaussian with $m^{(i)} = \mathsf{A}_i m + \mathsf{b}_i$ and $\Sigma^{(i)} = \mathsf{A}_i \Sigma \mathsf{A}_i^\top + \gamma^{-1} E_i K_{ii}^{-1} E_i^\top$. Fix any $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, and put $a = v^\top m$, $\sigma^2 = v^\top \Sigma v \ge 0$, $a_i = v^\top m^{(i)}$, and $\sigma_i^2 = v^\top \Sigma^{(i)} v \ge 0$. Equality of moment generating functions $\mathbb{E}_\mu[e^{s v^\top u}] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}_{\mu T_i}[e^{s v^\top u}]$ ($s \in \mathbb{R}$) divided by $\exp(s a + \frac{1}{2} s^2 \sigma^2)$ reads
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \exp\Bigl(s(a_i - a) + \frac{1}{2} s^2(\sigma_i^2 - \sigma^2)\Bigr) = 1 \qquad \forall s \in \mathbb{R}.$$
If $\sigma_i^2 > \sigma^2$ for some $i$, the left side diverges to $+\infty$ as $s \to +\infty$, a contradiction; hence $\sigma_i^2 \le \sigma^2$ for all $i$. Let $J := \{i : \sigma_i^2 = \sigma^2\}$. Terms with $i \notin J$ vanish as $|s| \to \infty$, so $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i \in J} e^{s(a_i - a)} \to 1$ as $s \to +\infty$ and as $s \to -\infty$. This excludes $a_i > a$ (by $s \to +\infty$) and $a_i < a$ (by $s \to -\infty$) for every $i \in J$, so $a_i = a$ for all $i \in J$, whence $|J|/n = 1$, i.e. $J = \{1, \dots, n\}$. Thus $v^\top m^{(i)} = v^\top m$ and $v^\top \Sigma^{(i)} v = v^\top \Sigma v$ for all $i$ and all $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, so $m^{(i)} = m$ and $\Sigma^{(i)} = \Sigma$ (by polarization), i.e. $\mu T_i = \mu$ for every $i = 1, \dots, n$.

When $\mu T_i = \mu$, for every bounded measurable $f(u_i, u_{-i})$, $\mathbb{E}_\mu[f(u_i, u_{-i})] = \mathbb{E}_{\mu T_i}[f(u_i, u_{-i})] = \int \mu_{-i}(\mathrm{d}u_{-i}) \int p_i(v_i \mid u_{-i}) f(v_i, u_{-i})\,\mathrm{d}v_i$, so the conditional law of $u_i$ given $u_{-i}$ under $\mu$ is $p_i(\cdot \mid u_{-i})$. Hence for any bounded measurable $g(u, v)$,
$$\int \mu(\mathrm{d}u) T_i(u, \mathrm{d}v) g(u, v) = \int \mu_{-i}(\mathrm{d}u_{-i}) \iint p_i(u_i \mid u_{-i}) p_i(v_i \mid u_{-i}) g\bigl((u_i, u_{-i}), (v_i, u_{-i})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}u_i\,\mathrm{d}v_i,$$
which is symmetric under $(u, v) \mapsto (v, u)$. Thus every $T_i$, and hence $T = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i T_i$, is reversible with respect to $\mu$. By `thm:obj-rotation`(b), $K_{\mathrm{a}} = 0$ and $K \succ 0$.

**Part 2 (Identifiability Lemma).**
Let $\rho := \operatorname{law}(V)$ on $(0, \infty)$. Equality of $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}(0, V)]$ and $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j \mathcal{N}(m_j, \sigma_j^2)$ implies equality of characteristic functions for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$\int_{(0, \infty)} e^{-\frac{1}{2} t^2 s}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(s) = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j e^{\mathrm{i} t m_j - \frac{1}{2} t^2 \sigma_j^2}.$$
We prove by induction on $M$ that any finite nonnegative Borel measure $\rho$ on $(0, \infty)$ satisfying this identity for $w_j > 0$, $\sigma_j^2 \ge 0$ (not requiring $\rho((0, \infty)) = 1$, only $\rho((0, \infty)) = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j$) satisfies $m_j = 0$ for all $j$ and $\rho = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j \delta_{\sigma_j^2}$.
- Set $s_0 := \min_{1 \le j \le M} \sigma_j^2 \ge 0$ and $J_0 := \{j : \sigma_j^2 = s_0\} \ne \varnothing$. Multiply both sides by $e^{+\frac{1}{2} t^2 s_0}$:
  $$\int_{(0, \infty)} e^{-\frac{1}{2} t^2 (s - s_0)}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(s) = \sum_{j \in J_0} w_j e^{\mathrm{i} t m_j} + \sum_{j \notin J_0} w_j e^{\mathrm{i} t m_j - \frac{1}{2} t^2 (\sigma_j^2 - s_0)}.$$
- If $\rho((0, s_0)) > 0$, choose $\delta > 0$ with $\rho((0, s_0 - \delta]) > 0$; then the left side is at least $e^{+\frac{1}{2} t^2 \delta}\rho((0, s_0 - \delta]) \to +\infty$ as $t \to +\infty$, whereas the right side is bounded in modulus by $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j$, a contradiction. Thus $\rho((0, s_0)) = 0$.
- For $s \ge s_0$, $0 \le e^{-\frac{1}{2} t^2 (s - s_0)} \le 1$ and converges pointwise to $\mathbf{1}_{\{s = s_0\}}$ as $t \to +\infty$. By Dominated Convergence the left side converges to $\rho(\{s_0\})$, while the $j \notin J_0$ sum converges to $0$. Therefore $f(t) := \sum_{j \in J_0} w_j e^{\mathrm{i} t m_j} \to \rho(\{s_0\})$ as $t \to +\infty$.
- For any $m \in \mathbb{R}$, computing the Cesàro limit $\lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T f(t) e^{-\mathrm{i} t m}\,\mathrm{d}t$ gives $\sum_{j \in J_0 : m_j = m} w_j$ directly, and equals $\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \rho(\{s_0\}) e^{-\mathrm{i} t m}\,\mathrm{d}t = \rho(\{s_0\})\mathbf{1}_{\{m = 0\}}$ because $f(t) \to \rho(\{s_0\})$. Since $w_j > 0$, every $j \in J_0$ has $m_j = 0$ and $\sum_{j \in J_0} w_j = \rho(\{s_0\})$ (which also forces $s_0 > 0$ because $\rho(\{0\}) = 0 < \sum_{j \in J_0} w_j$).
- Subtracting $\rho(\{s_0\}) e^{-\frac{1}{2} t^2 s_0}$ from both sides leaves the same identity for the restricted measure $\rho_1 := \rho|_{(s_0, \infty)}$ and the strictly smaller index set $\{1, \dots, M\} \setminus J_0$. If $J_0 = \{1, \dots, M\}$, then $\int_{(s_0, \infty)} e^{-\frac{1}{2} t^2 s}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(s) = 0$ (at $t = 0$ gives $\rho((s_0, \infty)) = 0$), so $\rho = \rho(\{s_0\})\delta_{s_0}$; otherwise the induction hypothesis applies to $\rho_1$, yielding $\rho = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j \delta_{\sigma_j^2}$ and $m_j = 0$ for all $j$.

Finally, if $\mu = \mathbb{E}_\omega[\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))]$ were a finite Gaussian mixture $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j \mathcal{N}(\tilde m_j, V_j)$, then for every $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, the scalar projection $v^\top(u - u^{\mathrm{N}}) \sim \mathbb{E}_\omega[\mathcal{N}(0, v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v)]$ would equal $\sum_{j=1}^M w_j \mathcal{N}(v^\top(\tilde m_j - u^{\mathrm{N}}), v^\top V_j v)$ (with $v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v > 0$ a.s. when $v \ne 0$ by `C1`(2)), forcing $|\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{law}(v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v))| \le M$, a contradiction whenever $v^\top \Sigma(\omega) v$ has infinite support. $\square$

### Proof of C3

Let $n = 2$, $\mathsf{A}_1 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & A_{12} \\ 0 & I \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\mathsf{A}_2 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} I & 0 \\ A_{21} & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $M = A_{12} A_{21}$, and $M' = A_{21} A_{12}$. Note that $M$ and $M'$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues, so $\rho(M) = \rho(M')$.

**Part 1 (Exact $\lambda_1$ and $\rho(\mathcal{T})$).**
*Formula for $\rho(\mathcal{T})$:* For $S = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} S_{11} & S_{12} \\ S_{12}^\top & S_{22} \end{smallmatrix}\bigr) \in \mathrm{Sym}_{d_1 + d_2}(\mathbb{R})$,
$$\mathcal{T}(S) = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} S_{11} + A_{12} S_{22} A_{12}^\top & A_{12} S_{22} + S_{11} A_{21}^\top \\ S_{22} A_{12}^\top + A_{21} S_{11} & S_{22} + A_{21} S_{11} A_{21}^\top \end{pmatrix}.$$
In the decomposition $(S_{11}, S_{22}) \oplus S_{12}$, $\mathcal{T}$ is block-lower-triangular with zero on the $(S_{12}, S_{12})$ block, so $\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal{T}) = \operatorname{spec}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}}) \cup \{0\}$, where $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}}(X_1, X_2) = \frac{1}{2}(X_1 + A_{12} X_2 A_{12}^\top, \, A_{21} X_1 A_{21}^\top + X_2)$ on $\mathrm{Sym}_{d_1}(\mathbb{C}) \times \mathrm{Sym}_{d_2}(\mathbb{C})$.
- *Upper bound:* Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ be any eigenvalue of $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}}$ with $(X_1, X_2) \ne (0, 0)$. Then $(2\lambda - 1) X_1 = A_{12} X_2 A_{12}^\top$ and $(2\lambda - 1) X_2 = A_{21} X_1 A_{21}^\top$. If $2\lambda - 1 = 0$, $|\lambda| = 1/2 \le (1 + \rho(M))/2$. Otherwise $X_1 \ne 0$ and $(2\lambda - 1)^2 X_1 = M X_1 M^\top$. Because $X \mapsto M X M^\top$ has matrix $M \otimes M$ with eigenvalues $\mu_a \mu_b$ ($\mu_a, \mu_b \in \operatorname{spec}(M)$), $|2\lambda - 1|^2 \le \rho(M)^2$, so $|2\lambda - 1| \le \rho(M)$ and $|\lambda| \le (1 + |2\lambda - 1|)/2 \le (1 + \rho(M))/2$.
- *Lower bound (attainment):* If $\rho(M) = 0$, then $(2\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}} - I)^2(X_1, X_2) = (M X_1 M^\top, M' X_2 (M')^\top)$ is nilpotent, so every eigenvalue of $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}}$ equals $1/2 = (1 + 0)/2$. If $\rho(M) > 0$, choose $v \in \mathbb{C}^{d_1} \setminus \{0\}$ with $M v = \mu v$ and $|\mu| = \rho(M)$. Since $M$ is real, $M \bar v = \bar \mu \bar v$, so $M(v \bar v^\top) M^\top = (M v)(M \bar v)^\top = |\mu|^2 v \bar v^\top = \rho(M)^2 v \bar v^\top$. Taking real parts, $S_0 := \operatorname{Re}(v \bar v^\top) \in \mathrm{Sym}_{d_1}(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(S_0) = \|v\|_2^2 > 0$ (hence $S_0 \ne 0$) and $M S_0 M^\top = \rho(M)^2 S_0$. Setting $Y_1 = S_0$ and $Y_2 = \rho(M)^{-1} A_{21} S_0 A_{21}^\top \in \mathrm{Sym}_{d_2}(\mathbb{R})$ yields $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{diag}}(Y_1, Y_2) = \frac{1 + \rho(M)}{2}(Y_1, Y_2)$ with $(Y_1, Y_2) \ne (0, 0)$. Hence $\rho(\mathcal{T}) = \frac{1 + \rho(M)}{2}$.

*Formula for $\lambda_1$:* Direct block multiplication and induction on $m \ge 1$ give
$$(\mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2)^m = \begin{pmatrix} M^m & 0 \\ A_{21} M^{m-1} & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (\mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2)^m \mathsf{A}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & M^m A_{12} \\ 0 & (M')^m \end{pmatrix},$$
$$(\mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_1)^m = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & A_{12}(M')^{m-1} \\ 0 & (M')^m \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (\mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_1)^m \mathsf{A}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} M^m & 0 \\ A_{21} M^m & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
If $\rho(M) = 0$, $M$ is nilpotent ($M^{d_1} = 0$), so $(\mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2)^{d_1 + 1} = 0$ occurs with positive probability in $P_{2d_1 + 2}$, giving $\mathbb{E}[\ln\|P_{2d_1 + 2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}] = -\infty$ and $\lambda_1 = -\infty = \frac{1}{4}\ln 0$. Suppose now $\rho(M) > 0$. Because $\mathsf{A}_1^2 = \mathsf{A}_1$ and $\mathsf{A}_2^2 = \mathsf{A}_2$, collapsing consecutive equal indices in $(I_0, \dots, I_{t-1})$ reduces $P_t(\omega)$ to an alternating product of length $N_t + 1$, where $N_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t-1} \mathbf{1}_{\{I_s \ne I_{s-1}\}} \sim \operatorname{Bin}(t - 1, 1/2)$. Putting $m_t := \lfloor (N_t + 1)/2 \rfloor$, every $t \ge 1$ and every $\omega$ has $P_t(\omega)$ containing either $M^{m_t}$ or $(M')^{m_t}$ (or $I$ when $m_t = 0$) as a subblock, whence $\|P_t(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \ge \rho(M)^{m_t}$ (using $\|B^m\|_{\mathrm{op}} \ge \rho(B)^m$ for all $m \ge 1$ and $\rho(M') = \rho(M)$). Conversely, for any $\epsilon > 0$, Gelfand's formula yields $C_\epsilon \ge 1$ such that $\|M^m\|_{\mathrm{op}}, \|(M')^m\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C_\epsilon (\rho(M) + \epsilon)^m$ for all $m \ge 0$, so by the four block formulas there exists $C'_\epsilon \ge 1$ (independent of $t, \omega$) with $\|P_t(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C'_\epsilon (\rho(M) + \epsilon)^{m_t}$. Taking logarithms, expectations, and dividing by $t$ using $\mathbb{E}[m_t]/t = (\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[N_t] + O(1))/t = \frac{t-1}{4t} + O(1/t) \to \frac{1}{4}$ gives $\frac{1}{4}\ln \rho(M) \le \lambda_1 \le \frac{1}{4}\ln(\rho(M) + \epsilon)$. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ yields $\lambda_1 = \frac{1}{4}\ln \rho(M)$. Thus $\lambda_1 < 0 \iff \rho(M) < 1 \iff \rho(\mathcal{T}) < 1$.

**Part 2 (Exact two-Gaussian mixture for $\rho(M) < 1$).**
When $\rho(M) < 1$, $Q_1 := \gamma^{-1}(K_{11}^{-1} + A_{12} K_{22}^{-1} A_{12}^\top) \succ 0$ and the series $S_1 := \sum_{k=0}^\infty M^k Q_1 (M^\top)^k \succeq Q_1 \succ 0$ converges and is the unique solution of $S_1 - M S_1 M^\top = Q_1$ (uniqueness because $\rho(M \otimes M) = \rho(M)^2 < 1$). Define $S_2 := A_{21} S_1 A_{21}^\top + (\gamma K_{22})^{-1} \succ 0$; then $A_{12} S_2 A_{12}^\top + (\gamma K_{11})^{-1} = M S_1 M^\top + Q_1 = S_1$. The Schur complements of $S_2 \succ 0$ in $\Sigma^{(1)}$ and of $S_1 \succ 0$ in $\Sigma^{(2)}$ are $(\gamma K_{11})^{-1} \succ 0$ and $(\gamma K_{22})^{-1} \succ 0$ respectively, so $\Sigma^{(1)}, \Sigma^{(2)} \succ 0$. Put $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}} := \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma^{(1)}) + \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma^{(2)})$. Both components have marginals $\mathcal{N}(u_1^{\mathrm{N}}, S_1)$ and $\mathcal{N}(u_2^{\mathrm{N}}, S_2)$, so $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}}$ has the same marginals. Because $T_1$ draws $u_1^+ - u_1^{\mathrm{N}} = A_{12}(u_2 - u_2^{\mathrm{N}}) + \xi_1$ depending only on the second marginal $\mathcal{N}(u_2^{\mathrm{N}}, S_2)$, $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}} T_1 = \mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma^{(1)})$; analogously $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}} T_2 = \mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma^{(2)})$. Hence $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}} T = \mu_{\mathrm{mix}}$, so $\mu = \mu_{\mathrm{mix}}$ by uniqueness in `C1`. Clearly $\mu_{\mathrm{mix}}$ has finite moments of all orders. By `C2`(1), $\mu$ is Gaussian iff $K = K^\top \succ 0$; since $K_{11}, K_{22} \succ 0$ and $\rho(M) < 1$, when $K_{12} = K_{21}^\top$ we have $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1/2} K K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1/2} = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} I & C \\ C^\top & I \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $C = K_{11}^{-1/2} K_{12} K_{22}^{-1/2}$ satisfying $\|C\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2 = \rho(C C^\top) = \rho(M) < 1$, whence $I - C^\top C \succ 0$ and $K \succ 0$ automatically. Thus $\mu$ is Gaussian iff $K_{12} = K_{21}^\top$.

**Part 3 (Counterexample $K_{(2,2)}$).**
For $K_{(2,2)}$, $K_{11} = \operatorname{diag}(10, 1) \succ 0$ and $K_{22} = \operatorname{diag}(1, 10) \succ 0$, so $K_{(2,2)}$ is realizable (`appext3_obj.tex`, line 121). Direct computation gives
$$A_{12} = -K_{11}^{-1} K_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ -1/2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A_{21} = -K_{22}^{-1} K_{21} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad M = A_{12} A_{21} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 0 \\ 3/4 & 1/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Thus $\rho(M) = 1/2 < 1$, $\rho(\mathcal{T}) = 3/4 < 1$, and $\lambda_1 = -\frac{1}{4}\ln 2 < 0$, so by Parts 1–2 heat-bath deliberation has a unique stationary law with all moments finite. Expanding $\det((-z)I - K_{(2,2)})$ yields $z^4 + 22 z^3 + \frac{73}{2} z^2 - 25 z + 25$. If every eigenvalue of $K_{(2,2)}$ had positive real part, then every real factor $(z + r)$ ($r > 0$) and every conjugate quadratic factor $(z^2 + 2\alpha z + \alpha^2 + \beta^2)$ ($\alpha > 0$) of $\det((-z)I - K_{(2,2)})$ would have strictly positive coefficients, forcing all coefficients of the product to be strictly positive. The $z$-coefficient is $-25 < 0$, so $K_{(2,2)}$ has an eigenvalue with nonpositive (in fact strictly negative) real part, and Langevin deliberation has no stationary law by `thm:obj-rotation`(c). $\square$

### Proof of C4

First, for any $S \succeq 0$, block $(j, j)$ of $\mathsf{A}_i S \mathsf{A}_i^\top$ equals $S_{jj}$ whenever $j \ne i$ and is positive semidefinite for $j = i$, so $\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf{A}_i S \mathsf{A}_i^\top) \ge \sum_{j \ne i} \operatorname{tr}(S_{jj})$. Averaging over $i = 1, \dots, n$ gives $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}(S)) \ge \frac{n-1}{n}\operatorname{tr}(S)$, and iterating gives $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}^t(I)) \ge (\frac{n-1}{n})^t d_u$. Since $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}^t(I)) \le d_u \|\mathcal{T}^t(I)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le d_u \|\mathcal{T}^t\|_{\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{op}}$, taking $t$-th roots and letting $t \to \infty$ (Gelfand's formula) gives $\rho(\mathcal{T}) \ge \frac{n-1}{n} > 0$.

Second, by induction on $t \ge 1$ using independence of $I_0$ from $(I_1, \dots, I_{t-1})$ and $P_t(\omega) = \mathsf{A}_{I_0} P_{t-1}(\theta\omega)$,
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[P_t(\omega) P_t(\omega)^\top\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathsf{A}_{I_0} \mathbb{E}[P_{t-1}(\theta\omega) P_{t-1}(\theta\omega)^\top] \mathsf{A}_{I_0}^\top\bigr] = \mathcal{T}\bigl(\mathcal{T}^{t-1}(I)\bigr) = \mathcal{T}^t(I),$$
so $\mathbb{E}[\|P_t(\omega)\|_F^2] = \operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}^t(I)) \le d_u \|\mathcal{T}^t\|_{\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{op}}$. By Jensen's inequality applied to the concave function $\ln$,
$$2 \cdot \frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln \|P_t(\omega)\|_F\bigr] = \frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln \|P_t(\omega)\|_F^2\bigr] \le \frac{1}{t}\ln \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|P_t(\omega)\|_F^2\bigr] \le \frac{\ln d_u}{t} + \frac{1}{t}\ln \|\mathcal{T}^t\|_{\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{op}}.$$
Letting $t \to \infty$ (using norm-independence of $\lambda_1$ from `C1` and Gelfand's formula on $\mathrm{Sym}_{d_u}(\mathbb{R})$) gives $2\lambda_1 \le \ln \rho(\mathcal{T})$.

Third, $\mathbb{E}[P_t(\omega)] = \bar{\mathsf{A}}^t$ because $I_0, \dots, I_{t-1}$ are i.i.d. For any unit vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, Jensen's inequality gives
$$\|\bar{\mathsf{A}}^t v\|_2^2 = \|\mathbb{E}[P_t(\omega) v]\|_2^2 \le \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|P_t(\omega) v\|_2^2\bigr] \le \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|P_t(\omega)\|_F^2\bigr] \le d_u \|\mathcal{T}^t\|_{\mathrm{op}\to\mathrm{op}}.$$
Taking $\sup_{\|v\|_2 = 1}$, raising to power $1/(2t)$, and letting $t \to \infty$ (Gelfand's formula) yields $\rho(\bar{\mathsf{A}}) \le \rho(\mathcal{T})^{1/2}$, i.e. $\rho(\bar{\mathsf{A}})^2 \le \rho(\mathcal{T})$. If $\rho(\mathcal{T}) < 1$, then $\lambda_1 \le \frac{1}{2}\ln\rho(\mathcal{T}) < 0$ and $\rho(\bar{\mathsf{A}}) < 1$; since $\bar{\mathsf{A}} = I - \frac{1}{n} K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$, every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K$ satisfies $|1 - \lambda/n| < 1$, hence $2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)/n > |\lambda|^2/n^2 \ge 0$, so $\operatorname{Re}\lambda > 0$. $\square$

### Proof of C5

For $K(c) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & -c & c \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $K_{\mathrm{d}} = I \succ 0$ so $K(c)$ is realizable, and
$$\mathsf{A}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & c & -c \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathsf{A}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathsf{A}_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Part 1.** Expanding $\det(\lambda I - K(c)) = (\lambda - 1)[(\lambda - 1)^2 - c + c] = (\lambda - 1)^3$, all eigenvalues of $K(c)$ equal $1$ (so $K(c)$ is invertible, Langevin is stable by `thm:obj-rotation`(c), and $\bar{\mathsf{A}} = I - \frac{1}{3}K(c)$ has all eigenvalues $2/3$, giving $\rho(\bar{\mathsf{A}}) = 2/3 < 1$). Direct matrix multiplication gives
$$\mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathsf{A}_1 (\mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_3) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_3 \mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2 \mathsf{A}_3 = \mathbf{0}.$$
Hence $\mathbb{P}(P_5(\omega) = 0) \ge 3^{-5} > 0$, so $\mathbb{E}[\ln\|P_5(\omega)\|_{\mathrm{op}}] = -\infty$, giving $\lambda_1(K(c)) = -\infty < 0$. By `C1` (and Case 2a of its proof), a unique stationary law $\mu_c$ exists for every $c \in \mathbb{R}$, and $P_k(\omega) = 0$ for all $k \ge \tau(\omega)$ with $\tau(\omega) < \infty$ a.s.

**Part 2.** In the basis $(E_{11}, E_{22}, E_{33}, E_{12}+E_{21}, E_{13}+E_{31}, E_{23}+E_{32})$ of $\mathrm{Sym}_3(\mathbb{R})$, the $6 \times 6$ matrix of $\mathcal{T}(S) = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 \mathsf{A}_i S \mathsf{A}_i^\top$ is
$$[\mathcal{T}] = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & c^2 & c^2 & 0 & 0 & -2c^2 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & c & 0 & 1 & 0 & -c \\ 1 & 0 & -c & 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
A direct cofactor/characteristic-polynomial computation yields
$$\det(\lambda I - \mathcal{T}) = \frac{1}{729}(3\lambda - 2)(3\lambda - 1)\bigl(81\lambda^4 - 162\lambda^3 + (117 - 18c^2)\lambda^2 - (36 - 24c^2)\lambda + (4 - 10c^2)\bigr),$$
and substituting $\lambda = 1$ gives $\det(I - \mathcal{T}) = \frac{8}{729}(1 - c^2)$ (since $81 - 162 + 117 - 36 + 4 = 4$ and $-18 + 24 - 10 = -4$).
- *Case $|c| < 1$:* Direct substitution verifies that
  $$\gamma S_\infty(c) := \frac{1}{1 - c^2}\begin{pmatrix} 2c^2 + 1 & (4c^2 + 3c + 2)/4 & (4c^2 - 3c + 2)/4 \\ (4c^2 + 3c + 2)/4 & c^2 + 2 & c^2 + 1/2 \\ (4c^2 - 3c + 2)/4 & c^2 + 1/2 & c^2 + 2 \end{pmatrix}$$
  solves $(I - \mathcal{T}) S_\infty(c) = \frac{1}{3\gamma} I$. Its leading principal minors are
  $$M_1 = \frac{2c^2 + 1}{1 - c^2} > 0, \qquad M_2 = \frac{16c^4 - 24c^3 + 55c^2 - 12c + 28}{16(1 - c^2)^2}, \qquad M_3 = \frac{3(4c^4 + 25c^2 + 16)}{16(1 - c^2)^3} > 0.$$
  Writing the numerator of $M_2$ as $(4c^2 - 3c)^2 + 46(c - \frac{3}{23})^2 + \bigl(28 - \frac{18}{23}\bigr) > 0$ shows $M_2 > 0$ for all $c \in (-1, 1)$. By Sylvester's criterion, $S_\infty(c) \succ 0$. Letting $X \succeq 0, X \ne 0$ be a Krein–Rutman eigenvector of $\mathcal{T}^\ast$ for eigenvalue $\rho(\mathcal{T})$ (as in `lem:obj-moments`), pairing $(I - \mathcal{T}) S_\infty(c) = \frac{1}{3\gamma} I$ with $X$ gives $(1 - \rho(\mathcal{T}))\operatorname{tr}(X S_\infty(c)) = \frac{1}{3\gamma}\operatorname{tr}(X) > 0$; since $\operatorname{tr}(X S_\infty(c)) > 0$, we obtain $\rho(\mathcal{T}) < 1$.
- *Case $|c| \ge 1$:* For $|c| > 1$, $\det(I - \mathcal{T}) = \frac{8}{729}(1 - c^2) < 0$ while $\det(\lambda I - \mathcal{T}) \to +\infty$ as $\lambda \to +\infty$, so by the Intermediate Value Theorem $\mathcal{T}$ has a real eigenvalue $> 1$, hence $\rho(\mathcal{T}) > 1$. For $|c| = 1$, $\det(I - \mathcal{T}) = 0$, so $1$ is an eigenvalue, giving $\rho(\mathcal{T}) \ge 1$ (and by continuity of $\rho(\mathcal{T})$ in $c$ as $|c| \uparrow 1$, $\rho(\mathcal{T}) = 1$). By `lem:obj-moments`, for all $|c| \ge 1$ no stationary law with finite second moments exists, so the unique stationary law $\mu_c$ of Part 1 satisfies $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_c}[\|u\|_2^2] = \infty$.

**Parts 3 and 4.**
Define the sequence of distinct consecutive updates in $\omega$: $\sigma_0 := 0$, $J_0 := I_0$, and inductively $\sigma_{r+1} := \inf\{k > \sigma_r : I_k \ne J_r\}$, $J_{r+1} := I_{\sigma_{r+1}}$ ($\sigma_r < \infty$ a.s. for all $r$). Because $(I_k)$ is i.i.d. uniform on $\{1, 2, 3\}$, $J_0 \sim \operatorname{Unif}\{1, 2, 3\}$ and conditional on $(J_0, \dots, J_r)$, $J_{r+1}$ is uniform on $\{1, 2, 3\} \setminus \{J_r\}$ (probability $1/2$ each). Because $\mathsf{A}_i^2 = \mathsf{A}_i$ and $\mathsf{A}_i E_i = 0$, $P_k(\omega) E_{I_k} = 0$ whenever $k \notin \{\sigma_r : r \ge 0\}$, while $P_{\sigma_r}(\omega) E_{I_{\sigma_r}} = \mathsf{A}_{J_0} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{J_{r-1}} E_{J_r}$. Define the pairwise disjoint events ($m \ge 1$)
$$H_m := \bigl\{J_0 = 1,\, J_1 = 2,\, J_2 = 1,\, \dots,\, J_{2m-1} = 2,\, J_{2m} = 1,\, J_{2m+1} = 3\bigr\},$$
which are disjoint because $2m + 1$ is the unique first index with $J_r = 3$, and satisfy $\mathbb{P}(H_m) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2^{-(2m+1)} = \frac{1}{6} 4^{-m} > 0$. By induction on $r \ge 1$, $(\mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2)^r E_1 = (c^r, c^{r-1}, 0)^\top$ (base: $\mathsf{A}_2 E_1 = (1, 1, 0)^\top$, $\mathsf{A}_1 \mathsf{A}_2 E_1 = (c, 1, 0)^\top$; step: $\mathsf{A}_2(c^r, c^{r-1}, 0)^\top = (c^r, c^r, 0)^\top$, $\mathsf{A}_1(c^r, c^r, 0)^\top = (c^{r+1}, c^r, 0)^\top$). Because every term in $\Sigma(\omega)$ is PSD, on $H_m$ retaining the terms $r = 0, 2, \dots, 2m$ yields
$$\Sigma_{11}(\omega) \ge \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{r=0}^m c^{2r} \ge \frac{c^{2m}}{\gamma}.$$
- For any $p > 0$, since $u_1 \mid \omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_{11}(\omega))$, $\mathbb{E}[|u_1|^p \mid \omega] = C_p \Sigma_{11}(\omega)^{p/2}$ with $C_p = \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)}[|Z|^p] > 0$. By Tonelli's theorem on the disjoint family $\{H_m\}_{m \ge 1}$,
  $$\mathbb{E}_{\mu_c}\bigl[|u_1|^p\bigr] \ge \sum_{m=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(H_m)\, C_p \Bigl(\frac{c^{2m}}{\gamma}\Bigr)^{p/2} = \frac{C_p}{6\gamma^{p/2}} \sum_{m=1}^\infty \Bigl(\frac{|c|^p}{4}\Bigr)^m = \infty \qquad \text{whenever } |c|^p \ge 4.$$
  At $p = 1$, $|c| \ge 4$ gives $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_c}[|u_1|] = \infty$.
- For $|c| \ge 1$, on $H_m$ we have $\Sigma_{11}(\omega) \ge (m + 1)/\gamma$ with $\mathbb{P}(H_m) = \frac{1}{6} 4^{-m} > 0$ for every $m \ge 1$, so $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_\omega \Sigma_{11}(\omega) = +\infty$. Hence $\Sigma_{11}(\omega)$ has unbounded (infinite) support, so by `C2`(2) $\mu_c$ is not a finite Gaussian mixture of any order, and $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_c}[e^{s u_1}] \ge \mathbb{P}(H_m) e^{\frac{1}{2} s^2 (m+1)/\gamma}$ rules out sub-Gaussianity ($e^{C s^2}$ upper bound for any fixed $C > 0$).

**Part 5 (Open-set persistence).**
Fix $|c| > 1$, let $K_0 = K(c)$, and fix the length-5 word $w_\ast = (2, 3, 1, 2, 3)$. On the closed ball $\overline{B_\delta(K_0)}$ with $\delta = 1/2$, every $K \in \overline{B_\delta(K_0)}$ has $K_{ii} \ge 1/2 > 0$, so $K \mapsto \mathsf{A}_i(K)$ is continuous and $M_0 := \max\bigl(1, \sup_{K \in \overline{B_\delta(K_0)}} \max_{1 \le i \le 3} \|\mathsf{A}_i(K)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr) < \infty$. For every $K \in B_\delta(K_0)$, all $242$ words $w \ne w_\ast$ in $\{1, 2, 3\}^5$ satisfy $\|P_5(K; w)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le M_0^5$, while $P_5(K; w_\ast) \to P_5(K_0; w_\ast) = \mathbf{0}$ as $K \to K_0$. Hence
$$\lambda_1(K) \le \frac{1}{5}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln \|P_5(K)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr] \le \frac{242}{243}\ln M_0 + \frac{1}{5 \cdot 243}\ln \|P_5(K; w_\ast)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \xrightarrow{K \to K_0} -\infty.$$
Since $K \mapsto \rho(\mathcal{T}(K))$ is continuous (as the spectral radius of a continuous matrix family) and $\rho(\mathcal{T}(K_0)) > 1$, there exists $\eta_c \in (0, 1/2)$ such that every $K$ with $\|K - K(c)\|_{\mathrm{op}} < \eta_c$ satisfies $K_{\mathrm{d}} \succ 0$, $\lambda_1(K) < 0$, and $\rho(\mathcal{T}(K)) > 1$. $\square$

### Proof of C6

Index members cyclically in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. For the directed $n$-cycle, $\mathsf{A}_i$ agrees with $I$ on rows $j \ne i$ and has row $i$ equal to $a_i e_{i+1}^\top$.

**Part 1 (Exact $\rho(\mathcal{T})$ and $\lambda_1$).**
*Formula for $\rho(\mathcal{T})$:* For any $S \in \mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})$, $(\mathcal{T}(S))_{ii} = \frac{n-1}{n} S_{ii} + \frac{1}{n} a_i^2 S_{i+1, i+1}$. Thus the diagonal map $d(S) := (S_{11}, \dots, S_{nn})^\top$ satisfies $d(\mathcal{T}(S)) = B \, d(S)$, where $B = \frac{n-1}{n} I + \frac{1}{n} C_{\mathrm{cyc}}(a_1^2, \dots, a_n^2)$ has $(i, i+1)$ entry $a_i^2/n$ and diagonal $(n-1)/n$. Its characteristic polynomial is $\det(\lambda I - B) = (\lambda - \frac{n-1}{n})^n - n^{-n} \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^2$, whose roots are $\frac{n - 1 + |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|^{2/n} e^{2\pi\mathrm{i} k/n}}{n}$ ($k = 0, \dots, n-1$), giving $\rho(B) = \frac{n - 1 + |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|^{2/n}}{n}$. By Krein–Rutman, $\mathcal{T}$ has a nonzero PSD eigenvector $S \succeq 0$ for $\rho(\mathcal{T})$; since $S \succeq 0$ and $S \ne 0$, $d(S) \ne 0$, and $B d(S) = d(\mathcal{T}(S)) = \rho(\mathcal{T}) d(S)$, whence $\rho(\mathcal{T}) \le \rho(B)$. Conversely, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $S \in \mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})$, $\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{diag}(x)\mathcal{T}(S)) = x^\top B d(S) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{diag}(B^\top x) S)$, so $\mathcal{T}^\ast(\operatorname{diag}(x)) = \operatorname{diag}(B^\top x)$, whence $\rho(B) = \rho(B^\top) \le \rho(\mathcal{T}^\ast) = \rho(\mathcal{T})$. Therefore $\rho(\mathcal{T}) = \frac{n - 1 + |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|^{2/n}}{n}$.

*Formula for $\lambda_1$:* Consider the forward product $Q_t := \mathsf{A}_{I_{t-1}} \cdots \mathsf{A}_{I_0}$ ($Q_0 = I$), which has the same law as $P_t(\omega)$. Define integer counters $L_t(i)$ ($i \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$) by $L_0(i) := 0$ and, at step $t$ with $I_t = i$, $L_{t+1}(i) := L_t(i+1) + 1$ and $L_{t+1}(j) := L_t(j)$ for $j \ne i$. By induction on $t \ge 0$, row $i$ of $Q_t$ has at most one nonzero entry, located at column $i + L_t(i) \pmod n$, with value
$$w_t(i) := \prod_{r=0}^{L_t(i) - 1} a_{i + r \pmod n}$$
(base $t = 0$: $e_i^\top$, empty product $= 1$; step $I_t = i$: row $i$ of $\mathsf{A}_i Q_t$ is $a_i$ times row $i+1$ of $Q_t$, moving to column $(i+1) + L_t(i+1) \equiv i + L_{t+1}(i)$ with value $a_i \prod_{r=0}^{L_t(i+1)-1} a_{i+1+r} = \prod_{r=0}^{L_{t+1}(i)-1} a_{i+r}$).
- If $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} = 0$, some $a_{i_0} = 0$. The deterministic length-$n$ reverse sweep $(I_0, \dots, I_{n-1}) = (i_0, i_0 - 1, \dots, i_0 + 1)$ gives $Q_n = 0$ with probability $n^{-n} > 0$, hence $\lambda_1 = -\infty = \frac{1}{n^2}\ln 0$.
- Suppose $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \ne 0$, so $|a_k| > 0$ for all $k$. We prove by induction on $t$ the deterministic invariant $L_t(j) \le L_t(j+1) + 1$ for all $j \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$: at $t = 0$ it is $0 \le 1$; when $I_t = i$, $L_{t+1}(i) = L_t(i+1) + 1 = L_{t+1}(i+1) + 1$, and $L_{t+1}(i-1) = L_t(i-1) \le L_t(i) + 1 \le L_t(i+1) + 2 = L_{t+1}(i) + 1$, while all other pairs are unchanged. Telescoping at most $n - 1$ steps around the cycle yields $\max_j L_t(j) - \min_j L_t(j) \le n - 1$ deterministically for all $t \ge 0$. Set $S_t := \sum_{i=1}^n L_t(i)$. Then $|L_t(i) - S_t/n| \le n - 1$ for every $i$, and
  $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[S_{t+1} - S_t \;\big|\; \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \bigl(L_t(i+1) + 1 - L_t(i)\bigr) = 1,$$
  so $\mathbb{E}[S_t] = t$ for every $t \ge 0$. Since each row $i$ of $Q_t$ has a single nonzero entry $w_t(i)$, the max-row-sum norm is $\|Q_t\|_\infty = \max_i |w_t(i)|$. Factoring $L_t(i) = q_i n + r_i$ ($0 \le r_i < n$) gives $|\ln|w_t(i)| - \frac{L_t(i)}{n}\ln|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|| \le C_1 + |\ln|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}||$ with $C_1 := \sum_{k=1}^n |\ln|a_k||$. Combining with $|L_t(i) - S_t/n| \le n - 1$ gives
  $$\Bigl|\ln \|Q_t\|_\infty - \frac{S_t}{n^2}\ln|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|\Bigr| \le C_1 + \frac{2n - 1}{n}|\ln|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}||$$
  deterministically. Taking expectations, dividing by $t$, using $\mathbb{E}[S_t] = t$ and $\operatorname{law}(P_t) = \operatorname{law}(Q_t)$, and letting $t \to \infty$ (norm-independence of $\lambda_1$ by `C1`) yields $\lambda_1 = \frac{1}{n^2}\ln|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}|$.

**Part 2 (Deterministic $\Sigma_{ii}(\omega) \equiv s_i$ and exact Gaussian 1D marginals).**
Assume $|\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}| < 1$, so $\lambda_1 < 0$ and `C1` applies. Right multiplication by $\mathsf{A}_j$ acts on standard row vectors by $e_c^\top \mathsf{A}_j = e_c^\top$ if $c \ne j$ and $e_j^\top \mathsf{A}_j = a_j e_{j+1}^\top$. Hence for each $i$, row $i$ of $P_k(\omega)$ has the form $r_i(k) = \alpha_i(k) e_{c_i(k)}^\top$, starting at $(c_i(0), \alpha_i(0)) = (i, 1)$, and changes **only** at steps $k$ where $I_k = c_i(k)$ — the exact steps where $r_i(k) e_{I_k} \ne 0$! At the $m$-th such step ($m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$), $c_i(k) = i + m \pmod n$ and $\alpha_i(k) = \prod_{q=0}^{m-1} a_{i+q \pmod n}$, contributing $\gamma^{-1} \alpha_i(k)^2 K_{i+m, i+m}^{-1}$ to $\Sigma_{ii}(\omega)$, after which $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ advances to $(i + m + 1 \pmod n, \, a_{i+m}\alpha_i)$. On the probability-$1$ event where every index $1, \dots, n$ appears infinitely often in $\omega$, every $m \ge 0$ is achieved, so summing over $m = qn + r$ ($q \ge 0, 0 \le r < n$) gives
$$\Sigma_{ii}(\omega) = \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{q=0}^\infty \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{2q} \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} \Biggl(\prod_{q'=0}^{r-1} a_{i+q'}^2\Biggr) K_{i+r, i+r}^{-1} = s_i \qquad \text{a.s.}$$
Thus $\Sigma_{ii}(\omega)$ is a.s. constant, so the $i$-th marginal of $\mu = \mathbb{E}_\omega[\mathcal{N}(u^{\mathrm{N}}, \Sigma(\omega))]$ is $\mathcal{N}(u_i^{\mathrm{N}}, s_i)$.

**Part 3 (Infinite Gaussian mixture for $n = 3$, $0 < |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}| < 1$).**
Take $n = 3$ and $0 < |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}| < 1$. Trace $r_1(k) = e_1^\top P_k(\omega)$ and $r_2(k) = e_2^\top P_k(\omega)$ under the 6-step block $(2, 1, 3, 2, 1, 3)$ starting from $(r_1, r_2) = (e_1^\top, e_2^\top)$:
- $I_0 = 2$: $(e_1^\top, a_2 e_3^\top)$; $I_1 = 1$: $(a_1 e_2^\top, a_2 e_3^\top)$; $I_2 = 3$: $(a_1 e_2^\top, a_2 a_3 e_1^\top)$;
- $I_3 = 2$: $(a_1 a_2 e_3^\top, a_2 a_3 e_1^\top)$; $I_4 = 1$: $(a_1 a_2 e_3^\top, \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} e_2^\top)$; $I_5 = 3$: $(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} e_1^\top, \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} e_2^\top)$.
At each of these 6 steps, $c_1(k) \ne c_2(k)$, so $(r_1(k) e_{I_k})(r_2(k) e_{I_k}) = 0$. By induction on $m \ge 0$, on the cylinder event $C_m$ where $\omega$ starts with $((2, 1, 3, 2, 1, 3)^m, 1)$ (of probability $3^{-(6m+1)} > 0$), we have $(r_1(6m), r_2(6m)) = (\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^m e_1^\top, \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^m e_2^\top)$ with zero cross terms for $0 \le k < 6m$. At step $k = 6m$ ($I_{6m} = 1$), the cross term is still $0$ and $(r_1(6m+1), r_2(6m+1)) = (\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^m a_1 e_2^\top, \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^m e_2^\top)$, so $r_1(k) = a_1 r_2(k) = a_1 \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^m e_2^\top P_{k-(6m+1)}(\theta^{6m+1}\omega)$ for all $k \ge 6m + 1$. Summing from $k = 6m + 1$ to $\infty$ gives, a.s. on $C_m$,
$$\Sigma_{12}(\omega) = a_1 \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{2m} \Sigma_{22}(\theta^{6m+1}\omega) = a_1 s_2 \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{2m}$$
by Part 2 applied to $\theta^{6m+1}\omega$. Since $0 < |\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}| < 1$, $a_1 \ne 0$, and $s_2 > 0$, the values $s_1 + s_2 + 2 a_1 s_2 \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{2m}$ ($m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) of $(e_1 + e_2)^\top \Sigma(\omega)(e_1 + e_2)$ are pairwise distinct and each occurs with probability at least $3^{-(6m+1)} > 0$. By `C2`(2), $\mu$ is not a finite Gaussian mixture of any order. $\square$

### Proof of C7

Clearly $K_{(3)} = D_{(3)} M_{(3)}$ has diagonal $D_{(3)} = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 1/100) \succ 0$ (hence is realizable by `appext3_obj.tex`, line 121) and $K_{\mathrm{d}}^{-1} K_{(3)} = M_{(3)}$, so its heat-bath matrices $\mathsf{A}_i = I - E_i E_i^\top M_{(3)}$ and operator $\mathcal{T}$ depend only on $M_{(3)}$.

Solving the $6 \times 6$ linear system $(I - \mathcal{T}) S_\infty = (3\gamma)^{-1} I$ in exact rational arithmetic (and directly verifiable by substituting into $\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 \mathsf{A}_i S_\infty \mathsf{A}_i^\top + (3\gamma)^{-1} I = S_\infty$) gives
$$\gamma S_\infty = \begin{pmatrix} 2125/294 & -645/98 & -115/21 \\ -645/98 & 2125/294 & 115/21 \\ -115/21 & 115/21 & 166/21 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Its leading principal minors are $M_1 = 2125/294 > 0$, $M_2 = 27550/3087 > 0$, and $M_3 = 228950/7203 > 0$, so by Sylvester's criterion $S_\infty \succ 0$. Pairing $(I - \mathcal{T}) S_\infty = (3\gamma)^{-1} I$ with a nonzero Krein–Rutman eigenvector $X \succeq 0$ of $\mathcal{T}^\ast$ for $\rho(\mathcal{T})$ gives $(1 - \rho(\mathcal{T}))\operatorname{tr}(X S_\infty) = (3\gamma)^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(X) > 0$, hence $\rho(\mathcal{T}) < 1$. By `C4` and `C1` (and `lem:obj-moments`), heat-bath deliberation for $K_{(3)}$ has a unique stationary law with finite second moments, even though the isolated $(1,2)$ sub-block $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 11/10 \\ 11/10 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has $\chi_{12} = 121/100 > 1$.

Finally, expanding the characteristic polynomial of
$$K_{(3)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 11/10 & -1/4 \\ 11/10 & 1 & 1/4 \\ 1/200 & -1/200 & 1/100 \end{pmatrix}$$
yields
$$\det(\lambda I - K_{(3)}) = \lambda^3 - \frac{201}{100}\lambda^2 - \frac{3}{16}\lambda - \frac{63}{20000} = \Bigl(\lambda - \frac{21}{10}\Bigr)\Bigl(\lambda^2 + \frac{9}{100}\lambda + \frac{3}{2000}\Bigr),$$
whose roots are $\frac{21}{10}$ and $\frac{-9 \pm \sqrt{21}}{200}$ (both strictly negative since $\sqrt{21} < 5 < 9$). By `thm:obj-rotation`(c), Langevin deliberation has no stationary law. $\square$
