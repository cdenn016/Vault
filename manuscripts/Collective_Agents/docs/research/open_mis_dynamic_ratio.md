## Derivations

### Proof of C1

**(a) Stationary vector regime ($\rho(F) < 1$).**
1. Because $\rho(F) < 1$ and $Q \succ 0$, the Lyapunov equation $P_0 = F P_0 F^\top + Q$ has unique symmetric solution $P_0 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty F^n Q (F^\top)^n \succeq Q \succ 0$, $(F, H^s_k)$ is detectable, and $(F, Q^{1/2})$ is controllable. With $R^s_k \succ 0$, the DARE $P^-_k = F P^-_k F^\top - F P^-_k (H^s_k)^\top(H^s_k P^-_k (H^s_k)^\top + R^s_k)^{-1} H^s_k P^-_k F^\top + Q$ has a unique symmetric stabilizing solution $P^-_k \succeq Q \succ 0$ with $\rho(F A_k) = \rho(A_k F) < 1$ ($A_k = I - K^s_k H^s_k$), and $\Sigma_{kk}^{-1} = (P^-_k)^{-1} + (H^s_k)^\top (R^s_k)^{-1} H^s_k \succ 0$ gives $\Sigma_{kk} = A_k P^-_k \succ 0$.
2. The filtered error $e_k(t) = m_k(t) - \eta_t$ obeys $e_k(t+1) = A_k(F e_k(t) - w_t) + K^s_k v_k(t+1)$. Taking $\mathbb{E}[e_i(t+1) e_j(t+1)^\top]$ in stationarity using mutual independence of $w_t, v_i(t+1), v_j(t+1)$ and $(e_i(t), e_j(t))$ gives $\Sigma_{ij} = A_i F \Sigma_{ij} F^\top A_j^\top + A_i Q A_j^\top$. Its Kronecker operator $I_{K^2} - (A_j F) \otimes (A_i F)$ has every eigenvalue of $(A_j F)\otimes(A_i F)$ bounded in modulus by $\rho(A_j F)\rho(A_i F) < 1$, so $\Sigma_{ij}$ is unique.
3. By Bayes orthogonality of the Kalman posterior mean $m_k(t) = \mathbb{E}[\eta_t \mid \mathcal{F}^k_t]$, $\mathbb{E}[e_k(t) m_k(t)^\top] = 0$, hence $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t, m_k) = \operatorname{Cov}(m_k) = P_0 - \Sigma_{kk} =: M_k$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(e_k, \eta_t) = \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t, e_k) = -\Sigma_{kk}$. Expanding $m_k = \eta_t + e_k$ gives $\operatorname{Cov}(m_i, m_j) = P_0 - \Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{jj} + \Sigma_{ij}$.
4. Conditioning the jointly Gaussian triple $(\eta_t, m_i, m_j)$ on $m_i$ (using $M_i = P_0 - \Sigma_{ii} \succ 0$):
   - $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t \mid m_i) = P_0 - M_i M_i^{-1} M_i = \Sigma_{ii}$.
   - $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_t, m_j \mid m_i) = (P_0 - \Sigma_{jj}) - M_i M_i^{-1}(P_0 - \Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{jj} + \Sigma_{ij}) = \Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{ij}$.
   - Writing $\operatorname{Cov}(m_i, m_j) = M_i - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij})$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(m_j, m_i) = M_i - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ji})$:
     $$\operatorname{Cov}(m_j \mid m_i) = (P_0 - \Sigma_{jj}) - \bigl(M_i - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ji})\bigr) M_i^{-1} \bigl(M_i - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij})\bigr) = (\Sigma_{ii} + \Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij} - \Sigma_{ji}) - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ji})M_i^{-1}(\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij}).$$
   - Taking the Schur complement with respect to $\eta_t \mid m_i$ ($\Sigma_{ii} \succ 0$):
     $$\operatorname{Cov}(m_j \mid \eta_t, m_i) = \operatorname{Cov}(m_j \mid m_i) - (\Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{ji})\Sigma_{ii}^{-1}(\Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{ij}) = (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ji}\Sigma_{ii}^{-1}\Sigma_{ij}) - (\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ji})M_i^{-1}(\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij}).$$
   - Pushing through $y_{ij} = H_j m_j + \nu$ ($\nu \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_c)$ independent) gives $S^{\mathrm{exact}}_{ij}$ and $R^{\mathrm{exact}}_{ij}$. Since $m_j(t) = A_j F m_j(t-1) + K^s_j H^s_j \eta_t + K^s_j v_j(t)$ with $v_j(t)$ independent of $(\eta_t, m_i(t), m_j(t-1))$, $\operatorname{Cov}(m_j(t) \mid \eta_t, m_i(t)) \succeq K^s_j R^s_j (K^s_j)^\top$, so $R^{\mathrm{exact}}_{ij} \succeq (H_j K^s_j) R^s_j (H_j K^s_j)^\top + R_c$, which is $\succ 0$ whenever $R_c \succ 0$ or $H_j K^s_j$ has full row rank. The mutual information and posterior covariance identities then follow by standard Gaussian determinant factorization.

**(b) Scalar nonstationary steady-state limit ($K = 1, |F| \ge 1$).**
Starting from any finite $\eta_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, P_{\mathrm{init}})$ ($P_{\mathrm{init}} > 0$), Bayes orthogonality $\operatorname{Cov}(e_k(t), m_k(t)) = 0$ holds at every finite $t \ge 0$, so the formula of (a) holds at each $t$ with $P_\eta(t) = F^{2t} P_{\mathrm{init}} + Q \sum_{s=0}^{t-1} F^{2s}$ replacing $P_0$. Since $(F, 1)$ is observable and $(F, Q^{1/2})$ controllable, $P^-_k(t) \to P^-_k > 0$, $a_k(t) \to a_k \in (0, 1)$ with $a_k F^2 = 1 - Q/P^-_k < 1$, $\Sigma_{kk}(t) \to \Sigma_{kk}$, and $\Sigma_{ij}(t) \to \Sigma_{ij}$ (contraction factor $a_i(t)a_j(t)F^2 \to a_i a_j F^2 < 1$). Since $|F| \ge 1, Q > 0$, $P_\eta(t) \ge Q t \to \infty$, so $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij}(t) = \frac{g^2(\Sigma_{jj}(t) - \Sigma_{ij}(t))^2}{P_\eta(t) - \Sigma_{ii}(t)} \to 0$, while $R^{\mathrm{tr}}_{ij}(t) - \Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij}(t) \ge g^2 k^s_j(t)^2 R^s_j + R_c \to g^2 (k^s_j)^2 R^s_j + R_c > 0$. By continuity of $\ln$, $\lim_{t\to\infty} I(\eta_t; y_{ij}(t) \mid m_i(t)) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(S^{\mathrm{tr}}_{ij}/R^{\mathrm{tr}}_{ij})$.

### Proof of C2

**(a) Asymmetric scalar.** From $\Sigma_{kk} = \frac{a_k Q}{1 - a_k F^2}$ and $\Sigma_{ij} = \frac{a_i a_j Q}{1 - a_i a_j F^2}$, $\Sigma_{ii} - \Sigma_{ij} = \frac{Q a_i(1-a_j)}{(1-a_i F^2)(1-a_i a_j F^2)}$, so $\mathrm{sig}_{ij} = \frac{(\Sigma_{ii}-\Sigma_{ij})^2}{\Sigma_{ii}} = \frac{Q a_i(1-a_j)^2}{(1-a_i F^2)(1-a_i a_j F^2)^2}$. For $R_{\mathrm{tr},0} = \Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij}^2/\Sigma_{ii}$, expanding $(1 - a_i a_j F^2)^2 - a_i a_j(1 - a_i F^2)(1 - a_j F^2) = 1 - a_i a_j - a_i a_j(2 - a_i - a_j)F^2$ gives the $|F| \ge 1$ expression. For $|F| < 1$, $P_0 - \Sigma_{ii} = \frac{Q(1-a_i)}{(1-F^2)(1-a_i F^2)}$ and $\Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij} = \frac{Q a_j(1-a_i)}{(1-a_j F^2)(1-a_i a_j F^2)}$ cancel $(1 - a_i)$ to give $\frac{(\Sigma_{jj}-\Sigma_{ij})^2}{P_0-\Sigma_{ii}} = \frac{Q a_j^2(1-a_i)(1-F^2)(1-a_i F^2)}{(1-a_j F^2)^2(1-a_i a_j F^2)^2}$; subtracting from $R_{\mathrm{tr},0}$ and factoring out $(1 - a_j)$ yields $R_{\mathrm{ex},0}$.

**(b) Symmetric scalar.** With $u = P^-/R^s > 0$, $\lambda = Q/R^s > 0$, $\delta = 1 - F^2$, the DARE reads $u^2 + (\delta - \lambda)u - \lambda = 0$, with $D_u = \sqrt{(\delta - \lambda)^2 + 4\lambda} = 2u + \delta - \lambda > 0$ and $a = \frac{1}{1+u}$. Thus $\rho = \frac{a(1-aF^2)}{1-a^2 F^2} = \frac{u + \delta}{u^2 + 2u + \delta}$. Since $(1+u)(2u + \delta - \lambda) - (u^2 + 2u + \delta) = u^2 + (\delta - \lambda)u - \lambda = 0$, the denominator is identically $(1 + u)D_u$, while $2(u + \delta) - (u^2 + 2u + \delta) = \delta - u^2 = (\delta - \lambda)(1 + u)$, yielding $2\rho - 1 = \frac{\delta - \lambda}{D_u}$ and $1 - \rho = \frac{u}{D_u} = \frac{1-a}{1-a^2 F^2}$. Setting $a_i = a_j = a$ in (a) and factoring $1 - 2 a^2 F^2 + a^2(2a-1)F^4 = (1 - a F^2)(1 + a F^2 - 2 a^2 F^2)$ yields $R_{\mathrm{ex},0}/\Sigma = (1 - \rho)(1 + a F^2(1 - \rho))$ for $|F| < 1$ and $R_{\mathrm{tr},0}/\Sigma = 1 - \rho^2$ for $|F| \ge 1$.

**(c) Posterior variance ratios.** By `prop:mis-correlated-fusion(b)` with $K_1 = \frac{g\Sigma_{ii}}{g^2(\Sigma_{ii}+\Sigma_{jj})+R_c}$, $\hat P^{(1)}_i = (1-K_1 g)\Sigma_{ii}$, and $X = g\Sigma_{ij}$, we have $P^{\mathrm{act},(1)}_i - \hat P^{(1)}_i = 2 K_1(1-K_1 g)g\Sigma_{ij}$, so dividing by $\hat P^{(1)}_i$ cancels $(1 - K_1 g)$ and yields $P^{\mathrm{act},(1)}_i/\hat P^{(1)}_i = 1 + \frac{2 g^2 \Sigma_{ij}}{g^2(\Sigma_{ii}+\Sigma_{jj})+R_c}$. From C1, $P^\ast_i/\hat P^{(1)}_i = \exp(2(\mathcal{V}^1_i - \mathcal{V}^\ast_i))$. In the symmetric case, the exact conditional mean $\bigl(1 - K^\ast g(1-\theta(1-\rho))\bigr)m_i + K^\ast y_{ij}$ ($\theta = \frac{a(1-F^2)}{1-a} > 0$ for $|F|<1$, $\theta = 0$ for $|F|\ge 1$) coincides with the level-1 mean $(1-K_1 g)m_i + K_1 y_{ij}$ iff $\theta = 0$ (i.e. $|F| \ge 1$) and $K^\ast = K_1$ (at $\theta = 0$, equivalent to $\rho\varrho_c = 0 \iff R_c = 0$).

### Proof of C3

**(a) Scalar strict non-inversion.** Both denominators of $\mathrm{SNR}^1 = \frac{g^2\Sigma_{ii}}{g^2\Sigma_{jj}+R_c}$ and $\mathrm{SNR}^\ast = \frac{g^2\mathrm{sig}_{ij}}{g^2 R_{\mathrm{ex},0}+R_c}$ are strictly positive, and direct algebraic expansion gives:
$$\Sigma_{ii} - \mathrm{sig}_{ij} = \frac{Q a_i a_j[(1 - a_j) + (1 - a_i a_j F^2)]}{(1 - a_i a_j F^2)^2} > 0,$$
$$\Sigma_{ii} R_{\mathrm{ex},0} - \mathrm{sig}_{ij}\Sigma_{jj} = \frac{Q^2 a_i a_j^2(1 - a_j)[(1 - a_i F^2) + F^2(1 - a_j)]}{(1 - a_j F^2)^2(1 - a_i a_j F^2)^2} > 0 \quad (|F| < 1),$$
$$\Sigma_{ii} R_{\mathrm{tr},0} - \mathrm{sig}_{ij}\Sigma_{jj} = \frac{Q^2 a_i a_j^2(2 - a_i - a_j)}{(1 - a_j F^2)(1 - a_i a_j F^2)^2} > 0 \quad (|F| \ge 1).$$
Every factor is strictly positive since $a_i, a_j \in (0, 1)$ and $a_k F^2 < 1$, so $\mathrm{SNR}^\ast < \mathrm{SNR}^1$ and $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i < \mathcal{V}^1_i < \mathcal{V}^0_i$. Furthermore, $x \mapsto \frac{x Q}{1 - x F^2}$ is strictly increasing on $(0, \min(1, 1/F^2))$ and $a_i a_j < \min(a_i, a_j)$, giving $0 < \Sigma_{ij} < \min(\Sigma_{ii}, \Sigma_{jj}) \le \frac{1}{2}(\Sigma_{ii} + \Sigma_{jj})$, hence $1 < P^\ast_i/\hat P^{(1)}_i \le P^{\mathrm{act},(1)}_i/\hat P^{(1)}_i < 2$.

**(b) Aligned and isotropic vector worlds.** In an aligned world, the orthogonal change of basis $\tilde\eta = U^\top\eta$ diagonalizes $F, Q, \Gamma^s_k = (H^s_k)^\top(R^s_k)^{-1}H^s_k$ and hence all DARE, Lyapunov, and Sylvester matrices, decoupling $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i, \mathcal{V}^1_i, \mathcal{V}^0_i$ into sums over the $r$ coded scalar modes. In an isotropic world ($F=fI_K, Q=qI_K, H^s_k=I_K, R^s_k=r_k I_K$), uniqueness gives scalar-multiple matrices $P_0 = p_0 I_K, \Sigma_{kk} = s_{kk}I_K, \Sigma_{ij} = s_{ij}I_K$, so $S^{\mathrm{exact}}_{ij} = s^{\mathrm{ex}} G + R_c$, $R^{\mathrm{exact}}_{ij} = r^{\mathrm{ex}} G + R_c$ with $G = H_j H_j^\top \succ 0$. Conjugating by $G^{-1/2}$ diagonalizes both in the eigenbasis of $G^{-1/2} R_c G^{-1/2}$, decomposing $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i$ and $\mathcal{V}^1_i$ into sums of $r$ scalar modes with effective channel noises $d_1, \dots, d_r \ge 0$; part (a) applies to each mode.

**(c) Boundedness dichotomy.** From C2(b), $\partial\rho/\partial\lambda = -(\delta + \lambda)/D_u^3$. For $|F| = 1$ ($\delta = 0$), $\rho$ strictly decreases from $\frac{1}{2}$ ($\lambda \to 0^+$) to $0$. For $|F| > 1$ ($\delta < 0$), $\rho$ attains its unique maximum at $\lambda^\ast = F^2 - 1$, where $\rho_{\max}(|F|) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \sqrt{1 - F^{-2}}) \le \frac{1}{2}$. Setting $x = (1 + \varrho_c)^{-1} \in (0, 1]$, strict concavity of $t \mapsto \ln(1+t)$ with $\ln(1+0) = 0$ and $0 < 1 - \rho^2 x < 1$ give
$$(1 - \rho)^2 \mathcal{V}^1_i = \frac{1}{2}(1-\rho)^2 \ln(1+x) < \frac{1}{2}\ln\bigl(1 + (1-\rho)^2 x\bigr) < \frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1 + \frac{(1-\rho)^2 x}{1 - \rho^2 x}\right) = \mathcal{V}^\ast_i,$$
hence $\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i < (1-\rho)^{-2} \le (1-\rho_{\max}(|F|))^{-2} \le 4$ (strict for all finite $R_c \ge 0$, approached as $\varrho_c \to \infty$ and $\rho \to \rho_{\max}(|F|)$). The $R_c = 0$ and variance bounds $\left.\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i\right|_{R_c=0} \le \ln 2/\ln(2/(1+\rho_{\max}(|F|))) \le \ln 2/\ln(4/3)$ and $P^{\mathrm{act},(1)}_i/\hat P^{(1)}_i \le 1 + \rho_{\max}(|F|) \le 3/2$ follow by monotonicity in $\rho$, with equality in the $|F|$-dependent bounds attained at $Q/R^s = F^2 - 1, R_c = 0$ for $|F| > 1$ and strict inequality for $|F| = 1$ (since $\rho < 1/2$). Unboundedness for fixed $|F| < 1$ follows from C4(3); for asymmetric sensors at $F = 1$ with fixed $Q, R^s_i > 0$ and $R^s_j \to \infty$, $k^s_j \sim \sqrt{Q/R^s_j}$, $\Sigma_{jj} \sim \sqrt{Q R^s_j}$, $\mathrm{sig}_{ij} \sim \Sigma_{ii}(k^s_j)^2$, $R_{\mathrm{tr},0} \sim \Sigma_{jj}$, giving $\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i \sim (k^s_j)^{-2} \sim R^s_j/Q \to \infty$.

### Proof of C4

1. $F \to 0$ ($\delta \to 1$): $D_u \to 1 + \lambda$, $\rho \to (1 + \lambda)^{-1} = \phi$, $a F^2 \to 0$, recovering `prop:mis-closed-ratios(a)` identically.
2. $F \to 1^-$ ($\delta \to 0^+$): $a F^2(1-\rho) \to \frac{a_1}{1+a_1} = \rho$, so $(1-\rho)(1 + a F^2(1-\rho)) \to 1 - \rho^2$, continuously matching $|F| = 1$.
3. $Q \to 0^+$ ($\lambda \to 0^+$): for $\delta > 0$, $D_u = \delta + \frac{2-\delta}{\delta}\lambda + O(\lambda^2)$, $u = \lambda/\delta + O(\lambda^2)$, $1 - \rho = u/D_u = \lambda/\delta^2 + O(\lambda^2)$, $\Sigma = Q/\delta + O(Q^2)$, yielding the stated $R_c = 0$, fixed $R_c > 0$, and fixed $\varrho_c > 0$ asymptotics. For $\delta = 0$, $\rho = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{4} + O(\lambda^{3/2})$ and $\Sigma \sim \sqrt{Q R^s} \to 0$, giving $\ln 2/\ln(4/3)$ ($R_c = 0$) and $4$ (fixed $R_c > 0$). For $\delta = -m < 0$, $D_u = m + \lambda + 2\lambda/m + O(\lambda^2)$, giving $\rho = \lambda/m^2 + O(\lambda^2) \to 0^+$ and $\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i \to 1$. Along $\delta = c\sqrt{\lambda}$, canceling $\sqrt{\lambda}$ gives $\rho = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + \frac{c - \sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{(c-\sqrt{\lambda})^2 + 4}}\bigr) \to \frac{1}{2}(1 + c/\sqrt{c^2+4})$.
4. $R_c \to 0^+$ and $R_c \to \infty$ follow immediately from $\varrho_c = R_c/(g^2\Sigma)$ and $\ln(1 + t) = t + O(t^2)$.
5. Substituting $\lambda = \delta \gamma_0$ ($\delta = 1 - F^2 > 0$) gives $\rho(F; \gamma_0) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + \frac{(1-\gamma_0)\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\delta(1-\gamma_0)^2 + 4\gamma_0}}\bigr)$, which is anti-symmetric around $\frac{1}{2}$ under $\gamma_0 \mapsto 1/\gamma_0$ (proving $\rho(F; \gamma_0) + \rho(F; 1/\gamma_0) = 1$) and monotone in $\delta$ toward $\frac{1}{2}$; at $\gamma_0 = 1$, $u = \sqrt{\delta}$ so $a F^2 = \frac{1-\delta}{1+\sqrt{\delta}}$ increases strictly from $0$ to $1$. At fixed $(Q, R^s)$ and $R_c = 0$, $\mathrm{SNR}^\ast(0) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}$ and $\mathrm{SNR}^\ast(1) = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda+4}+\sqrt{\lambda}}{3\sqrt{\lambda+4}-\sqrt{\lambda}}$; comparing their difference reduces on $\lambda > 1/2$ to $(2\lambda-1)^2(\lambda+4) - (2\lambda+1)^2\lambda = 8\lambda^2 - 16\lambda + 4$, whose unique root above $1/2$ is $\lambda_{\mathrm{cross}} = 1 + 1/\sqrt{2}$. Implicit differentiation of $u^2 + (1-F^2-\lambda)u - \lambda = 0$ at $F^2 = 0$ gives $(1-\rho)'(0) = \frac{2\lambda}{(\lambda+1)^3}$ and $\partial\mathrm{SNR}^\ast/\partial(F^2)|_{F=0} = \frac{\lambda(2-\lambda)}{(\lambda+1)^3}$. As $\lambda \to \infty$, $\rho(0) \sim \lambda^{-1}$ and $\rho(1) \sim \lambda^{-1}$, doubling $\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i - 1$ from $\frac{1}{2\lambda\ln 2}$ to $\frac{1}{\lambda\ln 2}$.

### Refutation of C5

Explicit rational-entry stationary dynamic worlds evaluated via C1(a) and independently confirmed by brute-force finite-horizon Kalman filtering (`open_mis_dynamic_ratio-checks/verify_dynamic_ratio.py`, `verify_dynamic_ratio_stdout.txt`):

Gives $\mathcal{V}^1_i = 3.3111831960 < \mathcal{V}^\ast_i = 3.3494340776$ (brute-force $T=200$: $3.3494340776$, diff $2.08 \times 10^{-13}$), $\mathcal{V}^1_i/\mathcal{V}^\ast_i = 0.98857990 < 1$.
2. **$K = 3$, $H_j = I_3, R_c = 0$, rank-1 sensors:** $F = [[0.7, -0.5, 0.6], [0.3, 0, -0.5], [0, 0.2, 0]]$ ($\rho(F) = 0.5830 < 1$), $Q = G_Q G_Q^\top + 0.05 I_3$, $H^s_i = [1.2, -1.3, 2.4]$, $H^s_j = [1.2, 0.3, -1.6]$, $R^s_i = 1.62, R^s_j = 0.80$. ### Two-Channel Independence Statement

Channel 1 (symbolic SymPy identities and factorizations in `verify_dynamic_ratio.py`) and Channel 2 (direct finite-horizon covariance recursion of the time-varying Kalman filter from $t = 0$ to $t = T$ without using any closed-form steady-state formula) share only the primitive definition of the linear-Gaussian state-space recursion $\eta_{t+1} = F\eta_t + w_t, s_k = H^s_k \eta_t + v_k$ and standard IEEE-754 linear algebra. The feared errors—omitting the proper-prior correction $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij}$, sign or factor errors in the closed-form rational expressions, false non-inversion in $K = 3$, or invalid limit interchanges—cannot live in that shared layer, as confirmed by the four planted-error negative controls.

## Failed routes

### R1: Naive track-model substitution for stationary $|F| < 1$

- Tried: Substituting the steady-state cross-covariance $\Sigma_{ij}$ of `prop:mis-dynamic(a)` directly into the track-model mutual-information formula `prop:mis-track-band` / `eq:fw-track` (which assumes $\operatorname{Cov}(e_k, \eta_t) = 0$, i.e. $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij} = 0$).
- Failed because: For every stationary world $\rho(F) < 1$, Bayes orthogonality forces $\operatorname{Cov}(e_k, \eta_t) = -\Sigma_{kk} \neq 0$, producing the proper-prior correction $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij} = H_j(\Sigma_{jj}-\Sigma_{ji})(P_0-\Sigma_{ii})^{-1}(\Sigma_{jj}-\Sigma_{ij})H_j^\top \succeq 0$. Dropping $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij}$ fails the registered test instance ($F = 0.5, Q = 0.75, R^s = 2, R_c = 0$) by $4.6558 \times 10^{-2}$ (predicting $0.09728$ instead of exact $0.14384$) and fails to recover the static common-source formula `prop:mis-closed-ratios(a)` as $F \to 0$.
- Reopen if: Only in the nonstationary/flat-prior limit $|F| \ge 1$ ($P_\eta(t) \to \infty$), where $\Delta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{ij}(t) \to 0$ and the track-model formula becomes exact (C1(b)).

### R2: Extending static invertible-noiseless overvaluation (`prop:mis-overvaluation-scope(c)`) to general dynamic vector worlds

- Tried: Checking whether invertible noiseless codes ($H_j = I_K, R_c = 0$) preserve $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i < \mathcal{V}^1_i$ in all stationary vector dynamic worlds, as they do in static common-source worlds (`prop:mis-overvaluation-scope(c)`).
- Failed because: Unlike the static common-source cross-covariance $P_i \Lambda^{\mathrm{sh}}_{ij} P_j$, the dynamic Sylvester solution $\Sigma_{ij}$ under non-symmetric/oscillatory $F$ can have negative eigenvalues in its symmetrized normalized part $\frac{1}{2}(C + C^\top)$ ($C = \Sigma_{ii}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{ij}\Sigma_{jj}^{-1/2}$) or skew-symmetric cross-mode coupling, yielding $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i > \mathcal{V}^1_i$ even for $H_j = I_3, R_c = 0$ and full-rank sensors (C5).
- Reopen if: Restricted to aligned or isotropic vector dynamic worlds, where C3(b) proves $\mathcal{V}^\ast_i < \mathcal{V}^1_i < \mathcal{V}^0_i$ holds unconditionally (or if one investigates whether $K = 2, H_j = I_2, R_c = 0$ has an obstruction to inversion, which remains unproved).
