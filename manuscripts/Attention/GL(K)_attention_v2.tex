\begin{abstract}
We formulate attention as communication among statistical agents that decide whom to hear and what to take from each sender. Each token holds a Gaussian belief and prior in a private frame, as a section of a statistical bundle associated with a principal $\mathrm{GL}(K)$-bundle over a single context. Each head has four interfaces: the sender emits a key and content, and the receiver forms a query and writes content through a receiving map. Group-valued links $\Theta_{ij}=U_ie^{L_{ij}}U_j^{-1}$ with Lie-algebra generators $L_{ij}$ give the communication graph a lattice gauge structure with inverse edges and holonomy. With square invertible query and key maps, routing is the divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij})_*q_j)$ of the receiver's belief from the sender's belief carried into its coordinates by a group-valued link. With tiled heads and the shared generator of the content maps, the routed multi-head message is a routed oblique projection followed by link transport, and a standard transformer layer is the affine chart with a full residual. The operator is gauge covariant and factors through frame-invariant reference moments. Gaussian-KL routing is exactly a dot product between query expectation parameters and key natural parameters with a key log-partition bias, and reduces to biased dot-product attention under stated conditions. At every positive temperature, routing weights are an exact source posterior and routed content an exact predictive mean. A four-layer exponential-chart model of width $K=128$ trained on WikiText-103 records test perplexity 34.48 at context 128 in a single seed; per-type tables that give each token a block frame hold most of its parameters.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

What does a neural language model compute when it predicts the next token, and could a system without a neural architecture compute the same thing? Communication and computation need no neurons: bacteria coordinate gene expression through diffusible signals \citep{MillerBassler2001QuorumSensing}, and a slime mold finds shortest paths and builds efficient transport networks \citep{Nakagaki2000Maze,Tero2010NetworkDesign}. Finite chemical reaction networks perform Turing-universal computation with arbitrarily small error probability \citep{Soloveichik2008CRN}, and DNA strand-displacement cascades implement associative memory \citep{Qian2011DNANeural}. This paper belongs to a program whose working hypothesis is that neural networks are one computational and biological realization of a more general information geometry and dynamics of interacting agents that hold beliefs, compare them, and exchange messages, and whose long-term goal is a language architecture with no neural components. This paper builds the first component of that architecture, the communication layer, in exact form and tests it in a language model.

Attention is already a communication protocol: each position issues a query, advertises a key, and offers a value, and routing weights decide which messages each receiver integrates \citep{vaswani2017attention}. Graph, mechanistic, and particle accounts describe the same coordination \citep{Gilmer2017MPNN,Joshi2025TransformersGNN,elhage2021mathematical,geshkovski2023mathematical} but leave open what each unit holds, in which coordinates, and how a message written in the sender's coordinates is re-expressed in the receiver's. One geometric construction answers all three.

Each token occurrence is a statistical agent $(q_i,p_i,U_i)$: a Gaussian belief, a prior, and a frame identifying its private coordinates with a shared reference space $\mathbb R^K$. The frames are points of a principal $\mathrm{GL}(K)$-bundle and the beliefs are sections of the associated statistical bundle, whose fiber is the Gaussian family $\mathbb R^K\times\mathrm{SPD}(K)$ with its Fisher metric \citep{AmariNagaoka2000} (Section~\ref{sec:principal_construction}, Figure~\ref{fig:link_construction}). The base is a single context $c_*$, so agents are labeled copies of one fiber differing in frame and belief, and no connection, curvature, or topology of a base enters.

Each head has four maps (Figure~\ref{fig:comparison_content_interfaces}). With $\xi_j=U_j^{-1}\mu_j$ the mean of sender $j$ in reference coordinates, the sender emits a key $S_K\xi_j$, which makes it findable, and content $T\xi_j$, which is what it says; receiver $i$ forms an untransmitted query $S_Q\xi_i$ and writes received content with a receiving map $\widetilde O$, which in the affine chart is the stored factor $O$. These are the $W_K$, $W_V$, $W_Q$, and $W_O$ of a transformer, a protocol shared by the agents of a layer. The routing weights $\beta_{ij}$, the decision of whom to hear, are a Gibbs distribution over relative entropies between the receiver's query law and the senders' key laws; for square invertible query and key maps the comparison is
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_i\|(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij})_*q_j\bigr),\qquad
\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}=U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1},
\end{equation}
the divergence of the receiver's belief from the sender's, carried into the receiver's coordinates by a group-valued comparison link that is the flat identification $\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$ when $S_Q=S_K$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link}).

Content is carried by group-valued links $\Theta_{ij}=U_ie^{L_{ij}}U_j^{-1}$ with Lie-algebra generators $L_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)$ in reference coordinates. They are invertible and obey the endpoint law $\Theta_{ij}\mapsto g_i\Theta_{ij}g_j^{-1}$ of lattice link variables under frame changes $U_i\mapsto g_iU_i$ \citep{WilsonConfinement1974,Creutz1983}, giving the communication graph a lattice gauge structure: a lattice of agents above one point, not a discretization of a base. Its lattice-gauge data are the invertible links, which compose, invert, and carry holonomy; the map $U_i\widetilde O_hT_hU_j^{-1}$ of a single head has rank at most the head width and is sheaf-type data on the same graph (Section~\ref{sec:lie_definition}). The implemented model shares one generator $L=OT$ across the edges of a layer, so its links are composites of site data; independent edge generators governed by an action are treated in Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}.

For this shared generator the group link splits exactly into the flat transport $\Omega_{ij}$ and a selective channel term that vanishes on the directions the heads do not read, takes values in those they write, and has the head maps of multi-head attention as its first-order part (Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_split}). With tiled heads (widths summing to $K$) and routing outside the exponent, the routed message is, in every parametrization,
\begin{equation}
m_i=\sum_jU_if(L)\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1}\mu_j,\qquad \Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=\sum_h\beta^{(h)}_{ij}\Pi_h,
\end{equation}
with complementary oblique projectors $\Pi_h$ fixed by the sending maps $T_h$ and $f(L)$ equal to $L$, $I+L$, or $e^L$ in the affine, first-order, and exponential charts (Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}). Multi-head attention is thus a routed oblique projection followed by link transport. With one head the exponential chart gives the lattice hopping term $\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Theta_{ij}\mu_j$, and a standard transformer layer is the affine chart with a full residual (Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}).

The operator is covariant under independent frame changes and factors through the frame-invariant reference moments $\xi_i$ and $C_i=U_i^{-1}\Sigma_iU_i^{-\top}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments}), so covariance constrains none of the shared maps: the frames are a dressing field \citep{Fournel2014Dressing,Francois2019Artificial}, not a symmetry that forces intertwiners, and act in reference coordinates as multiplicative, token- and position-conditioned linear maps. Gaussian-KL routing is exactly a dot product between query expectation parameters and key natural parameters, offset by the key log-partition function (Theorem~\ref{thm:dual_coordinates}), and with a shared projected key covariance it reduces to dot-product attention in whitened coordinates with a key-norm bias (Proposition~\ref{prop:projected_dot_product}). At every positive temperature the routing weights are an exact source posterior and routed content an exact predictive mean (Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}).

A four-layer exponential-chart language model of width $K=128$ with four heads of width 32, in which normalization, a multilayer perceptron (MLP), and a linear decoder surround the communication layer, records token-level test perplexity 34.48 on WikiText-103 at context 128 in a single seed. Here $K$ is the width of the evolving state, not a $d_{\mathrm{model}}$-equivalent capacity: per-type tables that give each vocabulary entry a $K\times K$ block-diagonal frame besides its prior mean and variance hold about 219 million of the 227 million parameters, and the conventional baselines matched in non-embedding parameters to the affine run at this width differ there and in the positional frames (Section~\ref{sec:param_accounting}).

The prototype retains normalization, the MLP, the decoder, and backpropagation, so it is not the program's non-neural architecture, and claims no free energy for the whole stack. Its links share one generator per layer, and its comparison with transformers is matched neither in protocol nor in per-type parameters.

Sections~\ref{sec:construction_overview} through~\ref{sec:probabilistic_status} develop the theory, Section~\ref{sec:lm_realization} reports the language model, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limits and the non-neural path, and the supplement holds secondary material.

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Attention as communication.} Learned communication among agents \citep{foerster2016learning,Sukhbaatar2016CommNet} became attention based with targeted and gated messaging \citep{Jiang2018ATOC,Das2019TarMAC}, and agents trained on shared tasks develop symbol systems \citep{Lazaridou2017Emergence,Mordatch2018Grounded,Lazaridou2020EmergentCommunication}. In TarMAC, the closest of these, a message is used in the receiver's coordinates exactly as sent; here every message is transported between frames.

\paragraph{Probabilistic attention.} Attention weights have been derived as alignment posteriors \citep{Kim2017StructuredAttention,Deng2018LatentAlignment,Bahuleyan2018VariationalAttention,Fan2020BayesianAttention}, as Gaussian-mixture responsibilities with the key-norm term that L2 self-attention also retains \citep{Movellan2020ProbabilisticTransformers,Gabbur2021ProbabilisticAttention,Nguyen2022MGK,Kim2021Lipschitz}, and from regularized, kernel, and energy formulations \citep{Martins2016Sparsemax,Niculae2017Regularized,tsai2019transformer,ramsauer2021hopfield,Hoover2023EnergyTransformer}. Gaussian word embeddings are compared by relative entropy \citep{Vilnis2015Gaussian,Athiwaratkun2017Multimodal}, as are token-specific distributions in Pro-Attention \citep{Bae2022ProAttention}. The routing here recovers the mixture form for a point query and shared key covariance, while a full query covariance adds a pair-dependent term and the compared laws sit in different learned frames.

\paragraph{Group structure in attention.} Rotary encodings rotate queries and keys by position \citep{su2024roformer}, LieRE learns the rotation generators \citep{Ostmeier2025LieRE}, and geometric transform attention applies $\rho(g_ig_j^{-1})$ built from known poses \citep{Miyato2024GTA}; each is a flat link $\Omega_{ij}$ with given frames and zero generator. Gauge-equivariant networks \citep{cohen2019gauge,weiler2021coordinate,He2021GaugeEquivariantTransformer,fuchs2020se3,bronstein2021geometric} treat the frame as geometric data defined up to the structure group, so shared maps must be intertwiners, and lattice gauge equivariant networks \citep{Favoni2022LGECNN,Kanwar2020EquivariantFlow,Boyda2021SUNFlows} transport features with physical link variables on a spacetime lattice. Here frames and generators are learned per token, and the lattice is the agents' communication graph.

\paragraph{Links, quivers, and sheaves.} Group-valued edge variables with the endpoint law are the link fields of lattice gauge theory \citep{WilsonConfinement1974,KogutSusskind1975,Kogut1979RMP,Creutz1983}, matrix links with a compact gauge group appear in linear lattice gauge theory \citep{Wetterich2014LinearLattice}, and linear maps on graph arrows modulo vertex groups are quiver representations \citep{King1994Quiver,DouglasMoore1996Quivers}. Linear maps from vertex spaces into edge spaces are cellular sheaves \citep{HansenGhrist2019}, used in discourse sheaves for agents with private opinions \citep{HansenGhrist2021Discourse} and in sheaf networks \citep{HansenGebhart2020SheafNN,Bodnar2022NeuralSheaf,Barbero2022SheafConnection}, and orthogonal edge transports underlie vector diffusion maps \citep{SingerWu2012}. The comparison and content maps here are sheaf-type data, the comparison cospan following the discourse-sheaf pattern, and the exponential link is the invertible, lattice-gauge member of the family, with a noncompact structure group.

\paragraph{Matrix-valued tokens.} Token matrices appear in compositional matrix-space models \citep{RudolphGiesbrecht2010CMSM,BaroniZamparelli2010Nouns,Mai2019CMOW}, the matrix-vector recursive network \citep{Socher2012MVRNN}, and multiplicative interactions \citep{Jayakumar2020Multiplicative}. There the matrix is an operator composed along the sequence; the frame $U_i$ is a coordinate system for the token's own belief, whose gauge freedom leaves every prediction unchanged.

\section{Statistical Agents on a Bundle over a Point}
\label{sec:construction_overview}

This section defines the agents, the four maps through which they communicate, and the attention operator and language model; Sections~\ref{sec:lie_links} to~\ref{sec:probabilistic_status} establish their properties.

\subsection{Principal frames and statistical associated bundles}
\label{sec:principal_construction}

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a smooth contextual base, \(G=\mathrm{GL}(K,\mathbb R)\), and
\begin{equation}
\pi:\mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal C
\label{eq:principal_bundle}
\end{equation}
a principal bundle of reference frames, on which \(G\) acts freely on the right, preserving \(\pi\) and transitively on each fiber, so one frame identifies a fiber with \(G\). States attach through associated bundles \citep{nakahara2003geometry,frankel2011geometry}. With \(\mathsf V=\mathbb R^K\) the defining representation, let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B=\{\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma):\mu\in\mathsf V,\ \Sigma\succ0\},
\qquad
\rho(g)\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)=\mathcal N(g\mu,g\Sigma g^\top).
\label{eq:gaussian_associated_action}
\end{equation}
The vector and Gaussian statistical bundles
\begin{equation}
E=\mathcal P\times_G\mathsf V,
\qquad
\mathcal G(E)=\mathcal P\times_\rho\mathcal B,
\label{eq:associated_state_bundles}
\end{equation}
with the quotient convention
\begin{equation}
(n\cdot g,b)\sim(n,\rho(g)b)
\label{eq:associated_quotient}
\end{equation}
and \(v\mapsto gv\) in place of \(\rho(g)\) for the vector bundle, carry latent values and distributions over those values, while the principal bundle carries gauge freedom.

The two fibers differ in kind. The fiber \(E_c\) of \(E\) is the sample space, a \(K\)-dimensional real vector space of latent values. A belief is instead a point of the statistical fiber \(\mathcal G(E_c)\), in one frame the manifold \(\mathbb R^K\times\mathrm{SPD}(K)\) of dimension \(K+K(K+1)/2\), not a vector space because the open convex cone \(\mathrm{SPD}(K)\) is not closed under subtraction. Its geometry is the Fisher metric \citep{AmariNagaoka2000,Skovgaard1984}, which at \((\mu,\Sigma)\) evaluates tangent vectors \((v,A),(w,B)\in\mathbb R^K\times\mathrm{Sym}(K)\) as
\begin{equation}
g_{(\mu,\Sigma)}\bigl((v,A),(w,B)\bigr)=v^\top\Sigma^{-1}w+\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Sigma^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}B\bigr).
\label{eq:fisher_metric}
\end{equation}
The fiber is curved; for \(K=1\) it is the hyperbolic plane of curvature \(-1/2\). The group acts by \((\mu,\Sigma)\mapsto(g\mu,g\Sigma g^\top)\), linearly on samples and isometrically on laws, since \((v,A)\mapsto(gv,gAg^\top)\) leaves both terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fisher_metric} unchanged; linear maps between sample fibers act on beliefs by pushforward, invertible ones as Fisher isometries. The attention operator updates the mean linearly in the sample fiber and the covariance by congruence; it is not a geodesic or natural-gradient step on the statistical fiber.

An agent over a contextual region \(\mathcal U_i\) selects a local principal section \(u_i\) and maintains belief and prior sections,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_i&:\mathcal U_i\longrightarrow\mathcal P,
&\pi\circ u_i&=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal U_i},\\
q_i,p_i&\in\Gamma\bigl(\mathcal G(E)|_{\mathcal U_i}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:agent_bundle_sections}
\end{equation}
where \(\Gamma\) denotes smooth sections and \((\mu_i,\Sigma_i)\), \((\mu_{p,i},\Sigma_{p,i})\) are coordinates in \(u_i\). Given a reference trivialization \(\sigma_0\) on the working domain, the frame \(U_i\), defined by \(u_i=\sigma_0\cdot U_i^{-1}\), is the linear isomorphism \(U_i(c):\mathsf V\to E_c\) from \(\sigma_0\)- to \(u_i\)-coordinates, so \(\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i\) are the \(\sigma_0\)-coordinates of the mean. On overlaps, agents may hold different beliefs at the same context, not local representatives of one common belief (Figure~\ref{fig:link_construction}).

\subsection{Agents at a single context}
\label{sec:framed_states}

The token realization evaluates the construction over the zero-dimensional base \(\mathcal C=\{c_*\}\), and each token position \(i\in\mathcal I=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) is an agent, as in targeted multi-agent communication through attention \citep{Das2019TarMAC}. The per-token fibers \(E_i\) are a pullback along a constant map, so no connection, curvature, or topology of the base enters (Appendix~\ref{app:bundle_details}). Agents communicate along a directed graph of allowed sender-receiver pairs \(j\to i\), causally \(j\le i\); this communication graph is incidence data above \(c_*\) and carries the links of Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}.

An agent is a triple \((q_i,p_i,U_i)\) with frame \(U_i:\mathsf V\to E_i\). Its belief and prior are points of the statistical fiber \(\mathcal G(E_i)\) of the bundle of Eq.~\eqref{eq:associated_state_bundles}, in frame coordinates
\begin{align}
q_i&=\mathcal N(\mu_i,\Sigma_i),\qquad \Sigma_i\succ0,\label{eq:gaussian_states}\\
p_i&=\mathcal N(\mu_{p,i},\Sigma_{p,i}),\qquad \Sigma_{p,i}\succ0,\label{eq:base_priors}
\end{align}
with reference-coordinate representation
\begin{equation}
\widetilde q_i=(U_i^{-1})_*q_i
=\mathcal N(\xi_i,C_i),\qquad
\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i,\quad
C_i=U_i^{-1}\Sigma_iU_i^{-\top},
\label{eq:overview_reference_state}
\end{equation}
and an unrestricted positive-definite covariance.

A rechoice \(u_i\mapsto u_i\cdot g_i^{-1}\) of the section of agent \(i\), with \(\sigma_0\) held fixed, changes the coordinates by Eq.~\eqref{eq:associated_quotient}:
\begin{equation}
(\mu_i,\Sigma_i,U_i)\mapsto
(g_i\mu_i,g_i\Sigma_i g_i^\top,g_iU_i),
\qquad g_i\in\mathrm{GL}(K).
\label{eq:passive_site_action}
\end{equation}
These independent passive changes leave the reference moments \((\xi_i,C_i)\) unchanged and hold the reference-space interface parameters fixed; covectors transform contragrediently. A change of \(\sigma_0\) is instead the common reference change \(U_i\mapsto U_ia\), which also transforms the shared interface maps (Appendix~\ref{app:bundle_details}).

\label{sec:embeddings}
Each token type \(c\in\{1,\ldots,V\}\) carries a learned prior and frame coefficients,
\begin{equation}
p_c=\mathcal N(\mu_p^c,\Sigma_p^c),\qquad \phi_c\in\mathfrak{gl}(K),
\label{eq:prior_bank}
\end{equation}
and each agent starts from the prior of its token, \(q_i^{(0)}=p_{x_i}\). This prior bank is a Gaussian embedding table \citep{Vilnis2015Gaussian} extended by frame coefficients.

Each frame composes the token coefficients with a learned positional generator \(Y_i\in\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)\), \(U_i=\exp(\phi_{x_i})\exp(Y_i)\), of positive determinant. In the evaluated runs both are block diagonal with \(B\) blocks of widths \(d_b\), so the frames lie in \(\prod_b\mathrm{GL}^+(d_b)\) (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:heads_irreps}); Appendix~\ref{app:bundle_details} records the restrictions of this chart.

\subsection{Communication: whom to hear and what to say}
\label{sec:role_measurements}

A receiver must decide whom to hear, and each sender's contribution must be put into a form the receiver can absorb; one linear map cannot do both. A head therefore carries four maps on the reference space, split by side and by role:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & Relevance: whom to hear & Content: what is said \\
\midrule
Sender \(j\) & key map \(S_{K,h}\), emits \(S_{K,h}\xi_j\) & sending map \(T_h\), emits \(T_h\xi_j\) \\
Receiver \(i\) & query map \(S_{Q,h}\), forms \(S_{Q,h}\xi_i\) & receiving map \(\widetilde O_h\), writes into \(\xi_i\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The sender emits two partial representations of its belief: its key \((S_{K,h})_*\widetilde q_j\), what makes it findable, and its content \(T_h\xi_j\), what it says. The receiver forms a query \((S_{Q,h})_*\widetilde q_i\), what it is looking for, which is never transmitted, and writes received content into its state with \(\widetilde O_h\). The routing weight \(\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\) is the decision of whom to hear; query and key are what it is computed from, and nothing they measure enters the receiver's state. The interface maps are a protocol shared by all agents of a layer; the private frames translate to the reference space, the sender reading through \(U_j^{-1}\) and the receiver writing through \(U_i\). None of the four maps selects a subset of coordinates or of the frame: each reads linear combinations of all \(K\) reference coordinates, \(T_h\) discards \(\ker T_h\), and \(\widetilde O_h\) writes into its image without reconstructing the sender's state. The maps correspond to the transformer projections \(W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O\) \citep{vaswani2017attention}, with \(\widetilde O_h=O_h\) in the affine chart, and separating an addressing signature from a transmitted value is the message structure of \citet{Das2019TarMAC}.

A layer \(\ell\) maps beliefs \(q_i^{(\ell)}=\mathcal N(\mu_i^{(\ell)},\Sigma_i^{(\ell)})\), with reference moments \(\xi_i^{(\ell)},C_i^{(\ell)}\), to \(q_i^{(\ell+1)}\) through \(H\) heads (Figure~\ref{fig:comparison_content_interfaces}), which need not partition the state or coincide with frame blocks. Head \(h\) has a comparison space, where compatibility is measured, and a content space, through which the message passes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal Y_h=\mathbb R^{r_h},
\qquad
\mathcal Z_h=\mathbb R^{s_h},
\label{eq:head_channel_spaces}
\end{equation}
both shared by all agents, and interfaces of the declared types
\begin{align}
S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)},S_{K,h}^{(\ell)}
&:\mathsf V\longrightarrow\mathcal Y_h,\\
T_h^{(\ell)}&:\mathsf V\longrightarrow\mathcal Z_h,
\qquad
O_h^{(\ell)}:\mathcal Z_h\longrightarrow\mathsf V,
\label{eq:head_interface_types}
\end{align}
with \(S_{Q,h}\) and \(S_{K,h}\) of full row rank, so \(r_h\le K\). The applied receiving map \(\widetilde O_h^{(\ell)}:\mathcal Z_h\to\mathsf V\) is \(O_h^{(\ell)}\) in the affine chart and is evaluated from the stored \((T,O)\) of all heads in the other charts of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_three_charts}. Layer indices are suppressed for a fixed layer, matrices act on column vectors, and \(ij\) maps sender \(j\) to receiver \(i\). Query and key laws share the comparison space:
\begin{align}
q_{Q,i}^{(\ell,h)}
&=\mathcal N\bigl(S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)}\xi_i^{(\ell)},
 S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)}C_i^{(\ell)}S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)\top}\bigr),\label{eq:relational_query_law}\\
p_{K,j}^{(\ell,h)}
&=\mathcal N\bigl(S_{K,h}^{(\ell)}\xi_j^{(\ell)},
 S_{K,h}^{(\ell)}C_j^{(\ell)}S_{K,h}^{(\ell)\top}\bigr),\label{eq:relational_key_law}\\
D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}
&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_{Q,i}^{(\ell,h)}\|p_{K,j}^{(\ell,h)}\bigr).
\label{eq:relational_role_kl}
\end{align}
Since \(C_i^{(\ell)}\succ0\) and each comparison map \(S\) has full row rank, \(y^\top SCS^\top y=(S^\top y)^\top C(S^\top y)>0\) for \(y\ne0\): the comparison uses full nondegenerate reduced covariances, not a singular \(K\)-dimensional projection. For \(S_{Q,h}=S_{K,h}\) every receiver attains the minimum \(D_{ii}^{(h)}=0\) at itself; otherwise identical beliefs can have positive cost, as the generally nonsymmetric form \(W_Q^\top W_K\) allows in dot-product attention. Even when square, these maps are learned parameters shared by all agents, not gauge transformations.

With the local measurements \(A_{Q,i,h}=S_{Q,h}U_i^{-1}\) and \(A_{K,j,h}=S_{K,h}U_j^{-1}\), two agents are compared through the cospan
\begin{equation}
E_i\xrightarrow{A_{Q,i,h}}\mathcal Y_h
\xleftarrow{A_{K,j,h}}E_j.
\label{eq:comparison_cospan}
\end{equation}
On one edge this is a cellular sheaf with vertex stalks \(E_i,E_j\), edge stalk \(\mathcal Y_h\), and restriction maps \(A_{Q,i,h},A_{K,j,h}\) \citep{HansenGhrist2019}, close to the discourse sheaves of opinion dynamics \citep{HansenGhrist2021Discourse}, except that an agent's restriction map depends on its role on the edge and is one reference map dressed by its frame. A cospan chooses no map back from \(\mathcal Y_h\) to \(E_i\); the content maps instead compose to a map from sender to receiver fiber,
\[
E_j\xrightarrow{T_hU_j^{-1}}\mathcal Z_h\xrightarrow{U_i\widetilde O_h}E_i,
\]
the head map \(M_{ij}^{(h)}\) of Section~\ref{sec:attention_operator}. The following proposition, proved in Appendix~\ref{app:bundle_details}, states when a comparison can itself be written through a map from sender to receiver.

\begin{proposition}[Comparison through a link]
\label{prop:comparison_link}
Fix a head, suppress \(h\), and let \(q_i\) and \(q_j\) be nondegenerate Gaussian beliefs with reference laws \(\widetilde q_i=(U_i^{-1})_*q_i\) and \(\widetilde q_j=(U_j^{-1})_*q_j\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(r_h=K\) and \(S_Q\) and \(S_K\) are invertible, then
\begin{equation}
D_{ij}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl((S_Q)_*\widetilde q_i\|(S_K)_*\widetilde q_j\bigr)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_i\|(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij})_*q_j\bigr),
\qquad
\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}=U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1}.
\label{eq:comparison_link}
\end{equation}
The comparison link \(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}\) is group valued, with reference representative \(\mathring\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}=S_Q^{-1}S_K\in\mathrm{GL}(K)\) common to all edges. It obeys the endpoint law \(\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}\prime}_{ij}=g_i\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}g_j^{-1}\) under Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action}; it is unchanged by the redundancy \((S_Q,S_K)\mapsto(AS_Q,AS_K)\) with \(A\in\mathrm{GL}(K)\) and by the common reference change, under which its reference representative transforms by the adjoint action. It is an exponential group link \(U_ie^{L^{\mathrm{cmp}}}U_j^{-1}\), with one comparison generator \(L^{\mathrm{cmp}}\in\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)\) for all edges, exactly when \(S_Q^{-1}S_K\) has a real logarithm \(L^{\mathrm{cmp}}\). For \(S_Q=S_K\) it is the flat identification \(\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}\), and \(D_{ij}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|(\Omega_{ij})_*q_j)\).
\item If \(M:E_j\to E_i\) is linear with \(\operatorname{rank}M<K\), then \(M_*q_j=\mathcal N(M\mu_j,M\Sigma_jM^\top)\) is degenerate and
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_i\|M_*q_j\bigr)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(M_*q_j\|q_i\bigr)=+\infty.
\label{eq:comparison_rank_obstruction}
\end{equation}
In particular, this holds for every head map \(M_{ij}^{(h)}\) with \(s_h<K\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Two square invertible comparison maps thus define a group link, and the routing cost is the divergence of the receiver's belief from the sender's belief transported by it, the flat identification being the case \(S_Q=S_K\); Section~\ref{sec:mixture_derivation} states when it is the content link of Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}. Item~2 gives the role of \(S_{Q,h}\): a partial channel can be compared with a belief only after the receiver is measured in the channel's own space, where both laws are nondegenerate and \(D_{ij}\) is finite for every \(r_h\le K\). Comparing a whole belief against a partial message would penalize disagreement on everything the sender did not address. For \(r_h<K\) the cospan induces only a state-dependent comparison map (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:comparison_lift}), and the evaluated predictor uses only \(D_{ij}\). When the key map factors through the sending map, \(S_{K,h}=A_hT_h\), the receiver selects sources by the content itself; every reported run uses independent key and sending maps.

\subsection{The attention operator and the language model}
\label{sec:attention_operator}
\label{sec:relational_communication}

One layer combines invariant routing weights with covariant content maps: each receiver adds a weighted message from its allowed senders to its mean.

\paragraph{Routing.} At fixed input beliefs and interface maps, source selection minimizes the headwise objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_i^{(\ell,h)}(\beta)
=\sum_j\beta_jD_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}
+\tau_h\sum_j\beta_j\log\frac{\beta_j}{\pi_{ij}^{(h)}},
\qquad \sum_j\beta_j=1,
\label{eq:relational_routing_objective}
\end{equation}
on the support of \(\pi_i^{(h)}\), causally \(\mathcal N_i=\{0,\ldots,i\}\) for every head. A normalization multiplier gives
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}
=\softmax_j\left(-D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}/\tau_h+\log\pi_{ij}^{(h)}\right),
\label{eq:relational_routing}
\end{equation}
the unique categorical minimizer for finite costs, nonempty support, and positive temperature. The source prior sets the communication graph and any positional preference; the temperature is an independent positive scalar (Section~\ref{sec:transformer_comparison_summary}). Section~\ref{sec:probabilistic_status} gives the probabilistic status of the optimizer, and Appendix~\ref{sec:nonuniform_priors} the causal, window, ALiBi, and relative-position priors.

\paragraph{Content.} The sending map \(T_h^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb R^{s_h\times K}\) extracts content, and the receiving map \(\widetilde O_h^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb R^{K\times s_h}\) places the head's aggregate in the full state space:
\begin{align}
v_j^{(\ell,h)}&=T_h^{(\ell)}\xi_j^{(\ell)},\\
z_i^{(\ell,h)}&=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}v_j^{(\ell,h)},\\
m_i^{(\ell)}&=U_i\sum_{h=1}^{H}\widetilde O_h^{(\ell)}z_i^{(\ell,h)},\\
\mu_i^{\mathrm{att},(\ell)}&=c_{\mathrm{res}}\mu_i^{(\ell)}+m_i^{(\ell)},\qquad c_{\mathrm{res}}\in\{0,1\}.
\label{eq:relational_predictive_residual}
\end{align}
For \(c_{\mathrm{res}}=1\) the summed message is added to the mean, as in a transformer residual; for \(c_{\mathrm{res}}=0\) it replaces the mean. The coefficient is architectural, not a belief-gradient step size; its relation to the link charts is discussed with Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}.

For a fixed layer, the content maps of head \(h\) composed with the frames give the head map
\begin{equation}
M_{ij}^{(h)}=(U_i\widetilde O_h)(T_hU_j^{-1})
=U_iM_hU_j^{-1}:E_j\longrightarrow E_i,
\qquad M_h=\widetilde O_hT_h,
\label{eq:main_content_link}
\end{equation}
whose factors need not be square; in the affine chart it is \(U_iO_hT_hU_j^{-1}\), derived in Section~\ref{sec:lie_links} as the first-order channel of a group link. Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action}, direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
(T_hU_j^{-1})'=T_hU_j^{-1}g_j^{-1},\qquad
(U_i\widetilde O_h)'=g_iU_i\widetilde O_h,\qquad
M_{ij}^{(h)\prime}=g_iM_{ij}^{(h)}g_j^{-1},
\label{eq:main_link_endpoint_law}
\end{equation}
an endpoint law carried by the frames, with the reference transfer \(M_h\) unchanged. For invariant scalar routing weights, the routed edge map and the message are
\begin{align}
\mathcal M_{ij}&=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_{ij}^{(h)}
=U_i\left(\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h\right)U_j^{-1}
=U_i\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}TU_j^{-1},\\
m_i&=\sum_j\mathcal M_{ij}\mu_j,
\label{eq:main_routed_link}
\end{align}
where \(T=[T_1;\cdots;T_H]\) and \(\widetilde O=[\widetilde O_1\ \cdots\ \widetilde O_H]\) stack the heads, \(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}=\bigoplus_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}I_{s_h}\), and \(\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}=\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h\) is the reference representative of the routed edge map. Since \(T_h\xi_j=T_hU_j^{-1}\mu_j\), this is the message of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual}. With invariant routing, Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_link_endpoint_law} gives \(\mathcal M_{ij}'=g_i\mathcal M_{ij}g_j^{-1}\) and \(m_i'=g_im_i\), so the attention update \(c_{\mathrm{res}}\mu_i+m_i\) is covariant (Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments}).

\paragraph{Depth.}
\label{sec:depth_prediction}
A model stacks \(N_\ell\) layers with maps \(S_Q^{(\ell)},S_K^{(\ell)},T^{(\ell)},O^{(\ell)}\); the frame field may be shared across depths. An affine normalization and a coordinate residual MLP after attention give
\begin{align}
\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}&=\operatorname{LayerNorm}_{\gamma,b}
 \bigl(\mu_i^{\mathrm{att},(\ell)}\bigr),\\
\mu_i^{(\ell+1)}&=\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}
+W_2^{(\ell)}\operatorname{GELU}\bigl(W_1^{(\ell)}\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}+b_1^{(\ell)}\bigr)
+b_2^{(\ell)},
\label{eq:relational_block_composition}
\end{align}
with layer-normalization parameters \(\gamma,b\) \citep{ba2016layernorm} shared across depths, per-depth MLP parameters, and the Gaussian error linear unit \citep{hendrycks2016gaussian}. Under covariance passthrough, \(\Sigma_i^{(\ell+1)}=\Sigma_i^{(\ell)}\), the covariance is a routing state rather than propagated uncertainty of the nonlinear mean map; under moment matching, attention returns the covariance of the predictive kernel of Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}, passed unchanged through normalization and MLP. Coordinatewise activations and a fixed affine normalization do not in general commute with \(\mathrm{GL}(K)\), so the covariance of Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments} does not extend automatically to this block; Corollary~\ref{cor:normal_form} gives its normal form, and Supplement~\ref{S-sec:nonlinear_gaussian_scope} the Gaussian-closure restriction.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
\label{sec:autoregressive_prediction}
For input tokens \(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\), the sequence model has the normalized factorization
\begin{equation}
p_\theta(x_0,\ldots,x_N)
=p_\theta(x_0)\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}p_\theta(x_{i+1}\mid x_{\le i}),
\label{eq:next_token_factorization_v2}
\end{equation}
completed by a proper initial-token law, and a linear categorical decoder specifies the next-token factors from the final mean,
\begin{equation}
p_\theta(x_{i+1}=v\mid x_{\le i})
=\operatorname{softmax}_v\left(
W_{\mathrm{out}}\mu_i^{(N_\ell)}+b_{\mathrm{out}}
+\kappa_{\mathrm{uni}}\log p_{\mathrm{uni}}\right),
\label{eq:predictive_linear_decoder}
\end{equation}
with a positive vocabulary prior \(p_{\mathrm{uni}}\) of weight \(\kappa_{\mathrm{uni}}\). This decoder is a Gibbs readout of shared-covariance Gaussian templates (Appendix~\ref{app:decoder_readout}); the token-bank decoder of Supplement~\ref{S-sec:prior_bank_readout} is another choice. Next-token cross-entropy trains the composed predictor through all differentiable components; the observed next token is a target, not an input to the causal state (Algorithm~\ref{alg:predictive_content_current}).

\section{Group-Valued Links and Multi-Head Attention}
\label{sec:lie_links}

The content map $M^{(h)}_{ij}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_content_link} is a composite of site frames and shared maps, singular when $s_h<K$, whereas a lattice link variable is a group element whose logarithmic coordinate is the algebra-valued potential. This section places such links on the communication graph and derives from them a head as the channel term of a group link (Section~\ref{sec:lie_lowrank}), the routed message as a routed oblique projection followed by link transport (Section~\ref{sec:lie_aggregation}), and the standard transformer layer as the affine chart with a full residual (Section~\ref{sec:lie_charts}).

\subsection{Group links on the communication graph}
\label{sec:lie_definition}

\begin{definition}[Exponential group link]
\label{def:lie_link}
For a generator $L_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)$ expressed in reference coordinates, the group link with Lie-algebra generator $L_{ij}$, or exponential group link, from sender $j$ to receiver $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{ij}=U_i\exp(L_{ij})U_j^{-1}:E_j\longrightarrow E_i,
\label{eq:lie_link}
\end{equation}
and its reference representative is $\mathring\Theta_{ij}=U_i^{-1}\Theta_{ij}U_j=\exp(L_{ij})$.
\end{definition}

The evaluated model shares one generator per layer, $L^{(\ell)}=O^{(\ell)}T^{(\ell)}$, written $L$ when the layer is fixed; edge-dependent generators are the latent-link extension of Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint law and invertibility]
\label{prop:lie_endpoint}
Let $L_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Under the passive site action of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action}, with the reference generator held fixed, $\Theta_{ij}'=g_i\Theta_{ij}g_j^{-1}$.
\item Under the common reference change $U_i\mapsto U_ia$, the same local link is represented by the generator $a^{-1}L_{ij}a$, the adjoint action of $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ on its Lie algebra.
\item $\det\Theta_{ij}=\det(U_iU_j^{-1})e^{\operatorname{tr}L_{ij}}\ne0$. Every exponential group link is invertible, including links with singular generators, and $\Theta_{ij}^{-1}=U_je^{-L_{ij}}U_i^{-1}$.
\item A matrix $A\in\mathrm{GL}(K,\mathbb R)$ equals $e^{L}$ for some real $L$ exactly when, for each negative eigenvalue of $A$ and each block size, the Jordan blocks of that eigenvalue and size occur an even number of times. Every such $A$ lies in the identity component $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$, and for $K\ge2$ not every element of $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ is of this form.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
Item 4 is the real-logarithm criterion of \citet{culver1966existence}, and item 1 is the endpoint law of a lattice link variable \citep{WilsonConfinement1974,Kogut1979RMP,Creutz1983}; the proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_lie_endpoint}.

The flat identification
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1},\qquad
\mu_j\mapsto\Omega_{ij}\mu_j,
\label{eq:gauge_frame_rotation}
\end{equation}
is the exponential group link with zero generator and the head map of a head with $M_h=I_K$.
\begin{remark}[Flat reference identification]
\label{rem:flat_identification}
For any invertible frames and any finite sequence of sites, $\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{jk}=U_iU_k^{-1}=\Omega_{ik}$, so an ordered product of $\Omega$ maps depends only on its endpoints and every closed product equals $I_K$. The reference identification is pure gauge: it is the coboundary of the frame field.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Matrix-valued and group-valued links.}
\label{sec:matrix_link_fields}
\label{sec:lie_motivation}
The edge objects of Table~\ref{tab:link_conventions} all obey the endpoint law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_link_endpoint_law}, a property of $\operatorname{Hom}(E_j,E_i)$, so covariance does not distinguish matrix maps from group links, and for $G=\mathrm{GL}(K)$ the two coincide exactly on the open dense set of invertible matrices. Group links form a groupoid: loop products are invertible with conjugacy-class invariants, the inverse edges $\Theta_{ij}^{-1}$ cost no parameters and give holonomy to the causal graph (Section~\ref{sec:lie_holonomy}), and the pushforward of a nondegenerate Gaussian is a Fisher isometry. Matrix maps form a monoid: rank cannot increase along a path, reverse edges must be declared, and a singular map leaves the statistical manifold (Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link}). The exponential reaches only $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_endpoint}), and its generator is a linear coordinate for priors, natural gradients, and latent inference (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}) along which no step crosses the singular set, as an additive step can (Supplement~\ref{S-supp:singular_boundary}); this concerns the optimization geometry, not the conditioning of trained links (Section~\ref{sec:trained_links}). Since $M^{(h)}_{ij}N$ is singular for every $N$ when $s_h<K$, no group element factors through a selective channel; the two relate through the Lie algebra (Section~\ref{sec:lie_lowrank}), and a routed sum of heads can be invertible (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:link_algebra}, Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}). With frames in $G=\prod_b\mathrm{GL}(d_b)$, as evaluated, the links take values in $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$, not in $G$, because the full-width interface maps couple the blocks.

\paragraph{Lattice and sheaf data.}
The lattice is the communication graph above $c_*$, with agents as vertices and allowed source relations as edges; it discretizes no base manifold and is not the single-site reduction of large-$N$ gauge theory, whose links are labeled by collapsed lattice directions \citep{EguchiKawai1982}. Invertible links with the endpoint law are lattice-gauge data \citep{WilsonConfinement1974,Kogut1979RMP,Creutz1983} and, as edge variables under independent vertex groups, bifundamental fields of a quiver gauge theory \citep{DouglasMoore1996Quivers}. The singular head maps and comparison maps are quiver-representation and cellular-sheaf data \citep{King1994Quiver,HansenGhrist2019}, with the comparison cospan of Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison_cospan} as the sheaf form; group links are their invertible restriction.

\subsection{Multi-head attention as the channel term of a group link}
\label{sec:lie_lowrank}

A generator built from the stored value and output maps has an exponential in closed form through the entire function
\begin{equation}
\varphi_1(Z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{Z^{n-1}}{n!},\qquad \varphi_1(Z)=(e^Z-I)Z^{-1}\ \text{for invertible}\ Z,
\label{eq:lie_phi1}
\end{equation}
the first of the $\varphi$-functions of exponential integrators \citep{Hochbruck2010}.

\begin{lemma}[Exponential of a low-rank generator]
\label{lem:lie_lowrank}
For $A\in\mathbb R^{K\times s}$ and $B\in\mathbb R^{s\times K}$,
\begin{equation}
\exp(AB)=I_K+A\varphi_1(BA)B.
\label{eq:lie_lowrank}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $n\ge1$, regrouping the product gives $(AB)^n=A(BA)^{n-1}B$. Summing the exponential series term by term gives $I_K+A\bigl(\sum_{n\ge1}(BA)^{n-1}/n!\bigr)B$, and the inner series converges for every $s\times s$ matrix.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Flat transport plus a channel term]
\label{prop:lie_split}
For one head with stored maps $T_h,O_h$ and the generator $L_{ij}=O_hT_h$,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}+U_iO_h\varphi_1(T_hO_h)T_hU_j^{-1}.
\label{eq:lie_split}
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\exp(O_hT_h)$ acts as the identity on $\ker T_h$, so on the subspace $U_j\ker T_h$ that the head does not read, $\Theta_{ij}$ coincides with the flat identification $\Omega_{ij}$.
\item The image of $\exp(O_hT_h)-I$ lies in $\operatorname{im}O_h$, so $\Theta_{ij}$ differs from $\Omega_{ij}$ only in directions that the head writes.
\item For $L_{ij}=sO_hT_h$, $\Theta_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}+sU_iO_hT_hU_j^{-1}+\mathcal O(s^2)$, so the affine-chart head map $U_iO_hT_hU_j^{-1}$ is the first-order channel.
\item $\det\Theta_{ij}=\det(U_iU_j^{-1})e^{\operatorname{tr}(T_hO_h)}$.
\end{enumerate}
The same statements hold for the stacked generator $L=OT=\sum_hO_hT_h$, with $O=[O_1\ \cdots\ O_H]\in\mathbb R^{K\times\bar s}$, $T=[T_1;\cdots;T_H]\in\mathbb R^{\bar s\times K}$, and $\bar s=\sum_hs_h$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:lie_split} is Lemma~\ref{lem:lie_lowrank} with $A=O_h$ and $B=T_h$, conjugated by the frames. Items 1 and 2 are read off from the correction term, which has the form $O_h(\cdots)T_h$. Item 3 follows from $\varphi_1(sZ)=I+\mathcal O(s)$, and item 4 from $\det e^{L}=e^{\operatorname{tr}L}$ with $\operatorname{tr}(O_hT_h)=\operatorname{tr}(T_hO_h)$.
\end{proof}

The link is the identity on the unread complement plus a channel term equal to the affine-chart head map at first order: multi-head attention is the channel term of a group link whose zeroth order is the flat identification. Along one routing row the two orders are the messages of complete-state alignment and of affine-chart prediction (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_two_orders}).

\subsection{Routed transport}
\label{sec:lie_aggregation}
\label{sec:lie_orders}

The group law does not choose an aggregation over sources, and placing the routing inside the exponent, $L_{ij}=O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$, leaves it unspecified (Remark~\ref{S-rem:lie_routed_exponent}). The construction keeps categorical routing outside the exponent with the routing-independent stacked generator $L=OT$, so that $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ remains the probability that head $h$ selects source $j$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}). The heads tile the reference space, $\bar s=K$, and the stacked sending map $T=[T_1;\cdots;T_H]\in\mathbb R^{K\times K}$ is invertible. Write $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}=\bigoplus_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}I_{s_h}$ for the head-diagonal routing matrix, let $\Delta_h$ be the coordinate projector onto the head-$h$ block of $\mathbb R^{K}$, and put
\begin{equation}
\Pi_h=T^{-1}\Delta_hT,\qquad
\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta)=T^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta T.
\label{eq:routed_projectors}
\end{equation}
The stored factors define the affine, first-order, and exponential charts of the implementation through their effective receiving maps,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde O=O,\qquad
\widetilde O=T^{-1}+O,\qquad
\widetilde O=T^{-1}+O\varphi_1(TO)=e^{OT}T^{-1}.
\label{eq:lie_three_charts}
\end{equation}
The affine chart keeps only the first-order channel, so $O=0$ means zero content; the first-order and exponential charts are centered at the head-routed flat transport and differ from second order in $O$ on; only the exponential chart keeps the effective transfer in $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$. Later statements use the content maps only through $\widetilde O_h$ and $T_h$ and so hold in every chart; initialization and $\varphi_1$ are treated in Appendix~\ref{app:chart_initialization}.

\begin{theorem}[Routed transport]
\label{thm:routed_transport}
\label{cor:lie_tiled}
\label{prop:lie_resummed}
\label{prop:lie_order_zero}
Assume tiled heads, $\bar s=K$, with an invertible stacked sending map $T$, routing outside the exponent, and the shared generator $L=OT$, and let $\Pi_h$ and $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta)$ be as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_projectors}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Oblique head projectors: $\Pi_h=(T^{-1})_{:,h}T_h$, $\Pi_h\Pi_g=\delta_{hg}\Pi_h$, $\sum_h\Pi_h=I_K$, $\ker\Pi_h=\ker T_h$, $\operatorname{im}\Pi_h=\bigcap_{g\ne h}\ker T_g$, and
\begin{equation}
\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=T^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\Pi_h.
\label{eq:lie_order_zero}
\end{equation}
The matrix $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalue $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ on $\operatorname{im}\Pi_h$, which has dimension $s_h$, so $\det\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=\prod_h(\beta_{ij}^{(h)})^{s_h}$.
\item Charts: the effective receiving maps of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_three_charts} satisfy $\widetilde OT=f(L)$ with $f(L)=L$, $I_K+L$, and $e^{L}$ in the affine, first-order, and exponential charts. In the exponential chart
\begin{equation}
\widetilde O=e^{L}T^{-1}=T^{-1}+O\varphi_1(TO),\qquad
T\widetilde O=e^{TO},
\label{eq:lie_resummed}
\end{equation}
so $\det(T\widetilde O)=e^{\operatorname{tr}L}>0$.
\item Routed transport: in each chart the routed edge map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_routed_link} and the message are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_{ij}&=U_if(L)\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1},\\
m_i&=\sum_jU_if(L)\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1}\mu_j
=\sum_h\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}U_i\widetilde O_hT_hU_j^{-1}\mu_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:routed_transport}
\end{equation}
\item Group-link factorization: in the exponential chart, with $\Theta_{ij}=U_ie^{L}U_j^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{ij}=\Theta_{ij}U_j\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1},
\label{eq:routed_factorization}
\end{equation}
and the reference representative $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}=U_i^{-1}\mathcal M_{ij}U_j$, which equals $e^{L}\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$, has determinant $e^{\operatorname{tr}L}\prod_h(\beta_{ij}^{(h)})^{s_h}$. If $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}>0$ for every head, $\mathcal M_{ij}$ is invertible and $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}\in\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$. If every head uses the same routing, $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}=\beta_{ij}$, as always holds for $H=1$, the message is
\begin{equation}
m_i=\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Theta_{ij}\mu_j
\label{eq:routed_hopping}
\end{equation}
in the exponential chart, $m_i=\sum_j\beta_{ij}U_iLU_j^{-1}\mu_j$ in the affine chart, and $m_i=\sum_j\beta_{ij}(\Omega_{ij}+U_iLU_j^{-1})\mu_j$ in the first-order chart.
\item Orders: replacing $O$ by $sO$ at fixed $T$ replaces $L$ by $sL$, and the exponential-chart receiving map becomes
\begin{equation}
\widetilde O(s)=T^{-1}+sO\varphi_1(sTO)=T^{-1}+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{s^n}{n!}O(TO)^{n-1},
\label{eq:lie_orders}
\end{equation}
which converges for every $s$. The order-$n$ term of $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}(s)=e^{sL}\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ at $s=1$ is $L^n\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})/n!$. It equals $\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\Pi_h$ at order zero, $O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$ at order one, and at order two
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12O(TO)\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=\tfrac12\sum_{g,h}O_g(T_gO_h)\beta_{ij}^{(h)}T_h.
\label{eq:lie_order_two}
\end{equation}
Under shared routing the order-zero message is $\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Omega_{ij}\mu_j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_routed_transport}.

Item 3 is the central identity: each head selects source $j$ with probability $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ and keeps its oblique component $\Pi_h\xi_j$, which no other head reads, and $f(L)$, dressed by the frames, transports the routed components into the receiver. In the exponential chart this transport is the group link (Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_factorization}), and softmax routing with finite costs makes every routed edge map invertible on the common support of the heads' priors. For shared routing, Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_hopping} is the gauge-covariant hopping term of lattice gauge theory \citep{Kogut1979RMP,Creutz1983} with row-stochastic, state-dependent amplitudes, so the correspondence holds at fixed routing.
In the order expansion of item 5, order zero is a head-routed flat transport, $U_i\sum_h\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\Pi_h\xi_j$, which includes the receiver through $\beta_{ii}^{(h)}$ and is not the residual $c_{\mathrm{res}}\mu_i$; order one, $U_i\sum_jO\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T\xi_j$, is the standard multi-head value path (Section~\ref{sec:value_aggregation}); order two, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_order_two}, is cross-head mixing, in which the block routed by head $h$ is written by $O_h$, read by every head $g$ through $T_gO_h$, and rewritten by $O_g$ without a second routing, and order $n$ makes $n-1$ such round trips with weight $1/n!$. The additive residual connection \citep{He2016ResNet} has no counterpart in tiled aggregation (Remark~\ref{rem:no_complement}).

\subsection{Charts of one group and the standard transformer}
\label{sec:lie_charts}

The three charts are coordinates on one group, not successive approximations: on a specified logarithm domain $\Omega_{ij}+U_iO_{\mathrm{lin}}TU_j^{-1}$ is exactly an exponential group link (Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_charts}), and for tiled heads the three represent the same mean maps up to the real-logarithm domain (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_routed_transport}).

\paragraph{The standard transformer layer.}
Take trivial frames and $H$ heads with value maps $W_V^{(h)}\in\mathbb R^{d_h\times K}$ and output maps $W_O^{(h)}\in\mathbb R^{K\times d_h}$, $Hd_h=K$, stacked as $T=[W_V^{(1)};\cdots;W_V^{(H)}]$ and $O=[W_O^{(1)}\ \cdots\ W_O^{(H)}]$, so that $OT=\sum_hW_O^{(h)}W_V^{(h)}$; in the affine chart $\widetilde O=O$ is the output projection. At fixed routing weights the residual multi-head layer is linear in the joint state $\boldsymbol\xi=(\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_{N-1})\in\mathbb R^{NK}$,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\xi\longmapsto(I_{NK}+\mathfrak L_\beta)\boldsymbol\xi,\qquad
\mathfrak L_\beta=\sum_hB^{(h)}\otimes M_h\in\mathfrak{gl}(NK,\mathbb R),
\label{eq:standard_joint_layer}
\end{equation}
where $B^{(h)}$ is the row-stochastic routing matrix of head $h$, with entries $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$, and $M_h=W_O^{(h)}W_V^{(h)}$. With frames, the $(i,j)$ block of $\mathfrak L_\beta$ is the affine-chart routed edge map $\mathcal M_{ij}$, covariant under $g=\bigoplus_ig_i$. Thus $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ is the first-order chart of $\exp(\mathfrak L_\beta)$, whose identity is the residual connection; it is block lower triangular under causal routing and, on the principal-logarithm domain, the exponential of a real block lower triangular generator. At a single edge the layer is $\delta_{ij}I+O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$, the affine chart with $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$, and neither the tiled exponential message nor its first-order truncation (proofs and qualifications in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_standard_layer}).

Whether $W_OW_V$ is invertible is thus beside the point: $W_OW_V=OT$ is a generator, and the group-valued objects are $e^{OT}\in\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$, always invertible, and $I+OT$, invertible exactly when $-1$ is not an eigenvalue of $OT$. The exponential chart replaces the output projection by $\widetilde O=e^{OT}T^{-1}$, confining the stacked transfer to $\exp(\mathfrak{gl}(K))$, with $O=0$ giving the head-routed flat transport rather than zero content.

\subsection{Holonomy on the causal graph}
\label{sec:lie_holonomy}
\label{sec:flat_identification}

On the causal support $\mathcal N_i=\{0,\ldots,i\}$ every closed directed walk is a self-loop (Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments}), but invertible links admit loop observables. For an invertible link $\Theta_{ij}$, $j<i$, let $\mathring\Theta_{ij}=U_i^{-1}\Theta_{ij}U_j$.

\begin{definition}[Triangle holonomy]
\label{def:lie_triangle}
For $k<j<i$ put
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_{ijk}=\Theta_{ik}^{-1}\Theta_{ij}\Theta_{jk}=U_k\mathring\Theta_{ik}^{-1}\mathring\Theta_{ij}\mathring\Theta_{jk}U_k^{-1}.
\label{eq:lie_triangle}
\end{equation}
The field is flat on the complete causal support when $\mathcal H_{ijk}=I$ for every strict triple.
\end{definition}

Since $\mathcal H_{ijk}\mapsto g_k\mathcal H_{ijk}g_k^{-1}$ under the passive site action, its conjugacy class is invariant; it is the holonomy of the loop $k\to j\to i\to k$ based at $k$, whose last step traverses the long edge backward with its inverse. On graphs with missing edges a cycle set must be declared, and the recurrence characterization below need not apply.

\begin{proposition}[Flatness and pure gauge]
\label{prop:lie_flat}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $\Theta_{ij}$, $j<i$, be arbitrary $K\times K$ matrices with $\Theta_{ij}\Theta_{jk}=\Theta_{ik}$ for all $k<j<i$. Then $\Theta_{ij}=\Theta_{i,i-1}\Theta_{i-1,i-2}\cdots\Theta_{j+1,j}$, and conversely every choice of adjacent matrices defines a flat field through these ordered products.
\item If every $\Theta_{ij}$ is invertible, the field is flat exactly when $\Theta_{ij}=g_ig_j^{-1}$ for some $g_i\in\mathrm{GL}(K)$, and then $g_i=\Theta_{i0}$ up to a common right factor. For singular matrices this equivalence fails: a shared projector $\Theta_{ij}=P=P^2$ is flat and is not of the form $g_ig_j^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
The proof, by induction on $i-j$ and the cocycle identity, is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_lie_flat}.

Hence $\Omega_{ij}$ and every reference field $h_ih_j^{-1}$, such as a rotary link $e^{(p_i-p_j)Y}$, are pure gauge. Since $\mathcal H_{ijk}$ is conjugate to $\mathring\Theta_{ik}^{-1}\mathring\Theta_{ij}\mathring\Theta_{jk}$, no choice of frames creates or removes curvature, and a constant reference link $\mathring\Theta_{ij}=A\ne I$ is never flat.

With one shared generator, $\Theta_{ij}=\Theta_{ii}\Omega_{ij}$ with the self-link factor $\Theta_{ii}=U_ie^{L}U_i^{-1}$, and likewise
\begin{equation}
M_{ij}^{(h)}=M_{ii}^{(h)}\Omega_{ij},\qquad
M_{ii}^{(h)}=U_i\widetilde O_hT_hU_i^{-1},
\label{eq:main_processing_transport}
\end{equation}
so $\mathcal H_{ijk}=U_ke^{L}U_k^{-1}$ on every triangle. The implemented field is flat only if $e^{L}=I$ and carries one conjugacy class per layer, that of $e^{L}$: it is group valued but composed of site data and one learned matrix. Edge-dependent generators give holonomy that varies between triangles, the latent-link model of Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}, whose zero-width limit is the implemented chart (Remark~\ref{S-rem:lie_static_limit}); routed holonomies and curvature measures on $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ are in Supplement~\ref{S-supp:holonomy}.

\section{Gauge Covariance and the Reference-Coordinate Normal Form}
\label{sec:passive_covariance}

The operator of Section~\ref{sec:attention_operator} is written with frames, but every routing cost and message factors through the reference moments $(\xi_i,C_i)$. This section makes that statement exact, identifies the parameter redundancies it implies, states what the covariance constrains, and gives the normal form showing where the learned frames act.

\begin{lemma}[Invariance of divergences under invertible maps]
\label{lem:glk_invariance}
For probability laws $P$ and $Q$ on $\mathbb R^K$ with densities and any $A\in\mathrm{GL}(K)$,
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(A_*P\|A_*Q)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q).
\label{eq:glk_invariance}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Reference-moment factorization]
\label{thm:reference_moments}
Fix a layer, its comparison maps $S_{Q,h}$, $S_{K,h}$, its stored content maps $T_h$, $O_h$ with effective receiving maps $\widetilde O_h$ in one chart of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_three_charts}, its source priors, its temperatures, and its residual coefficient $c_{\mathrm{res}}$, and write $M_h=\widetilde O_hT_h$ for the reference head transfers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every routing cost $D_{ij}^{(h)}$ and weight $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ depends on the states only through the reference moments $(\xi_j,C_j)$, and the attention update has the reference form
\begin{equation}
U_i^{-1}\mu_i^{\mathrm{att}}=c_{\mathrm{res}}\xi_i+\sum_h\widetilde O_hz_i^{(h)}
=c_{\mathrm{res}}\xi_i+\sum_{j,h}\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h\xi_j,
\label{eq:projected_transformer_residual}
\end{equation}
with the routed sent blocks $z_i^{(h)}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}T_h\xi_j$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual}.
\item Under the passive site action of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action}, the routing weights are unchanged, and the head maps $M_{ij}^{(h)}=U_iM_hU_j^{-1}$, the routed edge maps $\mathcal M_{ij}=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_{ij}^{(h)}$, and the messages transform as
\begin{equation}
M_{ij}^{(h)\prime}=g_iM_{ij}^{(h)}g_j^{-1},
\qquad \mathcal M_{ij}'=g_i\mathcal M_{ij}g_j^{-1},
\qquad m_i'=g_i m_i.
\label{eq:relational_covariance}
\end{equation}
\item The operator is unchanged by the common reference change $(U_i,S_{Q,h},S_{K,h},T_h,O_h)\mapsto(U_ia,S_{Q,h}a,S_{K,h}a,T_ha,a^{-1}O_h)$ with $a\in\mathrm{GL}(K)$, under which the stored generator $L=OT$ transforms by the adjoint action $L\mapsto a^{-1}La$, by $(S_{Q,h},S_{K,h})\mapsto(AS_{Q,h},AS_{K,h})$ with $A\in\mathrm{GL}(r_h)$, and by the headwise change $(T_h,O_h)\mapsto(B_hT_h,O_hB_h^{-1})$ with $B_h\in\mathrm{GL}(s_h)$, in each chart. The common reference change also leaves every operation in local coordinates unchanged.
\item For a closed walk $\gamma=(i_0,\ldots,i_n=i_0)$, the routed loop product $\mathcal H_\gamma=\mathcal M_{i_0i_1}\cdots\mathcal M_{i_{n-1}i_0}$ satisfies $\mathcal H_\gamma'=g_{i_0}\mathcal H_\gamma g_{i_0}^{-1}$, and its trace is a polynomial in the routing weights with loop-independent coefficients $\operatorname{Tr}(M_{h_1}\cdots M_{h_n})$. On the causal support $\mathcal N_i=\{0,\ldots,i\}$, every closed walk is a self-loop.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:proofs_covariance} proves the lemma, the theorem, and Corollary~\ref{cor:normal_form} below. Item (2) is a covariance under changes of each agent's coordinates; item (3) lists internal redundancies of the parameters, since the model determines $\mathcal Y_h$ and $\mathcal Z_h$ only as abstract vector spaces. The comparison redundancy is cogredient, unlike the contragredient redundancy of dot-product scores, and Appendix~\ref{app:interface_redundancies} gives the balance laws that gradient flow conserves along both.

\paragraph{What the covariance constrains.}
The theorem uses only that routing and content read the states through $(\xi_i,C_i)$, so $g_i=U_i^{-1}$ removes the frames, and any operator with this property, including dot-product attention on dressed means, is covariant in the same sense. In the terminology of \citet{Fournel2014Dressing}, $U_i$ is a local dressing field and the local $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ covariance is artificial \citep[Sec.~2.2]{Francois2019Artificial}: the learned frames fix a global trivialization, an $\{e\}$-structure \citep[Sec.~2.1.1]{weiler2021coordinate}, and the covariance places no constraint on $S_Q$, $S_K$, $T$, $O$, or a link generator. The intertwiner requirement of gauge-equivariant networks (Section~\ref{sec:related_work}) would remove the low rank, block mixing, and composition defect of the selective links for $G=\prod_b\mathrm{GL}(d_b)$; these are available because the frames are learned (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:heads_irreps}).

\subsection{The full Gaussian score and its query--key cross term}
\label{sec:dot_product_derivation}

Fix a layer, a head, and nonempty allowed source sets, and suppress layer and head indices except on $r_h$. Define the projected moments
\begin{equation}
a_i=S_Q\xi_i,\quad b_j=S_K\xi_j,\quad
V_{Q,i}=S_QC_iS_Q^\top,\quad V_{K,j}=S_KC_jS_K^\top.
\label{eq:projected_qk_moments}
\end{equation}
The state covariances are positive definite and both comparison maps have full row rank, so $V_{Q,i},V_{K,j}\succ0$. The Gaussian KL formula gives
\begin{align}
D_{ij}=\frac12\bigl[&\operatorname{Tr}(P_jV_{Q,i})
+a_i^\top P_ja_i-2a_i^\top P_jb_j+b_j^\top P_jb_j-r_h\nonumber\\
&+\log\det V_{K,j}-\log\det V_{Q,i}\bigr],
\qquad P_j=V_{K,j}^{-1}.
\label{eq:projected_full_kl}
\end{align}
The mean compatibility enters as $-a_i^\top P_jb_j$, in local coordinates $\mu_i^\top\mathsf B_{ij}\mu_j$ (Eq.~\eqref{S-eq:comparison_bilinear_tensor}), so independent $S_Q,S_K$ supply distinct query and key observables. When $P_j$ varies with the source, $a_i^\top P_ja_i$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(P_jV_{Q,i})$ are pair dependent, not row constants.

\begin{theorem}[Dual-coordinate form of Gaussian-KL routing]
\label{thm:dual_coordinates}
Write the query law in expectation coordinates and the key law in natural coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\eta_{Q,i}=\bigl(a_i,V_{Q,i}+a_ia_i^\top\bigr),\qquad
\theta_{K,j}=\bigl(P_jb_j,-\tfrac12P_j\bigr),\qquad
\langle\eta,\theta\rangle=\eta_1^\top\theta_1+\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_2\theta_2),
\end{equation}
and let $A(\theta_{K,j})=\tfrac12b_j^\top P_jb_j+\tfrac12\log\det V_{K,j}+\tfrac{r_h}2\log2\pi$ be the Gaussian log-partition, with conjugate $A^*(\eta_{Q,i})=-H(q_{Q,i})$. Then
\begin{equation}
D_{ij}=A(\theta_{K,j})+A^*(\eta_{Q,i})-\langle\eta_{Q,i},\theta_{K,j}\rangle,
\label{eq:dual_pairing}
\end{equation}
and the categorical optimizer is exactly $\beta_{ij}=\softmax_j\bigl(\langle\eta_{Q,i},\theta_{K,j}\rangle/\tau-A(\theta_{K,j})/\tau+\log\pi_{ij}\bigr)$: a dot product in $\mathbb R^{r_h+r_h(r_h+1)/2}$ between query expectation parameters and key natural parameters, with the key log-partition as a source bias. No covariance hypothesis is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With $-H(q_{Q,i})=-\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi eV_{Q,i})$, expanding the right side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual_pairing} reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected_full_kl} term by term. This is the mixed form of the divergence between members of an exponential family \citep[Sec.~5.2, Eq.~(5.11)]{Wainwright2008}. The term $A^*(\eta_{Q,i})$ is constant on row $i$ and cancels in the softmax.
\end{proof}
With a shared second-order key coordinate $-\tfrac12P_j$, only the mean block $a_i^\top V_*^{-1}b_j$ of the pairing varies with the source.

\begin{proposition}[Projected Gaussian reduction to biased dot-product routing]
\label{prop:projected_dot_product}
Suppose all keys used by a head have the same projected covariance $V_{K,j}=V_*\succ0$. Let $\tau>0$, let each prior row be a probability distribution with $\pi_{ij}>0$ on its allowed support, and let costs be the uncapped Gaussian divergences. Define $\mathsf q_i=V_*^{-1/2}a_i$ and $\mathsf k_j=V_*^{-1/2}b_j$, using the symmetric positive-definite inverse square root. Then the exact categorical optimizer is
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ij}=\softmax_j\left(
\frac{\mathsf q_i^\top\mathsf k_j}{\tau}
-\frac{\|\mathsf k_j\|^2}{2\tau}
+\log\pi_{ij}\right).
\label{eq:main_dot_product_scope}
\end{equation}
The query covariances may differ across receivers. If, more strongly, $V_{Q,i}=V_{K,j}=\sigma_h^2I_{r_h}$, then $D_{ij}=\|a_i-b_j\|^2/(2\sigma_h^2)$.
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:proof_projected_dot_product} gives the proof. Up to a row constant $D_{ij}=\tfrac12\|\mathsf q_i-\mathsf k_j\|^2$, so this is L2 attention in whitened coordinates, under the isotropic hypothesis that of \citet{Kim2021Lipschitz}. The query entropy is row constant, so the Gibbs row is the mean-field responsibility of a Gaussian mixture, for a point query that of \citet{Movellan2020ProbabilisticTransformers} and \citet{Nguyen2022MGK} (Remark~\ref{S-rem:gaussian_mixture_attention}).

\subsection{Key-norm bias and its compensation}
\label{sec:bias_cancellation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:main_dot_product_scope} is dot-product attention with the same prior exactly when the squared key norms are constant on the allowed support. A base prior $\pi^0_{ij}$ is reproduced by the state-dependent compensation
\begin{equation}
\pi_{ij}=
\frac{\pi^0_{ij}\exp(\|\mathsf k_j\|^2/(2\tau))}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal N_i}\pi^0_{ik}\exp(\|\mathsf k_k\|^2/(2\tau))},
\label{eq:projected_key_bias_compensation}
\end{equation}
which the evaluated model does not use. Otherwise the two routings differ: for $r_h=1$, $\tau=1$, $a_i=0$, keys $b_1=0,b_2=1$, shared unit variance, and uniform prior, dot-product routing gives $(1/2,1/2)$ and Gaussian routing $(1,e^{-1/2})/(1+e^{-1/2})$. Neither LayerNorm nor high-dimensional concentration removes the key-norm term at $\tau=\sqrt{r_h}$; Supplement~\ref{S-supp:routing} bounds the routing error sharply (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:sharp_bound}) and separates temperature from covariance scale.

\subsection{Value, output, and residual links}
\label{sec:value_aggregation}

Concatenating the $z_i^{(h)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected_transformer_residual} gives the multi-head output projection $\widetilde O\operatorname{concat}_h(z_i^{(h)})$ \citep[Sec.~3.2.2]{vaswani2017attention} with $W_V^{(h)}=T_h$ and $W_O^{(h)}=\widetilde O_h$ in column-vector convention, free in the affine chart and, for tiled heads in the exponential chart, restricted to $W_O=e^{L}W_V^{-1}$ on the real-logarithm domain (Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}). In local coordinates the operation is $\sum_{j,h}\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_{ij}^{(h)}\mu_j$, so $M_{ij}^{(h)}$ is the framed value/output composition, not the query--key tensor, and the residual $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$ is the persistence term of the predictive kernel of Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive_kernel}. Rotating both projected laws, as rotary embedding rotates queries and keys \citep{su2024roformer}, gives by Lemma~\ref{lem:glk_invariance} a relative-position score for arbitrary covariances (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:rope}).

\section{Probabilistic Status of Routing and Content}
\label{sec:probabilistic_status}

This section shows that the Gibbs row of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_routing} is an exact posterior of a declared local source model at every temperature and that the residual content update of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual} is an exact predictive mean.

\subsection{Variational source selection}
\label{sec:mixture_derivation}
For one layer, head, and receiver (indices suppressed), let $q_{Q,i}(y)$ be the projected query law, $p_{K,j}(y)$ the candidate key laws, and $\pi_{ij}>0$ prior probabilities summing to one on a nonempty allowed support $\mathcal N_i$. The local source model and factorized recognition law are
\begin{align}
\mathbb P_i(y,J=j)&=\pi_{ij}p_{K,j}(y),\label{eq:mixture_joint}\\
\mathbb Q_{i,\beta}(y,J=j)&=\beta_{ij}q_{Q,i}(y).\label{eq:mixture_posterior}
\end{align}
The states enter as data fixed by the prefix before routing; the query law is the clamped recognition marginal of $y$, and $\beta_i$ is the only recognition variable. Expanding the density ratio gives
\begin{align}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb Q_{i,\beta}\|\mathbb P_i)
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\beta_{ij}D_{ij}
+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\beta_{ij}\log\frac{\beta_{ij}}{\pi_{ij}},
\label{eq:mixture_free_energy}\\
D_{ij}&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{Q,i}\|p_{K,j}),
\label{eq:mixture_energy_entropy}
\end{align}
so the categorical entropy belongs to the variational objective. Key laws as generative components and the query law as recognition factor fix the direction of $D_{ij}$; Supplement~\ref{S-supp:routing} records the other directions. At a declared temperature $\tau>0$ the entropy-regularized objective has the unique interior minimizer over the simplex (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs_probabilistic})
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ij}^*=\frac{\pi_{ij}\exp(-D_{ij}/\tau)}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal N_i}\pi_{ik}\exp(-D_{ik}/\tau)},
\label{eq:mixture_softmax_general}
\end{equation}
which is the Gibbs row of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_routing}. The objective is the KL of the declared joint at $\tau=1$ and an entropy-regularized extension otherwise; Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status} supplies a normalized model in which the row is exact at every temperature.

Complete-state alignment, $S_Q=S_K=I_K$, scores sender $j$ by $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|(\Omega_{ij})_*q_j)$, and the comparison link is the content link $\Theta_{ij}$ exactly when $S_K=S_Qe^{L}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs_probabilistic}); the evaluated model imposes neither.

\subsection{Exact posterior and predictive mean}
\label{sec:exact_posterior}

\begin{theorem}[Exact probabilistic status of routing and content]
\label{thm:probabilistic_status}
Fix a layer and its input states, and let $D_{ij}^{(h)}\ge0$ be the routing costs.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\tau_h>0$, give each receiver and head a source label $J_i^{(h)}$ with prior $\pi_i^{(h)}$ and a binary compatibility record $o_i^{(h)}$ with $P(o_i^{(h)}=1\mid J_i^{(h)}=j)=\exp(-D_{ij}^{(h)}/\tau_h)$. The joint is normalized on any source graph, and $P(J_i^{(h)}=j\mid o_i^{(h)}=1)=\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_routing}. The objective of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_routing_objective} is $\tau_h$ times the label free energy $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\beta\|\pi_i^{(h)})-\sum_j\beta_j\log P(o_i^{(h)}=1\mid J_i^{(h)}=j)$, whose minimum is the surprisal $-\log P(o_i^{(h)}=1)$.
\item Let the head labels be independent with laws $\beta_i^{(h)}$, and take the directed kernel
\begin{equation}
k_i'\mid k,J_i\sim\mathcal N\Bigl(c_{\mathrm{res}}k_i+\sum_hM_{iJ_i^{(h)}}^{(h)}k_{J_i^{(h)}},Q_i\Bigr),\qquad c_{\mathrm{res}}\in\{0,1\},\quad Q_i\succeq0,
\label{eq:predictive_kernel}
\end{equation}
which is normalized for any rank of the head maps, as a degenerate Gaussian when $Q_i$ is singular. At state values $k_j=\mu_j$ its predictive mean is $\mu_i^{\mathrm{att}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual}. With independent source states $k_j\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)$, the self edge included, its moment-matched covariance in reference coordinates is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_i^{\mathrm{att}}={}&\widetilde Q_i+c_{\mathrm{res}}\Bigl[C_i+\sum_h\beta_{ii}^{(h)}\bigl(M_hC_i+C_iM_h^\top\bigr)\Bigr]
+\sum_j\sum_{h,h'}c_{ij}^{hh'}M_hC_jM_{h'}^\top\\
&+\sum_h\Bigl[\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}n_{ij}^{(h)}n_{ij}^{(h)\top}-\bar n_i^{(h)}\bar n_i^{(h)\top}\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:predictive_kernel_covariance}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde Q_i=U_i^{-1}Q_iU_i^{-\top}$, $M_h=\widetilde O_hT_h$, $c_{ij}^{hh}=\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$, $c_{ij}^{hh'}=\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\beta_{ij}^{(h')}$ for $h\ne h'$, $n_{ij}^{(h)}=M_h\xi_j$, and $\bar n_i^{(h)}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}n_{ij}^{(h)}$. The local covariance $U_iC_i^{\mathrm{att}}U_i^\top$ obeys the passive law.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of item 1]
Because $D_{ij}^{(h)}\ge0$, each record kernel takes values in $(0,1]$ and is normalized. Records attached to different receivers and heads are conditionally independent children of the states and labels, so the joint is normalized. Bayes' rule gives $P(J=j\mid o=1)=\pi_{ij}e^{-D_{ij}/\tau}/\sum_k\pi_{ik}e^{-D_{ik}/\tau}$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_routing}. Since $-\log P(o=1\mid J=j)=D_{ij}/\tau$, the label free energy equals $\tau^{-1}$ times the objective, and its minimum is $-\log\sum_j\pi_{ij}e^{-D_{ij}/\tau}=-\log P(o=1)$. Item 2 is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs_probabilistic}.
\end{proof}
The record is a declared modeling event, as for optimality variables in control as inference \citep{ToussaintStorkey2006,Kappen2012,Levine2018}, not an observed datum; since such exactness holds for any nonnegative cost, item 1 shows that Gibbs routing is consistent with exact inference, not that attention must be inference. Item 2 holds in every chart of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_three_charts} with $T_h$, $\widetilde O_h$, and $Q_i$ free; it declares the persistence coefficient, additivity across heads, and one label for record and content, and excludes normalization, the MLP, and the collapse of the predictive mixture to a point state between layers. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs_probabilistic} gives the covariance floor; Supplement~\ref{S-supp:scope} relates it to a normalized population-level latent-source model.

\subsection{Receiver inference and latent links}
\label{sec:probabilistic_extensions}
Read as evidence about the receiver, the routed messages give a Gaussian power posterior whose closed-form minimizer fuses a receiver prior with the messages by a Kalman-type gain and tends to the residual-free head sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual} in a stated limit (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:receiver_inference}). Promoted to latent variables with a normalized prior anchored at $L=OT$, edge- and depth-dependent generators $L^{\ell}_{ij}$ give an unimplemented model whose zero-width limit is the exponential chart and whose MAP stationarity condition is a link field equation, linearized for one head around a flat anchor to a screened, massive discrete Maxwell equation on the depth-by-token complex (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}).

\section{Language-Model Realization}
\label{sec:lm_realization}

The evaluated model realizes the causal architecture of Sections~\ref{sec:attention_operator} and~\ref{sec:depth_prediction} at $K=128$ with four layers, the exponential chart of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_three_charts}, moment-matched covariance, and learned positional frames, trained on WikiText-103 \citep{merity2017pointer}; its saved configuration and protocol define the empirical claim. Section~\ref{sec:param_accounting} sets it beside protocol-matched baselines, and Section~\ref{sec:chart_runs} compares the charts at $K=20$. An affine-chart run at $K=160$ reaches the lowest test perplexity reported here but forms no group-valued link; it and an affine run at $K=256$ are in Supplement~\ref{S-supp:runs}.

\subsection{Forward computation}
\label{sec:lm_forward}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:predictive_content_current} specifies the evaluated run; each layer performs one predictive readout and no iterative belief update. With $H=B=4$ heads and $d_b=r_h=s_h=32$, % config.json:embed_dim=128,n_heads=4; summary.json:relational_operators.score_rank_per_head=32,content_rank_per_head=32
the stacked $T^{(\ell)},O^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb R^{K\times K}$ define the shared generator $L^{(\ell)}=O^{(\ell)}T^{(\ell)}$ and the applied receiving map
\begin{equation}
\widetilde O^{(\ell)}=\bigl(T^{(\ell)}\bigr)^{-1}+O^{(\ell)}\varphi_1\bigl(T^{(\ell)}O^{(\ell)}\bigr)=e^{L^{(\ell)}}\bigl(T^{(\ell)}\bigr)^{-1},
\label{eq:lm_exponential_receiving_map}
\end{equation}
so $\widetilde O^{(\ell)}T^{(\ell)}=e^{L^{(\ell)}}$, and an edge on which every head gives the same source the same weight carries that weight times $\Theta_{ij}=U_ie^{L^{(\ell)}}U_j^{-1}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}). The source prior is uniform on the causal support $j\le i$, so positions enter only through the positional frames. Every KL cost is bounded to $[0,8K]=[0,1024]$, % config.json:kl_max=1024
which keeps the routing the exact categorical optimum and exact label posterior of Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}, the bounded cost being nonnegative, and equal to the unbounded routing when the bound is inactive (Appendix~\ref{app:run_numerics}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Causal Gaussian attention with the exponential content chart, as evaluated}
\label{alg:predictive_content_current}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Tokens $x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}$ and, in training, targets $y_i=x_{i+1}$; the prior bank $\{(\mu_p^c,\Sigma_p^c,\phi_c)\}_{c=1}^V$ with diagonal $\Sigma_p^c$; positional generators $Y_0,\ldots,Y_{N-1}$; layer maps $S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)},S_{K,h}^{(\ell)},T_h^{(\ell)},O_h^{(\ell)}$; decoder parameters.
\STATE Initialize $(\mu_i^{(0)},\Sigma_i^{(0)})=(\mu_p^{x_i},\Sigma_p^{x_i})$ and realize the frame $U_i=\exp(\phi_{x_i})\exp(Y_i)$ once for all layers.
\STATE Form each $\widetilde O^{(\ell)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lm_exponential_receiving_map} in double precision and split it into head blocks $\widetilde O_h^{(\ell)}$.
\STATE Set $\log\pi_{ij}=0$ for $j\le i$ and $\pi_{ij}=0$ otherwise.
\FOR{$\ell=0,\ldots,N_\ell-1$}
\STATE Form $\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i^{(\ell)}$ and $C_i=U_i^{-1}\Sigma_i^{(\ell)}U_i^{-\top}$.
\FOR{$h=1,\ldots,H$}
\STATE Form the query and key Gaussian pushforwards under $S_{Q,h}^{(\ell)}$ and $S_{K,h}^{(\ell)}$ with full reduced covariances; denote their KL divergence by $D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}$.
\STATE Bound the cost, $\bar D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}=\min\{\max\{D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)},0\},8K\}$, and set $\beta_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}\bigl(\log\pi_{ij}-\bar D_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}/\tau_h\bigr)$ with $\tau_h=\sqrt{r_h}$.
\STATE Compute $z_i^{(\ell,h)}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(\ell,h)}T_h^{(\ell)}\xi_j$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute $m_i^{(\ell)}=U_i\sum_h \widetilde O_h^{(\ell)}z_i^{(\ell,h)}$ and $\mu_i^{\mathrm{att},(\ell)}=c_{\mathrm{res}}\mu_i^{(\ell)}+m_i^{(\ell)}$ with $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$.
\STATE Set $\Sigma_i^{(\ell+1)}$ to the moment-matched covariance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive_kernel_covariance} with the reference floor $10^{-4}\operatorname{Tr}(C_i)I/K$, mapped to local coordinates and projected onto the local diagonal by matching marginal variances.
\STATE Set $\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}=\operatorname{LayerNorm}_{\gamma,b}(\mu_i^{\mathrm{att},(\ell)})$.
\STATE Set $\mu_i^{(\ell+1)}=\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}+W_2^{(\ell)}\operatorname{GELU}(W_1^{(\ell)}\bar\mu_i^{(\ell)}+b_1^{(\ell)})+b_2^{(\ell)}$; the normalization and MLP leave $\Sigma_i^{(\ell+1)}$ unchanged.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Compute $p(y_i\mid x_{\le i})=\operatorname{softmax}\bigl(W_{\mathrm{out}}\mu_i^{(N_\ell)}+b_{\mathrm{out}}+\kappa_{\mathrm{uni}}\log p_{\mathrm{uni}}\bigr)_{y_i}$.
\IF{training}
\STATE Backpropagate the mean next-token cross-entropy to every trainable parameter and update with AdamW \citep{Loshchilov2019AdamW}.
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Architecture and training of the evaluated run}
\label{sec:relational_run}

The evaluated run has $K=128$, four frame blocks of dimension $32$, $N_\ell=4$ layers, and model seed 6, % config.json:embed_dim,n_heads,n_layers,seed
with per-layer maps $S_{Q,h},S_{K,h},T_h\in\mathbb R^{32\times128}$ and $O_h\in\mathbb R^{128\times32}$ initialized so that $\widetilde OT=I$ (Appendix~\ref{app:run_optimization}). % summary.json:relational_operators.score_shape,content_shapes,score_initialization; REC128 Sec. 1-2 (relational_output_init=identity)

with no ALiBi bias \citep{press2022train} or rotary rotation. % summary.json:alibi_slopes=null; config.json:pos_rotation=none

Covariance is moment matched and stored diagonally, a partial gauge fixing (Appendix~\ref{app:run_configuration}), and the normalization and MLP pass it through. % config.json:predictive_covariance=moment_matched,predictive_covariance_floor=1e-4,block_mlp_covariance=passthrough

The residual coefficient is $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$, % config.json:attention_residual=true
which with the exponential chart counts the receiver twice, through $\beta_{ii}^{(h)}$ and through the residual (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_standard_layer}); the evaluated layer is thus an architectural choice that contains the exponential group link, not a derived update.

\subsection{Correspondence with a conventional transformer and parameter accounting}
\label{sec:param_accounting}

Read as a decoder-only transformer (Table~\ref{tab:transformer_correspondence}), the prior mean $\mu_p^c$ is the token embedding and the decoder the unembedding, untied and with the same unigram prior in the realization and its baselines. $S_{Q,h},S_{K,h},T_h,O_h$ have the shapes of $W_Q^{(h)},W_K^{(h)},W_V^{(h)},W_O^{(h)}$, giving $4K^2$ attention parameters per layer, and the feed-forward block of width $8K$ is the same. With identity frames and a common isotropic covariance the model is a width-$K$ transformer with distance-based attention plus a per-key bias (Section~\ref{sec:transformer_comparison_summary}) and one shared normalization.

The per-type and per-position storage has no transformer counterpart: each vocabulary entry also stores a diagonal variance and a block-diagonal frame generator with $\sum_bd_b^2=Kd_b$ entries, and each position a generator of the same size. The frame enters every layer, also through the content map $M^{(h)}_{ij}=U_i\widetilde O_hT_hU_j^{-1}$, which depends on the type and position of sender and receiver, where a transformer applies one $W_O^{(h)}W_V^{(h)}$ to every pair (Section~\ref{sec:passive_covariance}). Thus $K$ sets the shapes that $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ sets in a transformer, while each token type stores $K(d_b+2)=34K$ numbers, mostly as a matrix that multiplies the state in every layer: a matrix-space word representation \citep{RudolphGiesbrecht2010CMSM,Socher2012MVRNN,Mai2019CMOW} acting as a multiplicative token-conditioned map \citep{Jayakumar2020Multiplicative}, as in a hypernetwork \citep{Ha2017HyperNetworks}. The positional frames extend rotary query-key rotations \citep{su2024roformer} to invertible blocks that also act on content.

The token-frame table holds $50{,}257\times4\times32^2=205{,}852{,}672$ of them ($90.67\%$) and the vocabulary-indexed tables $225{,}201{,}617$ ($99.19\%$), % resources.json:parameter_breakdown.per_type_tables
while the context-processing parameters number $1{,}315{,}584$ (Appendix~\ref{app:run_parameters}). % resources.json:parameter_breakdown.context_processing

\paragraph{Trained exponential links.}
\label{sec:trained_links}
The trained links lack the conditioning of the small-width exponential arms of Section~\ref{sec:chart_runs}. In double precision from the final weights (Appendix~\ref{app:run_diagnostics}), every layer has $\det e^{L^{(\ell)}}=e^{\operatorname{tr}L^{(\ell)}}\le e^{-674}$ and an eigenvalue of $L^{(\ell)}$ with real part at most $-39.5$, so $\sigma_{\min}(e^{L^{(\ell)}})\le e^{-39.5}\approx7\times10^{-18}$ because $\sigma_{\min}(A)\le\min|\lambda(A)|$, and the numerical rank of $e^{L^{(\ell)}}$ at relative threshold $10^{-3}$ is 47, 61, 83, and 90 of 128. % TIL Sec. 3.4; recomputed from final_model.pt on CPU in float64 by build/checks/review_math/recompute_spectra.py (output recompute_spectra_output.txt)
The group link is invertible in exact arithmetic (Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_endpoint}) but numerically of low rank, a selective channel reached by contracting most directions rather than by the rank deficiency of a single head. Its inverse has spectral norm at least $e^{123}$ in one layer, since $\|A^{-1}\|_2\ge1/\min|\lambda(A)|$, so holonomy and inverse-edge diagnostics must be computed in log space (Supplement~\ref{S-supp:holonomy}). These describe one single-seed checkpoint, not a trend in width or training length.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Contribution and limits}
\label{sec:limitations}

The contribution is a construction in which token occurrences are statistical agents over one context and attention is their communication, with the exact identities of Theorems~\ref{thm:routed_transport}, \ref{thm:reference_moments}, \ref{thm:dual_coordinates}, and~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}, checked in float64 at small sizes (Supplement~\ref{S-app:identity_checks}). No priority is claimed for separate comparison and content maps or for Gaussian-KL or gauge-equivariant routing; the construction supplies the setting in which their roles are defined. The independent-content joint gives the attention sublayer a probabilistic reading but leaves the content maps underdetermined. With one shared generator per layer, the exponential chart changes the parametrization, center, and domain of the content maps but not the family of tiled mean maps (Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_charts}); whether group geometry helps optimization at larger width, or becomes expressive once edge-dependent generators carry an action (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links}), is open. The comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:lm_realization} establish no parameter or sample efficiency, no benefit from learned frames or a chart, and no necessity of any component.

\subsection{Toward non-neural language architectures}
\label{sec:nonneural}

The evaluated model is a hybrid. Its affine LayerNorm, coordinate GELU MLP, and linear decoder are standard network components, and every parameter is trained by backpropagation of the next-token cross-entropy. The communication layer is stated without neurons: its states are beliefs, its frames private coordinates, its interface maps a protocol shared within a layer, its routing weights a source posterior, its content a predictive mean, and its transport a group-valued link. That layer is the non-neural candidate, and Section~\ref{sec:lm_realization} shows that it trains inside a working predictor when the neural components are kept. Three pieces are missing before the working hypothesis can be tested. The MLP acts on each agent's mean with shared weights, a private computation rather than communication, but no agent-level interpretation of it is derived; that SiLU and GELU are exponential self-links (Remark~\ref{S-rem:lie_routed_exponent}) derives nothing further. Predictive-coding networks approximate backpropagation by local variational updates \citep{Whittington2017PCBackprop,Millidge2022PCBackprop}, but whether these agents can learn their priors, frames, and interface maps by local inference is untested. No joint law of the stack makes layer composition descend one free energy; the evidential receiver (Supplement~\ref{S-sec:receiver_inference}) acts within one layer. Reading the links as parallel transports along a discretized base would also require agents at distinct contexts (Appendix~\ref{app:bundle_details}). Tying the key map to the transmitted content and absorbing the message by Bayesian conditioning would derive the receiving map and the residual rather than learn them; this variant is not developed here.

\subsection{Experiments needed to separate the contributions}
\label{sec:needed_experiments}

A conventional transformer with a learned per-type block read map of the frame table's size, and realizations with frames frozen at initialization or set to the identity, would separate frame geometry from table size; both controls are implemented and unrun. The conventional baselines need a learning-rate sweep selected on validation, and isolating covariance-aware routing needs a mean-only or dot-product retraining control, which the current code no longer provides. The exponential chart should be trained with $c_{\mathrm{res}}=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}) at the protocol of the affine run at $K=160$ (Supplement~\ref{S-supp:run_k160}). Every arm needs replicates varying both model and data-order seeds, and trained-link spectra and holonomies should be recorded across training in log space (Section~\ref{sec:trained_links}). Tables~\ref{tab:placeholder_interventions} and~\ref{tab:placeholder_main_results} complete this program.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper builds the communication layer of a program that reads neural language models as one realization of interacting statistical agents. \section{Bundle Details}
\label{app:bundle_details}

This appendix records the bundle-theoretic details behind Section~\ref{sec:framed_states}. For token attention the contextual dependence is evaluated at a fixed context \(c_*\), while \(i\in\mathcal I=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) labels the token agents. Keeping a separately labeled copy of the fiber for each token is the pullback along the constant map \(\iota:\mathcal I\to\mathcal C\), \(\iota(i)=c_*\):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_{\mathcal I}=\iota^*\mathcal P,
\qquad E_{\mathcal I}=\iota^*E.
\label{eq:token_bundle_pullback}
\end{equation}
Write \(E_i\) for the vector fiber at token \(i\). This notation distinguishes token incidence from the contextual base of the general construction. Because \(\iota\) is constant, \(\iota^*\mathcal P=\mathcal I\times\mathcal P_{c_*}\) canonically, so the token construction sees no connection, curvature, or topology of \(\mathcal P\). A vertical automorphism of \(\iota^*\mathcal P\) is an independent automorphism of each copy of the \(G\)-torsor \(\mathcal P_{c_*}\), and once a reference frame is fixed each such automorphism is one element \(g_i\in G\); the gauge group of the token construction is therefore \(G^N\). A gauge transformation of the contextual bundle restricts at \(c_*\) to a single automorphism of \(\mathcal P_{c_*}\) and acts on the tokens only through the diagonal of \(G^N\). The independent site changes of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action} are thus gauge transformations of the pulled-back bundle that the contextual bundle induces only when all \(g_i\) coincide, and they act as passive changes of the agents' coordinates.

The section \(u_i\) of each token agent and the reference trivialization give the frame \(U_i:\mathsf V\to E_i\), and the statistical fiber of agent \(i\) is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(E_i)=\{\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma):\mu\in E_i,\ \Sigma\succ0\},
\label{eq:overview_statistical_fiber}
\end{equation}
with the Fisher metric of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fisher_metric} and the group action of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian_associated_action}.

A general contextual principal bundle need not admit a global section, and the factorized attention formulas use the reference trivialization \(\sigma_0\) only on their working domain. Over the point \(c_*\) a reference frame always exists, since \(\mathcal P_{c_*}\) is a nonempty \(G\)-torsor, and any two choices differ by right multiplication by one element of \(G\). Replacing \(\sigma_0\) by \(\sigma_0\cdot a\) with \(u_i\) held fixed gives \(U_i\mapsto U_ia\), the common reference change of Section~\ref{sec:framed_states}, with one \(a\in\mathrm{GL}(K)\) for all agents. It changes the reference moments as \(\xi_i\mapsto a^{-1}\xi_i\) and \(C_i\mapsto a^{-1}C_ia^{-\top}\), leaves the local moments unchanged, and changes the representations of the shared interface maps of Section~\ref{sec:role_measurements} as \(S_{Q,h}\mapsto S_{Q,h}a\), \(S_{K,h}\mapsto S_{K,h}a\), \(T_h\mapsto T_ha\), and \(O_h\mapsto a^{-1}O_h\). In each chart of Section~\ref{sec:lie_links} the applied receiving map then transforms as \(\widetilde O_h\mapsto a^{-1}\widetilde O_h\) and a generator by the adjoint action \(L\mapsto a^{-1}La\), so the measured laws and the content maps between agents are unchanged, and the operator is unchanged (Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments}). Holding the interface matrices fixed while changing the reference basis would specify a different communication rule.

The communication graph is additional data. Through the canonical identification of the copies of the fiber over \(c_*\), the flat identification \(\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}\) is the transition function between the sections \(u_j\) and \(u_i\): it converts the \(u_j\)-coordinates of a vector into its \(u_i\)-coordinates, which is why it is pure gauge (Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}). A group link with a nonzero generator, and every singular head map, is additional data on the edges of the graph rather than a transition function of \(\mathcal P\) or the parallel transport of a connection on the base. Placing tokens at distinct contexts \(\iota(i)=c_i\) and realizing links by parallel transport along declared base curves would make the contextual connection enter the attention operator; that generalization is not developed here.

The frame parametrization \(U_i=\exp(\phi_{x_i})\exp(Y_i)\) of Section~\ref{sec:framed_states} has positive determinant, while the passive law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive_site_action} is defined for arbitrary invertible frames. A single real exponential is not a global chart of \(\mathrm{GL}^+(K)\): for \(K\ge2\) the exponential map of \(\mathfrak{gl}(K,\mathbb R)\) is neither injective nor surjective onto \(\mathrm{GL}^+(K)\), and a product of real exponentials need not have a real logarithm \citep{culver1966existence}. These restrictions determine which frames the parametrization reaches; they do not alter the endpoint law of the links. With block-diagonal coefficients, as in the evaluated runs, the same statements hold blockwise in \(\prod_b\mathrm{GL}^+(d_b)\).

The comparison link of Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link} is built from the same frames and reference maps.
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link}]
(1) By the definition of the reference laws, \((S_Q)_*\widetilde q_i=(S_QU_i^{-1})_*q_i\) and \((S_K)_*\widetilde q_j=(S_KU_j^{-1})_*q_j\). Pushing both laws forward by the invertible map \(U_iS_Q^{-1}\) sends the first to \(q_i\) and the second to \((U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1})_*q_j\), and a common invertible pushforward leaves the divergence unchanged (Lemma~\ref{lem:glk_invariance}), which gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison_link}. Under \(U_i\mapsto g_iU_i\) the link becomes \(g_i\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}g_j^{-1}\). The redundancy leaves \((AS_Q)^{-1}AS_K=S_Q^{-1}S_K\) unchanged, and the common reference change gives \(U_ia(S_Qa)^{-1}S_Ka(U_ja)^{-1}=\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}\), with reference representative \(a^{-1}S_Q^{-1}S_Ka\). The equation \(U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1}=U_ie^{L^{\mathrm{cmp}}}U_j^{-1}\) holds exactly when \(S_Q^{-1}S_K=e^{L^{\mathrm{cmp}}}\), which is the exponential case of Definition~\ref{def:lie_link} with an edge-independent generator, and \(S_Q=S_K\) gives \(U_iU_j^{-1}=\Omega_{ij}\).
(2) The law \(M_*q_j\) is concentrated on the affine subspace \(M\mu_j+\operatorname{im}M\), which has dimension \(\operatorname{rank}M<K\) and is therefore a Lebesgue-null set, while \(q_i\) has an everywhere positive density. Hence \(q_i\) assigns this subspace zero mass and \(M_*q_j\) assigns it full mass, so neither law is absolutely continuous with respect to the other, and the divergence is infinite in both directions. A head map with \(s_h<K\) has rank at most \(s_h\).
\end{proof}
By \citet{culver1966existence}, the real logarithm in item~1 exists exactly when every Jordan block of a negative eigenvalue of \(S_Q^{-1}S_K\) occurs an even number of times.

\section{Proofs for Section~\ref{sec:lie_links}}
\label{app:proofs_links}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_endpoint}}
\label{app:proof_lie_endpoint}

Item 1 follows by substituting $U_i'=g_iU_i$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_link}. Item 2 follows from $\exp(a^{-1}La)=a^{-1}\exp(L)a$, which holds term by term in the exponential series, so that $(U_ia)\exp(a^{-1}L_{ij}a)(U_ja)^{-1}=\Theta_{ij}$. Item 3 is the identity $\det e^{L}=e^{\operatorname{tr}L}$ \citep{Hall2015}. The characterization in item 4 is Theorem~1 of \citet{culver1966existence}, and membership in $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ follows from item 3. For the last statement, $\operatorname{diag}(-1,-2)\oplus I_{K-2}$ has positive determinant and two negative eigenvalues, each with a single Jordan block of size one, so it has no real logarithm.

When the reference representative has no eigenvalue on the closed negative real axis, it has a unique real logarithm whose eigenvalues lie in the strip $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi$, the principal logarithm \citep[Thm.~1.31]{Higham2008}. The principal logarithm is therefore a chart on the open subset of $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ without eigenvalues on the closed negative real axis; it is not a global chart of the group. In lattice gauge theory the link $e^{aA}$ has the algebra-valued potential $A$ as its logarithmic coordinate, and $A$ transforms as a connection under local gauge transformations \citep{Creutz1983}; in Definition~\ref{def:lie_link} the frames dress the potential instead, which is why the generator is invariant under independent site changes of frame. The flat identification of Remark~\ref{rem:flat_identification} is trivialized explicitly by the global reference space, and a finite discrete base has no overlap or continuum topology obstruction; the communication graph may nevertheless carry independent connection data with nonidentity cycle products, so the identity of that remark applies to $\Omega$ and not to every link.

\subsection{The two orders of a single-head link}
\label{app:proof_two_orders}

Contracting the scaled link $\Theta_{ij}(s)=U_ie^{sO_hT_h}U_j^{-1}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_split} against one routing row at fixed source states gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Theta_{ij}(s)\mu_j
=\sum_j\beta_{ij}\Omega_{ij}\mu_j
+s\sum_j\beta_{ij}U_iO_hT_hU_j^{-1}\mu_j+\mathcal O(s^2).
\label{eq:lie_two_orders}
\end{equation}
The zeroth-order term transports complete source states by the flat identification. It is the message of complete-state alignment, the comparison with $S_Q=S_K=I$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link} (Section~\ref{sec:mixture_derivation}). The first-order term is the predictive message of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual} in the affine chart. Both message forms are thus orders of one exponential link. This is a statement about the form of the message and does not recover either objective. With an independent source label for each head, the zeroth-order term becomes a head-routed flat transport (Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}).

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}}
\label{app:proof_routed_transport}

Throughout, the heads tile the reference space, $\bar s=K$, the stacked sending map $T\in\mathbb R^{K\times K}$ is invertible, and $L=OT$.

\emph{Item 1.} The relations $\Delta_h\Delta_g=\delta_{hg}\Delta_h$ and $\sum_h\Delta_h=I$ survive conjugation by $T$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_order_zero} is $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\Delta_h$ conjugated by $T$. Writing $T^{-1}$ by block columns gives $\Pi_h=(T^{-1})_{:,h}T_h$. Since $\Delta_hTx$ has the single nonzero block $T_hx$, $\Pi_hx=0$ exactly when $T_hx=0$, and $x\in\operatorname{im}\Pi_h=T^{-1}\operatorname{im}\Delta_h$ exactly when $T_gx=0$ for every $g\ne h$. Finally, $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ is similar through $T$ to the diagonal matrix $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}$, whose eigenvalue $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ has the eigenspace $\operatorname{im}\Delta_h$ of dimension $s_h$. Conjugation by $T^{-1}$ carries this eigenspace to $\operatorname{im}\Pi_h$, and the determinant is the product of the eigenvalues.

\emph{Item 2.} In the affine chart $\widetilde OT=OT=L$, and in the first-order chart $(T^{-1}+O)T=I+L$. In the exponential chart, Lemma~\ref{lem:lie_lowrank} with $A=O$ and $B=T$ gives $e^{L}=I+O\varphi_1(TO)T$, and multiplying on the right by $T^{-1}$ gives both forms of $\widetilde O$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_resummed}, together with $\widetilde OT=e^{L}$. Since $T(OT)^n=(TO)^nT$ for every $n$, $T\widetilde O=Te^{OT}T^{-1}=e^{TO}$, and $\det e^{TO}=e^{\operatorname{tr}(TO)}=e^{\operatorname{tr}L}$.

\emph{Item 3.} Block multiplication gives $\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}\widetilde O_hT_h=\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$, the reference representative of the routed edge map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_routed_link}. Inserting $TT^{-1}$ and using item 2,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=(\widetilde OT)(T^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T)=f(L)\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}).
\label{eq:app_routed_reference}
\end{equation}
Conjugating by the frames and summing over the sources gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_transport}.

\emph{Item 4.} With $f(L)=e^{L}$, $U_ie^{L}\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1}=(U_ie^{L}U_j^{-1})(U_j\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})U_j^{-1})$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_factorization}. The determinant of $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}=e^{L}\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ follows from item 1 and $\det e^{L}=e^{\operatorname{tr}L}$. If every $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$ is positive, this determinant is positive, so $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}\in\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ and $\mathcal M_{ij}=U_i\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}U_j^{-1}$ is invertible. Shared routing has $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ij}I_K$, hence $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=\beta_{ij}I_K$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_transport} gives $\mathcal M_{ij}=\beta_{ij}U_if(L)U_j^{-1}$. For the three choices of $f$ this is $\beta_{ij}\Theta_{ij}$, $\beta_{ij}U_iLU_j^{-1}$, and $\beta_{ij}(\Omega_{ij}+U_iLU_j^{-1})$.

\emph{Item 5.} Under $O\mapsto sO$ the generator becomes $sL$ and the exponential-chart receiving map becomes $e^{sL}T^{-1}$. Since $L^nT^{-1}=(OT)^nT^{-1}=O(TO)^{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$, the exponential series gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_orders}, which converges absolutely for every $s$ because the exponential series does, and the first form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_orders} is Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_resummed} for the factor $sO$. Expanding $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}(s)=e^{sL}\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ term by term at fixed routing, the order-zero term is $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$, given by item 1. At order one $L\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=OTT^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$, and at order two $\tfrac12L^2\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})=\tfrac12OTOTT^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=\tfrac12O(TO)\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$. Because $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}$ is head diagonal, the $(g,h)$ block of $(TO)\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}$ is $(T_gO_h)\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$, which gives the head expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_order_two}. Under shared routing the order-zero reference map is $\beta_{ij}I_K$, and $U_i\beta_{ij}\xi_j=\beta_{ij}\Omega_{ij}\mu_j$.

\begin{remark}[No complement]
\label{rem:no_complement}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}, if $O$ is also invertible, the complement projector $O(TO)^{-1}T$ of Lemma~\ref{S-lem:lie_complement} equals $I_K$ and $Oe^{TO}(TO)^{-1}=e^{L}T^{-1}$, so the complement-preserving aggregation of Proposition~\ref{S-prop:lie_complement_persistence} has no complement term, and its message is the exponential-chart message of item 3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}.
\end{remark}
\begin{proof}
With $O$ and $T$ invertible, $O(TO)^{-1}T=OO^{-1}T^{-1}T=I$, and $O(TO)^nO^{-1}=(OT)^n$ gives $Oe^{TO}(TO)^{-1}=Oe^{TO}O^{-1}T^{-1}=e^{OT}T^{-1}$. The complement term of Eq.~\eqref{S-eq:lie_self_complement} therefore vanishes, and its head-sum term, with receiving blocks taken from $Oe^{TO}(TO)^{-1}=e^{L}T^{-1}$, is the exponential-chart message of item 3.
\end{proof}

The same message is the mean of a head-labeled transport with one pinned generator. If each head draws its own source label $J^h$ with probability $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}$, independently across heads, and the reference message is $\sum_he^{L}\Pi_h\xi_{J^h}$, then by Eq.~\eqref{eq:lie_order_zero} its mean is $\sum_h\sum_j\beta_{ij}^{(h)}e^{L}\Pi_h\xi_j=e^{L}\sum_jT^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T\xi_j=\sum_j\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T\xi_j$. This is the static limit of the latent-link model of Supplement~\ref{S-sec:latent_links} (Remark~\ref{S-rem:lie_static_limit}). Only the edge maps are group valued: the aggregate over sources is a predictive mean, a combination of transported vectors with routing weights (Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic_status}), and not a group operation. In the affine chart the transport is the frame-dressed Lie-algebra element $U_iLU_j^{-1}$, a matrix that may be singular, and in the first-order chart it is $U_i(I+L)U_j^{-1}=\Omega_{ij}+U_iLU_j^{-1}$.

\emph{Residuals.} The complement-preserving aggregation of Proposition~\ref{S-prop:lie_complement_persistence} keeps the receiver's state on the directions that no head writes, and by Remark~\ref{rem:no_complement} that complement is empty when the heads tile, so the receiver's own state enters only through the self-weights $\beta_{ii}^{(h)}$. A projected residual requires channels that do not tile, $\bar s<K$, whose unread coordinates pass through the layer unchanged, the role the residual stream plays for subspaces that no head reads or writes \citep{elhage2021mathematical}. The additive residual $c_{\mathrm{res}}\mu_i$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:relational_predictive_residual} is the residual connection of residual networks \citep{He2016ResNet}, an architectural term that this aggregation does not produce.

\emph{Reach of the charts.} The orders do not enlarge the family of mean maps. For invertible $T$ the map $O\mapsto T^{-1}+O$ is a bijection, so the first-order truncation reaches every receiving map, as the affine chart does. Since $OT$ ranges over all of $\mathfrak{gl}(K)$, the exponential chart reaches a receiving map $\widetilde O$ exactly when $\widetilde OT$ has a real logarithm $L$, with $O=LT^{-1}$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_endpoint} this forces $\det(\widetilde OT)>0$. The three parametrizations therefore represent the same tiled shared-generator mean maps up to the real-logarithm domain. They differ in what the zero of the stored factor means (zero content in the affine chart; in the first-order chart, which has the affine function class, a decay of the stored factor pulls toward the head-routed flat transport rather than toward zero content), in the reachable component, and in the conditioning and optimization geometry of the trained links, and these are the properties that a comparison between them tests.

The identities were also evaluated in float64 by the script \texttt{check\_\allowbreak routed\_\allowbreak transport.py}, distributed with the manuscript source, at $K=6$ with three heads of width two and four causal positions, a random invertible $T$, random $O$ and frames, and random positive routing weights. Items 1 to 5 and Remark~\ref{rem:no_complement} hold to a relative error of at most $2\times10^{-15}$, apart from the determinant identities, which hold to $1.3\times10^{-14}$; the factorization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:routed_factorization} holds to $8\times10^{-16}$, the computed eigenvalues of $\Pi(\boldsymbol\beta_{ij})$ agree with the head weights to $4\times10^{-16}$, and every reference representative $\mathring{\mathcal M}_{ij}$ had positive determinant. These sizes test the formulas and carry no information about trained models.

\subsection{Two charts of one group}
\label{app:proof_lie_charts}

The phrase ``first-order channel'' could suggest that the affine head map is a weak-field truncation of the exponential link. On a specified logarithm domain the relation is instead an exact change of coordinates. In this appendix the width $s\le K$ is arbitrary. Fix $O\in\mathbb R^{K\times s}$ and $T\in\mathbb R^{s\times K}$ with $TO$ invertible, so that $\mathbb R^K=W\oplus W'$ with $W=\operatorname{im}O$ and $W'=\ker T$, and let $\Pi=O(TO)^{-1}T$ be the projector onto $W$ along $W'$. For $B\in\mathrm{GL}(s)$ put
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon(B)=(I-\Pi)+OB(TO)^{-1}T,
\label{eq:lie_embed}
\end{equation}
the operator that fixes $W'$ pointwise and acts on $W$ as $B$ in the basis formed by the columns of $O$.

\begin{proposition}[Two charts of one group]
\label{prop:lie_charts}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Upsilon:\mathrm{GL}(s)\to\mathrm{GL}(K)$ is an injective homomorphism with $\det\Upsilon(B)=\det B$. Its image is the factor of the block-diagonal Levi subgroup $\mathrm{GL}(W)\times\mathrm{GL}(W')$ that acts trivially on $W'$.
\item Linear links: for $C\in\mathbb R^{s\times s}$, $I+OCT=\Upsilon(I_s+CTO)$, so $\det(I+OCT)=\det(I_s+CTO)$.
\item Exponential links: $\exp(OT)=\Upsilon(e^{TO})$.
\item Exact conversion: let $O_{\mathrm{lin}}\in\mathbb R^{K\times s}$ be such that $I_s+TO_{\mathrm{lin}}$ has no eigenvalue on $(-\infty,0]$, put $Z=\log(I_s+TO_{\mathrm{lin}})$ with the principal logarithm, and put $O=O_{\mathrm{lin}}\varphi_1(Z)^{-1}$. Then $\varphi_1(Z)$ is invertible, $TO=Z$, and
\begin{equation}
\exp(OT)=I_K+O_{\mathrm{lin}}T.
\label{eq:lie_conversion}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
Item 4 says that the flat identification plus a linear channel term, $\Omega_{ij}+U_iO_{\mathrm{lin}}TU_j^{-1}$, is exactly an exponential group link for every $O_{\mathrm{lin}}$ in an open set containing $O_{\mathrm{lin}}=0$. On their common domain the linear and logarithmic parametrizations describe the same group elements, related by $I_s+TO_{\mathrm{lin}}=e^{TO}$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_split} states that the two charts agree to first order at the identity. Using one numerical generator in both $I+L$ and $e^{L}$ is a first-order approximation only: at $L=I$, $e^{L}=eI$ while $I+L=2I$. The charts differ in three further ways. The linear parameter space contains the singular matrices, and a step in $(O_{\mathrm{lin}},T)$ can carry $\det(I_K+O_{\mathrm{lin}}T)=\det(I_s+TO_{\mathrm{lin}})$ through zero, whereas the logarithmic chart cannot reach that boundary and, by Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_endpoint}, also excludes the matrices without a real logarithm, including every matrix of negative determinant. An optimizer acting on the factors induces different changes of the composite matrix in each chart, so the two charts carry different optimization geometries. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:lie_charts} concerns an unrouted operator with one generator; routed head sums are covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport}, and edge-dependent generators leave the one-generator family. \subsection{The standard layer at fixed routing}
\label{app:proof_standard_layer}

Three qualifications fix the scope of the correspondence of Section~\ref{sec:lie_charts}. First, $\exp(\mathfrak L_\beta)$ composes routed messages along multi-step paths within one layer, so it differs from the per-edge link with routing outside the exponent of Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport} (shown below). Second, at a single edge the standard layer $\delta_{ij}I+O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$ is neither the tiled exponential message nor that message's first-order truncation, whose zeroth order is the head-routed flat transport rather than the identity on the receiver; the routing-independent receiver term $\xi_i$ equals $\sum_j\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T\xi_j$ for all states only when every head routes the receiver to itself with unit weight (shown below). Third, the routing weights depend on the states, so the layer is linear only at fixed routing. Outside the principal-logarithm domain the layer is not the exponential of a principal real generator.

Whether $W_OW_V$ is invertible does not decide the group structure. For $H\ge2$ a single head has $\operatorname{rank}W_O^{(h)}W_V^{(h)}\le K/H<K$ and is singular, while the stacked $W_OW_V$ is invertible for generic weights but is not constrained to be invertible by training. The exponential chart $\widetilde O=e^{OT}T^{-1}$ is defined on the set of invertible $T$. Combining the exponential chart with $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$, as the language model of Section~\ref{sec:relational_run} does, adds the receiver twice, once through the self-weights $\beta_{ii}^{(h)}$ inside the zeroth order and once through the residual; this paper gives no derivation of that combination.

At fixed routing weights the residual multi-head layer maps $\xi_i$ to $\xi_i+\sum_j\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h\xi_j$, which is the $i$th block of $(I_{NK}+\mathfrak L_\beta)\boldsymbol\xi$ with $\mathfrak L_\beta=\sum_hB^{(h)}\otimes M_h$; the $(i,j)$ block of $\mathfrak L_\beta$ is $\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h=O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T$. With frames the blocks are $U_i(\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}M_h)U_j^{-1}=\mathcal M_{ij}$, so $g\mathfrak L_\beta g^{-1}$ has the blocks $g_i\mathcal M_{ij}g_j^{-1}$ for $g=\bigoplus_ig_i$. Causal routing has $\beta_{ij}^{(h)}=0$ for $j>i$, so $\mathfrak L_\beta$ is block lower triangular, $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ is invertible exactly when every self-block $I_K+\sum_h\beta_{ii}^{(h)}M_h$ is, and the determinant of $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ is the product of the determinants of its diagonal blocks $I_K+\sum_h\beta_{ii}^{(h)}M_h$ (conjugated by $U_i$ when frames are present). The first position has itself as its only source, so $\beta_{00}^{(h)}=1$ for every head and its self-block is $I_K+\sum_hM_h=I_K+OT$.

The spectrum of $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ is the union of the spectra of its diagonal blocks. If none of these meets $(-\infty,0]$, the principal logarithm of $I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ exists, is real, and satisfies $\exp(\log(I+\mathfrak L_\beta))=I+\mathfrak L_\beta$ \citep[Thm.~1.31]{Higham2008}. As a primary matrix function it equals $p(I+\mathfrak L_\beta)$ for a polynomial $p$ that interpolates the logarithm on the spectrum \citep[Ch.~1]{Higham2008}, and every polynomial in a block lower triangular matrix is block lower triangular. The square $(\mathfrak L_\beta^2)_{ik}=\sum_j(\mathfrak L_\beta)_{ij}(\mathfrak L_\beta)_{jk}$ receives a contribution from every intermediate position $j$ with $k\le j\le i$, so $\exp(\mathfrak L_\beta)$ contains multi-step routed paths that the per-edge maps of Theorem~\ref{thm:routed_transport} do not.

For the receiver term, suppose that $\sum_j\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T\xi_j=\xi_i$ for all states. Then $\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ii}T=I_K$ and $\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}T=0$ for $j\ne i$. The first equation makes $\widetilde O\boldsymbol\beta_{ii}$ invertible, so $\widetilde O$ is invertible and every $\beta_{ii}^{(h)}$ is positive; the second then gives $\boldsymbol\beta_{ij}=0$ for $j\ne i$, so every head routes only to the receiver, $\boldsymbol\beta_{ii}=I_K$, and $\widetilde O=T^{-1}$. A float64 evaluation at $K=6$, $H=3$, and $N=4$ with random frames, maps, and causal routing reproduces the block-triangular determinant identity to a relative error of $7\times10^{-15}$, the affine-chart message with $c_{\mathrm{res}}=1$ to $4\times10^{-16}$, and a principal logarithm whose strictly upper blocks are below $2\times10^{-15}$ (script of Appendix~\ref{app:proof_routed_transport}).

\subsection{Interface redundancies and balance laws}
\label{app:interface_redundancies}

Item (3) of Theorem~\ref{thm:reference_moments} has the following consequences. When $S_{Q,h}=S_{K,h}$, the shared comparison map enters only through its row space. The cogredient redundancy $(S_{Q,h},S_{K,h})\mapsto(AS_{Q,h},AS_{K,h})$ differs from the contragredient redundancy $(S_{Q,h},S_{K,h})\mapsto(AS_{Q,h},A^{-\top}S_{K,h})$ of the dot-product score $\xi_i^\top S_{Q,h}^\top S_{K,h}\xi_j$; the two share only $A\in\mathrm O(r_h)$, the residual freedom of whitening in Proposition~\ref{prop:projected_dot_product}. In particular, a common rescaling of $S_{Q,h}$ and $S_{K,h}$ does not change the routing temperature. Only $\mathcal Y_h$ and $\mathcal Z_h$ as abstract vector spaces are determined by the model; their bases are internal parameter redundancies. Gradient flow on a loss $\mathcal E$ that depends on these maps only through the costs and messages therefore conserves $S_{Q,h}S_{Q,h}^\top+S_{K,h}S_{K,h}^\top$ and, in each chart, $T_hT_h^\top-O_h^\top O_h$ \citep{du2018balanced,Kunin2021NeuralMechanics}. For the second, invariance under $(T_h,O_h)\mapsto(e^{\epsilon Z}T_h,O_he^{-\epsilon Z})$ for every $Z\in\mathfrak{gl}(s_h)$ gives $G':=(\nabla_{T_h}\mathcal E)T_h^\top-O_h^\top(\nabla_{O_h}\mathcal E)=0$, and the time derivative of $T_hT_h^\top-O_h^\top O_h$ is $-(G'+G'^\top)=0$. Adaptive optimizers without weight decay can drift along these directions. The second law requires invariance of the entire loss under $\mathrm{GL}(s_h)$; the channel floor of the receiver of Supplement~\ref{S-sec:receiver_inference} reduces that invariance to the orthogonal group.

\paragraph{Variational source selection.}
For the laws of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mixture_joint} and~\eqref{eq:mixture_posterior}, the log density ratio on the event $J=j$ is $\log(\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij})+\log\bigl(q_{Q,i}(y)/p_{K,j}(y)\bigr)$. Integrating over $y\sim q_{Q,i}$ and summing over $j$ with weights $\beta_{ij}$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture_free_energy}. At a declared temperature $\tau>0$, use
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_i(\beta)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\beta_{ij}D_{ij}
+\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\beta_i\|\pi_i).
\label{eq:F_align_canonical_tau}
\end{equation}
The Hessian $\tau\operatorname{diag}(1/\beta_{ij})$ is positive definite on nonzero simplex tangent directions. The Lagrange condition is
$D_{ij}+\tau(\log(\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij})+1)+\lambda_i=0$.
For finite costs the unique minimizer is interior on $\mathcal N_i$, because the derivative of the entropy term diverges at the boundary of the simplex, and it is Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture_softmax_general}; for uniform $\pi_i$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{ij}^*=\operatorname{softmax}_{j\in\mathcal N_i}(-D_{ij}/\tau).
\label{eq:mixture_softmax}
\end{equation}
Substitution gives the minimum $-\tau\log Z_i$, with
$Z_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\pi_{ij}\exp(-D_{ij}/\tau)$, by the usual entropy-regularized variational calculation \citep{Boyd2004,Wainwright2008,Niculae2017Regularized}. The categorical minimizer needs no gradient-based update of $q_i$.

\paragraph{Comparison through the content link.}
In the comparison of Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison_link}, complete-state alignment $S_Q=S_K=I_K$ gives
\begin{equation}
D_{ij}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_i\|(\Omega_{ij})_*q_j\bigr),
\label{eq:complete_state_alignment}
\end{equation}
and square invertible comparison maps replace $\Omega_{ij}$ by $\Theta^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{ij}=U_iS_Q^{-1}S_KU_j^{-1}$, whose generator, when it exists, is shared by every edge because the comparison maps are shared by the layer; edge-dependent comparison generators would require edge-dependent comparison maps. \section{Positional and Masking Priors}
\label{sec:nonuniform_priors}

With a nonuniform prior, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture_softmax_general} adds $\log\pi_{ij}$ to the logits with unit coefficient while the cost is divided by $\tau$, so temperature never rescales a positional preference. Masks and positional biases therefore enter as priors over sources. This identification, and the entropy-regularized or transport form of the softmax that underlies it, is established in the attention literature \citep{Niculae2017Regularized,Litman2025EOT,Bianchessi2026BAM,Litman2026AttentionPriors}. On the causal support $\mathcal N_i=\{0,\ldots,i\}$, the uniform prior $\pi_{ij}=\mathbf{1}[j\le i]/(i+1)$ gives the support and row normalization of GPT-family attention \citep{radford2019language}. A window of radius $w$, $\pi_{ij}\propto\mathbf{1}[i-w\le j\le i]$, gives the sliding-window support of Longformer and Mistral \citep{beltagy2020longformer,jiang2023mistral}. The prior $\pi_{ij}\propto\exp[-\alpha_h(i-j)]$ gives the linear biases of attention with linear biases (ALiBi) \citep{press2022train}, and $\log\pi_{ij}=f_h(i-j)+\mathrm{const}$ gives learned relative biases, parameterized by sinusoids in the global positional bias of Transformer-XL and shared within logarithmic offset buckets in T5 \citep{dai2019transformerxl,raffel2020exploring}. \citet{press2022train} state the geometric slope schedule $\alpha_h=2^{-8h/H}$; their reference implementation uses it when $H$ is a power of two and otherwise appends alternate slopes of the next power-of-two schedule. The construction fixes only the form in which a prior enters. It does not select a prior, except that the autoregressive factorization requires $\pi_{ik}=0$ for $k>i$ at every layer, because each state depends on its own token.

\section{The Linear Decoder as a Gibbs Readout}
\label{app:decoder_readout}

Let $q_i$ be the Gaussian state after the chosen forward computation and $p_v$ a normalized class template on the same latent space, with finite $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|p_v)$. No convergence of $q_i$ is assumed. Specify a categorical readout $\hat p_i$ by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{decode}}(\hat p_i)
=\sum_v\hat p_i(v)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|p_v)
+\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat p_i\|\omega),
\qquad\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}>0,
\label{eq:readout_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\omega$ is a class prior. As for the routing row of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture_softmax_general}, the unique optimum on its support is
\begin{equation}
\hat p_i(v)\propto\omega_v\exp\left[-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|p_v)/\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}\right].
\label{eq:readout_gibbs}
\end{equation}
This is a specified Gibbs readout. At unit temperature it is also the categorical mean-field coordinate update for a fixed $q_i$ under the normalized mixture $p(v,x)=\omega_vp_v(x)$, as in the general expected-log update of \citet[\S2.4, Eqs.~17--18]{Blei2017}.

The linear decoder of Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive_linear_decoder} is itself the unit-temperature Gibbs readout for the shared-covariance templates $p_v=\mathcal N(\Sigma_*w_v,\Sigma_*)$, with $w_v$ the $v$th row of $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ and class prior $\log\omega_v=b_{\mathrm{out},v}+\kappa_{\mathrm{uni}}\log p_{\mathrm{uni}}(v)+\tfrac12w_v^\top\Sigma_*w_v$ up to a constant, for any $\Sigma_*\succ0$. Indeed, for $q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i^{(N_\ell)},\Sigma_i)$,
\begin{equation}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|p_v)=w_v^\top\mu_i^{(N_\ell)}-\tfrac12w_v^\top\Sigma_*w_v+c_i,
\label{eq:decoder_gibbs_identity}
\end{equation}
where $c_i$ collects the terms $-\tfrac12[\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma_*^{-1}\Sigma_i)+\mu_i^{(N_\ell)\top}\Sigma_*^{-1}\mu_i^{(N_\ell)}-K+\log\det\Sigma_*-\log\det\Sigma_i]$, which do not depend on $v$. Adding $\log\omega_v$ gives the logits of Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive_linear_decoder} up to the class-independent $c_i$, so the belief covariance cancels from every logit difference. The templates are declared in the local coordinates in which the decoder acts; in reference coordinates the same readout is the frame-dependent decoder $W_{\mathrm{out}}U_i$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:normal_form}, whereas templates declared in reference coordinates and compared with $(U_i^{-1})_*q_i$ would give a readout invariant under the passive action. Cross-entropy training of this readout does not become optimization of the full belief free energy merely because its scores are divergences. Supplement~\ref{S-sec:prior_bank_readout} develops the token-bank readout, which gives each class its own template covariance, and contrasts the Gibbs readout with the posterior-predictive average of a classifier.
