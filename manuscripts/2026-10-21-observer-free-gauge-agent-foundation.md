# A first foundation for an observer-free gauge-agent theory

October 21, 2026. This note constructs the first mathematical component of the proposed program: law-valued agent sections, one compatible collective probability description, normalized internal records, and a specified covariant inference dynamics. The construction admits nontrivial principal bundles and general probability laws. A compactly supported, non-Gaussian example makes connection holonomy change an internal record probability. Geometry therefore enters the probability model and its evolution.

This is an existence construction with supplied geometric and probabilistic data. It does not yet derive the base, the connection, the agents' interaction mechanisms, locally implementable correlated inference, stable species, recursive formation, or physical laws. Those are distinct obligations. The word observer-free means that this model introduces no external observing agent: its records are registered by its own participating agents. It does not settle the philosophical interpretation of probability or establish that these mathematical variables exhaust a physical universe.

## Geometric and probabilistic objects

Let \(\pi:P\to\mathcal C\) be a smooth right principal \(G\)-bundle over a compact smooth base. Let \(\mathsf Y\) be a standard Borel sample fiber with a measurable left \(G\)-action by bimeasurable bijections. Where smooth statements are used, give the fiber and its represented statistical families the required smooth structures. The associated sample and statistical bundles are

\[
E=P\times_G\mathsf Y,
\qquad
\mathcal S=P\times_G\mathcal P(\mathsf Y),
\qquad
(p g,y)\sim(p,g y).
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(\mathcal P(\mathsf Y)\) is the measurable space of probability measures; it carries no additional probability law here and is not automatically a smooth manifold. Differential calculations below take place in an explicitly dominated regular family. An agent law is a section \(\zeta_i\) over its support \(\mathcal U_i\). When \(\mathsf Y=\mathsf K\times\mathsf M\) and the action respects these projections, disintegration gives \(\zeta_i(dk,dm)=q_i(dk\mid m)s_i(dm)\). This state/model split is optional and does not by itself produce a slow model sector.

Choose a standard Borel space \(\mathsf Z\), a proper law \(P_0\) on it, and a measurable family of sample sections

\[
f_i:\mathsf Z\times\mathcal U_i\longrightarrow E,
\qquad
\pi_E(f_i(z,c))=c.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(z\), \(c\mapsto f_i(z,c)\) belongs to the declared section class. Equip the represented section collection with the sigma-algebra generated by evaluations. The section map is then measurable because every evaluation is measurable. For example, a continuous map from a finite-dimensional coefficient space into the space of smooth vector-bundle sections, equipped with its usual Borel sigma-algebra, has this property. The law induced on represented sample fields is the pushforward of \(P_0\); no nonexistent infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure is introduced.

The coefficients \(z\) describe possible contents represented by the agents' laws. A draw \(f_i(z,c)\) is not the law-valued agent \(\zeta_i(c)\). Nor is \(Q(dz)\), introduced below, a law over configurations of law-valued agents. It is a correlated law over represented contents. Treating those contents as additional physical objects would require a further ontological claim, which is not used in the derivation.

For any recognition law \(Q\) on \(\mathsf Z\), define the agent sections and finite-design laws by

\[
\zeta_i^Q(c)=(f_{i,c})_\#Q,
\qquad
Q_D=(F_D)_\#Q,
\qquad
F_D(z)=\bigl(f_i(z,c)\bigr)_{(i,c)\in I_D}.
\tag{3}
\]

The incidence set \(I_D\) contains only pairs for which the agent is defined at the chosen base point. The sections in (3) are measurable; weak continuity or smooth density dependence requires the corresponding evaluation and domination assumptions. These stronger properties are proved for the explicit example below.

For \(D\subseteq D'\), let \(r_{D'D}\) delete the added evaluation coordinates. Since \(F_D=r_{D'D}\circ F_{D'}\),

\[
(r_{D'D})_\#Q_{D'}=Q_D,
\qquad
\operatorname{Marg}_{i,c}Q_D=\zeta_i^Q(c).
\tag{4}
\]

This proves all finite-design consistency identities simultaneously. No reconstruction of a continuum law from finitely many values has been used. It also identifies the extra information retained by the global description: correlations between agent contents and between contexts. The list of one-point sections alone does not specify those correlations. If the ontology is stated as agents and their relations, the relational probability data must be retained explicitly.

## Normalized internal records and the collective objective

Choose finitely many record registers belonging to specified agents. A record mechanism is a measurable normalized kernel \(K_a(do_a\mid z)\). It may depend on selected field evaluations, model presentations, communication maps, and parallel transports along declared paths. Conditional independence of different records is a modeling choice. Under that choice,

\[
\mathbb P(dz,do)=P_0(dz)\prod_a K_a(do_a\mid z).
\tag{5}
\]

A single joint normalized record kernel can replace the product if records share noise. Sequential kernels conditional on earlier records provide another normalized construction. A shared record appears once in (5), however many agents can read it.

Normalization follows by integrating the record kernels before the prior. Let the records have a declared dominating reference \(\nu_O\), and choose pointwise density representatives. For an admitted record \(o\), set

\[
\ell_o(z)=\frac{dK(\cdot\mid z)}{d\nu_O}(o),
\qquad
Z_o=\int\ell_o(z)P_0(dz),
\qquad
\Pi_o(dz)=Z_o^{-1}\ell_o(z)P_0(dz),
\tag{6}
\]

with \(0<Z_o<\infty\). Counting measure is sufficient for the finite binary records used below. For continuous records, pointwise versions matter when a specified value is a null event; an abstract regular conditional law alone does not fix its likelihood density at that value.

For the required absolute continuity and finite logarithmic expectations,

\[
\mathcal F(Q;o)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\Vert P_0)-\mathbb E_Q\log\ell_o
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\Vert\Pi_o)-\log Z_o.
\tag{7}
\]

The proof is substitution of \(d\Pi_o/dP_0=\ell_o/Z_o\). The posterior-KL expression defines the extended functional even when the split expression is undefined. It is the negative ELBO of one specified model. A generative factor is held fixed during this recognition variation; replacing it by a live peer recognition law would change that optimization problem.

The model contains record registers and interaction mechanisms inside the declared agent population. Writing their joint probability law does not posit an external observer who is uncertain about the universe. Nevertheless, interpreting the entire law as a complete cosmology is a modeling claim beyond normalization. The theorem provides internal record semantics and probabilistic coherence, not empirical or ontological completeness.

For a finite projection \(F_D\), the relative-entropy chain rule gives

\[
\mathcal F(Q;o)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_D\Vert\Pi_{o,D})-\log Z_o
+\mathbb E_{Q_D}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(Q(dz\mid F_D)\Vert\Pi_o(dz\mid F_D)\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]

The conditional distributions exist on the declared standard Borel spaces. The last term is the exact cost of information omitted by the projected description. Even when the finite design contains every evaluation read by the records, this term need not vanish: recognition can still have the wrong conditional law in the unobserved directions. Keeping only the projected bound is exact at finite value precisely when those conditional laws agree almost everywhere. The same identity applies to one agent marginal, and explains why adding marginal KL terms does not recover the global objective.

The measure-theoretic ingredients are standard pushforward and disintegration operations; see Kallenberg, [*Foundations of Modern Probability*, chapters on kernels and conditioning](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-61871-1). Equations (4), (7), and (8) state the specific uses here.

## How geometry enters the law

Choose local coordinates with \(y_\beta=g_{\beta\alpha}y_\alpha\). A connection is represented by \(D=d+B_\alpha\), with

\[
B_\beta=g_{\beta\alpha}B_\alpha g_{\beta\alpha}^{-1}
-dg_{\beta\alpha}g_{\beta\alpha}^{-1},
\qquad
T_\gamma'=g(c_1)T_\gamma g(c_0)^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

The maps \(T_\gamma\) compare fibers along a specified base path. Coordinate transitions, independent links between agents at a fixed context, and base parallel transport remain distinct structures. The transition cocycle describes one bundle and need not be a global frame coboundary. A selective noisy communication channel can follow a parallel transport without itself being invertible.

A record kernel must transform with all the fiber data it reads. With scalar record coordinates, this means its probability for the same record event is unchanged after the sample evaluations, transport, and any fiber metric are re-expressed together. Equations (5) and (6) then describe the same intrinsic law and posterior. KL is invariant because the Radon–Nikodym derivative transforms by composition with the inverse coordinate map. If record coordinates change, transport their reference measure as well. Using a fresh coordinate density instead can shift the numerical evidence density and free energy by the same record Jacobian; it does not change record-event probabilities or the posterior.

This is covariance of a supplied physical comparison mechanism. Changing the connection while holding the other intrinsic data fixed can change record probabilities. The explicit witness below proves this distinction without choosing a global frame.

## A specified inference law and its exact solution

For a regular dominated family \(Q_\theta\), the Fisher metric is

\[
G_{ab}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{Q_\theta}
\bigl[\partial_a\log q_\theta\partial_b\log q_\theta\bigr].
\tag{10}
\]

Assume the scores are square-integrable, the parameter derivatives can pass under the integrals, and \(G\) is smooth and positive definite on the chosen identifiable stratum. The equation \(\dot\theta=-G^{-1}d\mathcal F\) specifies Fisher natural-gradient dynamics. It is an additional kinetic choice. The scalar objective alone does not choose it, set its rate, or identify the evolution parameter with physical time. Local existence follows when the vector field is locally Lipschitz, and

\[
\frac{d\mathcal F}{dt}
=-(d\mathcal F)^T G^{-1}d\mathcal F\leq0.
\tag{11}
\]

The metric transforms as a tensor under parameter changes, so the resulting tangent vector is intrinsic. This is the information-geometric natural gradient introduced by Amari, [“Natural Gradient Works Efficiently in Learning”](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017746); the dissipation statement here follows directly from the displayed equation. These are fixed-record equations. Time-dependent coordinate frames require the corresponding coordinate-velocity term or a temporal covariant derivative; one cannot transform an ordinary component derivative as though the chart were fixed.

There is also a fully explicit general-law solution for a bounded class. Fix \(\Pi=\Pi_o\), take \(Q_0\) equivalent to \(\Pi\), and suppose

\[
u_0=\log\frac{dQ_0}{d\Pi}\in L^\infty(\Pi).
\tag{12}
\]

Use bounded centered scores as tangent vectors, with the centered square-integrable scores as their Hilbert completion, and the metric \(g_Q(a,b)=\mathbb E_Q[ab]\). Variation of KL in the score direction \(a\) gives \(\mathbb E_Q[a\log(dQ/d\Pi)]\). The unit-rate descending score is consequently

\[
\partial_t\log\frac{dQ_t}{d\Pi}
=-\log\frac{dQ_t}{d\Pi}
+\mathbb E_{Q_t}\log\frac{dQ_t}{d\Pi}.
\tag{13}
\]

For every \(t\geq0\), its solution is

\[
Q_t(dz)
=\frac{e^{e^{-t}u_0(z)}}{\int e^{e^{-t}u_0}d\Pi}\Pi(dz).
\tag{14}
\]

Boundedness gives a positive finite denominator and justifies differentiation under the integral. Differentiating the logarithm proves (13), and at \(t=0\) the denominator is one. Conversely, subtracting the normalization contribution from (13) gives the same exponentially decaying log-density differences, so a differentiable solution in this bounded-log-density class must have (14). Dominated convergence gives \(Q_t\to\Pi\) in total variation and KL. Differentiating (7) gives the exact identity

\[
\frac{d\mathcal F(Q_t;o)}{dt}
=-\operatorname{Var}_{Q_t}
\left(\log\frac{dQ_t}{d\Pi}\right)\leq0.
\tag{15}
\]

This proves a global flow for the declared class; it is not an unrestricted existence theorem for every infinite-dimensional statistical family. Applying the field evaluations to (14) gives well-defined evolving agent sections for all inference times. KL and the Fisher score inner product are unchanged by a fixed bimeasurable re-expression of the sample representation. The evolving fiber laws therefore retain the bundle transition relations.

The full Fisher metric in (10) or (13) is the metric of the collective recognition law. It is generally not the sum of the Fisher metrics of its one-agent marginals. This distinction affects both dynamics and the possibility of local implementation.

## The exact obstruction to closing dynamics on marginal agents

Let \(X=f_{i,c}(Z)\), write \(q_t\) for its marginal, and let \(\pi_X\) be the corresponding posterior marginal. Under finite conditional terms, define

\[
C_t(x)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(Q_t(dz\mid X=x)\Vert\Pi(dz\mid X=x)\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]

Disintegrating the log-density ratio and conditioning the score equation gives

\[
\partial_t\log\frac{dq_t}{d\pi_X}(x)
=-\log\frac{dq_t}{d\pi_X}(x)-C_t(x)
+\mathbb E_{q_t}\left[\log\frac{dq_t}{d\pi_X}+C_t\right].
\tag{17}
\]

The conditional KL contributes to the marginal velocity even when the marginal itself matches its target. This is an exact dependence on retained joint information.

For an explicit counterexample take a uniform target on two binary variables and, with rows indexed by \(X\) and columns by \(Y\), set

\[
Q^A=\frac1{16}\begin{pmatrix}6&2\\6&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q^B=\frac1{16}\begin{pmatrix}7&1\\5&3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{18}
\]

Both laws have \(Q_X=(1/2,1/2)\) and \(Q_Y=(3/4,1/4)\). The first has equal conditional costs in its two rows, hence \(\dot Q_X^A(0)=0\). For the second,

\[
\dot Q_X^B(0)
=-\frac14\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(\operatorname{Ber}(7/8)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)\bigr)
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(\operatorname{Ber}(5/8)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)\bigr)
\right]<0.
\tag{19}
\]

The inequality follows because binary KL to \(1/2\) has derivative \(\log[p/(1-p)]>0\) for \(p>1/2\). All densities are strictly positive and finite. Thus even the complete list of singleton marginals does not determine their exact Fisher velocities. A single additional bit in an observer's notebook is not the issue; the missing object is the agents' relational joint probability data.

There is a precise simpler case. Suppose \(P_0=\prod_iP_{0,i}\), restrict \(Q=\prod_iQ_i\), and let each fixed record factor read a declared subset \(\partial a\). Then

\[
\mathcal F(Q;o)
=\sum_iD_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_i\Vert P_{0,i})
-\sum_a\mathbb E_Q\log k_a(o_a\mid Z_{\partial a}).
\tag{20}
\]

Holding the other blocks fixed, the variation with respect to \(Q_i\) equals the variation of

\[
\mathcal F_i(Q_i;Q_{-i},o)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_i\Vert P_{0,i})
-\sum_{a:i\in\partial a}\mathbb E_Q\log k_a(o_a\mid Z_{\partial a}).
\tag{21}
\]

Every nonincident term has zero block variation. The required message from factor \(a\) is the function \(m_{a\to i}(z_i)=\mathbb E_{Q_{\partial a\setminus i}}\log k_a(o_a\mid z_i,Z_{\partial a\setminus i})\). The update can be implemented from these functions and the local prior if the declared communication mechanisms supply them. This is an explicit access condition, not a consequence of drawing an interaction graph. It concerns agent blocks; it is not a proof of locality along the base or of a physical propagation speed. The sum of the local objectives (21) generally counts shared factors repeatedly and is not (20). Product restriction also changes the recognition problem; it does not prove closure of exact correlated inference.

## Internal event generation and successive updates

A closed stochastic interaction protocol can be specified without introducing an external observer or sampling an additional material world. At event \(n\), let the current relational law be \(Q_n\), let \(h_n\) contain the internally retained record history, and supply a measurable normalized interaction kernel \(K_n(do\mid z,h_n)\). Its predictive law is

\[
R_n(do\mid h_n)=\int K_n(do\mid z,h_n)Q_n(dz).
\tag{22}
\]

An internal register takes its next value according to this law. Freeze the current \(Q_n\) as the episode prior, write \(\ell_{n,o}\) for the positive likelihood of that record, and assume the logarithm is bounded. During this episode the posterior target is \(\Pi_{n,o}\propto\ell_{n,o}Q_n\). Starting Fisher descent from \(Q_n\) and running it for the supplied duration \(\Delta_n>0\) gives, by (14),

\[
Q_{n+1}(dz)
=\frac{\ell_{n,o}(z)^{a_n}Q_n(dz)}{\int\ell_{n,o}^{a_n}dQ_n},
\qquad a_n=1-e^{-\Delta_n}.
\tag{23}
\]

The update is deterministic given the old law and the registered record. With measurable choices and a nonanticipating schedule, the finite history law is the iterated product of the kernels (22); successive integration proves normalization. The law of the resulting random sequence of agent states is induced by these internal events. It is not a separately postulated epistemic distribution belonging to an external knower.

This protocol is a constitutive choice. It generates records from the population's current predictive law and uses a supplied schedule and inference rate. It is not derived from the ELBO or asserted to match physical experiments. For exact Bayes assimilation, \(a_n=1\), a fixed latent representation can recover the corresponding usual Bayesian history model. Finite-duration updates have \(a_n<1\) and generally give a different sequential process. Normalization of that process remains exact.

Equation (15) holds within an episode while its record and model are fixed. Changing the record or episode prior changes the objective; no monotonic free energy across the entire sequence follows. Nor does this construction identify event number, inference duration, and physical time. The geometry enters each episode through its interaction kernel, while selection or evolution of the connection requires an additional law.

## A non-Gaussian construction on the Hopf bundle

The following example meets the geometric, normalization, and kinetic requirements simultaneously. Full local-density and topology calculations are in the accompanying [geometric derivation](<C:/Users/chris and christine/Desktop/Research/docs/derivations/2026-10-21-gauge-agent-foundation/geometry-witness.md>).

Take \(\mathcal C=\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2\), \(P=S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1\), and \(G=U(1)\). A point of \(P\) is a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb C^2\), its base point is its complex span, and its right action is \(e\mapsto eh\). The associated complex line bundle has fiber \(L_c=c\subset\mathbb C^2\). Its Hermitian metric is supplied by the ambient complex vector space. This embedding of a line bundle into a trivial rank-two bundle is not a global basis of that line bundle.

On the north and south charts, choose

\[
e_N=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\vartheta/2)\\e^{i\varphi}\sin(\vartheta/2)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
e_S=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\varphi}\cos(\vartheta/2)\\\sin(\vartheta/2)\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
\]

The frames are smooth at their respective included poles. On the overlap, \(e_S=e_Ne^{-i\varphi}\), so sample coordinates satisfy \(u_S=e^{i\varphi}u_N\). A hypothetical global principal section would give circle-valued functions \(h_N,h_S\) extending over the two closed hemispheres and obeying \(h_N=e^{-i\varphi}h_S\) on the equator. Both extendible loops have winding zero, whereas this relation requires \(0=-1+0\). Thus the principal bundle has no continuous global section.

Let \(P_c=e_Ne_N^*=e_Se_S^*\) denote the globally smooth orthogonal projector onto \(L_c\). For two coefficient blocks \(a_i\in\mathbb C^2\), define

\[
f_i(a,c)=P_ca_i,
\qquad
P_0(da)=\prod_{i=1}^2 C\chi(\lVert a_i\rVert^2)d^4a_i,
\qquad
\chi(r)=
\begin{cases}
e^{-1/(1-r)},&r<1,\\
0,&r\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]

The constant \(C\) normalizes each positive compactly supported density. The represented fields are smooth for every coefficient. Their map into smooth sections is continuous, and the coefficients can be recovered from their evaluations on two independent complex lines. Hence this gives an actual probability measure on a finite-dimensional family of smooth sample fields. The two agents may restrict these fields to the north and south domains; both domains contain the paths used below.

In a local unit frame, the untilted fiber density is

\[
\rho_0(u)=C\pi\int_{|u|^2}^1\chi(r)dr
\quad\text{for }|u|<1,
\qquad \rho_0(u)=0\quad\text{otherwise}.
\tag{26}
\]

It is smooth, positive on the open disk, and compactly supported, so it is neither a nondegenerate Gaussian nor a degenerate Gaussian. A smooth positive weight \(w(a)\) on the compact coefficient support gives the more general law \(Q_w\propto wP_0\). To obtain its fiber density, write \(a_i=e(c)u+n(c)v\) in a smooth local unitary frame and integrate over \(v\) and the other coefficient block. All differentiated integrands have uniform compact support on compact chart sets. This proves smoothness of the local density in both \(c\) and \(u\), normalization, and the overlap rule \(\rho'(c,u')=\rho(c,g(c)^{-1}u')\). Posterior weights and the inference flow below belong to this class.

Now choose the connection

\[
B_N^{(0)}=\frac{i}{2}(1-\cos\vartheta)d\varphi,
\qquad
B_S^{(0)}=-\frac{i}{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)d\varphi.
\tag{27}
\]

Their difference is \(-i d\varphi\), as required by (9). The curvature integrates to \(2\pi i\) over the sphere. Define a second connection on the same bundle by

\[
B^{(1)}=B^{(0)}-\frac{i}{2}\beta,
\qquad
\beta=xdy-ydx=\sin^2\vartheta d\varphi.
\tag{28}
\]

The form \(\beta\) is globally smooth. The added curvature is exact and has zero integral, so both connections retain the same nontrivial bundle topology. The second connection has trivial holonomy around the particular equator loop used here; it is not globally flat.

Place the sender and receiver at opposite equatorial points \(c_s,c_r\). Let \(\gamma_0\) and \(\gamma_1\) be the two equatorial semicircles from source to receiver, and transmit the same sender content \(v_s=f_2(a,c_s)\) along both. The source-based loop holonomy is \(H=T_{\gamma_0}^{-1}T_{\gamma_1}\). Integration of (27)–(28) gives

\[
H^{(0)}=-I,
\qquad H^{(1)}=I,
\qquad
d^{(b)}(a)=\lVert T_{\gamma_1}^{(b)}v_s-T_{\gamma_0}^{(b)}v_s\rVert^2
=\lVert(H^{(b)}-I)v_s\rVert^2.
\tag{29}
\]

The receiver also has a represented state \(v_r=f_1(a,c_r)\). Supply a response function \(h(u)=u/(1+u)\) for \(u\geq0\), and a parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). Its binary register follows the normalized kernel

\[
k_b(1\mid a)=\frac12+\epsilon h(\lVert v_r\rVert^2)h(d^{(b)}(a)),
\qquad k_b(0\mid a)=1-k_b(1\mid a).
\tag{30}
\]

Both record probabilities are bounded away from zero and one. The receiver sensitivity in this equation is a supplied interaction mechanism. It has not been learned or derived from an extremum principle. Under a passive gauge change, both arrivals transform in the receiver's fiber and their squared norm is unchanged. Thus (30) is an intrinsic kernel, not a gauge-dependent comparison of coordinate entries.

With \(U=h(\lVert v_r\rVert^2)\) and \(V=h(4\lVert v_s\rVert^2)\), the product prior gives

\[
\mathbb P_1(R=1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb P_0(R=1)=\frac12+\epsilon\mathbb E_{P_0}U\mathbb E_{P_0}V>\frac12.
\tag{31}
\]

The strict inequality follows from positive disk densities. Only the connection changed. Source law, paths, receiver response, and fiber metric were held fixed. There is no external probe: the record belongs to the receiver, and both transmissions originate from the sender.

For connection zero and record \(R=1\), the posterior density relative to the prior is \((1/2+\epsilon UV)/Z\), where \(Z=1/2+\epsilon\mathbb E U\mathbb E V\). The receiver endpoint density is tilted by \((1/2+\epsilon U\mathbb E V)/Z\); the sender endpoint density is tilted by \((1/2+\epsilon V\mathbb E U)/Z\). Both laws change. Direct multiplication of these moments also gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_1}(U,V)
=\frac{\epsilon\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(U)\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(V)}{2Z^2}>0,
\tag{32}
\]

where \(\Pi_1\) denotes the posterior for record value one under connection zero. The record therefore creates nonproduct recognition even from a product prior. A product approximation would omit this dependence.

To make the dynamics finite-dimensional and explicit, hold connection zero and either record value fixed, set \(L(a)=\log k_0(R\mid a)\), and consider

\[
Q_\lambda(da)=e^{\lambda L(a)-\psi(\lambda)}P_0(da),
\qquad
\psi(\lambda)=\log\int e^{\lambda L}dP_0,
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R.
\tag{33}
\]

The bounded smooth function \(L\) is nonconstant. Therefore \(G(\lambda)=\psi''(\lambda)=\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\lambda}L>0\), with all derivative interchanges justified by boundedness. The exact posterior is \(Q_1\). Substituting in the VFE gives \(\mathcal F(Q_\lambda)=(\lambda-1)\psi'(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)\), hence

\[
\dot\lambda=-G(\lambda)^{-1}\partial_\lambda\mathcal F=1-\lambda,
\qquad
\lambda(t)=1+(\lambda(0)-1)e^{-t},
\qquad
\dot{\mathcal F}=-(\lambda-1)^2G(\lambda)\leq0.
\tag{34}
\]

This is a global regular Fisher flow within a non-Gaussian model on a nontrivial bundle. Its entire trajectory induces smooth agent sections and consistent finite restrictions. For connection one, the selected likelihood is constant; the posterior equals the prior and this particular one-parameter family collapses. The geometry has changed whether this internal record carries inferential information.

Several limits of the example are informative. Independently redrawing rotationally invariant sender content for each route destroys this holonomy signal, so the shared-source correlation is part of the mechanism. The binary norm record cannot distinguish a holonomy angle from its negative and does not reconstruct a connection. The initial radial agent laws have vanishing covariant base derivative, so nontrivial bundle topology and informative path records do not imply a nondegenerate base Fisher metric. None of these limitations prevents the stated existence result.

## What has been established, and the next construction

The first mathematical milestone is a conditional foundation plus an explicit witness. One proper law on represented smooth fields gives statistical agent sections, all finite-design laws, and a normalized collective VFE. Connection-dependent internal records preserve gauge covariance. The Hopf example proves that this can be done with nontrivial topology and non-Gaussian laws, with an actual change of both participating marginal laws and their correlation after a record. A specified Fisher kinetic law has a globally defined solution for the exhibited regular family.

The construction has not yet made full correlated inference an autonomous law of the marginal sections. Equations (17)–(19) show exactly why that inference fails in general. The next construction should specify which joint information is retained in communication or memory, and prove that the resulting locally accessible updates preserve the intended global model. The product-family result (20)–(21) is one available baseline; a sufficient-message construction retaining correlations would be a stronger result.

Once that access and closure choice is explicit, the next scientific question is whether interactions dynamically generate informative shared predictive models while allowing distinct current beliefs. Agreement, predictive informativeness, and persistence must each follow from the selected dynamics. An infinite-rank continuum theory, emergent physical time, gravitational dynamics, and empirical identification remain separate later targets.
