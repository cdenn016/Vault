\chapter{Multivariate-Gaussian Coarse-Graining}
\label{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining}

This chapter realizes the three operations of
\Cref{ch:coarsegraining} in the multivariate-Gaussian interaction family. It
does not repeat the Markov or graph-exponential theory. Its claims concern
hard identification, Gaussian marginalization and recognition restrictions,
all at finite dimension. Unless a channel index is displayed, the symbols
\(K,\Theta,\Lambda,L,A,W\) in this chapter denote the belief/recognition
channel. A model-channel realization is separate and uses its own
\(K_m,\Theta^m,\Lambda_m,L_m,A_m,W_m\); no equality of the two channels is
implied. \status{DEFINITION}

Within the connection-Laplacian realization,
\Cref{prop:cg-kernel-holonomy} applies only to invertible represented
transports with inverse reverse transports and positive-definite represented
internal weights. Under those hypotheses, trivial represented internal
holonomy is the criterion for a connected cluster to retain a full
\(K\)-dimensional, state-independent structural fixed section; nontrivial
holonomy may still leave a lower-dimensional fixed sector.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Marginal similarity is not identified with this structural criterion.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\section{Hard aggregation of the interaction precision}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-aggregation}

Assume the declared interaction family of \Cref{ch:gaussian},
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{ii}=A_i+\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij},\qquad
\Lambda_{ij}=-W_{ij}\quad(i\neq j),
\qquad
A_i\succeq0,\quad W_{ij}=W_{ji}\succeq0.
\label{eq:cg-interaction-family}
\end{equation}
In flat trivialized coordinates its energy is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E(z)
=\sum_i z_i^\top A_i z_i
+\sum_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^\top W_{ij}(z_i-z_j).
\label{eq:cg-trivialized-energy}
\end{equation}
\status{HYPOTHESIS}

This form is a Gaussian subfamily, not a consequence of an arbitrary
linear-Gaussian directed model. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

For a hard partition \(\mathcal P\), let
\[
S=\widehat S\otimes I_K,\qquad
\widehat S_{iI}=\mathbf1_{\{i\in I\}},\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}=S^\top\Lambda S.
\]
The columns of \(S\) are orthogonal but not normalized:
\begin{equation}
S^\top S=\operatorname{diag}(n_I)\otimes I_K.
\label{eq:cg-aggregation-matrix}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Exact Gaussian aggregation}{prop:cg-gaussian-aggregation}
For every hard partition,
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_{\rm c})_{IJ}
=-\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\in J}}W_{ij}\quad(I\neq J),
\qquad
(\Lambda_{\rm c})_{II}
=\sum_{i\in I}A_i
+\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\notin I}}W_{ij}.
\label{eq:cg-coarse-blocks}
\end{equation}
Thus \(A_I=\sum_{i\in I}A_i\),
\(W_{IJ}=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}W_{ij}\), and every internal edge is
annihilated. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Substitute one coordinate \(z_I\) for every \(z_i\) in a cluster. An internal
edge contributes zero. A cut edge contributes its difference energy between
\(z_I\) and \(z_J\), and self terms add. Finite sums preserve positive
semidefiniteness; independently,
\(\Lambda_{\rm c}\succeq0\) follows by congruence. \(\square\)

This is the Galerkin coarse operator with a piecewise-constant prolongator,
not a new numerical construction \citep{VanekMandelBrezina1996,Lee2024AMGSystems}.
The parameter map is exact for hard identification. A separately fitted
coupling is outside that exact trace construction, while learned or soft
pooling is a different approximate model. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\section{Kron reduction: coefficient families and graph boundaries}
\label{sec:cg-kron}

Gaussian marginalization eliminates nodes by a Schur complement. It is not
the aggregation above and is not closed on the unrestricted matrix-weighted
interaction family.

\propositionheading{Unrestricted Kron reduction leaves the family}{prop:cg-kron-leaves-family}
Take \(N=3\), \(K=2\), \(A_1=A_2=A_3=I_2\), and
\begin{equation}
W_{12}=0,\qquad
W_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
W_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-witness}
\end{equation}
Then the assembled \(\Lambda\) is positive definite. Eliminating node \(3\)
manufactures
\begin{equation}
-\left(\Lambda^{\rm Kron}\right)_{12}
=W_{13}\Lambda_{33}^{-1}W_{23}
=\frac1{19}\begin{pmatrix}9&3\\4&14\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:cg-kron-asymmetric}
\end{equation}
which is not symmetric and hence is not a positive-semidefinite interaction
weight. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
\(\Lambda_{33}=\begin{psmallmatrix}4&1\\1&5\end{psmallmatrix}\) has
determinant \(19\), and direct multiplication gives
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-asymmetric}. The leading principal minors of the full
precision are \(2,6,18,48,102,233\), so Sylvester's criterion gives
\(\Lambda\succ0\). The full Schur complement is symmetric; its two mirrored
off-diagonal blocks are transposes. Membership in
\eqref{eq:cg-interaction-family} additionally requires each individual block
to be symmetric, which fails. \(\square\)

The scalar loopy-Laplacian family is closed under every defined elimination
\citep{DorflerBullo2013Kron}. The matrix analog is closed on a larger
subfamily than common orthogonal diagonalization.

\definitionheading{Congruence-diagonal cone}{def:cg-congruence-diagonal-cone}
For \(H\in\GL(K)\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_H
=\{HDH^\top:D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_K),\ d_a\geq0\}.
\label{eq:cg-congruence-diagonal-cone}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{Congruence-diagonal Kron closure}{thm:cg-congruence-diagonal-kron}
If every \(A_i\) and \(W_{ij}\) lies in one fixed \(\mathcal C_H\), then
every defined node-block Schur complement remains in the interaction family
with all reduced parameters in \(\mathcal C_H\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Factor
\begin{equation}
\Lambda=(I_N\otimes H)\Lambda_D(I_N\otimes H^\top),
\label{eq:cg-kron-congruence-factor}
\end{equation}
where, after grouping coordinates by channel, \(\Lambda_D\) is a direct sum
of \(K\) scalar loopy Laplacians. For retained nodes \(R\) and eliminated
nodes \(E\),
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Sc}_E(\Lambda)
&=(I_R\otimes H)\Big[
(\Lambda_D)_{RR}
-(\Lambda_D)_{RE}(\Lambda_D)_{EE}^{-1}(\Lambda_D)_{ER}
\Big](I_R\otimes H^\top).
\label{eq:cg-kron-congruence-schur}
\end{align}
The middle operator eliminates channel by channel. Each scalar Schur
complement is another loopy Laplacian: its off-diagonals remain nonpositive
because the eliminated block is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix, and its row sums
remain nonnegative. Reassembling the channels gives reduced self terms and
weights in \(\mathcal C_H\). \(\square\)

This family strictly contains the common-orthogonal-eigenbasis family.
For
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
X=H\operatorname{diag}(1,2)H^\top,\quad
Y=H\operatorname{diag}(3,1)H^\top,
\]
one has \(XY-YX=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-5\\5&0\end{psmallmatrix}\neq0\),
so \(X,Y\) cannot be simultaneously orthogonally diagonalized, while they
belong to the same \(\mathcal C_H\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Qualified maximality}{thm:cg-qualified-maximality}
Let \(\mathcal C\subseteq\PSD^K\) be a closed convex cone with an SPD order
unit \(M\in\operatorname{relint}\mathcal C\). Assume that anchors and incident
edge coefficients range independently over \(\mathcal C\), arbitrary positive
edge scaling is allowed, every defined one-node elimination must remain in
the family, and every manufactured off-diagonal block must be minus a
symmetric member of \(\mathcal C\). Then
\(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal C_H\) for some \(H\), and a maximal cone under
these assumptions equals \(\mathcal C_H\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For \(X,Y\in\mathcal C\), relative interior gives an
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
A_e=M-\varepsilon(X+Y)\in\mathcal C.
\]
Use \(\varepsilon X,\varepsilon Y\) as the two incident weights and \(M\)
as retained anchors. The eliminated diagonal is \(M\), and the manufactured
coupling is \(\varepsilon^2XM^{-1}Y\). Its required symmetry forces
\[
XM^{-1}Y=YM^{-1}X.
\]
After whitening by \(M^{-1/2}\), every pair in the whitened cone commutes.
Commuting real symmetric matrices are simultaneously orthogonally
diagonalizable, so
\[
\mathcal C\subseteq
\{M^{1/2}ODO^\top M^{1/2}:D\succeq0\text{ diagonal}\}
=\mathcal C_H.
\]
The preceding theorem proves that \(\mathcal C_H\) itself is closed, yielding
maximality. \(\square\)

The qualified theorem concerns coefficient cones with independently variable
anchors. Three further questions have different quantifiers: which arbitrary
coefficient families pass tests at one fixed positive anchor, which fixed
graphs close for every independent SPD assignment, and which nonflat graphs
close while retaining their link marks. We treat these questions separately.
In every case, Schur reduction means integration over coordinates, with the
linear source and scalar normalizer retained. \status{DEFINITION}

\subsection{The complete Gaussian marginal action}
\label{sec:cg-kron-normalized-action}

Fix a finite coordinate split \(z=(z_R,z_E)\), an SPD precision
\(\Lambda\), a source \(h\), and a real scalar offset \(\mathfrak c\).
Write \(n=\dim z\), \(n_E=\dim z_E\), and use the declared coordinate
Lebesgue measures. Write \(\mathbb P_\Lambda(dz)=p_\Lambda(z)dz\). The normalized
Gaussian prior is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(z)&=\frac12z^\top\Lambda z-h^\top z+\mathfrak c,\\
p_\Lambda(z)&=Z_\Lambda^{-1}e^{-\Phi(z)},\\
Z_\Lambda&=e^{-\mathfrak c}(2\pi)^{n/2}
(\det\Lambda)^{-1/2}
\exp\left(\frac12h^\top\Lambda^{-1}h\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-normalized-prior}
\end{equation}
The first line declares the action; the last line follows by translating
\(z\) by \(\Lambda^{-1}h\), orthogonally diagonalizing \(\Lambda\), and
multiplying the one-dimensional integrals
\(\int_{\R}e^{-\lambda x^2/2}dx=(2\pi/\lambda)^{1/2}\) for
\(\lambda>0\). Thus the prior is the actual probability law
\(\mathcal N(\Lambda^{-1}h,\Lambda^{-1})\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Normalized Schur marginal and reverse kernel}{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}
Let \(M=\Lambda_{EE}\). The exact marginal action and all-input conditional
kernel are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_R^{\rm eff}
 &=\Lambda_{RR}-\Lambda_{RE}M^{-1}\Lambda_{ER},\\
h_R^{\rm eff}
 &=h_R-\Lambda_{RE}M^{-1}h_E,\\
\mathfrak c_R^{\rm eff}
 &=\mathfrak c+\frac12\log\det M-\frac{n_E}{2}\log(2\pi)
                     -\frac12h_E^\top M^{-1}h_E,\\
\mathbb P_\Lambda(dz_E\mid z_R=x)
 &=\mathcal N\left(M^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x),M^{-1}\right)(dz_E).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-complete-schur}
\end{equation}
Both \(M\) and \(\Lambda_R^{\rm eff}\) are SPD. Integration preserves the
raw mass \(Z_\Lambda\), and the normalized marginal is the coordinate
pushforward of \(p_\Lambda\). Successive eliminations agree with direct
elimination, including their sources and scalar offsets.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The hidden square has center \(M^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x)\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(x,z_E)
={}&\frac12
\bigl(z_E-M^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x)\bigr)^\top
M\bigl(z_E-M^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x)\bigr)\\
&+\frac12x^\top\Lambda_{RR}x-h_R^\top x+\mathfrak c
-\frac12(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x)^\top
M^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}x).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-completed-square}
\end{equation}
Integrating this square gives
\((2\pi)^{n_E/2}(\det M)^{-1/2}\); expansion of the last term proves every
line of \eqref{eq:cg-kron-complete-schur}. A principal block of an SPD
matrix is SPD. For any nonzero retained \(x\), minimizing
\((x,z_E)^\top\Lambda(x,z_E)\) over \(z_E\) gives
\(x^\top\Lambda_R^{\rm eff}x>0\), proving the other positivity assertion.
For a nonnegative Borel function on a finite product of Euclidean spaces,
the product integral equals its iterated integrals in any order, with
infinite values allowed. Here the displayed Gaussian integrals are finite,
so this fact proves equality of all elimination orders. It also proves
composition of the conditional reconstruction kernels when each is formed
from the corresponding marginal. \(\square\)

A normalized observed experiment can be retained as well. Declare the
independent noise model
\(o=D_Rz_R+D_Ez_E+\eta\), with
\(\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_o)\) and \(\Gamma_o\succ0\).
Integrating this actual sensor against the conditional kernel above gives
\begin{equation}
o\mid z_R=x\sim
\mathcal N\left(
(D_R-D_EM^{-1}\Lambda_{ER})x+D_EM^{-1}h_E,
\Gamma_o+D_EM^{-1}D_E^\top
\right).
\label{eq:cg-kron-induced-sensor}
\end{equation}
The covariance is SPD, and mixing this likelihood with the retained prior
recovers the original positive, finite Gaussian evidence at every record.
This follows by writing the hidden conditional as its affine mean plus
independent centered Gaussian noise. Hence the same coordinate projection
pushes the entire observed joint, not just its prior. The finite-KL chain
identity of \Cref{sec:cg-kl-recovery} then gives the exact VFE difference
using the same evidence on both sides. The posterior hidden conditional
generally differs from the prior conditional in
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-complete-schur}; when \(D_E=0\), the sensor depends only
on retained coordinates and those conditionals agree.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Arbitrary independent families at a fixed anchor}
\label{sec:cg-independent-kron-families}

Fix \(Q_0=Q_0^\top\succ0\) and an arbitrary subset
\(\mathscr W\subseteq\PSD^K\). No closedness, convexity, scaling closure,
or membership of \(Q_0\) in \(\mathscr W\) is assumed. For each
\(A,B\in\mathscr W\), require that a two-edge test with some \(t,s>0\)
is admitted, with eliminated anchor \(Q_0\), incident weights \(tA,sB\),
and manufactured coefficient
\begin{equation}
M=Q_0+tA+sB,\qquad
W_{A,B}(t,s)=tsAM^{-1}B.
\label{eq:cg-independent-pair-test}
\end{equation}
This is independent pair coverage of the test domain. It does not assert
that \(tA,sB\), or the output already belong to \(\mathscr W\).
\status{HYPOTHESIS}

Giving the two retained vertices the anchor \(Q_0\) realizes every test
as a proper three-node Gaussian: its energy is at least
\(\lambda_{\min}(Q_0)\|z\|^2/2\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Fixed-anchor classification of arbitrary families}{thm:cg-independent-kron-classification}
Under the preceding test hypothesis, symmetry of at least one
\(W_{A,B}(t,s)\) for every pair \(A,B\in\mathscr W\) is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
AQ_0^{-1}B=BQ_0^{-1}A
\quad\text{for all }A,B\in\mathscr W.
\label{eq:cg-anchor-commutation}
\end{equation}
It is also equivalent to the existence of one \(O\in O(K)\) such that
\begin{equation}
H=Q_0^{1/2}O,\qquad Q_0=HH^\top,\qquad
\mathscr W\subseteq\mathcal C_H.
\label{eq:cg-anchored-common-cone}
\end{equation}
The same \(H\) works for the whole family, even if it is uncountable.
Under these equivalent conditions every positive pair of scales gives
\(W_{A,B}(t,s)\in\mathcal C_H\). The inclusion-maximal families satisfying
the test property are precisely these full cones \(\mathcal C_H\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Whiten by \(Q_0\), writing
\(a=Q_0^{-1/2}AQ_0^{-1/2}\),
\(b=Q_0^{-1/2}BQ_0^{-1/2}\), and \(N=I+ta+sb\).
Substituting \(b=(N-I-ta)/s\) into the two terms gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
aN^{-1}b-bN^{-1}a
 &=s^{-1}(N^{-1}a-aN^{-1})
  =N^{-1}(ab-ba)N^{-1},\\
W_{A,B}-W_{A,B}^\top
 &=tsQ_0M^{-1}(AQ_0^{-1}B-BQ_0^{-1}A)M^{-1}Q_0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-exact-pair-antisymmetry}
\end{equation}
All outside factors are invertible or positive scalars. Thus one positive
tested pair already forces \eqref{eq:cg-anchor-commutation}; zero scales
would give no information.

The whitened family is real symmetric and pairwise commuting. Simultaneous
orthogonal diagonalization for the entire family follows by induction on
dimension. If every member is scalar, choose any orthonormal basis.
Otherwise select one nonscalar member. Every other member preserves its
orthogonal eigenspaces, since commutation preserves each eigenspace.
Those spaces have smaller dimensions, so apply the induction to the
restricted families and concatenate their bases. Only finitely many
dimension reductions are used; no countability premise is needed.
For an empty family any basis works. PSD positivity makes all diagonal
entries nonnegative, proving \eqref{eq:cg-anchored-common-cone}.
Conversely, in these coordinates
\begin{equation}
W_{A,B}(t,s)=
H\operatorname{diag}\left(
\frac{ts a_\ell b_\ell}{1+ta_\ell+sb_\ell}
\right)_{\ell=1}^K H^\top,
\qquad a_\ell,b_\ell\geq0.
\label{eq:cg-independent-diagonal-output}
\end{equation}
This proves sufficiency and positivity.

Every tested family therefore lies in a full tested cone. A maximal
family equals that cone. Conversely, a PSD matrix compatible with every
member of a full cone becomes, after whitening and the same orthogonal
change, a matrix commuting with every coordinate rank-one projector.
Commutation with the \(i\)-th projector forces all off-diagonal entries
in its row and column to vanish. The matrix is already nonnegative
diagonal, so it belongs to the cone. This proves maximality. \(\square\)

The fixed anchor and independent pair coverage are material. If only equal
pairs \(A=B\) are admitted, every
\(A(I+(t+s)A)^{-1}A\) is symmetric PSD, even for the family
\(\{I,\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}\),
which has no common congruence-diagonal cone containing all three matrices.
Indeed, a cone containing \(I=HD_0H^\top\) gives the common orthogonal
basis \(HD_0^{1/2}\), whereas the latter two matrices do not commute.
A cone accommodating the weights but not the anchor is also insufficient:
for \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), \(B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\),
\(Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
and \(t=s=1\), the manufactured coefficient is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1/8\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
These are exact failures of the omitted premises.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Stability inside a prescribed diagonal domain}{prop:cg-kron-scalar-domain}
Fix \(H\) and independent coordinate domains
\(\mathscr D_A,\mathscr D_W\subseteq[0,\infty)^K\), with
\(0\in\mathscr D_W\). For
\(A_i=H\operatorname{diag}(a_i)H^\top\) and
\(W_{ij}=H\operatorname{diag}(w_{ij})H^\top\), a defined elimination
of \(e\) has \(d=a_e+\sum_{i\ne e}w_{ie}>0\) coordinatewise and updates
\begin{equation}
w'_{ij}=w_{ij}+\frac{w_{ie}\odot w_{ej}}{d},\qquad
a'_i=a_i+\frac{w_{ie}\odot a_e}{d}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-scalar-updates}
\end{equation}
Products and division are coordinatewise. Closure inside the prescribed
domains for every finite PSD graph and every defined one-node elimination
is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
b_i+\frac{u_i\odot a}{d}\in\mathscr D_A,\qquad
c_{ij}+\frac{u_i\odot u_j}{d}\in\mathscr D_W,\qquad
d=a+\sum_i u_i>0,
\label{eq:cg-kron-star-domain}
\end{equation}
for every finite star inventory
\(a,b_i\in\mathscr D_A\) and \(u_i,c_{ij}\in\mathscr D_W\).
If only full-space SPD graphs are admitted, necessity is restricted to
the inventories producing such graphs.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The off-diagonal Schur blocks give the first update. In each scalar
coordinate, subtracting the newly computed incident-weight sum from the
new diagonal leaves
\begin{equation}
a_i+w_{ie}
-\frac{w_{ie}(w_{ie}+\sum_{j\ne i,e}w_{ej})}{d}
=a_i+\frac{w_{ie}a_e}{d}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-scalar-anchor-remainder}
\end{equation}
This proves the anchor update. Applying these formulas at every actual
star proves sufficiency of \eqref{eq:cg-kron-star-domain}. Conversely,
realize any displayed inventory as a star with the specified retained
anchors and pair weights, and eliminate its center. Its pivot is
positive by the assumption on \(d\), so the outputs must satisfy exactly
the displayed membership conditions. For several eliminated vertices,
positivity of the eliminated principal block ensures positive successive
pivots by the minimum-energy argument in
\Cref{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}. \(\square\)

Containing a family in \(\mathcal C_H\) therefore does not imply closure
inside that family. Already in one dimension, the coefficient set
\(\{1\}\), with anchor one at all three vertices and two unit edges,
produces a weight \(1/3\) and retained anchors \(4/3\).
The failure is membership in the chosen scalar domain, despite positivity
and diagonalization. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\corollaryheading{The shared-kernel version}{cor:cg-kron-shared-kernel}
If \(Q_0\succeq0\), \(N=\ker Q_0\), and
\(N\subseteq\ker A\) for every \(A\in\mathscr W\), all forms descend
to \(\R^K/N\). The descended anchor is SPD, and
\Cref{thm:cg-independent-kron-classification} applies there using an
ordinary inverse. In particular, if \(E_0:\R^r\to N^\perp\) is an
orthonormal inclusion and \(r=\operatorname{rank}Q_0\), the pair-test
property is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
H_r=E_0(E_0^\top Q_0E_0)^{1/2}O,\qquad
Q_0=H_rH_r^\top,\qquad
A=H_r\operatorname{diag}(a_A)H_r^\top,\quad a_A\geq0,
\label{eq:cg-kron-quotient-cone}
\end{equation}
for one \(O\in O(r)\) and all members \(A\).
This is a quotient statement, not an inverse or a normalized density on
the original null directions. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Symmetry and \(An=0\) make each form independent of the representative
of a quotient class. Positivity gives
\(\ker(Q_0+tA+sB)=N\), since a zero of its quadratic must annihilate
every nonnegative summand, including \(Q_0\). The quotient pivot is
therefore positive, and whitening in the coordinates \(E_0\) gives
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-quotient-cone}. When \(r=0\), all the forms vanish
and the quotient is a point. Without the shared-kernel hypothesis,
\(Q_0=0\) and SPD \(A,B\) instead satisfy
\begin{equation}
\bigl[A(tA+sB)^{-1}B\bigr]^{-1}=tB^{-1}+sA^{-1}\succ0.
\label{eq:cg-kron-zero-anchor-parallel}
\end{equation}
even when \(A,B\) do not commute. The three-matrix family containing
\(I\) above excludes a common congruence cone, so it is also a
counterexample to dropping the support premise. A positive-dimensional
null direction has infinite Lebesgue integral, with either a constant
or an exponential linear factor along it; no full-space Gaussian
normalizer has been supplied. \(\square\)

\subsection{Variable contexts and ordered diagonal frames}
\label{sec:cg-kron-variable-context}

On a declared smooth context domain \(U\subseteq\mathcal C\), let
\(Q_0(c)\succ0\) vary smoothly and let \(\mathscr W_c\) be an
inclusion-maximal family for the fixed-anchor pair test at each \(c\).
Assume that the whitened spans
\begin{equation}
\mathscr D_c
=\operatorname{span}_{\R}
\{Q_0(c)^{-1/2}AQ_0(c)^{-1/2}:A\in\mathscr W_c\}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-context-algebra}
\end{equation}
form a smooth rank-\(K\) vector subbundle of
\(U\times\Sym^K\). Pointwise commutation does not supply this regularity
assumption, and no pair using coefficients from different contexts is
admitted by the present hypothesis. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

\theoremheading{Local frames and a global ordering obstruction}{thm:cg-kron-local-context-frame}
Every \(c_0\in U\) has a neighborhood \(U_0\) and a smooth
\(O:U_0\to O(K)\) for which
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr D_c&=O(c)\operatorname{Diag}(K)O(c)^\top,\\
\mathscr W_c&=H(c)\operatorname{Diag}_+(K)H(c)^\top,\\
H(c)&=Q_0(c)^{1/2}O(c).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-local-context-cone}
\end{equation}
A global continuous ordered diagonal frame need not exist, even for a
smooth maximal family over the circle with \(Q_0=I_2\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The finite-dimensional implicit-function theorem says that a smooth
equation \(F(x,y)=0\) with invertible derivative in \(y\) at a solution
has a unique nearby smooth solution \(y(x)\). Apply it on symmetric
matrices to \(S^2-Q_0=0\) at the positive square root. In an
orthonormal eigenbasis of \(S\), its derivative
\(X\mapsto SX+XS\) multiplies entry \(ij\) by \(s_i+s_j>0\).
Thus \(Q_0^{1/2}\), and hence its inverse, are smooth; uniqueness of
the positive square root identifies the local solutions.

By \Cref{thm:cg-independent-kron-classification}, \(\mathscr D_{c_0}\)
contains a matrix with \(K\) distinct eigenvalues. A smooth local
subbundle frame extends it to a smooth section \(T(c)\in\mathscr D_c\).
For \(\det(\lambda I-T(c))=0\), the derivative in \(\lambda\) at
each initial eigenvalue is the product of its nonzero spectral gaps.
The same implicit-function theorem therefore supplies \(K\) distinct
smooth real eigenvalues \(\lambda_i(c)\) on a smaller neighborhood.
Their spectral projectors are
\begin{equation}
P_i(c)=\prod_{j\ne i}
\frac{T(c)-\lambda_j(c)I}{\lambda_i(c)-\lambda_j(c)}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-smooth-projectors}
\end{equation}
In an eigenbasis this polynomial is one on the \(i\)-th eigenspace
and zero on the others, so these are smooth orthogonal rank-one
projectors. Choose a unit \(v_i\in\operatorname{im}P_i(c_0)\).
After shrinking the neighborhood,
\(e_i(c)=P_i(c)v_i/\|P_i(c)v_i\|\) is defined and smooth.
Its columns form \(O(c)\). Every member of \(\mathscr D_c\)
commutes with \(T(c)\), hence preserves these one-dimensional
eigenspaces. Both algebra spaces have dimension \(K\), so the
resulting diagonal containment is equality. Undoing whitening gives
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-local-context-cone}.

For the global obstruction write
\(\theta\in S^1=\R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\), let
\(\mathsf R(\varphi)\) be the planar rotation by \(\varphi\), and set
\begin{equation}
\mathscr W_\theta=
\mathsf R(\theta/4)\operatorname{Diag}_+(2)
\mathsf R(\theta/4)^\top,\qquad
T_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(\theta/2)&\sin(\theta/2)\\
\sin(\theta/2)&-\cos(\theta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-circle-cone}
\end{equation}
Increasing \(\theta\) by \(2\pi\) rotates by another \(\pi/2\),
which permutes the two coordinate axes and preserves the cone.
The algebra is \(\operatorname{span}\{I_2,T_\theta\}\).
Its trace-inner-product projection is
\begin{equation}
X\longmapsto
\frac12\Tr(X)I_2+\frac12\Tr(T_\theta X)T_\theta.
\label{eq:cg-kron-circle-algebra-projector}
\end{equation}
This is smooth and periodic because
\(T_{\theta+2\pi}=-T_\theta\), and has constant rank two.
It therefore defines the required smooth subbundle. The two
trace-one extreme projectors are
\(P_\pm(\theta)=(I_2\pm T_\theta)/2\), which interchange after a
full circuit. Any continuous ordered first projector has
\(\Tr(T_\theta P(\theta))\in\{1,-1\}\), a continuous function
on \([0,2\pi]\), so its sign is constant. Its endpoint is therefore
the opposite projector from its initial value, contradicting
periodicity. An ordered orthogonal diagonal frame would supply such
an ordered projector, so none exists. \(\square\)

Smooth global coefficients still give smooth Schur complements, determinant
offsets, and normalized Gaussian laws wherever the actual precision blocks
remain SPD: matrix inversion is the adjugate divided by a nonzero
determinant, and \(\log\det\) is smooth there. No diagonal frame is
needed to evaluate those formulas. The obstruction is global ordering
of the axes, not probabilistic marginalization. If every cross-context
pair were instead tested against one common fixed anchor,
\Cref{thm:cg-independent-kron-classification} would apply to the union
and force one common frame. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{A graph criterion for unrestricted matrix weights}
\label{sec:cg-kron-graph-boundary}

Now vary every present edge weight independently over all SPD
\(K\times K\) matrices and restrict the graph instead. Let
\(\mathcal G\) be a finite undirected graph with parallel edge copies
allowed and no self-loops. If anchors occur, add a ground vertex \(0\),
represent each present SPD anchor by an edge to \(0\), and retain \(0\)
throughout elimination. Fix its coordinate to zero only when forming
the ordinary anchored probability law. In the augmented common frame,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{\rm aug}(z)=
\frac12\sum_{e\in\mathscr E(\mathcal G)}
(z_{i_e}-z_{j_e})^\top W_e(z_{i_e}-z_{j_e}),\qquad W_e\succ0.
\label{eq:cg-kron-augmented-energy}
\end{equation}
Let \(E\) be the eliminated ordinary vertices and \(R\) the remaining
vertices, including ground. For each connected component \(C\) of
the induced graph on \(E\), define its retained terminal boundary
\begin{equation}
B(C)=\{r\in R:\text{some edge joins }r\text{ to a vertex of }C\}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-terminal-boundary}
\end{equation}
Assume \(B(C)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(C\).
\status{HYPOTHESIS}

This is precisely the structural condition making the hidden Dirichlet
block positive for
every SPD assignment: with retained coordinates zero, zero edge
energy forces equality throughout a hidden component and then zero
at its boundary. A component without boundary instead has a
nonzero constant hidden null vector. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Universal closure at a fixed retained boundary}{thm:cg-kron-boundary-classification}
For \(K\geq2\), the Schur complement of \eqref{eq:cg-kron-augmented-energy}
is an augmented matrix-weighted Laplacian for every independent SPD
edge assignment if and only if
\begin{equation}
|B(C)|\leq2\quad\text{for every connected hidden component }C.
\label{eq:cg-kron-two-terminal-criterion}
\end{equation}
Ground counts as one terminal. Here membership means symmetric
nonpositive off-diagonal blocks and diagonal blocks equal to the
negative sum of the off-diagonal blocks. A two-terminal component
contributes one SPD edge, and a one-terminal component contributes
zero quadratic form. For \(K=1\), every graph satisfying the
nonempty-boundary hypothesis closes, so the bound of two is not
necessary. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The hidden Dirichlet block is block diagonal over the components.
Each component's Schur form is the minimum of its energy with
boundary values fixed, hence is PSD. Equal boundary values admit
the same constant value at every hidden vertex and give zero energy.
With one terminal this makes the entire form zero. With two terminals,
write its symmetric block matrix as \(M\). The equations
\(M_{11}+M_{12}=0\) and \(M_{21}+M_{22}=0\), together with
\(M_{21}=M_{12}^\top\), force
\begin{equation}
M=\begin{pmatrix}W_C&-W_C\\-W_C&W_C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad W_C=W_C^\top\succeq0.
\label{eq:cg-kron-two-terminal-response}
\end{equation}
If a nonzero terminal difference had zero energy, its unique minimizing
hidden extension would make every edge difference zero. Connectedness
would force the two terminal values equal, a contradiction. Thus
\(W_C\succ0\). Summing these component contributions and direct
retained edges proves sufficiency, including an anchor when one
terminal is ground.

For necessity, a hidden star with three retained terminals \(a,b,c\)
and weights
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=I_2,\qquad
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-three-terminal-weights}
\end{equation}
has pivot and manufactured \(ab\) coefficient
\begin{equation}
D=A+B+C_0=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{ab}=AD^{-1}B=
\frac1{19}\begin{pmatrix}5&-1\\-2&8\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-three-terminal-defect}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is nonsymmetric. If one terminal is ground,
give it the \(C_0\) leg, leaving the defect between two ordinary
terminals. Direct sums with \(I_{K-2}\) extend the obstruction to
every \(K>2\).

Consider any hidden component with at least three terminal neighbors.
Select one incident edge from each of three such terminals, connect
their hidden endpoints by a minimal tree inside the component, and
extend it to a tree containing every remaining hidden vertex.
The three-terminal core has a unique branching vertex, allowing
coincident hidden endpoints and legs of length one. Extra hidden
branches have no terminal and minimize to their attachment value,
so they contribute no quadratic form. On a core leg, minimize
\(\sum_j d_j^\top W_jd_j/2\) subject to
\(\sum_jd_j=d\). Stationarity gives \(W_jd_j=\lambda\), hence
\begin{equation}
W_{\rm leg}=\left(\sum_jW_j^{-1}\right)^{-1}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-flat-series}
\end{equation}
The positive Hessian makes this the unique minimum. For a leg of
length \(l\), assigning \(lA\) to each edge realizes \(A\);
do the same for \(B,C_0\). Assign \(I_K\) to the extra hidden tree
edges. Temporarily set unused edges to zero. The selected tree
still joins every hidden vertex to a terminal, so its Dirichlet
block is SPD and its reduced core has the defect
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-three-terminal-defect}. Restore every unused
present edge with weight \(\varepsilon I_K\). The Dirichlet inverse
and Schur complement are continuous at \(\varepsilon=0\), where
the determinant is nonzero. Their nonzero antisymmetric block
therefore persists for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\).
Every final edge weight is SPD. Other hidden components can use
scalar identity weights, and direct retained edges are symmetric;
neither can cancel this antisymmetric block. This constructs a
failure on every excluded graph.

In a scalar graph, one-node elimination with pivot
\(d=\sum_iw_i>0\) adds \(w_iw_j/d\geq0\) between distinct retained
neighbors. The constant-vector kernel preserves row sums.
Every successive pivot is positive because the eliminated
Dirichlet block and its Schur complements are SPD.
Repeating the formula proves scalar closure, including the
scalar-identity assertion used above. \(\square\)

The sufficiency implication extends to PSD weights whenever the specified
hidden Dirichlet block remains SPD. Add \(\varepsilon I_K\) to every
present weight and pass to \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
Continuity of the inverse at the positive limiting block preserves
the Schur matrix, and symmetry, zero row sums, and PSD edge weights
are closed conditions. The two-terminal output may then be only
semidefinite. A singular limiting Dirichlet block is excluded from
this argument. \status{ESTABLISHED}

For a declared nested collection of eliminated sets, universal closure
at every declared level is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-two-terminal-criterion} at every such set.
Direct and successive Schur elimination agree by
\Cref{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}. This does not assert closure
at all intermediate one-vertex prefixes: a component with two final
terminals can expose three or more temporary terminals during an
ordering. Closure at every prefix requires the same criterion at
every prefix. An excluded graph has some failing SPD assignments,
not necessarily failure for every assignment. Flatness here means
plain differences in a common frame; a graph coboundary can first
be trivialized, but genuinely nonflat links require another result.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{A proper Gaussian beyond every positive two-node decomposition}
\label{sec:cg-kron-positive-pair-obstruction}

The three-terminal star also distinguishes failure of the original
difference-weight ansatz from failure of every positive pair
representation on the same retained node spaces.

\propositionheading{Rational obstruction to positive pair decomposition}{prop:cg-kron-positive-pair-obstruction}
There is a proper four-node Gaussian with SPD edge weights and
retained anchors whose marginal on three two-dimensional nodes is
not a sum of PSD quadratic terms each supported on at most two
of those nodes. Thus arbitrary new invertible link transports,
rectangular pair residuals, parallel edges, self-loops, and freely
redistributed PSD anchors cannot represent that marginal within
the positive two-node class. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Let \(L_\star\) be the six-dimensional retained Schur Laplacian of
the unanchored star
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-three-terminal-weights}, and put
\begin{equation}
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
t=\frac1{104},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{20000}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-rational-separator-constants}
\end{equation}
Define a symmetric matrix \(Y\) in three two-dimensional blocks by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y_{ii}&=(1+t^2)I_2,\\
Y_{ab}&=I_2+tJ,\qquad Y_{ba}=I_2-tJ,\\
Y_{ij}&=I_2\quad\text{for the other distinct pairs}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-rational-separator}
\end{equation}
Every principal submatrix on one or two nodes is PSD.
For the special pair,
\((I_2+tJ)^\top(I_2+tJ)=(1+t^2)I_2\), so its cross-block norm is
\(\sqrt{1+t^2}\leq1+t^2\); other cross-block norms equal one.
For diagonal blocks \(\alpha I\) and a cross block \(C\),
the quadratic is bounded below by
\(\alpha(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2)-2\|C\|\|u\|\|v\|\geq0\)
when \(\|C\|\leq\alpha\).
Consequently \(\Tr(YP)\geq0\) for any PSD matrix \(P\) supported
on at most two nodes: diagonalize its supporting principal block
as a sum of nonnegative multiples of vector outer products and
pair each with that PSD principal block of \(Y\).
Every sum of positive one-node or two-node terms has nonnegative
pairing with \(Y\).

Direct substitution into \(A-AD^{-1}A\), \(B-BD^{-1}B\), and
\(C_0-C_0D^{-1}C_0\) gives diagonal traces
\(36/19,29/19,39/19\), respectively, hence
\(\Tr L_\star=104/19\).
Write \(Y=(\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top)\otimes I_2+tS+t^2I_6\),
where \(S_{ab}=J\), \(S_{ba}=-J\), and other blocks are zero.
The constant-vector kernel makes the first pairing vanish.
Since \(M_{ab}-M_{ab}^\top=J/19\), the two skew pairings give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Tr(SL_\star)
&=\Tr\bigl(J(M_{ab}-M_{ab}^\top)\bigr)=-\frac2{19},\\
\Tr(YL_\star)
&=\frac{-2t+104t^2}{19}=-\frac1{1976}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-negative-dual-pairing}
\end{equation}
Put the actual anchor \(\varepsilon I_2\) at each of the three
retained vertices in the original star, leaving its hidden anchor
zero. Zero total energy forces all anchored terminal coordinates
zero and then the connected hidden coordinate zero, so the full
precision is SPD. Its retained precision is
\(\Lambda_R=L_\star+\varepsilon I_6\succ0\), but
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Tr(Y\Lambda_R)&<-\frac1{1976}+\frac7{20000}<0,\\
\Tr Y&=6(1+t^2)<7.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-kron-proper-dual-obstruction}
\end{equation}
The final inequality follows from \(7\cdot1976=13832<20000\).
This contradicts the necessary nonnegative pairing for every
positive two-node decomposition. All constants are rational,
and \eqref{eq:cg-kron-normalized-prior} supplies its finite
Gaussian normalizer. \(\square\)

Block-diagonal invertible changes of retained vertex frames preserve the
positive two-node support class by congruence, so they cannot remove
this obstruction. Mixing distinct retained nodes, adding latent
vertices, using signed pair terms, or allowing positive terms
supported on three or more nodes changes the representation
problem. The SPD marginal remains a valid complete quadratic
Gaussian action. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Nonflat series and cycle marginalization}
\label{sec:cg-nonflat-series-kron}

We now retain link data instead of requiring a flat difference
presentation. Fix a finite multigraph, one orientation for each edge
copy \(e=(i,j)\), invertible represented links
\(\Theta_e:\R^K\to\R^K\), SPD weights \(W_e\) at the receiving
endpoint, PSD vertex anchors \(A_i\), arbitrary sources \(h_i\),
and a scalar offset \(\mathfrak c\). The action is
\begin{equation}
\Phi(z)=\mathfrak c
+\frac12\sum_{e=(i,j)}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)
+\frac12\sum_i z_i^\top A_i z_i-\sum_i h_i^\top z_i.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-action}
\end{equation}
Choose retained vertices \(R\) and hidden vertices \(E\).
Every hidden vertex has total degree two, counting a self-loop twice,
and has zero quadratic anchor \(A_i=0\). Hidden sources need not
vanish. Retained anchors are protected throughout elimination.
Assume the assembled precision \(\Lambda\) is SPD.
These are hypotheses on a finite Gaussian realization, not on
arbitrary statistical fibers. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

Positive definiteness has an explicit criterion. The kernel of
\(\Lambda\) consists exactly of vectors satisfying
\(z_i=\Theta_ez_j\) on every edge and \(A_iz_i=0\) at every
vertex, since its quadratic is a sum of nonnegative residuals and
anchor terms. In each connected component choose a root and a
spanning tree, with invertible tree transports \(T_i\) from that
root and \(T_r=I\). Every tree-compatible vector has the form
\(z_i=T_i u\). Therefore
\begin{equation}
\Lambda\succ0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigcap_{\substack{e=(i,j)\\\text{non-tree}}}
\ker(T_i-\Theta_eT_j)
\quad\cap\quad\bigcap_i\ker(A_iT_i)=\{0\}
\quad\text{in each component}.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-properness}
\end{equation}
One retained SPD anchor in every component is sufficient. An all-hidden
unanchored cycle with holonomy \(M\) is admitted exactly when
\(I-M\) is invertible; \(M\ne I\) alone is insufficient.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Exact nonflat series pivot}{prop:cg-nonflat-series-pivot}
Orient the two non-loop edges through a hidden vertex \(e\) so their
residuals are \(x-Tz_e\) and \(z_e-Sy\), with SPD weights \(W,V\),
respectively. The endpoints \(x,y\) may be coordinates of the same
retained or hidden vertex. Set
\begin{equation}
D=T^\top WT+V,\qquad
U=(W^{-1}+TV^{-1}T^\top)^{-1},\qquad
\Theta_{\rm new}=TS.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-weight}
\end{equation}
Eliminating \(z_e\) replaces the two quadratic edge terms by
\((x-TSy)^\top U(x-TSy)/2\). With endpoints \(i,j\), the complete
source and scalar updates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_i^{\rm new}&=h_i+WT D^{-1}h_e,\\
h_j^{\rm new}&=h_j+S^\top V D^{-1}h_e,\\
\mathfrak c^{\rm new}
&=\mathfrak c+\frac12\log\det D-\frac K2\log(2\pi)
                         -\frac12h_e^\top D^{-1}h_e.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-source-scalar}
\end{equation}
If \(i=j\), both source increments add at that vertex and the
new edge is the marked self-loop \((TS,U)\). No new quadratic
vertex anchor is generated. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The hidden terms are
\begin{equation}
\frac12z_e^\top Dz_e-z_e^\top q
+\frac12x^\top Wx+\frac12y^\top S^\top VSy,
\qquad q=T^\top Wx+VSy+h_e.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-square-data}
\end{equation}
Here \(D\succ0\), so completing the square multiplies the remaining
density by
\begin{equation*}
(2\pi)^{K/2}(\det D)^{-1/2}\exp(q^\top D^{-1}q/2).
\end{equation*}
Let \(P=W-WTD^{-1}T^\top W\). From \(D=T^\top WT+V\),
\(PT=WTD^{-1}V\); consequently
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P(W^{-1}+TV^{-1}T^\top)&=I,\\
V-VD^{-1}V&=T^\top PT.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-matrix-identities}
\end{equation}
The first factor's inverse is SPD, so \(P=U\).
The second identity and \(PT=WTD^{-1}V\) identify the endpoint
diagonal and cross terms as exactly the new edge energy.
The linear and constant terms in \(q^\top D^{-1}q/2\) give
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-source-scalar}. If the two endpoints
coincide, substituting \(x=y\) gives the loop and the sum of source
increments. Reversing an original edge requires the exact pair
\((\Theta,W)\mapsto(\Theta^{-1},\Theta^\top W\Theta)\),
because the old residual is minus \(\Theta\) times the reversed
residual; this permits the displayed orientation without changing
the energy. \(\square\)

\theoremheading{Arbitrary-order nonflat series closure}{thm:cg-nonflat-series-kron}
Under the hypotheses of \eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-action},
eliminating any subset of hidden vertices in any order gives the
exact normalized Gaussian marginal within the same marked
multigraph class. Every surviving hidden vertex still has degree
two and zero quadratic anchor, and the remaining precision is SPD.
Distinct parallel paths remain distinct edges. Along an oriented
path \(v_0,\ldots,v_m\), define
\(P_0=I\) and \(P_j=\Theta_1\cdots\Theta_j\), where
\(\Theta_i\) maps from \(v_i\) to \(v_{i-1}\). Its fused data are
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{\rm path}=P_m,\qquad
W_{\rm path}^{-1}
=\sum_{i=1}^m P_{i-1}W_i^{-1}P_{i-1}^\top.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-path-formula}
\end{equation}
A returning path has retained loop holonomy \(M=P_m\) and quadratic
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm loop}=(I-M)^\top W_{\rm path}(I-M).
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-retained-loop}
\end{equation}
Each cycle meeting the retained set keeps its nonflat holonomy mark.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
A degree-two hidden vertex without a loop has exactly two incident
edges counted with multiplicity, so
\Cref{prop:cg-nonflat-series-pivot} applies. If its neighbors differ,
replacing the two edges by one preserves their degrees. If they
coincide, a loop carries the same two incidences. No surviving
hidden anchor is introduced. A degree-two vertex already carrying
a loop is an isolated component. It occurs initially or as the
last vertex of an all-hidden cycle. Its loop block is SPD by the
global positivity hypothesis and can be integrated as one Gaussian
block, contributing only the scalar in
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-complete-schur}. Positivity of every remaining
Schur complement was proved in
\Cref{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}. These observations prove
closure by induction for every elimination order.

Write a path segment as \((\Theta,C)\), with \(C=W^{-1}\).
The binary series operation is
\begin{equation}
(\Theta,C)*(\Psi,F)
=(\Theta\Psi,\ C+\Theta F\Theta^\top).
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-composition}
\end{equation}
For a third pair \((\Xi,G)\), either parenthesization gives
transport \(\Theta\Psi\Xi\) and covariance
\(C+\Theta F\Theta^\top+\Theta\Psi G\Psi^\top\Theta^\top\).
This proves associativity with the stated matrix order and yields
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-path-formula} by induction. Each covariance
summand is SPD. Substitution of equal endpoints proves
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-retained-loop}. The complete scalar and source
terms agree for all orders by the finite nonnegative Gaussian
integration argument in
\Cref{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}; equivalently, the product
of successive pivot determinants is the eliminated-block
determinant in that proposition. \(\square\)

A component meeting \(R\) decomposes into maximal hidden paths
between retained endpoints, which may coincide. A cycle with one
retained vertex becomes one loop; with several retained vertices
its ordered coarse link product equals its original cycle product.
An all-hidden cycle can instead be integrated away entirely only
under the invertibility condition in
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-properness}. Its raw partition factor
survives, but its normalized marginal on the empty coordinate set
does not identify its holonomy. More generally, a retained
quadratic \(Q_{\rm loop}\) need not identify its separate marks
\(M,W_{\rm path}\). These marks are declared structural data,
and the theorem does not infer them uniquely from the Gaussian
law. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Gauge and rescaling covariance with scalar factors}{prop:cg-nonflat-series-gauge}
For nodewise passive coordinates \(z_i'=G_i z_i\),
\(G_i\in\GL(K)\), the transformed coefficients are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_e'&=G_i\Theta_eG_j^{-1},&
W_e'&=G_i^{-\top}W_eG_i^{-1},\\
A_i'&=G_i^{-\top}A_iG_i^{-1},&
h_i'&=G_i^{-\top}h_i.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-gauge-law}
\end{equation}
The series rule commutes with this change:
\(D'=G_e^{-\top}DG_e^{-1}\),
\(U'=G_i^{-\top}UG_i^{-1}\), and
\(\Theta_{\rm new}'=G_iTSG_j^{-1}\).
With the raw scalar \(\mathfrak c\) unchanged,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak c_R'
 &=\mathfrak c_R-\sum_{e\in E}\log|\det G_e|,\\
Z'&=\left(\prod_{i\in R\cup E}|\det G_i|\right)Z,\\
p_R'(x')&=|\det G_R|^{-1}p_R(G_R^{-1}x'),
\qquad G_R=\operatorname{blkdiag}_{i\in R}G_i.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-jacobians}
\end{equation}
Alternatively, transforming the raw measure itself uses
\(\mathfrak c'=\mathfrak c+\sum_{i\in R\cup E}\log|\det G_i|\).
Then \(Z'=Z\) and
\(\mathfrak c_R'=\mathfrak c_R+\sum_{i\in R}\log|\det G_i|\).
Both conventions give the same normalized pushforward law.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Substitution into each residual proves
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-gauge-law}.
The inverse covariance in \eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-weight}
transforms by
\begin{equation}
W'^{-1}+T'V'^{-1}T'^\top
=G_i(W^{-1}+TV^{-1}T^\top)G_i^\top.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-covariance-gauge}
\end{equation}
The source increments transform as endpoint covectors;
\(h_e^\top D^{-1}h_e\) is invariant, while
\(\det D'=\det D/|\det G_e|^2\).
This proves the hidden scalar shift. Changing variables in the
full integral gives its total Jacobian and then the retained
Jacobian in \eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-jacobians}. Adding the
total log-Jacobian to the initial action instead gives the
raw-measure convention. A retained loop transforms by
\(M'=G_rMG_r^{-1}\), preserving its conjugacy class and
noncommutation with another loop based at \(r\). \(\square\)

A declared invertible retained-coordinate rescaling \(x'=Tx\) has
the normalized Gaussian pushforward
\begin{equation}
\mu_R'=T\mu_R,\qquad C_R'=TC_RT^\top,\qquad
p_R'(x')=|\det T|^{-1}p_R(T^{-1}x').
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-rescaling}
\end{equation}
This follows from the same change of variables. Block-diagonal
\(T\) preserves the marked graph presentation; general \(T\)
can mix vertices and need not do so. Any restriction of the links
to a matrix subgroup requires closure under products and inverses,
and gauges in that subgroup preserve the restricted domain.
Arbitrary \(\GL(K)\) gauges carry it to its transformed domain.
No preferred physical scale transformation is selected by this
finite pushforward. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{A noncommuting nonflat proper example}{prop:cg-nonflat-series-example}
Take one retained vertex \(a\), hidden vertices \(e,f\), and two
distinct two-edge cycles \(a-e-a\) and \(a-f-a\). Their edge
copies are distinct interactions, not repeated orientations of
one edge. Give \(a\) the anchor \(I_2\), both hidden vertices zero
quadratic anchors, and choose
\begin{equation}
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
W=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-example-data}
\end{equation}
The first cycle has transports \(T_1,I_2\) and weights \(W,V\);
the second has transports \(T_2,I_2\) and weights \(I_2,I_2\).
Put \(h_e=(1,0)^\top\), all other sources zero, and
\(\mathfrak c=0\). This is a proper Gaussian with noncommuting
loop holonomies and coefficients outside every one fixed
congruence-diagonal cone containing its anchor.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Every edge weight is SPD, and the retained SPD anchor makes
\eqref{eq:cg-nonflat-series-properness} hold. The two holonomies
satisfy \(T_1T_2-T_2T_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\ne0\).
They cannot both become flat or diagonal under one based
conjugation. The coefficient triple \(I_2,W,V\) cannot lie in
a common congruence-diagonal cone: including \(I_2\) would give
a common orthogonal basis as above, but
\(WV-VW=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
This fixed-chart coefficient statement and the gauge-invariant
holonomy statement are distinct.

For the exact marginal, the hidden pivots and loop weights are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D_e&=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&5\end{pmatrix},&
U_e&=\frac1{11}\begin{pmatrix}7&-2\\-2&10\end{pmatrix},\\
D_f&=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},&
U_f&=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-example-pivots}
\end{equation}
Here \(\det D_e=11\), \(\det D_f=5\), and the two loop
quadratics are
\(\operatorname{diag}(0,7/11)\) and
\(\operatorname{diag}(2/5,0)\).
The formulas above therefore give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_a^{\rm eff}&=\operatorname{diag}(7/5,18/11),&
h_a^{\rm eff}&=(1,-3/11)^\top,\\
\mathfrak c_a^{\rm eff}
 &=\frac12\log55-2\log(2\pi)-\frac5{22},&
\mu_a&=(5/7,-1/6)^\top,\\
C_a&=\operatorname{diag}(5/7,11/18),&
Z&=(2\pi)^3\exp(17/28)/\sqrt{126}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-example-law}
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\det\Lambda=11\cdot5\cdot(126/55)=126\),
\(D_e^{-1}h_e=(5,-2)^\top/11\), and the total
completed-square value is
\(5/11+5/7+1/22=17/14\).
These calculations verify the source, covariance, scalar,
and normalization in the same proper law. \(\square\)

The zero hidden quadratic anchor is essential for the particular series
rule. In the scalar chain with unit edges and hidden anchor
\(\alpha>0\), the actual pivot is \(D=2+\alpha\), and the retained
quadratic matrix is
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}1-1/D&-1/D\\-1/D&1-1/D\end{pmatrix}
=\frac1D\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
+\frac{\alpha}{D}I_2.
\label{eq:cg-nonflat-series-hidden-anchor-boundary}
\end{equation}
New anchors arise, so the zero-anchor rule cannot be applied
unchanged. This does not refute all larger anchored closure
classes. In the flat graph theorem a hidden anchor is an edge to
ground, which can create a third terminal; in the independent-family
theorem the fixed positive hidden anchor is precisely the premise
forcing the commutation constraint. Degree two in the nonflat
theorem guarantees every intermediate order, whereas the graph
criterion concerns each selected final eliminated set. These
quantifiers remain distinct. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Finite semialgebraic classification}
\label{sec:cg-semialgebraic-kron}

The preceding analytical classifications impose recognizable structural
conditions. A broader exact decision theorem is available after specifying
how the admissible families are described. Fix finitely many labeled
nodes with finite real coordinate spaces, a finite list of oriented edge
copies and loops, and a finite list of retained coordinate sets. The
reference measures are the declared coordinate Lebesgue measures. A fine
parameter \(x\in\R^p\) belongs to \(A\) when it satisfies a supplied finite
formula of polynomial equalities and strict or weak inequalities with
real algebraic coefficients. Each coefficient has an exact finite
representation, for example a defining integer polynomial and a rational
isolating interval. Finite Boolean combinations and existential auxiliary
variables are allowed. Matrix entries are rational expressions in the
parameters on an explicitly specified nonzero-denominator domain.
Every admitted fine precision \(\Lambda(x)\) is the actual assembled
symmetric positive-definite precision, with source \(h(x)\).
These finite-description and properness hypotheses are introduced for
the decision theorem; they are not assumed for every possible RG family.
Singular or rank-changing Gaussian supports are excluded from this
full-SPD statement. \Cref{sec:cg-variable-support-quotients} supplies a
separate finite extension with a declared quotient reference and map
contract.

A retained presentation has a finite tuple \(y\in\R^q\), a supplied
semialgebraic admissibility predicate \(B(y)\), and rational precision and
source matrices \(\Lambda_{\rm c}(y),h_{\rm c}(y)\). Let \(\mathcal E(x,y)\)
record any further finite semialgebraic compatibility conditions.
Correlated weights, nonconvex domains, variable anchors, nonflat
transports, and polynomial path identities are allowed. A finite union
of permitted graph inventories is allowed as well. The target is the
statement that \emph{every} admitted fine parameter has \emph{some}
admitted retained presentation of its exact normalized Gaussian marginal,
satisfying every declared compatibility condition. It does not ask for
an unencoded continuous function, a global naturality law, or an
unbounded inventory of individually marked edges.
\status{DEFINITION}

The imported real-closed-field theorem has the following effective form:
given a finite first-order formula in polynomial equalities and
inequalities over an effective real algebraic coefficient field, an
algorithm returns an equivalent quantifier-free formula in its free
variables; closed sentences are therefore decidable
\cite[Sec.~6]{CohenMahboubi2012}. The operations, coefficient
representations, and finite variables just specified match these
hypotheses. This theorem asserts termination, without a useful complexity
bound or a numerical sign tolerance.

\paragraph{Encoding the exact Gaussian marginal.}
For a nonempty hidden coordinate set \(E\), write
\(D=\Lambda_{EE}\), \(d=\det D>0\), and \(J=\operatorname{adj}D\).
Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_\Lambda(x)&=d\Lambda_{RR}-\Lambda_{RE}J\Lambda_{ER},\\
N_h(x)&=dh_R-\Lambda_{RE}Jh_E,\\
d\Lambda_{\rm c}(y)&=N_\Lambda(x),\qquad
dh_{\rm c}(y)=N_h(x).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-semialgebraic-schur}
\end{equation}
For an empty hidden set, use \(d=1\), \(N_\Lambda=\Lambda\),
and \(N_h=h\). Thus no empty-block inverse is needed.
The adjugate identity \(D^{-1}=J/d\), together with
\Cref{prop:cg-normalized-schur-action}, proves that the last line
is exactly equality of marginal precision and source.

All admissions in this formula have polynomial encodings.
For a symmetric matrix, positive leading principal determinants are
equivalent to SPD: completion of the first coordinate's square gives
a positive first pivot and a Schur block whose leading determinants
are the subsequent leading determinants divided by that pivot;
induction proves the assertion. PSD is equivalent to an existential
Gram equation \(M=TT^\top\), since a real symmetric PSD matrix has an
orthogonal eigendecomposition with nonnegative eigenvalues, and the
converse follows from \(v^\top TT^\top v=\|T^\top v\|^2\).
Invertibility is \((\det\Theta)^2>0\). A rational denominator \(q\)
can be replaced by an inverse variable \(u\) with \(uq=1\).
This keeps its nonzero domain and makes every remaining expression
polynomial. Multiplying an equation by a nonzero denominator is valid;
multiplying an inequality by a denominator of unknown sign is not.
The inverse-variable construction, or a finite split into its two
sign cases, handles that distinction exactly.

There is no loss of information about the normalized Gaussian law.
The Schur precision is SPD because for nonzero retained \(z\),
\begin{equation}
z^\top\Lambda_R^{\rm eff}z
=\min_t (z,t)^\top\Lambda(z,t)>0,
\qquad
t_{\min}=-D^{-1}\Lambda_{ER}z.
\label{eq:cg-semialgebraic-schur-positive}
\end{equation}
The minimum is attained, and its full vector still has nonzero
retained part. For nonsingular Gaussian laws, equality of covariances
gives equality of precisions; equality of means then gives equality
of sources because \(h=\Lambda\mu\). The reverse implication follows
from their explicit normalized densities. Equation
\eqref{eq:cg-semialgebraic-schur} therefore encodes exact law equality
on every retained coordinate, not equality at sampled points.

\theoremheading{Exact finite semialgebraic Kron classification}{thm:cg-semialgebraic-kron-classification}
Under the preceding finite-description hypotheses, let \(M(x,y)\)
be the conjunction of \(B(y)\), \(\mathcal E(x,y)\), the retained SPD conditions,
all denominator admissions, and
\eqref{eq:cg-semialgebraic-schur}. There is an algorithm producing a
quantifier-free formula \(G(x)\) for exactly the admissible fine
parameters with an exact retained presentation. It also constructs
the full failure locus and decides both universal closure and
nonemptiness:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G(x)&\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 A(x)\wedge\exists y \bigl[M(x,y)\bigr],\\
F(x)&=A(x)\wedge\neg G(x),\\
&\forall x\bigl(A(x)\Rightarrow\exists y \bigl[M(x,y)\bigr]\bigr),
\qquad
\exists x \bigl[A(x)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-semialgebraic-classification}
\end{equation}
The witnesses include any introduced inverse or Gram variables.
The nonemptiness sentence is separate: vacuous closure is not an
inhabited construction. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Determinants, adjugates, and matrix products use finitely many
additions and multiplications. The preceding encodings therefore
produce a finite polynomial-sign formula over exactly the stated
coefficient field. Effective quantifier elimination produces \(G\)
and \(F\), and decides the two closed sentences. Conversely, every
satisfying retained tuple gives an admitted precision and source
equal to those of the exact marginal. Every admitted presentation
of that law satisfies those equalities and all specified constraints,
so it supplies a witness for the formula. This proves both directions.
The corresponding existential sentences distinguish no good inputs,
some good inputs, and all good inputs. The theorem decides the
declared problem; it does not assert that closure always holds.
\(\square\)

A nonempty failure locus has a point with real algebraic coordinates.
Indeed, apply quantifier elimination to its existential sentence
over both \(\R\) and the real closed field of real algebraic numbers.
The resulting signs of algebraic constants agree, so the sentence
has the same truth value in the two fields. Enumeration of finite
tuples of algebraic numbers and exact sign evaluation of \(F\)
therefore eventually produces a witness. Density alone would not
justify this assertion for a thin algebraic set. The witness need
not be rational or small. Formula construction is effective from
the declared finite input; evaluation at arbitrary real parameters
given only by approximations still lacks an exact equality or
sign oracle.

\paragraph{Sources, raw scalars, and observed laws.}
The source equation in \eqref{eq:cg-semialgebraic-schur} is part of
the classification. The raw scalar is retained by the exact analytic
formula
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak c_{\rm c}
=\mathfrak c+\frac12\log\det D
-\frac{\dim z_E}{2}\log(2\pi)
-\frac12h_E^\top D^{-1}h_E.
\label{eq:cg-semialgebraic-raw-scalar}
\end{equation}
The prior reverse conditional is
\(\mathcal N(D^{-1}(h_E-\Lambda_{ER}z_R),D^{-1})\);
integration preserves the full raw mass. These statements follow
from the completed square
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-completed-square}. Formula
\eqref{eq:cg-semialgebraic-raw-scalar} is not an ordered-ring
constraint. Additional conditions on logarithms, exponentials,
fixed local-factor normalizers, or numerical evidence enter the
decision theorem only after a valid polynomial reduction has
actually been supplied. Introducing an unconstrained variable
named for a logarithm does not encode its graph.

One additive constant independent of every random coordinate and
observation cancels from a globally normalized joint and hence
from its evidence, although it changes the raw density and its
mass. An offset depending only on an externally fixed model
parameter likewise cancels for each fixed model. A factor
depending on retained random coordinates, an observation, or
a random branch is not such a constant and need not cancel.
Exact raw marginal equality retains the scalar pointwise.
For a linear Gaussian sensor with algebraic matrix data and SPD
noise covariance, the full observed joint is again a proper
Gaussian with rational natural parameters: its precision is
positive because its quadratic is the sum of the positive prior
quadratic and
\((o-Lz)^\top\Gamma_o^{-1}(o-Lz)\).
Keeping the observation coordinates in the marginal therefore
includes the entire observed experiment in the same finite
classification. Its evidence and the conditional-KL identity
of \Cref{sec:cg-kl-recovery} are preserved under their stated
support and finite-KL conventions. A sensor using hidden
coordinates generally changes the posterior reverse conditional.
An arbitrary nonlinear sensor or singular noise is not admitted
by this proper-Gaussian polynomial argument.

\paragraph{Simultaneous finite diagrams.}
For a fixed finite acyclic diagram of retained coordinate sets,
introduce one common tuple \(y_v\) at each object. Put all arrow
relations, path identities, and specified structural conditions
into one formula, and quantify the whole tuple simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
\forall x\left(A(x)\Rightarrow
\exists (y_v)_{v\in V_{\rm diag}}
M_{\rm diag}\bigl(x,(y_v)_{v\in V_{\rm diag}}\bigr)\right).
\label{eq:cg-semialgebraic-diagram}
\end{equation}
The same theorem decides this sentence. Separate witnesses for
each arrow would not give a compatible value at a shared object.
At the law level, coordinate marginalization composes by the
equality of iterated integrals for the positive, integrable
Gaussian density; the inherited sources and raw scalars agree
as well. Extra marks remain additional constraints, since equal
densities do not identify holonomies or choose a structural frame.

For a fixed finite list of rational operations on one declared
domain, totality and universal one-step domain invariance imply
admission of every finite composition by induction. Undefined
denominators must count as failure of totality, rather than being
removed to make the implication vacuous. This implication does
not require an a priori bound on the number of iterations, but
it supplies neither a limit nor a fixed-point theorem and does
not encode an arbitrary changing-dimension hierarchy.

\paragraph{Bounding an unmarked positive presentation.}
An unrestricted number of independently scalable unmarked
summands can also be reduced to finite input. Let
\(\R^N=\bigoplus_{i\in V}\R^{d_i}\), let \(P_i\) denote the
coordinate projection, and put \(D_N=N(N+1)/2\).
An anchor contributes \(P_i^\top A_iP_i\) with \(A_i\succeq0\).
For nodes of equal dimension an allowed square link contributes
\begin{equation}
S_e=(P_i-\Theta_eP_j)^\top W_e(P_i-\Theta_eP_j),
\qquad W_e\succ0,\quad\det\Theta_e\ne0.
\label{eq:cg-finite-pair-generator}
\end{equation}
The target equivalence is equality of the summed matrix in these
fixed retained coordinates. Allowed endpoint types form a fixed
finite list. Every surviving generator may be independently
rescaled by an arbitrary positive number, and unmarked copies
may be deleted. These are substantive assumptions; a correlated
admissibility predicate need not permit either operation.

\propositionheading{Finite positive pair-presentation bound}{prop:cg-finite-pair-presentation-bound}
If \(Q\in\operatorname{Sym}_N\) has any finite sum representation
by the anchors and links just declared, it has one with at most
\(D_N\) nonzero summands. With unrestricted PSD anchors, it can
have at most one anchor at each node and no self-loops.
Every retained edge weight remains strictly positive definite
and every transport remains invertible. Membership in this
unmarked presentation class is equivalent to one finite
disjunction of existential polynomial systems.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
A loop is the PSD anchor
\[
P_i^\top(I-\Theta_e)^\top W_e(I-\Theta_e)P_i.
\]
Absorb unmarked loops into anchors, sum anchors at each node,
and delete zero terms. Reversal of a non-loop orientation
replaces \(\Theta\) by \(\Theta^{-1}\) and \(W\) by
\(\Theta^\top W\Theta\), preserving the residual quadratic;
a fixed orientation can therefore be used when the transport
domain is the unrestricted general linear group. For a
restricted domain, retain its original finite orientation types.

Write the remaining sum as \(Q=\sum_{r=1}^m c_rT_r\), initially
with \(c_r=1\), where each \(T_r\ne0\) is an admitted PSD
generator. If \(m>D_N=\dim\operatorname{Sym}_N\), choose a
nonzero linear dependence \(\sum_r\alpha_rT_r=0\).
Every nonzero PSD matrix has positive trace, so the coefficients
\(\alpha_r\) have both signs. Set
\begin{equation}
\tau=\min_{\alpha_r>0}\frac{c_r}{\alpha_r},
\qquad c'_r=c_r-\tau\alpha_r.
\label{eq:cg-finite-pair-support-reduction}
\end{equation}
All \(c'_r\geq0\), at least one is zero, and
\(\sum_r c'_rT_r=Q\). Delete the zero coefficients. Repetition
terminates because the number of summands strictly decreases.
Absorb each surviving positive coefficient into its \(A_i\)
or \(W_e\). PSD anchors, strict SPD weights, and unchanged
invertible transports satisfy the same admission conditions.
There is no limiting argument. The zero matrix uses an empty
presentation; when \(N=0\), the bound is zero.

Only finitely many endpoint and anchor patterns have at most
\(D_N\) terms. For each such pattern, use symmetric anchor
variables, symmetric weight variables, and unrestricted
transport variables, and impose
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\sum_iP_i^\top A_iP_i+\sum_e
(P_i-\Theta_eP_j)^\top W_e(P_i-\Theta_eP_j),\\
A_i&=T_iT_i^\top,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(T_iT_i^\top)>0,\\
\det W_e[1:k,1:k]&>0\quad(1\leq k\leq d_e),
\qquad(\det\Theta_e)^2>0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-finite-pair-polynomial-system}
\end{equation}
All factor variables \(T_i\) are existential. These are
polynomial equalities and inequalities; the Gram and
leading-minor arguments above give their exact meanings.
Every solution is a permitted presentation, and the support
reduction puts every finite presentation into one listed
pattern. The finite disjunction is therefore necessary and
sufficient. Quantifier elimination makes its projection onto
the entries of \(Q\) semialgebraic and decides exact algebraic
inputs. \(\square\)

This compression cannot be applied to arbitrary correlated
predicates \(B\). For instance, in one scalar dimension,
generators restricted to the fixed value \(1\) represent \(2\)
with two copies, but cannot meet the bound \(D_N=1\) without
rescaling. Individually marked edges, their inventories, or
their prescribed loop products also cannot be discarded.
Such problems retain their declared finite inventory in
\Cref{thm:cg-semialgebraic-kron-classification}.
If \(Q\succ0\), repacking \(Q\) while carrying the original
\(h,\mathfrak c\), and reference measure unchanged preserves
the complete Gaussian action, its mass, and its normalized
law. Independently normalized local factors introduce
determinant scalars which must be compensated if exact raw
action equality is required.

The positive GL-link class is smaller than arbitrary PSD
two-node forms. For two two-dimensional nodes and
\(u=(1,0)^\top\), consider
\begin{equation}
Q_0=\begin{pmatrix}u\\-u\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}u\\-u\end{pmatrix}^{\top}.
\label{eq:cg-finite-pair-strict-boundary}
\end{equation}
It has rank one and nonzero cross block. Every non-loop term
\eqref{eq:cg-finite-pair-generator} on those nodes has rank two,
because \((I,-\Theta)\) has full row rank and \(W\succ0\).
If PSD matrices sum to \(Q_0\), each vanishes on
\(\ker Q_0\): their nonnegative quadratic forms sum to zero
there. Their ranges are therefore contained in its
one-dimensional range, excluding every non-loop term.
Anchors and loops have zero cross blocks, so they cannot
represent \(Q_0\) either. Conversely the admissible single
links with \(\Theta=I\) and
\(W_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)\),
\(\varepsilon>0\), tend to \(Q_0\). Thus the presentation
class need not be closed, and replacing strict SPD by PSD
would change membership. This singular operator example is
not a normalized full-space Gaussian; the proper example in
\Cref{prop:cg-kron-positive-pair-obstruction} excludes even
the larger PSD two-node class.

\propositionheading{Measurable selection and structural obstructions}{prop:cg-kron-selector-boundary}
For the good locus \(G\) of
\Cref{thm:cg-semialgebraic-kron-classification}, there is a
semialgebraic, hence Borel, selector \(s:G\to\R^q\) with
\(M(x,s(x))\). Neither a continuous selector nor a gauge
equivariant structural selector follows from these hypotheses,
even when every accompanying Gaussian law is proper and
identical. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
First select from a nonempty one-dimensional semialgebraic
fiber \(T\subset\R\). Prefer its nonnegative part when
nonempty; otherwise reflect the negative part and reverse
the selected sign. Select zero if present. The remaining
set is positive and has finite infimum \(a\geq0\). Select
\(a\) if it belongs. If not, the finitely many real roots of
the nonzero defining univariate polynomials partition the line into
intervals of constant polynomial signs. Hence some interval
\((a,b)\) lies in \(T\). Let \(b\) be the supremum of the
right endpoints of such intervals. Select \((a+b)/2\) if
\(b<\infty\), and \(a+1\) if \(b=\infty\). The selected
point lies in \(T\): every point strictly between \(a\) and
that supremum belongs to a defining interval.

Infimum, supremum, interval inclusion, and unboundedness
are expressible by finite first-order formulas in the
fiber parameters. Quantifier elimination therefore makes
this selection rule semialgebraic, including each branch.
For a fiber in \(\R^q\), select from its first-coordinate
projection, restrict to that choice, select from the next
projection, and continue. Each selected prefix has a
nonempty completion, so the resulting tuple is feasible.
Projection and composition are semialgebraic by the same
theorem. Preimages of rational open boxes are semialgebraic
projections, hence Borel; such boxes form a countable base.
The empty locus and zero-dimensional target have their
unique empty or singleton selections.

Continuity fails in an explicit polynomial family. Let the
context be \(z=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\in S^1\), attach its
rotation \(R_\theta\), and require \(B\in\mathrm{SO}(2)\)
with \(B^2=R_\theta\). Each fiber has two elements. Along
\([0,2\pi]\), a continuous selection must be
\(B(\theta)=\sigma R_{\theta/2}\), where
\(\sigma\in\{I,-I\}\) is continuous and therefore constant.
Then \(B(2\pi)=-B(0)\), although the two endpoints are the
same context, a contradiction. All membership conditions
are polynomial in \(z\) and the entries of \(B\), and the
state law may remain \(\mathcal N(0,I_2)\) throughout.

For the distinct symmetry obstruction, let the input
covariance be \(I_2\) and require a factor \(B\) from a
fixed reference space with \(BB^\top=I_2\). The gauge
action is \(B\mapsto gB\) for \(g\in\mathrm{SO}(2)\).
The input is fixed by every such \(g\), so an equivariant
selector would obey \(B=gB\) for every \(g\). Taking
\(g=-I_2\) forces \(B=0\), contradicting the factor
condition. The isotropic Gaussian and its tensor
transformation law remain well-defined. \(\square\)

The selected tuple supplies a measurable presentation of
the exact marginal; it does not promise a smooth frame,
an equivariant section, or compatibility with an
unspecified global composition law. Explicit finite
diagram compatibility is already handled by
\eqref{eq:cg-semialgebraic-diagram}, with its shared variables.

\propositionheading{Obstruction for computably enumerated domains}{prop:cg-kron-enumerator-obstruction}
There is no terminating algorithm which, from arbitrary
code for a total computable sequence of rational parameters,
decides whether every sequence member has a positive
pairwise coarse presentation. This remains true when the
topology and node dimensions are fixed, every fine law is
proper, and the range is promised to be a subset of one
fixed two-point set. The obstruction concerns the input
description, even though every such range is finite and
semialgebraic as a set. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Use the four-node, two-dimensional star of
\Cref{prop:cg-kron-positive-pair-obstruction}. Let
\(x_{\rm bad}\) be its rational edge data and retained
anchors \(\varepsilon I\), with \(\varepsilon=1/20000\).
Its proper marginal has no positive one-node or two-node
presentation on the declared retained nodes. Let
\(x_{\rm good}\) have the same topology and anchors, with
all three star weights equal to \(I\). Both complete
precisions are SPD. In the good case the hidden pivot is
\(3I\), so the retained interaction diagonal blocks are
\(2I/3\) and off-diagonal blocks are \(-I/3\). The
complete retained graph with weights \(I/3\), together
with the original anchors, is an exact positive presentation.

Given a finite program \(e\), define
\begin{equation}
x_n(e)=
\begin{cases}
x_{\rm bad},&e\text{ halts within }n\text{ steps},\\
x_{\rm good},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cg-kron-enumerator-reduction}
\end{equation}
Computing a term requires only \(n\) simulation steps,
so this is always a total computable rational sequence.
Its range is contained in
\(\{x_{\rm good},x_{\rm bad}\}\), and all its members
admit positive pairwise coarse presentations if and
only if \(e\) never halts. An algorithm deciding the
claimed property from the enumerator code would decide
halting by reversing its answer.

For completeness, suppose \(H(p,q)\) decided whether
program \(p\) halts on input \(q\). Construct \(D(q)\)
to loop when \(H(q,q)\) predicts halting and to halt
otherwise. Applying \(D\) to its own code contradicts
either prediction. A procedure for empty-input programs
would supply \(H\) by hardcoding \(q\) into a wrapper.
Thus the procedure implied by
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-enumerator-reduction} cannot exist.
\(\square\)

There is no conflict with the finite decision theorem:
an explicit complete semialgebraic formula for the range
is different input from code that enumerates it.
The proposition does not forbid a theorem about a
particular infinite family, a nonalgorithmic
classification, or exact full-Gaussian marginalization.

\subsection{Finite Gaussian quotients with variable support}
\label{sec:cg-variable-support-quotients}

The full-SPD assumption in
\Cref{thm:cg-semialgebraic-kron-classification} can be replaced
by an explicit quotient reference, giving a different finite
classification that includes variable ranks. Fix \(V=\R^n\),
a symmetric PSD quadratic form \(Q\), an SPD reference metric
\(H\), a covector \(h\in\operatorname{ran}Q\), an affine origin
\(a\in V\), and a real scalar \(\mathfrak c\). Represent
\(V/\ker Q\) by
\begin{equation}
S=(\ker Q)^{\perp_H},\qquad
\Phi(a+s)=\mathfrak c+\frac12s^\top Qs-h^\top s,\quad s\in S.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-reference-action}
\end{equation}
Use the volume induced by \(H\) on \(S\), translated to
\(a+S\); in dimension zero it is unit mass on the point.
Because \(Q\) is symmetric, \(h\in\operatorname{ran}Q\) is
equivalent to annihilation of \(\ker Q\). Thus both the
quadratic and linear energies descend to the quotient.
The metric, affine embedding, and reference volume are
introduced data. They specify an actual law on a
representative slice, not a normalization of singular
ambient Lebesgue density. Adding a law on the discarded
null coordinates would define a different completion.
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Polynomial quotient covariance}{prop:cg-quotient-covariance-polynomial}
For every such \(Q,H\), there is exactly one symmetric \(C\)
satisfying
\begin{equation}
QCQ=Q,\qquad CQC=C,\qquad HCQ=QCH.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph}
\end{equation}
It is PSD, its range is \(S\), and \(P=CQ\) is the
\(H\)-orthogonal projection onto \(S\) with kernel
\(\ker Q\). In particular these polynomial equations
specify the covariance at every rank.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The SPD square root of \(H\) exists by orthogonal
diagonalization. Put
\begin{equation}
M=H^{-1/2}QH^{-1/2},\qquad
Z=H^{1/2}CH^{1/2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-whitened-graph}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph} becomes
\(MZM=M\), \(ZMZ=Z\), and \(ZM=MZ\), with \(Z\) symmetric.
Decompose into the positive eigenspace of \(M\) and its
kernel. Commutation makes the off-diagonal blocks between
these spaces vanish. On the positive block \(MZM=M\)
gives \(Z=M^{-1}\); on the null block \(ZMZ=Z\) gives
\(Z=0\). This proves uniqueness and
\begin{equation}
C=H^{-1/2}M^+H^{-1/2},
\label{eq:cg-quotient-metric-inverse}
\end{equation}
where \(M^+\) inverts the positive eigenvalues and vanishes
on the kernel. It also proves positivity. In the whitened
coordinates \(ZM\) is the Euclidean orthogonal projection
onto \(\operatorname{ran}M\). Since
\(\ker M=H^{1/2}\ker Q\), undoing the isometry
\(s\mapsto H^{1/2}s\) gives the asserted projection and
range. Equivalently \(P^2=P\), \(\ker P=\ker Q\), and
\(CQC=C\) makes \(\operatorname{ran}P=\operatorname{ran}C\).
\(\square\)

The covariance graph uses only polynomial equations in
\(Q,H,C\). PSD and SPD admissions have the finite
encodings already proved, and \(h\in\operatorname{ran}Q\)
is the existential equation \(Qw=h\).
The square root and spectral inverse in the proof
establish the equations' semantics; neither is an
operation required in the polynomial input.

\theoremheading{Normalized quotient Gaussian law}{thm:cg-quotient-gaussian-law}
Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}Q\) and let
\(\det_+(M)\) be the product of the positive eigenvalues
of \(M\), with empty product one. Relative to the
declared volume on \(a+S\), the action
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-reference-action} has positive
finite mass and defines the Gaussian probability
measure with ambient mean \(\mu\), covariance \(C\),
and characteristic function
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_Q&=e^{-\mathfrak c}(2\pi)^{r/2}
       \det_+(M)^{-1/2}
       \exp\left(\frac12h^\top Ch\right),\\
\mu&=a+Ch,\\
\widehat{\mathbb P}_Q(t)
 &=\exp\left(i t^\top\mu-\frac12t^\top Ct\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-quotient-gaussian-law}
\end{equation}
For \(Q=0\), these formulas give \(h=C=0\),
\(Z_Q=e^{-\mathfrak c}\), and the point mass at \(a\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
In the coordinates \(y=H^{1/2}s\), the slice becomes
\(\operatorname{ran}M\) and its \(H\)-volume becomes
Euclidean volume. The restriction of \(M\) there is
positive definite. The source \(H^{-1/2}h\) belongs to
that range because it annihilates \(\ker M\).
Diagonalizing the positive restriction and completing
its \(r\) scalar squares gives the first line of
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-gaussian-law}. Transforming the
mean and covariance back gives \(Ch\) and \(C\);
translation adds \(a\). The same one-dimensional
Gaussian integrations give the displayed characteristic
function. In dimension zero the integral is over one
point. All cases have \(0<Z_Q<\infty\).
\(\square\)

\paragraph{Passive coordinates and the reference metric.}
Under an invertible change \(x'=Gx\), transform all
declared tensors:
\begin{equation}
Q'=G^{-\top}QG^{-1},\quad
H'=G^{-\top}HG^{-1},\quad
h'=G^{-\top}h,\quad a'=Ga.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-gauge-data}
\end{equation}
Substitution into
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph} and uniqueness
give \(C'=GCG^\top\). Hence \(\mu'=G\mu\) and
\(\mathbb P' =G_\#\mathbb P\). The map is an isometry
between the declared representative metrics, so it
pushes their volume measures and leaves \(Z_Q\) and
\(\mathfrak c\) unchanged. A convention fixing coordinate
Lebesgue measures instead must include the relevant
support Jacobian.

The metric condition is material. Take
\(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), \(H=I_2\), and
\(G=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\).
The covariance pushed from the original slice is
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), while
\begin{equation}
Q'=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(Q')^+_{\rm Euclidean}
=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-reference-counterexample}
\end{equation}
Resetting the transformed metric to \(I_2\) has changed
the slice and its law. This does not contradict covariance
when \(H\) transforms as in
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-gauge-data}.

\paragraph{Ambient affine maps and intrinsic quotient maps.}
Every declared affine map \(x\mapsto Lx+b\), including
rectangular or rank-deficient \(L\), has exact pushforward
moments
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm push}=L(a+Ch)+b,\qquad
C_{\rm push}=LCL^\top.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-affine-push}
\end{equation}
This follows by substituting \(L^\top t\) in the
characteristic function and multiplying by
\(e^{it^\top b}\). Two Gaussian measures on the same
finite ambient space, singular or nonsingular, agree
if and only if their means and covariances agree.
Necessity follows from their finite first and second
moments; sufficiency follows from the same orthogonal
Gaussian construction with the given PSD covariance,
including its zero directions.

A target tuple
\((Q_{\rm c},H_{\rm c},h_{\rm c},a_{\rm c})\) has its
own unique covariance \(C_{\rm c}\) from
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph}. It represents
this ambient affine push exactly when
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm c}=LCL^\top,\qquad
a_{\rm c}+C_{\rm c}h_{\rm c}=L(a+Ch)+b.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-ambient-law-equality}
\end{equation}
These are equalities of probability measures, including
their supports, rather than equalities of densities
against different support volumes.

An intrinsic map \([x]\mapsto[Lx]\) between the energy
quotients has the additional necessary and sufficient
condition \(L\ker Q\subseteq\ker Q_{\rm c}\).
Because \(I-CQ\) projects onto \(\ker Q\), this is
exactly
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm c}L(I-CQ)=0.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-map-descent}
\end{equation}
Necessity follows by replacing \(x\) by \(x+k\) with
\(k\in\ker Q\); the same calculation proves
sufficiency. Let \(P_{\rm c}=C_{\rm c}Q_{\rm c}\).
With a declared affine quotient offset \(b_0\) relative
to the origins \(a,a_{\rm c}\), the actual target
representative map is
\[
a+s\longmapsto
a_{\rm c}+P_{\rm c}(Ls+b_0).
\]
Its exact law conditions replace
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-ambient-law-equality} by
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm c}=P_{\rm c}LCL^\top P_{\rm c}^\top,\qquad
C_{\rm c}h_{\rm c}=P_{\rm c}(LCh+b_0).
\label{eq:cg-quotient-intrinsic-law-equality}
\end{equation}
They follow from the affine push calculation applied
to the displayed representative map. Together with
descent, they encode an intrinsic quotient push.
Choosing a map on one slice alone would not prove
descent. A target metric or projection that varies
with the model parameter does not by itself define
one parameter-independent statistical channel, so
Fisher contraction for a family requires that
additional admission separately.

\paragraph{A descending unanchored chain.}
Take three scalar nodes with two unit edges, no anchors,
zero source, and \(H=I_3\). The fine precision and its
quotient covariance are
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm f}=\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_{\rm f}=\frac19\begin{pmatrix}
5&-1&-4\\-1&2&-1\\-4&-1&5
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-chain-data}
\end{equation}
Multiplication gives
\(Q_{\rm f}C_{\rm f}=I_3-\boldsymbol1\boldsymbol1^\top/3\),
which verifies the covariance equations.
Let \(L\) select the first and third coordinates.
At the two coarse nodes use \(H_{\rm c}=I_2\) and
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm c}=C_{\rm c}=P_{\rm c}
=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
LC_{\rm f}L^\top
=\frac19\begin{pmatrix}5&-4\\-4&5\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-chain-push}
\end{equation}
The fine common-translation kernel maps into the
coarse common-translation kernel, establishing descent.
Direct multiplication gives
\(P_{\rm c}LC_{\rm f}L^\top P_{\rm c}^\top=C_{\rm c}\).
Thus the target positive edge of weight \(1/2\) is an
exact quotient push with zero sources and offsets.
Equivalently, the fine residuals \(x_1-x_2\) and
\(x_2-x_3\) are independent standard normals on
normalization, and their sum has variance two.
Their fixed coordinate Jacobian cancels from that
normalized law. The unprojected endpoint covariance
in \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-chain-push} differs from
\(C_{\rm c}\); omitting \(P_{\rm c}\) would confuse
the ambient endpoint law with the quotient law.

\theoremheading{Finite variable-support Gaussian classification}{thm:cg-variable-support-classification}
Fix finite ambient dimensions, finitely many declared
affine or intrinsic quotient maps, and finite
semialgebraic input and target predicates over effectively

Ranks and supports may vary with the parameters.
Then an algorithm constructs the exact good and

and decides universal closure, separate nonemptiness,

polynomial structural compatibilities.
For ambient affine maps it uses
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-ambient-law-equality}; for intrinsic
quotient maps it uses both
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-map-descent} and
\eqref{eq:cg-quotient-intrinsic-law-equality}.
The asserted equivalence is equality of the normalized
measures on their declared representative spaces.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Introduce a symmetric covariance variable at each object and
impose \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph}, together
with \(Qw=h\) and the PSD and SPD admission formulas.
Uniqueness in
\Cref{prop:cg-quotient-covariance-polynomial} ensures

The selected map equations are then finite polynomial

are necessary and sufficient for the specified
normalized pushforward, with descent included
precisely when an intrinsic map is requested.

Additional support restrictions can be encoded in

\((r+1)\)-minors vanish and the sum of squares of all
\(r\)-minors is positive; the rank-zero condition is
that all entries vanish. The finitely many possible

For declared column matrices \(U,V\),
\(\operatorname{ran}U\subseteq\operatorname{ran}V\)
is the existential equation \(U=VB\); equality uses

Write \(\mathcal A(x)\) for the admitted fine data

G_{\rm quot}(x)&\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal A(x)\wedge\exists y\bigl[\mathcal M(x,y)\bigr],\\
F_{\rm quot}(x)&=\mathcal A(x)\wedge\neg G_{\rm quot}(x),

\label{eq:cg-variable-support-good-locus}

and decides both

For a finite diagram, use one common target tuple

\(\square\)

A preferred positive pair factorization is an extra

\paragraph{Complete raw finite measures.}
The normalized classification retains an exact

\mathcal A_i^{\rm mass}
&=\frac{r_i}{2}\log(2\pi)

When the target scalar is free, this choice gives

\propositionheading{Rank change obstructs statistical regularity}{prop:cg-rank-change-statistical-obstruction}
Finite semialgebraic feasibility across ranks does
not imply total-variation continuity, differentiability
in quadratic mean, or a smooth positive Fisher
geometry. These failures occur even for a smooth
PSD coefficient path whose quotient law is normalized
at every parameter. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Take
\begin{equation}
Q(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t^2),\qquad
H=I_2,\qquad h=a=0.
\label{eq:cg-rank-change-example}
\end{equation}
For \(t\ne0\), the covariance is
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,t^{-2})\) and the support
is \(\R^2\). At zero, the covariance is
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\) and the support is the
first coordinate axis. That axis has probability
zero for every \(t\ne0\) and one at zero.
In the convention
\(\|\mathbb P-\mathbb Q\|_{\rm TV}
=\sup_B|\mathbb P(B)-\mathbb Q(B)|\),
the distance is therefore exactly one.
For every finite \(R\),
\begin{equation}
\mathbb P_t(|X_2|\leq R)
=\mathbb P(|Z|\leq R|t|)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1).
\label{eq:cg-rank-change-escape}
\end{equation}
There is no weak probability limit as \(t\to0\).
For example, the bounded continuous positive
function \(e^{-X_2^2}\) has expectation
\(|t|/\sqrt{t^2+2}\to0\), whereas its integral
against any probability measure on \(\R^2\)
is strictly positive. This directly contradicts
the defining bounded-continuous-function test for
a putative weak probability limit.

On \(t>0\), the second-coordinate log density has
derivative \(1/t-tX_2^2\). Its mean is zero and
\(\operatorname{Var}(X_2^2)=2/t^4\), giving Fisher
information \(2/t^2\). Here two-dimensional
Lebesgue measure plus the one-dimensional measure
on the axis is a common dominating measure.
For densities relative to that measure, differentiability
in quadratic mean at zero would require a
square-integrable score \(\ell_0\) with
\[
\left\|\sqrt{p_t}-\sqrt{p_0}
-\frac{t}{2}\ell_0\sqrt{p_0}\right\|_2=o(|t|).
\]
The triangle inequality would give
\(\|\sqrt{p_t}-\sqrt{p_0}\|_2\to0\).
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\((\sqrt{p_t}-\sqrt{p_0})(\sqrt{p_t}+\sqrt{p_0})\)
then gives
\(\|\mathbb P_t-\mathbb P_0\|_{\rm TV}
\leq\|\sqrt{p_t}-\sqrt{p_0}\|_2\to0\),
contradicting the distance-one calculation.
Thus the normalized quotient laws admit no such
DQM continuation across this rank change.
\(\square\)

Even the smooth covariance path
\(C(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t^2)\) has total-variation
distance one from its line-supported law at zero.
Those laws do converge weakly: couple them as
\((Z_1,tZ_2)\) and use dominated convergence for
every bounded continuous function. Their quotient
precisions form the finite semialgebraic graph
specified by \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-covariance-graph}.
Thus weak convergence alone would not supply the
missing regular statistical geometry. The finite
classification concerns exact laws at each
parameter; it does not assert an automatic
smooth passage between support strata.

\paragraph{Remaining classification.}
The finite algebraically encoded full-SPD problem is
decided by \Cref{thm:cg-semialgebraic-kron-classification};
\Cref{thm:cg-variable-support-classification} extends
the decision result to the declared metric quotient
laws with variable ranks and supports. Correlated,
nonconvex, variable-context, and branching nonflat
domains are included when their actual constraints
fit the corresponding finite input contract.
These are algorithmic classifications, not a
closed-form catalog of every such family.
The scalar formulas remain exact analytic outputs;
extra transcendental scalar constraints require
their own reduction or argument. General smooth,
equivariant, or functorial structural selection
does not follow, and the explicit selector and
rank-change counterexamples rule out the stated
universal continuity and equivariance claims.
Arbitrary enumerator input admits no universal
decision procedure by
\Cref{prop:cg-kron-enumerator-obstruction}.
For a family outside these finite encodings or a
specified global regularity target, the remaining
obligation is to supply its domain and compatibility
conditions and establish the requested invariance,
limit, or global construction. Nonalgebraic domains,
changing-dimension limits, alternative null-coordinate
completions, and global constructions outside the
declared hypotheses retain their separate proof
obligations. \status{OPEN}

\section{Nonflat links and effective support}
\label{sec:cg-effective-support}

We now return to hard identification. The exact Gaussian marginal
constructed in \Cref{thm:cg-nonflat-series-kron} does not identify
this hard energy trace with a coordinate pushforward.
\status{DEFINITION}

For a graph edge \(e=(i,j)\), write the twisted energy as
\begin{equation}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j).
\label{eq:cg-twisted-pair}
\end{equation}
An internal edge under hard identification contributes
\begin{equation}
(I-\Theta_e)^\top W_e(I-\Theta_e),
\label{eq:cg-internal-residual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes exactly when
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0.
\label{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Positive-definite \(W_e\) forces \(\Theta_e=I\); a singular weight may hide a
nonidentity twist. \status{ESTABLISHED}

For \(N_e=\ker W_e\) and quotient map \(q_e:\R^K\to\R^K/N_e\),
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_e\Theta_e=q_e.
\label{eq:cg-edge-quotient}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This is edge-local. A single common quotient
\(\R^K/N\) on which every induced weight is positive definite exists exactly
when
\begin{equation}
\ker W_e=N,\qquad \Theta_eN=N
\quad\text{for every relevant edge}.
\label{eq:cg-common-quotient}
\end{equation}
On that quotient ordinary group-valued holonomy is defined. When the kernels
differ, there is no canonical product of the edge-local quotients and no
single ``represented support.'' \status{ESTABLISHED}

A triangle makes the boundary exact:
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{12}&=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),&
\Theta_{12}&=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\\
W_{23}&=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),&
\Theta_{23}&=\operatorname{diag}(2,1),\\
W_{31}&=I,&\Theta_{31}&=I.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every edge satisfies \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}, yet the loop product
is \(2I\), fixing no nonzero vector. The local kernels differ, so no common
effective quotient exists. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Cut links and rectangular sheaf closure}
\label{sec:cg-sheaf-closure}

For positive-definite cut weights between two already trivialized clusters,
define
\[
C=\sum W_e,\qquad B=\sum W_e\Theta_e,\qquad
A=\sum\Theta_e^\top W_e\Theta_e.
\]
One group-valued coarse pair reproduces the cut exactly if and only if all
cut twists coincide. Indeed, with \(\bar\Theta=C^{-1}B\),
\begin{equation}
\Delta=A-B^\top C^{-1}B
=\sum_e(\Theta_e-\bar\Theta)^\top W_e
(\Theta_e-\bar\Theta)\succeq0,
\label{eq:cg-cut-excess}
\end{equation}
and \(\Delta=0\) exactly under common twists. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The weighted mean has the correct gauge law but can be singular; equal weights
with twists \(I_2\) and \(-I_2\) give \(\bar\Theta=0\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The exact closed category is larger than one invertible link.

\theoremheading{Rectangular endpoint-map closure}{thm:cg-rectangular-endpoint-closure}
Choose a minimal factor
\[
W_e=C_e^\top C_e,\qquad C_e:\R^K\to E_e .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)
=\|R_{i,e}z_i-R_{j,e}z_j\|_{E_e}^2,
\qquad
R_{i,e}=C_e,\quad R_{j,e}=C_e\Theta_e.
\label{eq:cg-sheaf-factor}
\end{equation}
For partial injections \(S_{iI}:V_I\to\R^K\), the coarse endpoint maps are
\begin{equation}
R^c_{I,e}=R_{i,e}S_{iI},\qquad
R^c_{J,e}=R_{j,e}S_{jJ}.
\label{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}
\end{equation}
They reproduce \(S^\top\Lambda S\) exactly, parallel cut edges compress
losslessly by the direct sum of their \(E_e\), and nested blocking is
associative. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expanding \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-factor} gives the original pair form.
Precomposition gives \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}; its cross block is
\[
-(R^c_{I,e})^\top R^c_{J,e}
=-S_{iI}^\top W_e\Theta_eS_{jJ},
\]
and the diagonal blocks agree by the same expansion. Vertical stacking in
\(\bigoplus_eE_e\) sums the energies without loss. A second injection \(T\)
gives \((RS)T=R(ST)\), proving repeated closure. \(\square\)

This is the degree-zero cellular-sheaf Laplacian category
\citep{HansenGhrist2019}. Vertex spaces may have unequal dimensions and
automorphism groups \(\GL(V_I)\); edge basis changes act on both endpoint
maps and the edge metric. It is gauge covariant and strictly larger than the
fixed-\(K\) group-link family. Open questions are canonical minimal
compression, characterization of compression to one invertible transporter
and one SPD weight, and compatibility with normalized laws and RG rescaling.
\status{OPEN}

\section{Partial fixed-section aggregation}
\label{sec:cg-partial}

For each undirected internal edge, choose one orientation \(e=(i,j)\), so
\(\Theta_e\) transports from \(j\) to \(i\). Assume
\(\Theta_e\in\GL(K)\), \(\Theta_{\bar e}=\Theta_e^{-1}\), and
\(W_e\succ0\). Positive definiteness is the nondegeneracy assumption used
below. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

The cluster connection Laplacian \(L_I\) is defined by
\begin{equation}
z^\top L_Iz
=\sum_{e=(i,j)\in E_I}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j).
\label{eq:cg-connection-laplacian-energy}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

With semidefinite weights, the exact condition remains the edgewise constraint
in \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}, and the resulting effective-support
problem is not replaced by the following proposition. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Let \((I_\alpha)_\alpha\) be the connected components of the internal graph.
Choose a root \(r_\alpha\) and a spanning tree in each component. For a tree
path from \(r_\alpha\) to \(v\), let \(P_{v\leftarrow r_\alpha}\) be the
ordered product of its represented transports. Let
\(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}\) be the group of represented transport
products around all closed walks based at \(r_\alpha\), and define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})
=\{u\in\R^K:Hu=u\text{ for every }
H\in\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}\}.
\label{eq:cg-holonomy-fixed-space}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Kernel--holonomy fixed-space isomorphism}{prop:cg-kernel-holonomy}
On every connected component \(I_\alpha\), root evaluation is a linear
isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{ev}_{r_\alpha}:\ker L_{I_\alpha}
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}),
\qquad z\longmapsto z_{r_\alpha}.
\label{eq:cg-kernel-holonomy-isomorphism}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\ker L_I\cong
\bigoplus_\alpha\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}),
\qquad
f_I:=\dim\ker L_I
=\sum_\alpha\dim\operatorname{Fix}
(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-rank}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
By \eqref{eq:cg-connection-laplacian-energy} and \(W_e\succ0\), a vector is
in \(\ker L_{I_\alpha}\) exactly when
\(z_i=\Theta_ez_j\) on every edge. Such a parallel section is determined by
its root value, so root evaluation is injective. Transporting its value
around any rooted closed walk returns the same value, placing its image in
\(\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})\).

Conversely, take
\(u\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})\) and define
\(z_v=P_{v\leftarrow r_\alpha}u\) along the spanning tree. Tree-edge
constraints hold by construction. For a non-tree edge \(e=(i,j)\), the tree
path from \(r_\alpha\) to \(j\), followed by \(e\), followed by the reverse
tree path from \(i\) to \(r_\alpha\), has holonomy
\begin{equation}
H_e=P_{r_\alpha\leftarrow i}\Theta_e
P_{j\leftarrow r_\alpha}.
\label{eq:cg-fundamental-cycle-holonomy}
\end{equation}
Since \(H_eu=u\) and
\(P_{r_\alpha\leftarrow i}=P_{i\leftarrow r_\alpha}^{-1}\), multiplication
by \(P_{i\leftarrow r_\alpha}\) gives
\(\Theta_ez_j=z_i\). Thus every edge constraint holds, proving surjectivity.
The full Laplacian is the direct sum over connected components, which yields
\eqref{eq:cg-fixed-rank}. \(\square\)

For a connected cluster, \(f_I=K\) exactly when every represented holonomy
element is the identity. This is the full fixed sector. The case
\(0<f_I<K\) is a partial fixed sector, while \(f_I=0\) leaves no retained
cluster degree. A nonfaithful representation can make represented holonomy
trivial even when principal holonomy is not. \status{ESTABLISHED}

This rank is a state-independent structural admissibility criterion. It assigns no
partition cost, singletons remain admissible, and it does not require the
constituent belief means to coincide. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

Define the abstract fixed fiber
\begin{equation}
\mathsf F_I
=\bigoplus_\alpha\operatorname{Fix}
(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}).
\label{eq:cg-abstract-fixed-fiber}
\end{equation}
After choosing bases, let
\(\iota_I:\mathsf F_I\hookrightarrow(\R^K)^I\) be the full-column matrix
that extends each root value by the spanning-tree construction in the proof.
Its range is independent of those choices and equals \(\ker L_I\). For a
partition \(\mathcal P\), put
\(\iota_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{blkdiag}_{I\in\mathcal P}\iota_I\).
Then
\begin{equation}
L_I\iota_I=0,\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}=\iota_{\mathcal P}^\top\Lambda
\iota_{\mathcal P}\succeq0,\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}\succ0
\Longleftrightarrow
\operatorname{range}(\iota_{\mathcal P})\cap\ker\Lambda=\{0\}.
\label{eq:cg-partial-properness}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Internal edges vanish, while cut edges become the
rectangular endpoint data of \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}. An ordinary
smooth vector bundle over varying parameters additionally requires locally
constant \(f_I\); rank jumps produce strata rather than a smooth bundle.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Nested fixed sections compose exactly}{prop:cg-nested-sections-compose}
Let a larger cluster \(A\) be a union of first-level clusters, and let
\begin{equation}
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}
=\operatorname{blkdiag}_{I\subset A}\iota_I,
\qquad
L_A^{(1)}
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}^\top L_A
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-first-compression}
\end{equation}
Then the fine and first-level kernels obey
\begin{equation}
\ker L_A
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}\ker L_A^{(1)}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-fixed-sections}
\end{equation}
If
\(\iota_A^{(2)}:\mathsf F_A\hookrightarrow
\bigoplus_{I\subset A}\mathsf F_I\) has range \(\ker L_A^{(1)}\), then the
fine-level fixed-section injection is
\begin{equation}
\iota_A^{\rm fine}
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}\iota_A^{(2)}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-injection-composition}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Every zero mode of \(L_A\) has zero energy in each positive-semidefinite
edge summand. Its restriction to every first-level cluster therefore belongs
to \(\ker L_I\), so it lies in
\(\operatorname{range}(\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P})\). Write it as
\(\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}y\). Since \(L_A\succeq0\),
\begin{equation}
y\in\ker L_A^{(1)}
\Longleftrightarrow
y^\top L_A^{(1)}y=0
\Longleftrightarrow
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}y\in\ker L_A.
\label{eq:cg-nested-kernel-equivalence}
\end{equation}
This proves \eqref{eq:cg-nested-fixed-sections}; substituting the range of
\(\iota_A^{(2)}\) gives \eqref{eq:cg-nested-injection-composition}. Matrix
composition is associative, so the same statement iterates through further
levels. \(\square\)

Thus the vertex fixed spaces and sheaf endpoint maps are exactly functorial
under nested partitions. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Probability normalization, rescaling, and minimal group-link compression remain
separate obligations. \status{OPEN}

\subsection{Constant-metric Gaussian relaxation}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}

\propositionheading{Relaxation onto the fixed sector}{prop:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}
Let \(R=R^\top\succ0\) and \(L=L^\top\succeq0\) be constant matrices on
\(\R^d\). If the columns of \(N_0\) form a basis of \(\ker L\), define the
\(R\)-orthogonal projector
\begin{equation}
\Pi_0^R=N_0(N_0^\top RN_0)^{-1}N_0^\top R,
\label{eq:cg-r-projector}
\end{equation}
and set \(\Pi_0^R=0\) when \(\ker L=\{0\}\). The flow
\begin{equation}
R\dot z=-Lz,\qquad z(0)=z_0,
\label{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow}
\end{equation}
converges to \(\Pi_0^Rz_0\). If the positive spectrum is nonempty and
\begin{equation}
\lambda_+
=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(R^{-1/2}LR^{-1/2}),
\label{eq:cg-transverse-rate}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\|z(t)-\Pi_0^Rz_0\|_R
\leq e^{-\lambda_+t}
\|z_0-\Pi_0^Rz_0\|_R.
\label{eq:cg-transverse-decay}
\end{equation}
For \(\mathcal E(z)=\tfrac12z^\top Lz\), the exact dissipation identity is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal E(z(t))
=-\dot z^\top R\dot z
=-z^\top LR^{-1}Lz\leq0.
\label{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Set \(y=R^{1/2}z\) and
\(A=R^{-1/2}LR^{-1/2}\succeq0\). Then
\(\dot y=-Ay\). The spectral theorem gives convergence to the Euclidean
projection of \(y_0\) onto \(\ker A=R^{1/2}\ker L\), with transverse norm
decaying at the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(A\). Transforming back
gives \eqref{eq:cg-r-projector} and \eqref{eq:cg-transverse-decay}. Direct
differentiation gives \eqref{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}. \(\square\)

For a constant passive reframing \(z'=Tz\), \(T\in\GL(d)\), covariance of
the equation requires the congruence laws
\begin{equation}
R'=T^{-\top}RT^{-1},\qquad
L'=T^{-\top}LT^{-1},\qquad
\Pi_0^{R'}=T\Pi_0^RT^{-1}.
\label{eq:cg-flow-congruence}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The choice \(R=I_d\) is therefore equivariant only
under orthogonal reframings. If \(T=T(t)\), the ordinary derivative acquires
the term \(\dot TT^{-1}z'\). Covariance requires a time covariant derivative;
when the original temporal connection is zero, it is
\begin{equation}
D_t'z'=\dot z'-\dot TT^{-1}z'=T\dot z,
\qquad R'D_t'z'=-L'z'.
\label{eq:cg-time-covariant-flow}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The information-geometric identification is exact on the fixed-covariance
Gaussian mean family
\(q_z=\mathcal N(z,R^{-1})\). Its mean Fisher matrix is \(R\), and
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_z\Vert q_w)
=\frac12(z-w)^\top R(z-w).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-kl}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow} is exactly the Fisher natural-gradient
flow of \(\mathcal E\) on that mean family. For a general regular statistical
family, only the local expansion
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_\theta\Vert q_{\theta+d\theta})
=\frac12d\theta^\top F(\theta)d\theta
+o(\|d\theta\|^2),
\label{eq:cg-local-fisher-kl}
\end{equation}
is available under the standard regularity hypotheses
\citep{AmariNagaoka2000}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Freezing
\(F(\theta_0)=R\) yields a local linearization. The development does not
identify a generic, state-dependent Fisher flow with the constant global
projector and rate above. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

The solution of \eqref{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow} is a parameterized
dynamical trajectory.  Away from stationary points, its oriented
unparameterized orbit is a history in the sense of
\Cref{def:hist-oriented-history}, and its Fisher arclength is invariant under
orientation-preserving reparameterization by
\Cref{thm:hist-fisher-clock-invariance}. The exponential rate and the
dissipation per unit \(t\) are properties of this chosen parameterization, not
of the unparameterized history. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Here \(t\) is a declared flow parameter; it is neither a coordinate on the
timeless contextual base, an RG-depth index, nor physical time. No
physical-time identification is claimed. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\subsection{Gaussian marginal-law specialization}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-marginal-law}

Specialize \Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal} after transporting every
constituent marginal to one root. For this specialization, declare the
admissible parent family to be the full nondegenerate Gaussian family.
Trivial represented holonomy then makes the holonomy-fixed admissible
subfamily equal to that declared parent; it does not itself select the parent
family. Write the transported marginals as
\(q_i^{(r)}=\mathcal N(m_i,C_i)\), with \(C_i\succ0\), and choose weights
\(a_i>0\) satisfying \(\sum_i a_i=1\). \status{HYPOTHESIS}

\propositionheading{Full-Gaussian forward-KL barycenter}{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}
The unique minimizer of
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I(m,C)
=\sum_i a_i\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(m_i,C_i)
\Vert\mathcal N(m,C)\bigr),
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective}
\end{equation}
matches the first two moments:
\begin{equation}
\bar m=\sum_i a_im_i,\qquad
\bar C=\sum_i a_i\left[C_i+(m_i-\bar m)(m_i-\bar m)^\top\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}
\end{equation}
Its minimized score is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I^\star
=\frac12\left[
\log\det\bar C-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i
\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The Gaussian KL formula gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
2\mathcal D_I(m,C)
&=\Tr(C^{-1}M(m))-K+\log\det C
-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i,\\
M(m)&=\sum_i a_i\left[C_i+(m_i-m)(m_i-m)^\top\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-expansion}
\end{equation}
Completing the weighted square gives
\(M(m)=\bar C+(m-\bar m)(m-\bar m)^\top\), so \(m=\bar m\) is the unique
minimizer. At that value,
\(\Tr(C^{-1}\bar C)-\log\det(C^{-1}\bar C)\geq K\), with equality exactly
when \(C=\bar C\). Substitution proves
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}. \(\square\)

Under nontrivial represented holonomy, the moment-matched Gaussian need not
belong to the holonomy-fixed parent family. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The
applicable optimization is then the constrained one in
\Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal}. The development does not claim that
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments} removes that constraint.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED}

For an explicit witness, let \(K=2\),
\(\mathcal H=\{I_2,-I_2\}\subset\mathrm{SO}(2)\), and take the one-law
ensemble \(\mathcal N(u,I_2)\) with \(u\neq0\). Its unconstrained
moment-matched Gaussian is itself and is not \(\mathcal H\)-invariant. The
Haar-constrained optimizer below is instead
\(\mathcal N(0,I_2+uu^\top)\), with score
\(\tfrac12\log(1+\lVert u\rVert^2)>0\). Thus the holonomy constraint can be
active even when there is no pairwise disagreement among the transported
constituents.

\corollaryheading{Compact-closure holonomy Gaussian barycenter}{cor:cg-compact-holonomy-barycenter}
Let \(\mathcal H\subset\GL(K)\) be a represented holonomy group whose closure
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\) is compact in \(\GL(K)\), and let
\(\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\) be normalized Haar probability measure on
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\). A nondegenerate Gaussian is
\(\mathcal H\)-invariant exactly when it is
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\)-invariant. Define
\begin{equation}
a=\sum_i a_im_i,\qquad
M_2=\sum_i a_i(C_i+m_im_i^\top),
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-raw-moments}
\end{equation}
and their Haar projections
\begin{equation}
a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}ha
d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h),\qquad
M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}hM_2h^\top
d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h),\qquad
C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}
=M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}
-a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}^\top.
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-moments}
\end{equation}
Then \(C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\succ0\), and
\(\mathcal N(a_{\overline{\mathcal H}},C_{\overline{\mathcal H}})\) is the unique minimizer of
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective} over all
\(\mathcal H\)-invariant nondegenerate Gaussians. Its score retains the
log-determinant form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_{I,\overline{\mathcal H}}^\star
=\frac12\left[
\log\det C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i
\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The stabilizer of a Gaussian under the continuous \(\GL(K)\) pushforward
action is closed. Hence invariance under \(\mathcal H\) is equivalent to
invariance under \(\overline{\mathcal H}\). For every such invariant
candidate \(q\), invariance of KL under a common invertible pushforward gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_i a_i\KL(q_i^{(r)}\Vert q)
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\sum_i a_i
\KL(h_\#q_i^{(r)}\Vert q)d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h).
\label{eq:cg-haar-objective-average}
\end{equation}
The orbit-averaged source ensemble on the right has first moment
\(a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\) and second moment
\(M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}\). Haar invariance makes both moments
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\)-invariant, so its moment-matched Gaussian
is an admissible candidate. Its covariance is positive definite because it
contains the Haar average of the positive-definite matrices \(hC_ih^\top\).
The calculation in
\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter} therefore proves uniqueness.
Every element of the compact matrix group
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\) has \(\lvert\det h\rvert=1\), so
\(\log\det(hC_ih^\top)=\log\det C_i\), which yields
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}. \(\square\)

Compactness cannot be dropped from this averaging formula. If the represented
holonomy contains \(2I_K\), an invariant Gaussian would require
\(m=2m\) and \(C=4C\); hence \(m=0\) and \(C=0\), contradicting
nondegeneracy. The generated expanding holonomy group therefore admits no
invariant SPD Gaussian. \status{ESTABLISHED}

There is a separate exact identity when every transported constituent and
the parent are restricted to one fixed covariance \(C_0\succ0\). Define the
symmetrized convention
\begin{equation}
D_{\rm SKL}(p,q)
:=\frac12\left[\KL(p\Vert q)+\KL(q\Vert p)\right].
\label{eq:cg-symmetric-kl-convention}
\end{equation}
For \(q_i^{(r)}=\mathcal N(m_i,C_0)\) and
\(\bar q_{C_0}=\mathcal N(\bar m,C_0)\), the fixed-covariance barycenter
score satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sum_i a_i\KL(q_i^{(r)}\Vert\bar q_{C_0})
=\sum_{i<j}a_ia_jD_{\rm SKL}(q_i^{(r)},q_j^{(r)})
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}a_ia_jD_{\rm SKL}(q_i^{(r)},q_j^{(r)}).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-pairwise-identity}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This follows from the weighted variance identity in the \(C_0^{-1}\) inner
product. It is not the free-covariance optimization of
\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}, whose covariance includes the
between-mean term in \eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Ordinary pairwise KL between untransported local marginals is not a
cross-fiber quantity. After choosing a path, a second path can change the
transported law by a loop-holonomy action. Stabilization by that action
guarantees path independence; without it the two KL values need not agree.
For example, using the holonomy \(H\) in
\eqref{eq:cg-isotropic-holonomy-counterexample} and
\(q_i=q_j=\mathcal N(e_2,I_3)\), the identity path gives KL zero, whereas a
path differing by \(H\) gives KL equal to \(2\). Moreover, an unpenalized sum
of nonnegative within-cluster pairwise KL values is minimized by the
singleton partition, where it vanishes. It therefore supplies no
nondegenerate partition selector by itself. It also contains only marginal
information and does not test the common recovery-kernel condition for joint
recovery in \Cref{sec:cg-kl-recovery}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Two elementary examples separate the structural and marginal statements.
On a two-vertex tree with identity transport and \(W=I_K\), represented
holonomy is trivial, yet the marginals
\(\mathcal N(-ae_1,I_K)\) and \(\mathcal N(ae_1,I_K)\) have transported
divergence
\begin{equation}
\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(-ae_1,I_K)\Vert
\mathcal N(ae_1,I_K)\bigr)=2a^2,
\label{eq:cg-trivial-holonomy-large-kl}
\end{equation}
which is arbitrarily large. Conversely, take a triangle with \(K=3\), unit
edge weights, identity transports on two edges, and closing transport
\begin{equation}
H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)\in\operatorname{SO}(3).
\label{eq:cg-isotropic-holonomy-counterexample}
\end{equation}
Its represented holonomy is nontrivial and
\(\operatorname{Fix}(H)=\operatorname{span}(e_1)\), so the structural fixed
sector has \(f_I=1<K\). Nevertheless, \(H\) preserves
\(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_3)\); assigning that marginal at every vertex makes
all transported marginal KL values zero. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Transported KL therefore measures marginal occupation of the available law
modes, not the dimension of the state-independent structural fixed section.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Quotient volume for hard membership}
\label{sec:cg-quotient-volume}

Let \(P=\widehat S\in\{0,1\}^{n\times m}\), with nonempty cluster sizes
\(s_1,\ldots,s_m\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(s_i)\),
\(s=(s_i)\), and \(n=\sum_i s_i\). For a positive-semidefinite matrix,
\(\operatorname{pdet}\) denotes the product of its positive eigenvalues.
On the translation quotient, the metric
induced from the fine Euclidean quotient is
\begin{equation}
H=D-\frac{ss^\top}{n},\qquad
\ker H=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1_m),\qquad
\operatorname{pdet}H=\frac{m\prod_i s_i}{n}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-metric}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For a coarse class \([x]\),
\[
\min_a\|Px+a\mathbf1_n\|^2
=x^\top\left(D-\frac{ss^\top}{n}\right)x.
\]
Deleting row and column \(r\), the matrix determinant lemma gives the
principal cofactor \(\prod_i s_i/n\). Since a rank-\((m-1)\) Laplacian with
kernel \(\mathbf1_m\) has pseudodeterminant \(m\) times any principal
cofactor, \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-metric} follows. \(\square\)

For \(K\) channels, standard quotient Lebesgue measure and fine-induced
Hausdorff measure are related by
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm ind}=J_{\mathcal P}\mu_{\rm std},\qquad
J_{\mathcal P}
=\left(\frac{m\prod_i s_i}{n}\right)^{K/2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-jacobian}
\end{equation}
If
\[
\operatorname{range}(S)\cap\ker\Lambda
=\mathbf1_n\otimes\R^K,
\]
the quotient Gaussian is proper and its generalized determinant satisfies
\begin{equation}
\det_{H\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\rm c})
:=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c}}
{(\operatorname{pdet}H)^K}
=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c}}{J_{\mathcal P}^2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-generalized-determinant}
\end{equation}
This is the determinant of the precision relative to the quotient metric
\(H\otimes I_K\). For the common unnormalized factor
\(\exp(-x^\top\Lambda_{\rm c}x/2)\), let \(d_q=(m-1)K\). Its normalizers are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm std}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
(\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c})^{-1/2},\\
Z_{\rm ind}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
\det_{H\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\rm c})^{-1/2}
=J_{\mathcal P}Z_{\rm std}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-quotient-normalizers}
\end{equation}
The factor cancels from the normalized law because the measure changes by
the same amount. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Raw partition functions differ by \(\log J_{\mathcal P}\), so a partition
score needs a fixed volume convention, an exact correction, or a compensating
prior. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Recognition-side costs}
\label{sec:cg-restriction-costs}

Let \(B\in\R^{NK\times mK}\) have orthonormal columns spanning
\(\operatorname{range}S\), let
\(B_\perp\in\R^{NK\times r}\), \(r=NK-mK\), complete it so that
\([B\ B_\perp]\) is orthogonal, and write
\(p=\mathcal N(\mu,\Lambda^{-1})\). These definitions apply throughout this
section.

The admissible mean-only family leaves covariance unrestricted. For
\(q=\mathcal N(\nu,\Sigma)\), \(\nu\in\operatorname{range}S\), its exact
optimized cost is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{\rm tie}
=\inf_{\nu\in\operatorname{range}S,\ \Sigma\succ0}
\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(\nu,\Sigma)\Vert p\bigr)
=\frac12m_\perp^\top
\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}m_\perp,
\qquad
m_\perp=B_\perp^\top\mu.
\label{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Optimizing the Gaussian KL over covariance gives
\(\Lambda^{-1}\), leaving
\(\frac12(\nu-\mu)^\top\Lambda(\nu-\mu)\). Minimizing over
\(\nu=Ba\) gives the \(\Lambda\)-projection of \(\mu\). In the orthogonal
basis \([B\ B_\perp]\), its remaining block is the Schur complement
\[
\Lambda_{22}-\Lambda_{21}\Lambda_{11}^{-1}\Lambda_{12}
=\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost}. \(\square\)

A recognition law supported exactly on \(\operatorname{range}S\) is singular
against \(p\), so its relative entropy to \(p\) is \(+\infty\). The following
declared full-rank regularization makes the divergence and every finite term
explicit. Let \(\nu_\star\) be the mean optimizer above and set
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_\varepsilon
=B(B^\top\Lambda B)^{-1}B^\top
+\varepsilon B_\perp B_\perp^\top,
\qquad
q_\varepsilon=\mathcal N(\nu_\star,\Sigma_\varepsilon),
\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\label{eq:cg-epsilon-regularized-law}
\end{equation}
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G_\varepsilon
:=\KL(q_\varepsilon\Vert p)
={}&\mathcal G_{\rm tie}
+\frac{r}{2}\log\frac1\varepsilon-\frac r2
+\frac12\log\det(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp)\\
&+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\Tr(B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-epsilon-divergence}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
In the orthogonal basis \([B\ B_\perp]\), the covariance in
\eqref{eq:cg-epsilon-regularized-law} has determinant
\(\det(B^\top\Lambda B)^{-1}\varepsilon^r\), while
\[
\Tr(\Lambda\Sigma_\varepsilon)
=mK+\varepsilon\Tr(B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp).
\]
The Gaussian KL formula and the optimized mean term give all terms in
\eqref{eq:cg-epsilon-divergence} after using
\[
\det\Lambda
=\det(B^\top\Lambda B)
\det\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1},
\]
which is the block determinant identity. \(\square\)

Thus \(\mathcal G_\varepsilon\to+\infty\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\): identification is a generative construction, not
a finite recognition restriction.

The marginal precision in \eqref{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost} obeys
\begin{equation}
\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}
\preceq B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp,
\label{eq:cg-loewner}
\end{equation}
with equality exactly when
\(B^\top\Lambda B_\perp=0\). Using the restriction precision instead
therefore overstates the cost except under \(\Lambda\)-orthogonality.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

If only covariance factorization across a partition is imposed, the exact
Gaussian optimum has gap
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{\rm fact}
=\frac12\left[
\sum_I\log\det\Lambda_{II}-\log\det\Lambda
\right].
\label{eq:cg-factorization-gap}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The mean-tie cost is nonnegative and vanishes at the
finest partition; the factorization gap decreases under merging by Fischer's
determinant inequality and vanishes at the coarsest partition. The quotient
volume is convention dependent across partitions. Therefore these terms do
not supply a nondegenerate intrinsic scale without an additional coefficient
or a different nonmonotone functional. Whether such a selector exists remains
open. \status{OPEN}

The corrected cross-inventory boundary is the general theorem of
\Cref{sec:cg-elbo}: a common evidence-preserving Markov pushforward makes
ELBOs comparable and ordered. The hard energy trace analyzed here is not such
a pushforward unless a separate theorem proves it, so its cross-partition
difference can mix evidence and tightness. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{The frame sector}
\label{sec:cg-frame}

If a coarse Gaussian \((U_I,\mu_I,\Sigma_I)\) is sent to constituent \(i\)
through \(U_iU_I^{-1}\), then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N(U_iU_I^{-1}\mu_I,
U_iU_I^{-1}\Sigma_IU_I^{-\top}U_i^\top)
=\mathcal N(U_i\bar m,U_i\bar S U_i^\top),
\label{eq:cg-frame-cancellation}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar m=U_I^{-1}\mu_I\) and
\(\bar S=U_I^{-1}\Sigma_IU_I^{-\top}\).
Thus the coarse frame cancels. With faithful representation the level set is
exactly one gauge orbit; without faithfulness only unidentifiability follows.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

For \(K\geq2\), \(n\geq2\), no map
\(F:\GL^+(K)^n\to\GL^+(K)\) is both left equivariant and permutation
symmetric. Choose an order-\(n\) rotation \(v\in\mathrm{SO}(K)\) and inputs
\((I,v,\ldots,v^{n-1})\). Left multiplication by \(v\) cyclically permutes
the tuple. Symmetry keeps \(F\) fixed, while equivariance sends it to \(vF\);
hence \((I-v)F=0\), making \(F\) singular. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Physicist summary.}
Hard Gaussian aggregation is exact Galerkin restriction: internal springs
disappear and cut springs add. Nonflat or rank-changing hard restrictions
retain their energy in the larger sheaf category of endpoint maps.
With positive-definite internal weights, holonomy determines the parallel
modes available to a structural fixed section: trivial represented
holonomy retains all \(K\) dimensions, while nontrivial holonomy can
retain a smaller sector or none. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Gaussian marginalization is a different operation and keeps the hidden
Gaussian normalization. Its closure has distinct exact boundaries.
Independent coefficient tests at one fixed positive anchor force common
congruence axes. For \(K\geq2\), a defined Schur reduction on a fixed
flat graph closes for every independent SPD weight assignment precisely
when each hidden component has at most two retained terminals, counting
ground. Proper nonflat degree-two hidden paths and cycles with zero
hidden quadratic anchors instead close by composing their marked
transports and covariance contributions, with sources, scalar factors,
and loop holonomies retained. Branching can produce a proper Gaussian
marginal outside every positive two-node representation, so a complete
quadratic action may be necessary.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

When attained, transported KL tests whether marginal laws occupy an
available holonomy-fixed mode; without attainment, a zero infimum proves
only approximability. It is not a structural-holonomy test or a partition
selector, and joint recovery remains separate. Quotient volumes, mean
tying, and determinant gaps are explicit, but these terms alone supply
no nondegenerate intrinsic blocking scale. \status{ESTABLISHED}
