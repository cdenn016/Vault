\chapter[Thermodynamic Fisher Geometry of Retained Ensembles]{Thermodynamic Fisher Geometry\\of Retained Ensembles}
\label{ch:thermodynamic-fisher-draft}

The results in this chapter hold on the stated finite supports, compact
positive source boxes, and fixed blocking words. Strict phases, pressure
ties, and reciprocal-volume experiments retain their separate hypotheses;
none supplies a regular metric across arbitrary ties or at all depths.

The finite geometry label is retained as an observed component of the
statistical experiment. Its score contributes information even when the
conditional branch laws coincide. This chapter fixes the complete finite
experiment and its exact information accounting before considering a
volume limit. The accompanying proof appendix distinguishes actual
finite free laws, exact fixed-word pushes, and marginals of an infinite
law. It then treats strict phases, pressure ties, and their different
parameter scales without identifying these regimes.\thermoestablishedstatus


\section{Normalized finite experiments and retained information}
\label{sec:rg-thermo-finite-experiment}

The finite geometry ensembles of \Cref{def:rg-geometry-ensemble} are the
starting point. This section keeps the geometry label in the sample space,
allows every declared generative factor to vary with a statistical
parameter, and distinguishes the complete observation joint from its
latent marginal and a selected posterior. The structural descriptive data
remain outside the stochastic channel. No volume limit or metric inverse
is part of these finite definitions. \status{DEFINITION}

\definitionheading{Smooth finite branch data}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-branch}
Let $\Theta\subset\R^d$ be open, with $1\leq d<\infty$, and let
$\mathfrak G$ be a finite nonempty geometry catalog. Each label has a
finite nonempty declared carrier $\mathsf X_\gamma$. On a fixed nonempty
subset $\mathsf X_\gamma^+$, let $\nu_{\gamma,\theta}$ be a positive
probability reference and let the physical raw factor be
$r_{\gamma,\theta}=s_{\gamma,\theta}\widehat r_{\gamma,\theta}>0$.
The scalar $s_\gamma$ is part of the physical factor. Retained carrier atoms
outside $\mathsf X_\gamma^+$ have zero reference mass; raw-density
quotients there are undefined and are never evaluated. Define
\begin{equation}
 Z_\gamma=\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_\gamma^+}\nu_\gamma(x)r_\gamma(x),
 \qquad
 p_\gamma(x)=\frac{\nu_\gamma(x)r_\gamma(x)}{Z_\gamma}
 \quad (x\in\mathsf X_\gamma^+),
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-branch}
\end{equation}
and set $p_\gamma(x)=0$ on the remaining declared atoms. All parameter
arguments are suppressed only after their dependence has been declared.
A native free square is the special case of
\Cref{def:rg-pressure-native}: $V=L^2$, all $2V$ signs, exactly
$(L-1)^2$ internal plaquettes, and reference $4^{-V}$ on its complete
carrier. Its source notation is $b_\gamma=\tanh h_\gamma$ and
$t_\gamma=\tanh j_\gamma$. \status{DEFINITION}

\hypothesisheading{Finite differentiable support stratum}
{hyp:rg-thermo-finite-support}
The reference masses, positive raw factors, scalars, positive geometry
weights $a_{\gamma,\theta}$, and likelihood entries on active reference atoms are actual $C^3$
functions on $\Theta$. Every zero reference or likelihood entry is
identically zero throughout this stratum; every other likelihood entry is
strictly positive there. Each likelihood row
$\ell_{\gamma,\theta}(o\mid x)$ sums to one on one fixed finite
observation alphabet $\mathsf O$. These are generative inputs independent
of recognition. The fixed-support restriction excludes a family that
creates or removes an atom at the parameter under consideration. It is
used when differentiating scores and conditional laws; it is not an
assumption of positive Fisher rank. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

\definitionheading{Two normalization modes and their complete joints}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-modes}
Write $A_0=\sum_\delta a_\delta$ and
$Z_{\mathrm{all}}=\sum_\delta a_\delta Z_\delta$. The hierarchical and
global-factor label weights are respectively
\begin{equation}
 W_\gamma^{\mathrm h}=\frac{a_\gamma}{A_0},
 \qquad
 W_\gamma^{\mathrm f}=\frac{a_\gamma Z_\gamma}{Z_{\mathrm{all}}}.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-mode-weights}
\end{equation}
A separately prescribed positive normalized hierarchy $w_\gamma$ instead
uses $W_\gamma^{\mathrm h}=w_\gamma$ directly. For either chosen mode,
the latent and observation laws are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P^{\mathrm{lat}}(\gamma,x)=W_\gamma p_\gamma(x),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(\gamma,x,o)=W_\gamma p_\gamma(x)
                           \ell_\gamma(o\mid x).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-full-joint}
\end{equation}
The displayed joint product is evaluated only on active reference atoms;
on all remaining declared atoms its mass is defined to be zero without
evaluating a raw factor or likelihood. The same convention applies to
selected posterior masses below.
The sample space is the disjoint union of the labeled branch carriers;
identical branch distributions do not erase their labels. These formulas
use the two modes of
\cref{eq:rg-geometry-hierarchy,eq:rg-geometry-factor}, with an explicit
physical scalar and structural-zero convention. \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Normalization and physical scalar dependence}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-normalization}
Each law in \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-full-joint} is normalized.
Multiplying $r_\gamma$ by a positive branch scalar $d_\gamma$ leaves
$p_\gamma$ unchanged. It leaves the hierarchy unchanged and changes the
global-factor label masses by the corresponding relative factors
$d_\gamma$, unless the geometry weights are compensated. One common
scalar cancels from both modes.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Every $Z_\gamma$ is finite and positive because its reference is a
probability law and its factor is positive on a finite nonempty support.
Summing the joint over $o$, then $x$, then $\gamma$ gives one. Under the
rescaling, $Z_\gamma$ becomes $d_\gamma Z_\gamma$, so the same factor
cancels between the numerator and denominator of $p_\gamma$. Substitution
in \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-mode-weights} gives the assertions about
labels. Replacing $a_\gamma$ by $a_\gamma/d_\gamma$ compensates the
global-factor change. Thus omitting $s_\gamma$ from a physical partition
is not justified by its cancellation within a conditional branch.
$\square$

\definitionheading{Effective label log masses and complete scores}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-score}
For the chosen latent law use an effective log-mass representative
$\lambda_\gamma$ with
\begin{equation}
 W_\gamma=\frac{e^{\lambda_\gamma}}
                 {\sum_\delta e^{\lambda_\delta}},
 \qquad
 \lambda_\gamma^{\mathrm f}=\log a_\gamma+\log Z_\gamma,
 \qquad
 \lambda_\gamma^{\mathrm h}=\log a_\gamma.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-log-masses}
\end{equation}
For a prescribed hierarchy use $\lambda_\gamma=\log w_\gamma$.
On positive atoms set $\xi_\gamma=\nabla\lambda_\gamma$,
$\overline\xi=\sum_\gamma W_\gamma\xi_\gamma$, and
$T_\gamma(x)=\nabla\log p_\gamma(x)$. The Fisher tensor of a finite
fixed-support law $P_\theta$ is
\begin{equation}
 I(P_\theta)=\sum_{z:P_\theta(z)>0}P_\theta(z)
       S_\theta(z)S_\theta(z)^\top,
 \qquad S_\theta(z)=\nabla_\theta\log P_\theta(z).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-fisher-definition}
\end{equation}
A structural zero has no score and contributes no summand. The tensor is
positive semidefinite. A Riemannian metric additionally requires positive
definiteness. \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Complete labeled Fisher decomposition}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-labeled-fisher}
The latent score and Fisher tensor satisfy
\begin{align}
 S(\gamma,x)
 &=\xi_\gamma-\overline\xi+T_\gamma(x),
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-complete-score}\\
 I(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{lat}})
 &=\operatorname{Cov}_W(\xi_\gamma)
     +\sum_\gamma W_\gamma I(p_\gamma).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-labeled-fisher}
\end{align}
For every ordinary multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\leq2$, the
within-branch contribution differentiates as
\begin{equation}
 \partial^\alpha\left(\sum_\gamma W_\gamma I(p_\gamma)\right)
 =\sum_\gamma\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
 {\alpha\choose\beta}
 (\partial^\beta W_\gamma)
 (\partial^{\alpha-\beta}I(p_\gamma)).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-weighted-fisher-derivatives}
\end{equation}
A common parameter-dependent additive scalar in every
$\lambda_\gamma$ leaves these identities and their derivatives unchanged.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Differentiating the finite softmax gives
$\nabla\log W_\gamma=\xi_\gamma-\overline\xi$.
Differentiating $\sum_xp_\gamma(x)=1$ on its fixed support gives
$\sum_xp_\gamma(x)T_\gamma(x)=0$. Expanding the outer product of
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-complete-score} and summing within each branch
therefore removes both cross terms and yields
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-labeled-fisher}. The conditional score includes
all varying factors:
\begin{equation}
 T_\gamma(x)=\nabla\log\nu_\gamma(x)
 +\nabla\log s_\gamma+\nabla\log\widehat r_\gamma(x)
 -\nabla\log Z_\gamma.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-branch-score}
\end{equation}
Every positive denominator is locally bounded away from zero because the
support is finite. The $C^3$ mass family consequently has a $C^2$ Fisher
tensor, and the ordinary product rule gives
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-weighted-fisher-derivatives}.
Keeping only $W_\gamma$ times a derivative of $I(p_\gamma)$ would omit
terms. A common shift cancels from the normalized weights and adds the
same vector to each $\xi_\gamma$, leaving its covariance unchanged.
These are identities of parameter-dependent functions, so the same
cancellation holds after differentiation. Finally,
$v^\top I(P)v=\sum_zP(z)(v^\top S(z))^2\geq0$ proves semidefiniteness
without an inverse or rank assumption. $\square$

\definitionheading{Selected finite posterior}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-posterior}
For a fixed observation $o$, define
\begin{equation}
 Z_\gamma^o=\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_\gamma^+}
              \nu_\gamma(x)r_\gamma(x)\ell_\gamma(o\mid x),
 \qquad e_\gamma(o)=\frac{Z_\gamma^o}{Z_\gamma},
 \qquad E_o=\sum_\gamma W_\gamma e_\gamma(o).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-evidence}
\end{equation}
On a region where $E_o>0$, the posterior is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\Pi_o(\gamma,x)
 =\frac{W_\gamma p_\gamma(x)\ell_\gamma(o\mid x)}{E_o},
 \qquad W_\gamma^o=\frac{W_\gamma e_\gamma(o)}{E_o}.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior}
\end{equation}
When $Z_\gamma^o>0$, its conditional branch is
$p_\gamma^o(x)=\nu_\gamma(x)r_\gamma(x)\ell_\gamma(o\mid x)/Z_\gamma^o$.
When $Z_\gamma^o=0$, that label remains on the declared carrier with zero
posterior mass, but $p_\gamma^o$ and its conditional Fisher tensor are
undefined. Zero total evidence leaves the entire selected posterior
undefined. \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Posterior normalization and complete generative score}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-score}
On occupied posterior labels, the unnormalized positive label masses are
\begin{equation}
 e^{\lambda_\gamma^{o,\mathrm f}}=a_\gamma Z_\gamma^o,
 \qquad
 e^{\lambda_\gamma^{o,\mathrm h}}
       =a_\gamma\frac{Z_\gamma^o}{Z_\gamma}.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-labels}
\end{equation}
For a prescribed hierarchy replace $a_\gamma$ in the second expression
by $w_\gamma$. On the positive posterior support the complete score is
\begin{equation}
 S^o(\gamma,x)=S^{\mathrm{lat}}(\gamma,x)
       +\nabla\log\ell_\gamma(o\mid x)-\nabla\log E_o.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-score}
\end{equation}
Its labeled decomposition is
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-labeled-fisher}, applied to the occupied
posterior labels and their conditional posterior laws. An empty label
contributes zero weighted information and no assigned conditional score.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Summing \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior} over all labeled atoms gives
one. Substitution of the two prior weights gives
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-labels}; the common prior normalizer
cancels, but the hierarchical branch divisor $Z_\gamma$ remains.
Taking the logarithm of the positive posterior mass and differentiating
gives \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-score}. Within an occupied branch,
\begin{equation}
 \nabla\log p_\gamma^o(x)
 =\nabla\log\nu_\gamma(x)+\nabla\log r_\gamma(x)
   +\nabla\log\ell_\gamma(o\mid x)-\nabla\log Z_\gamma^o.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-conditional-posterior-score}
\end{equation}
The fixed zero pattern of the likelihood makes each unoccupied posterior
fiber identically zero on the stratum. Thus its mass derivatives vanish;
there is no division by zero. On occupied fibers the normalization proof
of \Cref{prop:rg-thermo-finite-labeled-fisher} applies verbatim to the
actual conditional posterior, including the varying likelihood and
evidence terms. $\square$

For the native all-positive edge sensor of
\Cref{prop:rg-pressure-sensor}, write
$A_\gamma^{\mathrm{sens}}=(1+b_\gamma\eta_\gamma)/2$ and
$b'_\gamma=(b_\gamma+\eta_\gamma)/(1+b_\gamma\eta_\gamma)$.
The finite identity and its branch normalizers are
\begin{equation}
 (1+bz)\frac{1+\eta z}{2}
 =A^{\mathrm{sens}}(1+b'z),
 \qquad
 Z_{\gamma,L}^o=(A_\gamma^{\mathrm{sens}})^{2V}
                          Z_L(b'_\gamma,t_\gamma).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-sensor-shift}
\end{equation}
Here the scalar-free native raw factor is used; any additional physical
scalar multiplies both its original and observed partitions. Substituting
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-sensor-shift} into
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-posterior-labels} retains
$Z_L(b_\gamma,t_\gamma)$ in the hierarchical denominator.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Expand the first product using $z^2=1$, then multiply over the actual
$2V$ free edge signs. Plaquette factors and their finite boundary inventory
are unchanged, giving the second identity after summing against normalized
counting. For $|b|<1$ and $|\eta|<1$, the scalar is positive and
$1-(b')^2=(1-b^2)(1-\eta^2)/(1+b\eta)^2>0$. Parameter-dependent sensor
strengths remain inside these expressions when derivatives are taken.
This finite calculation neither invokes an infinite all-positive event
nor supplies a thermodynamic derivative theorem for an arbitrary positive
likelihood. $\square$

\definitionheading{Fixed stochastic channel retaining geometry}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-channel}
For each label choose a finite nonempty target carrier
$\mathsf Y_\gamma$ and a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix
$C_\gamma(x,y)$, independent of $\theta$ and recognition. Its lifted
channel is zero between different geometry labels. In the right-acting
convention of \Cref{def:cg-coarse-channel}, a labeled law $P$ pushes to
\begin{equation}
 P^C(\gamma,y)=\sum_xP(\gamma,x)C_\gamma(x,y).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-channel}
\end{equation}
The chosen source law can be the latent prior or a positive selected
posterior. For the complete random observation joint, retain $o$ as well
and apply this channel only to $x$. Conditioning in the following score
formula then uses $(\gamma,y,o)$. \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Stochastic score projection and retained-label loss}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-fisher-loss}
For an admitted source law $P$, its coarse score on positive targets and
its Fisher loss satisfy
\begin{align}
 S^C(\gamma,y)
 &=\E_{P C}[S(\gamma,X)\mid\gamma,y],
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-score-projection}\\
 I(P)-I(P^C)
 &=\E_{P C}\bigl[(S-S^C)(S-S^C)^\top\bigr]\succeq0.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-fisher-loss}
\end{align}
Every geometry marginal, including its parameter derivatives, is retained.
If $P(\gamma,x)=W_\gamma p_\gamma(x)$ on its occupied fibers, then
\begin{equation}
 I(P)-I(P^C)=\sum_{\gamma:W_\gamma>0}W_\gamma
                  \bigl[I(p_\gamma)-I(p_\gamma C_\gamma)\bigr].
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-within-loss}
\end{equation}
These are finite specializations of
\Cref{thm:cg-fisher-contraction,prop:rg-geometry-score}.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
For every positive target, differentiate
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-channel}. The channel has no parameter
derivative, so division by $P^C(\gamma,y)$ gives the conditional
expectation in \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-score-projection}.
Under the augmented law $P(\gamma,x)C_\gamma(x,y)$, write
$S=S^C+(S-S^C)$. The residual has zero conditional expectation given the
target, so both cross terms in its second moment vanish. This proves
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-fisher-loss}. A directional loss is zero exactly
when the corresponding fine score equals its target conditional
expectation almost surely.

Summing each channel row gives
$\sum_yP^C(\gamma,y)=\sum_xP(\gamma,x)=W_\gamma$ as a function of
$\theta$. Hence every available derivative of the label marginal is
unchanged. Its score is already measurable from the retained label and
cancels from $S-S^C$; equivalently subtract the two complete labeled Fisher
decompositions to obtain \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-within-loss}.
If a target has zero mass, nonnegativity forces every incidence into it
from a positive source atom to vanish. It is consequently a structural
zero, and no reverse probability or score is assigned to it. A channel
varying with $\theta$ would contribute a channel derivative and is outside
this proof. Positive-semidefinite loss at order zero does not impose a
sign on its parameter derivatives. $\square$

\propositionheading{Transport of the complete observation experiment}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-observation-push}
The complete push preserves $E_o$ and obeys
$\boldsymbol\Pi_o^C=\boldsymbol\Pi_o C$. It uses the actual induced
likelihood of \Cref{thm:rg-geometry-transport}, not an independently
chosen likelihood on coarse states. Within a branch, on a reachable
reference target, define the following quantities. Sums containing a raw
factor or likelihood run over $\mathsf X_\gamma^+$; the branch label
is suppressed within these formulas:
\begin{align}
 \nu^C(y)&=\sum_x\nu(x)C(x,y),
 &r^C(y)&=\frac{\sum_x\nu(x)r(x)C(x,y)}{\nu^C(y)},
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-raw-push}\\
 B_p(x\mid y)&=\frac{p(x)C(x,y)}{p^C(y)},
 &\ell^C(o\mid y)&=\sum_xB_p(x\mid y)\ell(o\mid x).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-induced-likelihood}
\end{align}
The physical partition stays $Z$. For $r=s\widehat r$, let
$\widehat r^C=(\sum_x\nu(x)\widehat r(x)C(x,\cdot))/\nu^C$
on reachable targets. At a reachable anchor $y_*$ set
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_C=\widehat r^C(y_*),\qquad
 \widehat r_{\mathrm{new}}=\widehat r^C/\zeta_C,\qquad
 s_{\mathrm{new}}=s\zeta_C.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-anchor}
\end{equation}
The physical product is unchanged, as in \Cref{prop:rg-geometry-scalar}.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
The reference sums to one, and positivity of the physical raw factor
makes its reachable support the same as the prior's. Multiplying the
first line by $\nu^C$ and summing gives $\sum_y\nu^C(y)r^C(y)=Z$.
The prior reverse row $B_p$ sums to one, so its averaged likelihood is
normalized. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 p^C(y)\ell^C(o\mid y)
       =\sum_xp(x)\ell(o\mid x)C(x,y).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-joint-push}
\end{equation}
Multiply by the retained $W_\gamma$, sum over targets and labels, and
use row normalization to obtain the same evidence $E_o$. Division by
$E_o>0$ proves posterior commutation. This is an identity of $C^3$
functions, so its existing derivatives agree as well. The reference
reverse row proportional to $\nu(x)C(x,y)$ is generally different from
$B_p$ and cannot replace it in the likelihood. An anchor division changes
the representative only when the compensating scalar is retained; their
product recovers the original physical push. On a reference-null target
both raw quotient and reverse row remain undefined. Such a target
contributes zero to every pushed law, even if a subsequent channel has a
nonzero row there. Finite-sum associativity proves composition along any
fixed finite word. $\square$

\definitionheading{Exact selected-observation free energy}
{def:rg-thermo-finite-vfe}
At a fixed parameter and $E_o>0$, let
$\mathbb Q(\gamma,x)=q_\gamma Q_\gamma(x)$ be a recognition law on the
complete labeled carrier. The conditional $Q_\gamma$ is defined only
when $q_\gamma>0$. With $0\log0=0$ and a positive recognition mass over
zero selected joint mass assigned infinite cost, define
\begin{equation}
 \Fenergy_o(\mathbb Q)
 =\sum_{\gamma,x:\mathbb Q(\gamma,x)>0}
 \mathbb Q(\gamma,x)
 \log\frac{\mathbb Q(\gamma,x)}{\mathbb P(\gamma,x,o)}.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-definition}
\end{equation}
The exact ELBO is $-\Fenergy_o$. This is the same finite selected-joint
objective as \cref{eq:rg-geometry-vfe-h,eq:rg-geometry-vfe-f}, before any
restriction of the recognition family. \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Full recognition entropy and the posterior form}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-expansion}
On the finite dominated support $\mathbb Q\ll\boldsymbol\Pi_o$,
\begin{align}
 \Fenergy_o(\mathbb Q)
 &=\KL(q\Vert W)+\sum_{\gamma:q_\gamma>0}q_\gamma
       \KL(Q_\gamma\Vert p_\gamma)
   -\sum_{\gamma:q_\gamma>0}q_\gamma
       \E_{Q_\gamma}\log\ell_\gamma(o\mid X)
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-expansion}\\
 &=-\log E_o+\KL(\mathbb Q\Vert\boldsymbol\Pi_o).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-posterior}
\end{align}
No additional recognition-entropy or correlation-entropy term is required.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Insert the factors in \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-full-joint} into the
logarithm in \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-definition}. Split
$\log\mathbb Q=\log q_\gamma+\log Q_\gamma(x)$ on positive
recognition atoms and sum by label. This gives the first expression,
including both parts of the single joint recognition entropy. Alternatively,
use $\mathbb P(\gamma,x,o)=E_o\boldsymbol\Pi_o(\gamma,x)$ inside
the same logarithm. Since $\sum\mathbb Q=1$, its common scalar contributes
$-\log E_o$ and gives the second expression. Domination makes every
logarithm at a recognition-positive atom finite. If domination fails, the
second expression is still valid as an extended value $+\infty$; no
difference of infinities is used. $\square$

\propositionheading{Exact stochastic conditional-KL loss}
{prop:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-loss}
Use the same fixed channel $C$ for recognition and the selected posterior,
and let $\mathbb Q^C=\mathbb Q C$. Define the coarse objective from
the full induced observation experiment. If
$\mathbb Q\ll\boldsymbol\Pi_o$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Fenergy_o(\mathbb Q)-\Fenergy_o^C(\mathbb Q^C)
 =\sum_{\gamma,y:\mathbb Q^C(\gamma,y)>0}
   \mathbb Q^C(\gamma,y)
   \KL\bigl(\mathbb Q(X\mid\gamma,y)\Vert
             \boldsymbol\Pi_o(X\mid\gamma,y)\bigr)\geq0.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-loss}
\end{equation}
The reverse conditionals are those of the augmented recognition and
posterior laws. The loss vanishes exactly when they coincide on every
recognition-positive target. This is the finite stochastic chain rule
underlying \Cref{prop:rg-geometry-fiber-vfe}.\thermoestablishedstatus


\paragraph{Proof.}
Define the two augmented laws explicitly by
\begin{equation}
 \overline Q(\gamma,x,y)=\mathbb Q(\gamma,x)C_\gamma(x,y),
 \qquad
 \overline\Pi(\gamma,x,y)=\boldsymbol\Pi_o(\gamma,x)C_\gamma(x,y).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-augmented-laws}
\end{equation}
On every $\overline Q$-positive pair, the common channel factor cancels
from the likelihood ratio. Summing over $y$ therefore gives
$\KL(\overline Q\Vert\overline\Pi)
 =\KL(\mathbb Q\Vert\boldsymbol\Pi_o)$.
For a positive coarse recognition atom, domination implies a positive
coarse posterior atom, and the same ratio also factors as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\overline Q(\gamma,x,y)}{\overline\Pi(\gamma,x,y)}
 =\frac{\mathbb Q^C(\gamma,y)}{\boldsymbol\Pi_o^C(\gamma,y)}
   \frac{\mathbb Q(x\mid\gamma,y)}
        {\boldsymbol\Pi_o(x\mid\gamma,y)}.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-kl-factorization}
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms and summing yields the coarse KL plus the displayed
conditional KL. The evidence term $-\log E_o$ is identical by
\Cref{prop:rg-thermo-finite-observation-push}, so
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-posterior} gives
\cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-vfe-loss}.
For finite normalized probability vectors, $\log t\leq t-1$ implies
$\KL(q\Vert p)\geq0$, with equality only for $q=p$; applying this on
each occupied reverse fiber proves the sign and equality statements.
Applying the same identity successively telescopes the finite-word loss
on the same complete experiment. $\square$

If fine recognition is not dominated but its coarse image is dominated,
a violating fine atom reaches a positive coarse recognition atom whose
posterior reverse fiber is defined and assigns zero probability to that
fine atom. Its conditional KL is infinite. The additive identity
$\Fenergy_o(\mathbb Q)=\Fenergy_o^C(\mathbb Q^C)
 +\text{conditional KL}$ then remains an extended-value identity.
If coarse recognition instead charges a posterior-null target, both
fine and coarse VFE are infinite, the posterior fiber at that target is
undefined, and their subtraction is not a numerical loss. A target with
zero coarse recognition weight contributes zero without defining its
recognition reverse fiber. These cases are separate from zero evidence,
where no selected posterior exists.\thermoestablishedstatus

\paragraph{Stochastic loss and deterministic minimization have different types.}
The preceding identity compares one fine recognition law and its actual
stochastic image. It does not assert
\begin{equation}
 \Fenergy_o^C(q')
   =\inf_{\mathbb Q:\mathbb Q C=q'}\Fenergy_o(\mathbb Q)
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-unwarranted-infimum}
\end{equation}
for an arbitrary stochastic channel. For a deterministic retained-label
statistic $c$, a prescribed $q'\ll c_\#\boldsymbol\Pi_o$ does admit
the fine law
$\mathbb Q_*(\gamma,x)
 =q'(\gamma,c_\gamma(x))
   \boldsymbol\Pi_o(x\mid\gamma,c_\gamma(x))$.
Define this expression to be zero when $q'(\gamma,c_\gamma(x))=0$,
without evaluating a posterior fiber at a null target.
Its deterministic image is $q'$ and its occupied reverse fibers equal
the posterior fibers, so the chain rule gives equality with the coarse
objective and proves the corresponding deterministic infimum statement
when all such fine probability laws are allowed. A restricted recognition
family need not contain this lift. For a stochastic channel, prescribing
the output and choosing posterior reverse fibers need not define a joint
with the required conditional channel $C$ given the fine input.\thermoestablishedstatus

\paragraph{Finite witness.}
Take one geometry label, a one-point observation alphabet with likelihood
one, $\boldsymbol\Pi_o=(1/2,1/2)$, and
\begin{equation}
 C=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathbb Q=(1,0),
 \qquad q'=\mathbb Q C=(3/4,1/4).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-finite-stochastic-infimum-witness}
\end{equation}
The matrix has determinant $1/2$, so $\mathbb Q$ is the unique fine
probability vector with output $q'$. Its VFE is $\log2$, since $E_o=1$,
whereas the coarse VFE is
$(3/4)\log(3/2)+(1/4)\log(1/2)$.
Their difference is $2\log2-(3/4)\log3>0$, because
$2^8>3^3$. Thus \cref{eq:rg-thermo-finite-unwarranted-infimum} is false
for this positive stochastic channel even though the exact conditional-KL
loss identity holds. No failure of normalization or discarded geometry
label is involved. $\square$\thermoestablishedstatus

\section{Volume limits and exact renormalization}
\label{sec:rg-thermo-draft-limit-organization}

The arguments in Appendix~\ref{app:thermodynamic-fisher-draft-proofs} apply to
actual finite laws. For the native model in
\Cref{def:rg-pressure-native}, the source map
$\chi_\gamma:\Theta\to\R^2$, with
$\chi_\gamma(\theta)=(h_\gamma(\theta),j_\gamma(\theta))$, is $C^4$ and
has image in a compact positive high-temperature box. The native
pressure theorem obtains $C^4$ convergence from local response, and its
Fisher consequence is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{I(p_{\gamma,L,\theta})}{V}
 \longrightarrow
 g_\gamma(\theta)
 =(D\chi_\gamma)^\top
   (D^2\mathsf A\circ\chi_\gamma)D\chi_\gamma
 \quad\hbox{in }C^2,\qquad V=L^2.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-draft-native-density}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathsf A$ is the natural log-partition density. The raw-factor
pressure differs by the explicit edge and plaquette normalization
terms in \Cref{thm:rg-thermo-native-cfour-pressure}. The pullback in
\Cref{prop:rg-thermo-native-ctwo-metric} therefore does not identify
Fisher information with a Hessian of the raw pressure after a nonlinear
source substitution. The separate free-boundary theorem
\Cref{thm:rg-thermo-native-free-push-ctwo} supplies the derivative limit
for actual fixed-word pushes. Neither a matching bulk pressure nor a
surface bound on log-density ratios supplies that conclusion by itself;
\Cref{prop:rg-thermo-native-boundary-score-counterexample} gives a
counterexample to that inference.\thermoestablishedstatus

For the retained-label family, write its actual effective log masses as
$\lambda_{\gamma,L}$ and use the hypotheses of
\Cref{eq:rg-thermo-label-effective-growth}:
$\lambda_{\gamma,L}/V\to s_\gamma$ in $C^3$, with $C^3$ norms of the
log masses bounded by a constant times $V$ on each admitted compact
neighborhood. The global-factor convention includes $\log Z_{\gamma,L}$
in $\lambda_{\gamma,L}$; the prescribed hierarchy does not. A common
label-independent representative cancels from the normalized weights
and their scores. These choices affect the bulk functions and therefore
which branch dominates. If one fixed label has a uniform positive gap
in $s_\gamma$ over the other labels, and all actual branch densities
converge in $C^2$, then
\Cref{thm:rg-thermo-label-strict} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{I(P_{L,\theta})}{V}\longrightarrow g_{\gamma_*}(\theta)
 \quad\hbox{in }C^2.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-draft-strict-density}
\end{equation}
This remains a semimetric statement unless the limiting tensor has a
positive lower bound on the declared chart. Only on such charts do the
inverse metric, Levi-Civita connection, and Fisher curvature inherit
the convergence proved in \Cref{prop:rg-thermo-label-regular-geometry}.
A two-source branch pulled back into a higher-dimensional parameter
space does not acquire a full-rank metric from this argument.\thermoestablishedstatus

At a tie $\theta_0$, actual finite-volume weights are additional data.
If they converge to $w_\gamma$, let
$v_\gamma=\nabla s_\gamma(\theta_0)$. Under the branch $O(V)$ bound,
\Cref{eq:rg-thermo-label-tie-limit} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{I(P_{L,\theta_0})}{V^2}
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cov}_{w}(v_\gamma).
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-draft-tie-density}
\end{equation}
A positive covariance direction has order-$V^2$ information. A zero
direction implies only $o(V^2)$; it does not imply an order-$V$ bound.
Residual log-weight gradients and rare labels enter before score
squaring. The examples in \Cref{sec:rg-thermo-label-proof} show why
bulk pressure data alone cannot determine their contribution or the
existence of the tie-weight limit.\thermoestablishedstatus

A different experiment uses $u=V(\theta-\theta_0)$. Assume the actual
center ratios in \Cref{eq:rg-thermo-label-center-ratios} converge to
positive $\kappa_\gamma$ on the tied set $S$, and retain the branch
$C^2$ and effective-log-mass $C^3$ hypotheses. Then
\Cref{thm:rg-thermo-label-crossover} identifies
\begin{align}
 \pi_\gamma(u)
 &=\frac{\kappa_\gamma e^{u\cdot v_\gamma}}
         {\sum_{\eta\in S}\kappa_\eta e^{u\cdot v_\eta}},
 \qquad \gamma\in S,
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-draft-window-weights}\\
 I_L^{(u)}(u)
 &=V^{-2}I(P_{L,\theta_0+u/V})
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi(u)}(v_\gamma)
 \quad\hbox{in }C^2.
 \label{eq:rg-thermo-draft-window-metric}
\end{align}
The appendix constructs the comparison kernels for this experiment and
its identifiable quotient. It also treats an actual moving center for a
transverse two-label tie. The reciprocal-volume chart is not a smooth
extension of the extensive density across every tie, nor a universal
physical time parameter.\thermoestablishedstatus

Exact renormalization retains one normalized fine experiment, its
reference, its observation, and its declared recognition law. For a
fixed word of total block factor $B$, the fine and coarse cell counts
are $M=B^2V$. The finite score and conditional-KL identities above
therefore give the scale accounting in
\Cref{sec:rg-thermo-accounting-proof}: an extensive density carries
$B^2$, while a density normalized by squared volume carries $B^4$.
The denominator, parameter coordinate, and observed experiment must be
the same on both sides of each comparison. Every limiting argument
keeps the word fixed before volume tends to infinity. The exact finite
VFE difference is a posterior conditional relative entropy; a general
stochastic channel does not turn it into an equality of variational
infima.\thermoestablishedstatus

Prescribed geometry actions change the declared raw weights. Their
relative center values, as well as their volume orders and derivative
bounds, govern the resulting label experiments. Gauge connection
curvature, statistical Fisher curvature, and curvature of the base
manifold remain distinct inputs with separate metric and measure
conventions. This mapping does not solve a self-consistent curvature
action. Unbounded-depth regular inverses, critical or continuum limits,
arbitrary local-law gluing, and physical identification require
additional constructions outside the present chapter.\thermoestablishedstatus
