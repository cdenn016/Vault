\chapter{A constructive spatial renormalization theory}
\label{ch:constructive-spatial-rg}

The general scale diagram admits a concrete infinite-volume realization in a
positive lattice gauge--matter family. This chapter constructs its complete
coarse laws, their generated interactions, a differentiable RG map on an open
interaction ball, and a finite procedure with quantified errors. The family is
a declared realization of the general probability theory. Its limiting law and
its region of attraction do not identify a continuum theory or a physical law.
\status{DEFINITION}

\section{The lattice law and its geometric channels}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-source}

\definitionheading{Dressed square-lattice source}{def:rg-spatial-source}
Let \(E=\mathbb Z^2\times\{1,2\}\), with \(e=(x,a)\) joining \(x\) to
\(x+e_a\), and retain the four distinct edge copies of every unit plaquette.
The matter sign is \(\phi_x\in\{-1,1\}\), the raw link is \(u_e\in\{-1,1\}\),
and the dressed sign is \(z_e=\phi_xu_e\phi_{x+e_a}\). In this chapter only,
\(b=\tanh h\) and \(t=\tanh j\) denote the field and plaquette strengths;
\(t\) is not time and neither parameter is the contextual point \(c\).
For a finite free edge set \(\Lambda\) and fixed exterior dressed signs
\(\eta\), define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 W_\Lambda^\eta(z)&=\prod_{e\in\Lambda}(1+bz_e)
 \prod_{p:p\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}(1+t\chi_p(z\eta)),\\
 Z_\Lambda^\eta&=2^{-|\Lambda|}\sum_zW_\Lambda^\eta(z),\qquad
 \gamma_\Lambda^\eta(z)=\frac{2^{-|\Lambda|}W_\Lambda^\eta(z)}{Z_\Lambda^\eta}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-source-law}
\end{equation}
where \(\chi_S(z)=\prod_{e\in S}z_e\). All touching plaquettes, including
crossing plaquettes, occur. The reference is normalized counting. The finite
algebra allows \(0\le b,t<1\); subsequent theorems impose smaller domains
explicitly. Periodic versions retain all sectors and require side at least
three, with distinct edge and plaquette copies. These are definitions, not
consequences of locality alone. \status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{An actual infinite-volume source}{thm:rg-spatial-source-law}
For \(0<b<1\) and \(0<t<1/6\), the specification
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-source-law} has a unique dressed Gibbs law
\(\mu_{b,t}\), without a translation-invariance premise. It is translation
invariant. Every fixed finite cylinder is the boundary-independent limit of
expanding boxes and locally embedded full tori. If \(D(e,\Lambda^c)\) is
co-plaquette graph distance, then, for \(A\subset\Lambda\),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}((\gamma_\Lambda^\eta)_A,(\gamma_\Lambda^\zeta)_A)
 \le\min\left\{1,\sum_{e\in A}(6t)^{D(e,\Lambda^c)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-boundary-comparison}
\end{equation}
Independent uniform matter signs and
\(u_e=\phi_xz_e\phi_{x+e_a}\) give the unique Gibbs lift for the declared
raw gauge--matter factors. The proof in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-measure} constructs a countably additive law and
establishes its DLR equations separately from uniqueness. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\definitionheading{Straight-path blocking}{def:rg-spatial-path-channel}
For an integer \(B\ge1\), define the normalized deterministic channel \(C_B\)
by retaining \(\phi'_x=\phi_{Bx}\) and multiplying the raw links along
\(\mathcal P_B(x,a)=\{(Bx+se_a,a):0\le s<B\}\). Its dressed output is
\begin{equation}
 z'_{(x,a)}=\prod_{e\in\mathcal P_B(x,a)}z_e,
 \qquad \mu_B=(C_B)_*\mu_{b,t}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-path-channel}
\end{equation}
Every output lattice is identified with \(\mathbb Z^2\), and its cell alphabet
is \(\mathsf E=\{-1,1\}^2\). This declared identification makes fixed-law
statements meaningful. On a torus of side \(L\), require \(B\mid L\) and
\(L/B\ge3\). \status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Exact geometry and reference square}{prop:rg-spatial-path-square}
Distinct retained paths have disjoint edge supports, although their endpoints
may coincide. The channel is gauge equivariant, pushes normalized counting to
normalized counting, and satisfies \(C_aC_B=C_{aB}\), including
\(C_2C_3=C_3C_2=C_6\). For any positive finite density \(f\), its complete
coarse density is \(\E_{\nu}[f\mid C_B]\), where \(\nu\) is the fine counting
reference. A coarse plaquette holonomy equals the product of all fine
plaquette holonomies in its square; a retained winding Wilson loop retains
the corresponding fine winding loop. These statements retain the complete
law and its normalization. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Along a path every internal matter sign occurs twice and cancels. Distinct
axes distinguish edge copies; along each grid line consecutive intervals of
length \(B\) partition its edges. Each prescribed parity has exactly half the
assignments on its path, so disjoint parities and the retained uniform frames
have the asserted reference. Concatenating intervals gives indices
\(Bs+r\), once each from zero to \(aB-1\), proving composition. Interior
edges occur twice in the plaquette product and cancel. Conditional summation
over counting fibers proves the density formula and its composition without
any change of reference. \hfill\(\square\)

\propositionheading{Large blocks converge as full cylinder laws}{prop:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}
Put
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\tanh(h+2j)=\frac{b(1+t^2)+2t}{1+t^2+2bt}<1.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-rho}
\end{equation}
For any fixed \(0\le b,t<1\), any Gibbs law of the displayed specification,
and any \(M\) distinct retained \(B\)-paths, their parities satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(\mu_{B,M},\nu_M)
 \le\min\left\{1,\frac12\sum_{s=1}^M {M\choose s}
              \rho^{\lceil sB/7\rceil}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}
\end{equation}
The same bound is uniform over finite volumes and dressed boundaries when all
counted path edges are free. In the source uniqueness domain, fixed blocking
commutes with the thermodynamic cylinder limit. Both iterated limits and the
joint box or torus limit \(B_n\to\infty\), \(L_n/B_n\to\infty\), under the
stated embedding and divisibility conditions, yield the fair product law on
each fixed coarse cylinder. The raw limit has independent uniform matter and
links, and negative plaquette holonomy has probability \(1/2\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The single-edge conditional field has absolute value at most \(h+2j\).
A greedy independent subset of an \(sB\)-edge support has at least
\(\lceil sB/7\rceil\) members, because co-plaquette degree is at most six.
Conditional on its complement the selected signs are independent and each
conditional mean has magnitude at most \(\rho\). This bounds every nonempty
Walsh moment. Fourier inversion and the triangle inequality give
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}. A fixed channel pulls a cylinder
back to finitely many fine signs, so \cref{eq:rg-spatial-boundary-comparison}
gives the fixed-block passage. The independent volume-uniform moment bound
gives the increasing-block and stated joint passages directly; it does not
amplify a fixed-support boundary estimate to growing support. Independent
uniform frames preserve the total-variation bound, and a nonempty plaquette
character is fair at the product limit. \hfill\(\square\)

\section{Every-exterior coarse laws and all generated interactions}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-generated}

\theoremheading{A common high-temperature domain at every depth}{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification}
Fix \(0<b<1\), \(0<t<1/12\), and choose \(\theta>0\) with
\(q=12t e^\theta<1\). Put \(C=16q/(1-q)\) and
\(m=(1-\rho)^2/4\). For every integer \(B\ge1\), \(\mu_B\) has a unique
positive, translation-covariant, continuous specification
\(\gamma^{(B)}_A\) defined at every coarse exterior. It is the unique Gibbs
law of that specification. For exterior patterns agreeing in the radius
\(R\) neighborhood of finite cell set \(A\),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}\bigl(\gamma_A^{(B)}(\cdot\mid y),
                       \gamma_A^{(B)}(\cdot\mid y')\bigr)
 \le\min\{1,C|A|B^2(R+1)e^{-\theta(R+1)}\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-coarse-modulus}
\end{equation}
Each one-cell atom lies between \((1-\rho^B)^2/4\) and
\((1+\rho^B)^2/4\), hence is at least \(m\). The same modulus compares a
whole-block finite conditional calculation against its matching infinite
kernel. These statements include infinite exterior prescriptions of
probability zero, constructed by normalized finite limits.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The proof in \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-constrained} uses fine Hamming
transport and column incidence. It gives \(12t<1\) independently of \(B\);
a block total-variation row estimate proportional to \(Bt\) would not give
this domain. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\definitionheading{Canonical vacuum interaction}{def:rg-spatial-vacuum-potential}
Fix the reference cell \(*=(1,1)\) and a translation-invariant lexicographic
order. At \(x\), let \(\eta^{x,r}\) use \(\sigma_y\) for past cells
\(y<x\) within distance \(r\), and \(*\) elsewhere. Define
\begin{equation}
 e_{x,r}^{(B)}(\sigma)=-\log
 \frac{\gamma_x^{(B)}(\sigma_x\mid\eta^{x,r})}
      {\gamma_x^{(B)}(*\mid\eta^{x,r})},\qquad
 A_{x,r}=\{x\}\cup\{y<x:|y-x|_\infty\le r\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-odds}
\end{equation}
Set \(\Phi^{(B)}_{\{x\}}=e_{x,0}^{(B)}\),
\(\Phi^{(B)}_{A_{x,r}}=e_{x,r}^{(B)}-e_{x,r-1}^{(B)}\) for \(r\ge1\),
and all other terms to zero. This convention fixes an interaction
representative; it does not set the finite partition function to one.
\status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{Complete interaction and norm attraction}{thm:rg-spatial-generated-interaction}
Under \cref{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification}, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 A_0&=\frac4{1-\rho^2},\qquad D_0=\frac{2C}{m},\qquad
 \lambda_v=\theta/8,\qquad z_v=e^{-\theta/4},\\
 \mathcal E_B&=A_0\rho^B+\sqrt{2A_0D_0}B\rho^{B/2}
 \frac{z_v(9+14z_v+z_v^2)}{(1-z_v)^4}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-bound}
\end{equation}
Then the entire canonical interaction generates exactly
\(\gamma^{(B)}\), and
\begin{equation}
 \|\Phi^{(B)}\|_{\lambda_v}
 :=\sum_{A\ni0}e^{\lambda_v\operatorname{diam}A}
                   \|\Phi_A^{(B)}\|_\infty
 \le\mathcal E_B\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{A\ni0}\operatorname{diam}(A)\|\Phi_A^{(B)}\|_\infty
 \le\mathcal E_B/\lambda_v.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-norm}
\end{equation}
No generated shell is removed. Canonical reconstruction after \(C_a\) defines
an exact integer-block action on this orbit, with
\(\mathcal R_a\Phi^{(B)}=\Phi^{(aB)}\), composition, and fixed point zero.
The proof and explicit spatial tail are in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-potential}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The potential norm is a cell-interaction norm. The stronger character norm introduced next
is a different topology; its basin theorem requires a separate argument.
\status{DEFINITION}

\section{An RG map on an open interaction ball}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-open-ball}

\definitionheading{Consolidated character coordinates}{def:rg-spatial-character-space}
Let \(J=(J_S)\) be a translation-invariant real coefficient family over
nonempty finite edge supports, with exponential density convention
\(\exp(\sum_SJ_S\chi_S)\). Its finite conditional energies retain all terms
touching the free set. For prescribed \(\kappa,\lambda>0\), use the Banach norm
\begin{equation}
 \|J\|_{\kappa,\lambda}
 =\sup_e\sum_{S\ni e}|S|e^{\kappa|S|+\lambda\operatorname{diam}S}|J_S|.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm}
\end{equation}
Diameters use fine-edge basepoints and Chebyshev distance. The empty character
is a separate scalar. It is absent from these coordinates, while every
finite law retains its own normalizer. \status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{Same-norm differentiability, remainder, and composition}{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}
For \(a\in\{2,3,6\}\), put
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_a&=\kappa(1-1/a),\qquad
 K_a=\frac{8ae^{\lambda(1-1/a)}}{\delta_a},\\
 \ell_a&=a^{-1}e^{-\kappa(a-1)},\qquad M_a=11K_a/a,\\
 \varepsilon&=\min_a\left\{\frac1{48},\frac1{2K_a},
                              \frac{1-\ell_a}{2M_a}\right\}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-local-radius}
\end{equation}
On the open \(\varepsilon\)-ball, the actual path push of the unique Gibbs
law has one unique scalar-free representative \(\mathcal R_aJ\) in the same
ball. Its specification is continuous at every exterior. The map is
Fr\'echet \(C^1\), and, writing \(L_a=D\mathcal R_a(0)\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 (L_aJ)_T&=J_{\operatorname{lift}_a(T)},\qquad \|L_a\|\le\ell_a<1,\\
 \|\mathcal R_aJ-L_aJ\|&\le M_a\|J\|^2,\qquad
 \|D\mathcal R_a(J)-L_a\|\le2M_a\|J\|.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-local-estimates}
\end{equation}
It contracts radially by at most \((1+\ell_a)/2\), and
\(\mathcal R_2\mathcal R_3=\mathcal R_3\mathcal R_2=\mathcal R_6\).
There is no area multiplier in the coefficient map. The complete
coefficient construction and law passage are proved in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-operator}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{The two interaction norms are not interchangeable}{prop:rg-spatial-norm-obstruction}
Arbitrarily small vacuum-vanishing interactions in the diameter norm can have
infinite norm \cref{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm}. Thus
\cref{thm:rg-spatial-generated-interaction} alone does not imply entry into
the ball of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
On an \(R\times R\) cell square put a translated term
\(-a_R(z_{x_R,1}-1)\prod_{x\ne x_R}z_{x,1}\), anchored at its maximal cell,
with \(a_R=\eta e^{-2\lambda R}/R^4\), \(R\ge2\). Its diameter-norm sum is
at most \(2\eta\sum_{R\ge2}e^{-\lambda(R+1)}/R^2\). Its full-square
character has coefficient \(a_R\), distinct from every removed-corner
character, so cannot cancel. Its contribution at a horizontal edge to
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm} is
\(\eta e^{\kappa R^2-\lambda R-\lambda}\), which does not tend to zero.
The ordinary anchored absolute coefficient sums still converge. The failure
is precisely the stronger norm requirement. \hfill\(\square\)

\theoremheading{Complete finite-amplitude approximation}{thm:rg-spatial-finite-amplitude}
For a finite rational translation-orbit library in the strict ball, every
complete Taylor polynomial \(\widehat J_N\) of \(\mathcal R_aJ\) is a finite
rational library retaining all generated supports through order \(N\). With
\(x=K_a\|J\|<1\), its entire error satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal R_aJ-\widehat J_N\|
 \le\frac{\|J\|}{a}
       \frac{x^N[(N+1)-Nx]}{(1-x)^2}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-taylor-tail}
\end{equation}
Every prescribed positive rational tolerance is attainable with sufficiently
large finite arithmetic and enumeration resources. For a finite word, choose
one certified radius \(r<\varepsilon\); then
\(L_a(r)=\ell_a+2M_ar<1\) is a Lipschitz bound. If every approximate input
remains in that ball, errors propagate as
\(e_{k+1}\le L_{a_k}(r)e_k+\tau_k\). Incomplete enumeration gives no
complete-polynomial conclusion. The proof in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-finite-amplitude} counts all translations,
repeated source occurrences, and omitted orders. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Constructive entry of the spatial source into any prescribed norm}{thm:rg-spatial-basin-entrance}
Fix a compact rational box
\(0<b_-\le b\le b_+<1\), \(0<t_-\le t\le t_+<1/12\), and prescribed
positive rational \(\kappa,\lambda\). There is a computable \(B_0\) such
that, for every integer \(B\ge B_0\), the actual law \(\mu_B\) has a unique
translation-invariant scalar-free character representative \(J^{(B)}\)
in the open ball of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}. An explicit bound
decays exponentially in \(B\), uniformly on that box. For rational source
parameters, a finite rational library approximates \(J^{(B)}\) to any
prescribed positive error in the full original
\(\|\cdot\|_{\kappa,\lambda}\) norm, with sufficiently large finite
resources. The construction and its constants are given in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-basin}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Once this entry holds, \(\mathcal R_aJ^{(B)}=J^{(aB)}\). For an oracle input
error \(\delta\) and a finite-word approximation error \(e\), with both
exact trajectories in the same certified ball, the total error is
\(e+\delta\prod_kL_{a_k}(r)\). This approximates the actual path-pushed
law's interaction. It does not compute a discarded partition scalar or an
ELBO from scalar-free coefficients. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Regular statistical geometry at fixed depths}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-fisher}

\theoremheading{Actual Fisher density and regular finite hierarchies}{thm:rg-spatial-fisher-density}
In natural parameters \(g=(h,j)\), fix a compact rectangle in
\(h>0\), \(0<j<\operatorname{atanh}(1/12)\), and an integer \(B\).
The complete coarse finite marginals and every constrained local row are
actual \(C^3\) functions. The complete canonical interaction is \(C^3\)
in one exponential diameter norm; its derivatives through order three tend
to zero with increasing \(B\), uniformly on each such compact rectangle.
Let \(I_{B,\Lambda_L}\) be the Fisher matrix of the actual \(L\)-square
marginal, and \(I_{\mathrm{cell},B}(g\mid\mathrm{past})\) the predictive
row Fisher matrix with its complete past fixed and its future integrated.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_B(g)=\E_{\mu_B}I_{\mathrm{cell},B}(g\mid\mathrm{past})
 =\lim_{L\to\infty}L^{-2}I_{B,\Lambda_L}(g)
 \quad\hbox{in }C^2\hbox{ on compact parameter sets}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density}
\end{equation}
For each fixed depth, and for every prescribed finite list of depths, there
is a nonempty open region with positive \(j\) on which these densities are
positive-definite metrics. Their Levi--Civita connections are \(C^1\),
their curvatures are continuous, and the connections and curvatures of the
area-normalized finite-marginal metrics converge to them on compact regular
subcharts. The complete response, moving-expectation, and positive-floor
arguments appear in \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-fisher}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The predictive row in \cref{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density} integrates its
future. The canonical interaction row in
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-odds} fixes its future to \(*\). Replacing the
former with the latter changes the Fisher-density construction.
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Area-qualified information contraction}{prop:rg-spatial-fisher-contraction}
For integer blocking by \(a\),
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{aB}\preceq a^2\mathcal I_B,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal I_{aB}}{(aB)^2}\preceq\frac{\mathcal I_B}{B^2}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-contraction}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For a fixed parameter-independent finite channel, the output score is the
conditional mean of the input score. Its Fisher defect is the expected
conditional covariance, hence positive semidefinite. A coarse \(L\)-square
is the deterministic image of the fine \((aL+1)\)-square marginal,
which contains every required path. Apply the finite channel inequality
and divide by \(L^2\). The fixed-depth marginal limit in
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density}, together with
\((aL+1)^2/L^2\to a^2\), gives the factor \(a^2\). Dividing by the
original fine-cell area gives the second inequality. \hfill\(\square\)

\openproblemheading{Limits beyond the admitted high-temperature construction}{open:rg-spatial-generality}
The construction does not supply an interacting critical fixed point,
blocking-independent critical exponents, a continuum gauge-field measure,
or a continuous-scale generator. Closing those obligations requires new
parameter domains and corresponding tightness, interaction, and scaling
estimates. The finite-depth Fisher floors may vanish as depth increases;
an all-depth inverse or curvature limit requires estimates not supplied
here. Gauge holonomy, parameter Fisher curvature, and base curvature remain
distinct geometric objects. A curvature term in VFE requires its declared
normalized generative model and its scalar normalizer.
\status{OPEN}
