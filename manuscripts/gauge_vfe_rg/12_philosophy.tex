\chapter{Interpretive Content}
\label{ch:philosophy}

Nothing in this chapter is a mathematical result. No claim made below carries \status{ESTABLISHED}, and an occurrence of that tag anywhere in this chapter should be read as an error rather than as a strong statement. What the chapter does instead is say what the formalism of the preceding chapters does and does not license one to believe about it. Declared readings carry \status{DEFINITION}, because a reading is a choice and nothing is proved by making it. Interpretive commitments that the mathematics of earlier chapters actually consumes carry \status{HYPOTHESIS}. There is exactly one such commitment, the observational closure declared in Section~\ref{sec:phil-participatory}. Its tag recurs wherever that same commitment is restated or wherever a later statement inherits it, so the tag occurs more often than the commitment does, and each occurrence names the commitment it carries. Readings whose consequences are unsettled carry \status{OPEN}, with the obstruction named. Where a position is merely available rather than supported, the text says that it is available and says what would be needed to support it, and the difference between availability and support is the distinction this chapter exists to police.

A chapter of this kind is worth having only if the interpretive choices are not idle, and here they are not. One of them is a hypothesis that Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} uses in a proposition. Two of them make demands on the geometry that are in direct conflict, in the precise sense that the regime one requires is the regime the other forbids. And one widely used word names two conditions that play opposite roles in Chapters~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} and~\ref{ch:renormalization}, so that conflating them would mistake an operator's degenerate mode for the criterion governing when the operator applies. Sorting this out is work the mathematics does not do for itself, and leaving it undone invites a reader to supply from outside whichever reading they arrived with.

\section{What the base is, and what it is not}
\label{sec:phil-base}

Probability enters this construction through the associated bundles of Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} and through the finite recognition law of Chapter~\ref{ch:probability}, and nowhere else. An agent is a section-bearing object, so its state channel is the assignment
\begin{equation}
q_i:\mathcal C_i\longrightarrow\mathcal B_{\mathrm{state}},
\qquad
c\longmapsto q_i(c),
\label{eq:phil-fiber-measures}
\end{equation}
whose value at each context is a probability measure on a sample fiber. The contextual base is the index of that assignment. It carries no probability measure of its own at any point in the development: the design $D=\{c_a\}$ is a declared finite subset rather than a sample from any law, no chapter forms an expectation over contexts, and Open~3.15 records that the construction supplies no probability measure even on a space of sections over $\mathcal C$. Beliefs are measures on the fibers, indexed by base points. \status{DEFINITION} Nothing is proved by saying so; this paragraph restates the typing of Definitions 2.4 and 2.7 and of Chapter~\ref{ch:probability}, and it is recorded here because the readings below turn on it.

One misreading is worth foreclosing before it starts. That agent $i$'s own belief in \eqref{eq:phil-fiber-measures} is defined only on its own support $\mathcal C_i$ does not make belief a locally-only-definable notion, and it should not be confused with the separate fact that a common frame is unavailable outside the flat regime. Proposition 2.22 establishes that the associated bundle $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{state}}$ admits a global continuous cross-section over the whole of $\mathcal C_0$ regardless of the bundle's topological class, because the Gaussian fiber is contractible and every obstruction to extending a section over a cell complex vanishes on a contractible fiber. What Hypothesis 2.10 and its failure control is a trivialization, an identification of every fiber with one model fiber, not the existence of a section. Beliefs, in this precise sense, are globally definable; frames are not. \status{DEFINITION}

Two readings of that arrangement compete, and neither is preferred by anything in the mathematics.

On the first, the base is a form of intuition in Kant's sense: the a priori structure under which appearances are ordered, rather than the object standing behind them \citep{Kant1781}. What is inferred on this reading is the latent state, the variables $k_i$ and $m_i$ that the generative law of Chapter~\ref{ch:generative} governs and that the recognition law approximates. The base is scaffolding. It exists so that the transports of Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} are well typed and so that an agent's laws can be indexed at all, and it is not among the things anyone is trying to find out about. \status{DEFINITION}

On the second, the base together with its bundle is noumenal: it is what the representations are representations of, and the latents are the agent-side proxies through which it is approached but never reached. \status{DEFINITION}

The absence of a measure on the base is the first thing a reader will reach for in adjudicating between these, and it does not do the work it appears to do. On the noumenal reading the absence is not an embarrassment but a prediction, and this is the strongest single point in that reading's favor: a noumenon is precisely what one cannot have credences over, so a formalism that equipped the base with a probability would be misdescribing what it had posited. The point must be stated with its limit attached, however, because the competing reading predicts the same absence for its own reason, scaffolding being no more a candidate for credences than a thing-in-itself is. A prediction shared by two hypotheses discriminates neither. The silence of the construction about measures on $\mathcal C$ is therefore evidence-neutral rather than evidence for either party, and the reason the next section is needed is that a criterion which does discriminate has to come from somewhere else. \status{DEFINITION}

Neither reading changes an equation: every proposition of Chapters~\ref{ch:geometry} through~\ref{ch:obstructions} is provable, and every hypothesis statable, under either. That does not make the choice empty, but it does mean the choice cannot be made by checking a derivation, and a reader who wants it settled must accept some criterion the derivations do not supply.

\section{The condition under which a noumenal reading earns its keep}
\label{sec:phil-noumenon}

The criterion this chapter adopts is that a posit which leaves no trace in anything the construction can compute is an idle wheel, and that parsimony removes idle wheels. \status{DEFINITION} This is a declared standard and not a theorem. It is familiar in the form that belief should extend only as far as empirical adequacy requires \citep{vanFraassen1980}, a reader who declines it will reach a different verdict in this section, and nothing in the mathematics forces it on anyone. It is stated openly because the section's conclusion is worthless without it.

Under the standard, the flat regime settles the matter against the noumenal reading. Hypothesis 2.10 posits a single reference trivialization over the union of the agents' supports, so every frame is referable to one global section; Hypothesis 2.14 sets every interaction link equal to the pointwise comparison $\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$. Proposition 2.11 then returns the identity for the ordered product of comparisons around any closed walk at a common context, and Proposition 2.15 shows that in trivialized coordinates every transported residual becomes a plain difference. The frames themselves are moved by a single global relabeling and carry no invariant content. In that regime nothing about the base leaves a detectable trace in any quantity the population can assemble, and the criterion removes it: the intuitional reading is the one to prefer, and the noumenal reading buys nothing at all.

Under non-trivial topology the situation reverses, and it reverses for a reason worth stating slowly. In Regime~II the link is declared independently of the frames, and the ordered product around a closed walk,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Hol}(\gamma)=\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}L_{i_ai_{a+1}},
\qquad
\operatorname{Hol}(\gamma)\longmapsto g_{i_0}\operatorname{Hol}(\gamma)g_{i_0}^{-1},
\label{eq:phil-holonomy}
\end{equation}
has a gauge-invariant conjugacy class by Proposition 2.13, and by the loop statement of Proposition 2.15 its represented form is conjugate to the ordered product of the trivialized twists $H_{ij}$. Every ingredient of that product is a comparison between a pair of agents. No agent holds a belief whose argument is a point of the base, and none is required to: the population's own pairwise comparisons suffice to compute an invariant that is the identity exactly when the underlying structure is flat. What the construction thereby permits one to say is that a structure which is never an object of representation in it can still leave a signature in the relations among representations.

The conclusion is therefore that the noumenal reading becomes substantive exactly when the bundle is non-trivial, and this is a mathematical condition placed on an interpretive question rather than a matter of taste. \status{DEFINITION} The same question receives opposite answers in the two regimes, and which regime obtains is a property of the model rather than of the reader. That is the sharpest thing this chapter has to say, and two qualifications are needed to keep it from saying more.

The first concerns what the invariant in Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-holonomy} is a signature \emph{of}. Definition 2.12 declares the links on a finite interaction complex that carries no smooth structure and no declared relation to $\mathcal C$, and Open 2.16 records that whether the link holonomy can be realized as the parallel transport of a principal connection along a corresponding closed curve in the base is not settled. Until it is, what the comparisons detect is non-trivial structure in the declared links, and the further inference to non-trivial structure in the base is unlicensed. \status{OPEN} What would close it is exactly what Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} names: a declared embedding of the interaction complex into $\mathcal C$ sending edges to piecewise-smooth curves, together with a proof that the ordered link product equals the path-ordered exponential of the connection along the concatenated loop.

The second is that the condition under which the reading earns its keep is the condition this document declines to work under. Hypothesis 2.10 assumes the bundle trivial over the union of supports and Hypothesis 2.14 sets the links to coboundaries; Chapters~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} and~\ref{ch:renormalization} consume both, and Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} shows what breaks without them. The interpretive payoff of this section therefore sits outside the scope the mathematics adopts, and a reader who wants it must first want the non-flat development that Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} lists as open. Recording that plainly is more useful than asserting the reading would be.

Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} now distinguishes a third holonomy that this section's argument did not use: the base holonomy of the connection Propositions 2.29 through 2.31 construct canonically from the frames. It is logically independent of the graph holonomy of Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-holonomy}, on which the whole of the argument above rests, because Proposition 2.31 shows the base holonomy can be nontrivial even under Hypothesis 2.14, the very regime this section used to declare the noumenal reading idle. The conjecture worth naming, and not acting on further, is that the noumenal reading's substantive-ness could be regrounded in nontrivial third-kind holonomy rather than second-kind, in which case it would stop coinciding with the flat, second-kind-trivial regime that the participatory reading and the flow of Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} require, and the two readings could in principle be jointly non-idle. \status{CONJECTURE} What keeps this a conjecture rather than a rescue is that the idle-wheel standard adopted above demands a trace in something the construction can compute, and every trace this section actually exhibited, Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-holonomy} and the loop statement of Proposition 2.15, is assembled from pairwise agent comparisons alone, while Open 2.16 and Open 2.32 leave undemonstrated any bridge from such comparisons to the base holonomy of Proposition 2.31. Settling the conjecture needs that bridge built in one direction, a population-computable quantity shown sensitive to the base curvature, or refuted in the other, a proof that the base curvature is invisible to every such quantity, which would leave the idle-wheel verdict against the noumenal reading exactly where this section left it.

\section{Which realism this is}
\label{sec:phil-realism}

The desktop analogy, on which what an agent perceives stands to what there is as icons stand to machine code, belongs to the interface theory of perception of Hoffman, Singh and Prakash \citep{Hoffman2015,Hoffman2019}. Their argument is evolutionary: perceptual strategies tuned to fitness drive truth-tracking strategies to extinction except where fitness varies monotonically with truth, and the conclusion drawn is that perception is systematically non-veridical, with the representational space related to the world by an arbitrary measurable map carrying no structural constraint whatever.

This construction is doing something different, and the difference is not a matter of emphasis. Agents here are not maps from a world into a private space; by Definition 2.7 they are sections of associated bundles over the base. That typing supports a statement the interface picture cannot make. Hypothesis 2.10 requires the principal bundle to be trivial over the union of the agents' supports, and says exactly what it excludes: configurations whose frames cannot all be referred to one trivialization. Read in the other direction, that exclusion is a topological constraint on collective belief. Within the construction, whether a population can carry a globally consistent assignment of frames at all is decided by the topology of the bundle over the union of their supports, and it is decided independently of anything the agents believe or do. An arbitrary measurable map admits no analogue of this. So the formalism has room for a constraint linking representation to what is represented, where interface theory denies that any such constraint is to be expected. \status{DEFINITION}

Two restraints belong immediately beside that. The room is empty as this document is written, because Hypothesis 2.10 assumes the triviality that would make the constraint bite, and no non-trivial case is developed anywhere. And nothing above bears on the veridicality of any individual agent's belief, which is the subject interface theory is actually about; the present construction is compatible with every agent being systematically wrong about everything it represents, and it contains no evolutionary argument of any kind. Nothing here refutes the interface theory, and nothing here is supported by it.

What the mathematics does exhibit is a separation between quantities that survive a change of frame and quantities that do not. On the surviving side stand the conjugacy class of the holonomy of Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-holonomy}, the generalized spectrum of the pair consisting of the Laplacian part and the full interaction operator, and the rank of the coupling matrix,
\begin{equation}
\bigl[\operatorname{Hol}(\gamma)\bigr]\in\operatorname{Conj}(G),
\qquad
d_1,\dots,d_{NK}\ \text{of}\ (L,\Lambda),
\qquad
r=\operatorname{rank}M\in\{0,1,\dots,K\},
\label{eq:phil-invariants}
\end{equation}
invariant respectively by Propositions 2.13, 10.9 and 10.11. On the other side stand an agent's frame, which a global relabeling moves; the coarse frame, which by Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} occupies a $K^{2}$-dimensional orbit along which nothing observable varies; and the coupling matrix beyond its rank, since congruence orbits of a positive semidefinite matrix are indexed by rank alone. What the construction licenses one to say, then, is that structure is recoverable from it and intrinsic nature is not.

That is structural realism in the epistemic sense given the position by Worrall, on which what survives is structure rather than nature \citep{Worrall1989}, and it is what this document's reading declares itself to be. \status{DEFINITION} The stronger ontic version, on which structure is not merely what can be known but all that there is, is argued for by Ladyman and collaborators \citep{Ladyman2007,Ladyman2014}, and a moderate form due to Esfeld and Lam places objects and relations on the same footing, individuating objects only relationally and observing that the fiber-bundle formulation is where such a position is most naturally expressed \citep{esfeld2008moderate}. That last observation describes the present setting closely enough to be tempting. It should nonetheless be declined here, because the chapters above exhibit invariants and exhibit unidentifiable quantities, and say nothing whatever about whether the unidentifiable quantities exist. The ontic reading is available and is not supported. \status{OPEN} What would support it is an argument that the unidentifiable directions are absent rather than merely unidentifiable, and no chapter attempts one; an unidentifiability result and an elimination result are different results, and only the first is proved.

\section{Two agreements, and the cost of conflating them}
\label{sec:phil-agreements}

Two conditions on a population are routinely called agreement. They are conditions on different objects, they play opposite roles in the mathematics, and the ambiguity of the single word that names both is expensive. Write them as
\begin{equation}
\text{common trivialization:}\quad L_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}\ \text{for every edge},
\qquad
\text{belief agreement:}\quad z_i=z_j\ \text{for all}\ i,j,
\label{eq:phil-two-agreements}
\end{equation}
the first a condition on the frames and the links, the second a condition on the trivialized means. \status{DEFINITION}

The first is the coarse-graining admissibility criterion. Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining}'s flat comparison hypothesis is exactly the left condition of Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-two-agreements}, and that chapter shows what fails without it: an edge internal to a cluster leaves the residual $(I-\Theta_{ij})^{\top}W_{ij}(I-\Theta_{ij})$ in the coarse self term rather than vanishing, and a cut edge contributes an asymmetric off-diagonal block, so the coarse operator leaves the declared family at the first step. The open item of that chapter conjectures that closure survives when the twists around every cycle internal to a cluster are trivial, which is to say when each cluster admits a common trivialization even though the population does not. Whatever the fate of that conjecture, the criterion in play is triviality of internal holonomy, and it is a statement about the connection.

The second is a null direction. Under the flat hypothesis the trivialized energy is a sum of self terms and of pair terms in $z_i-z_j$, so the interaction part annihilates the configurations in Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-two-agreements}; Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining}'s translation-invariance theorem states this as one of four equivalent characterizations of the declared family, which is to say that being annihilated on belief agreement is part of what the family \emph{is}. Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} then records that at the fixed ray of the declared flow the self sector has vanished, leaving a pure matrix-weighted Laplacian whose kernel contains that configuration; and Chapter~\ref{ch:obstructions} finds the same configuration twice more, as the kernel of a reciprocal pair independently of the link covariances, and as the direction on which a declared apex prior charges exactly $v^{\top}P_0v$. Belief agreement is the degenerate mode. It is what an interaction term costs nothing for, and what a declared self term or prior must charge for if anything is going to.

The two conditions are independent in both directions. A common trivialization constrains the frames and the links and leaves the means entirely free, since setting $L_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$ says nothing about $z$ and every configuration of $z$ remains admissible under it. Conversely, transported belief agreement is possible under non-trivial holonomy, but only on a subspace: requiring $\mu_i=\rho_k(L_{ij})\mu_j$ along every edge of a closed walk forces
\begin{equation}
\mu_{i_0}\in\ker\bigl(\rho_k(\operatorname{Hol}(\gamma))-I\bigr),
\label{eq:phil-agreement-kernel}
\end{equation}
which is Proposition 11.1 read as a statement about configurations rather than about a precision. Non-trivial holonomy restricts the belief-agreement set to a subspace without emptying it, and trivial holonomy leaves the set whole without entailing that any two agents occupy it. The two conditions constrain one another and neither implies the other.

Because the word covers both, this document avoids ``consensus'' entirely, using ``common trivialization'' for the first condition and reserving ``belief agreement'' for the second. \status{DEFINITION} The terminological rule matters because the philosophical temptation runs hard toward the conflation. That coarse agents form when agents agree is an appealing sentence; it is correct on the first reading and wrong on the second, and wrong in a specific and expensive way. Aggregation annihilates internal edges by the algebra of the aggregation matrix regardless of what any agent believes, so belief agreement is not a precondition for forming a coarse agent, and a rule that waited for it would be waiting on a quantity the operation never consults. The deeper error is that belief agreement is the null direction of the very operator being coarse-grained, and that at the fixed ray of the flow the self sector which could charge for it has gone to zero. A criterion built on belief agreement would therefore be reading an operator's degenerate mode as the condition governing when to apply the operator. One of the two conditions is what makes coarse-graining well defined; the other is the direction along which the coarse operator at the flow's fixed ray fails to be definite. \status{DEFINITION} They should not share a name.

\section{The participatory reading and its exact cost}
\label{sec:phil-participatory}

If an agent's observations are the states of other agents, then what an agent models is the remainder of the population, and a fixed point of the rescaled flow is an effective law at which the population has arrived rather than one it was handed. \status{HYPOTHESIS} This is the one interpretive commitment in the chapter that the mathematics uses rather than merely tolerates. Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} names it, in the form that an observation between two agents is an edge of the interaction graph, as the hypothesis on which its whole disconnection section rests, and combines it with Proposition 10.12 to conclude that agents which exchange anything at all lie in one component and are carried by one orbit. That much is unconditional. The further step, that they reach one limiting ray, is not: it is Corollary 10.15 there, an implication whose antecedents are the convergence, uniqueness and scheme-independence questions recorded as Open Problems 10.5, 10.13 and 10.14, and none of the three is settled. \status{OPEN} The conditionality is inherited here in full and is discharged nowhere in this chapter, so every sentence below about a shared effective law is to be read with those three antecedents attached. What the commitment excludes is every model in which an agent's data have a source outside the population, and it is used nowhere else in this document.

What the reading buys, beyond the disconnection result, is supplied by Chapter~\ref{ch:obstructions} rather than asserted here. At a variational fixed point of the apex construction, the apex factor's mean is a transported pooling of every constituent's current belief and each constituent's effective prior is that pooling transported back down, so the loop is closed and it lives in the inference, on a joint that remains fixed, acyclic and normalized throughout. The participatory reading is the interpretation under which that loop is the content of the construction rather than a curiosity of its fixed-point equations. Its resemblance to Wheeler's participatory proposal \citep{Wheeler1990} is thematic; nothing here is derived from that proposal and nothing here supports it.

The cost is exact and should be stated without softening, and stating it exactly means separating three commitments that travel together in the usual slogan and do not entail one another. The first is observational closure: every datum any agent has is the state of another agent. That is the commitment named above, it is the only one the disconnection argument of Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} consumes, and it is a restriction on the model. \status{HYPOTHESIS} The second is the identification of a limiting ray of the rescaled flow with a physical law, which Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} marks a declaration and which is logically independent of the first. \status{DEFINITION} The third is ontological closure: that there is nothing behind the appearances at all. \status{NOT-CLAIMED} The third does not follow from the first two, and the reason is elementary rather than subtle. An unobserved environment may act on every agent alike, entering the effective couplings as a common influence, without ever being the argument of any agent's belief; observational closure constrains what a population can represent, not what can exist or act on it. This document therefore asserts the first where it is used, records the second as a declaration, and declines the third. On the first alone, what an agent models is other agents, the base retains only its indexing and typing roles, and no interpretive weight can be placed on the base's topology, because the reading has already committed to the claim that the effective law is exhausted, \emph{as an object of representation}, by the population's self-consistent description of itself. The participatory reading and the noumenal reading of Section~\ref{sec:phil-base} therefore pull against one another. Both are available. They are not jointly available without the base becoming idle, and the idleness would be the noumenal reading's, not the participatory one's.

The conflict is sharper than a clash of temperaments, because each reading requires a regime the other forbids. Section~\ref{sec:phil-noumenon} concludes that the noumenal reading earns its keep only when the link structure is non-flat, in the second-kind-holonomy sense of Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-holonomy}. The participatory reading requires the flow, and Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} is a Regime~I statement from beginning to end: under an independently declared link the coarse operator leaves the family at the first step and the iteration is not defined, so there is no fixed point for a population to have arrived at. Each reading's enabling condition is the other's disabling condition, on that shared notion of holonomy. Whether that is essential or an artifact of the flat hypothesis is unsettled and turns on questions already on the books. \status{OPEN} Two independent dissolutions are named, and neither is established. If the enlarged class of connection Laplacians is closed under aggregation with an explicit coarse link rule, which is the open item Chapter~\ref{ch:coarsegraining} identifies as the more interesting of the two it leaves, then a flow exists in the non-flat regime and the two readings could be jointly non-idle on the second-kind holonomy itself. Separately, and inequivalently, Section~\ref{sec:phil-noumenon} names the conjecture that the noumenal reading could instead rest on the third-kind, base-level holonomy of Proposition 2.31, which survives the flat regime that the flow requires; that route is gated on the same open bridge as Open 2.16, and is a named conjecture rather than a resolution for the reason given there. If neither dissolution goes through, the tension is structural.

This chapter does not adjudicate. A reader who takes the noumenal reading gets a referent for the invariants of Equation~\eqref{eq:phil-invariants} and pays for it with a regime this document does not develop. A reader who takes the participatory reading gets the disconnection result and the inference-level loop and pays for it with the base. Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} already records the parallel refusal for the narrower question of whether a fixed point is a physical law, marking that identification a declaration and neither claiming nor refuting it; the present refusal is the same refusal at wider scope. The formalism does not decide between these readings, and this document claims neither of them and claims neither of them false.

\section{What would make any of this testable}
\label{sec:phil-testable}

The construction's own accounting of what it forbids has already been given in Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} and is not improved by restating it interpretively. Under the commitment of Section~\ref{sec:phil-participatory} an observation is an edge, and by Proposition 10.12 the flow respects the components of the interaction graph, so a population operating under a different fixed point is a population with which nothing is exchanged. Deciding whether another class is occupied would require an exchange; an exchange is an edge; an edge merges the components. The plurality of universality classes is therefore not empirically accessible from within one. Whether that inaccessibility amounts to a prediction of no observable variation in the effective law across a connected population depends on there being an effective law for the component to have, and that is the part which is not settled: the statement is the consequent of Corollary 10.15 and inherits its three open antecedents, convergence, uniqueness of the limit within the component, and independence of the blocking scheme. \status{OPEN} The status is therefore compound and worth separating. The decomposition is a mathematical result of Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} and carries its status there rather than here: Proposition 10.12 gives one dynamically independent subsystem per component, and that much needs nothing further. The interpretive input, that an observation is an edge, is the commitment named above. \status{HYPOTHESIS} The step from a decomposition to a shared reached law is neither, and this chapter asserts it nowhere without its antecedents. \status{NOT-CLAIMED} Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} is right to call the conditional result a constraint the theory places on itself rather than a test the theory can pass; the present remark adds only that the constraint is not yet in force, because its premises are open.

Two things do remain accessible, and they are unequal in maturity. The regime question of Section~\ref{sec:phil-noumenon} is decidable from inside a component, because by the loop statement of Proposition 2.15 the ordered product of trivialized twists around a closed walk is assembled entirely from pairwise residuals between agents, and it is either the identity or it is not. \status{DEFINITION} Nothing new is proved by observing this; what it settles, if it were carried out, is whether the base posit is idle in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:phil-noumenon}, and by Open 2.16 it would still not settle what a non-identity is a signature of. That decidability is specific to the second kind of holonomy. Nothing analogous is available for the third: the base holonomy of Proposition 2.31 is a functional of how the frames vary along curves of $\mathcal C$, no agent computes such a curve integral from population data alone, and Open 2.16 together with Open 2.32 leave no demonstrated route from pairwise comparisons to that quantity. The conjecture named in Section~\ref{sec:phil-noumenon} is therefore not merely unproved but, on present evidence, untestable from inside a component in the same sense the second-kind regime question is testable.\status{DEFINITION} The second accessible quantity is the running of effective couplings away from a fixed point, which Chapter~\ref{ch:renormalization} identifies as the falsifiable residue of the whole construction and records as conditional. \status{OPEN} Making it a sharp test needs the attraction result of Open 10.5, a linearization about the fixed ray to supply exponents, and a declared blocking rule and rescaling, without which the phrase naming one population at two resolutions does not pick out two definite parameter sets.

The interpretive content of this chapter is therefore constrained by the mathematics and is not confirmed by it. The constraints are specific: a reading is idle in one regime and substantive in another, and which regime obtains is a property of the model rather than of the reader; two conditions that a single word conflates play opposite roles, one enabling the coarse-graining and one degenerate under it; and two individually coherent readings require regimes that exclude one another, with the permanence of that exclusion an open question about connection Laplacians rather than about interpretation. None of this confirms a reading. It says which readings cost what, which is the most a chapter of this kind should claim.
