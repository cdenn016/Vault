\chapter{The Lattice Cluster: Typing Question and Constructive Spatial RG}
\label{ch:constructive-spatial-rg}

This chapter asks whether the coarse-channel calculus of the main manuscript
can be carried out on an infinite-volume law with local interactions so that
every coarse law, every generated interaction, and every Fisher density is
constructed rather than assumed. For the square-lattice \(\mathbb Z_2\)
gauge--matter law at high temperature it can. Under straight-path blocking
the pushed law has a unique coarse specification at every exterior, on a
coupling domain that does not shrink with the block size
(\cref{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification}); its canonical interaction
is complete and exponentially small in the block size
(\cref{thm:rg-spatial-generated-interaction}); on an open ball of character
coefficients the coarse-graining map is Fr\'echet differentiable and
composes exactly (\cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}), and the source law
enters that ball at every sufficiently large block size
(\cref{thm:rg-spatial-basin-entrance}); at each fixed depth the Fisher
information per cell is a regular metric on an open parameter region
(\cref{thm:rg-spatial-fisher-density}). The proofs are in
\cref{app:spatial-rg-proofs}. The every-exterior specification and the
interaction bound are the inputs of the pressure and specific-VFE chapter,
\cref{ch:geometry-pressure}, and the response, jet, and chart estimates of
\cref{app:spatial-rg-proofs} are the inputs that
\cref{ch:thermodynamic-fisher-draft,app:thermodynamic-fisher-draft-proofs}
transfer to free boundaries.

The lattice law realizes the coarse-channel layer of the main manuscript
(\cref{ch:coarsegraining}), not its gauge-variational agent theory: it
carries no agent sections, belief or model channels, recognition law, or
ELBO, and it imposes no restriction on that theory. Whether its sites are
agents at \(c_*\) or points of a discretized base is
\cref{open:rg-lattice-typing}, which heads this part. The two chapters that
follow place a finite catalog of such laws on one cell lattice.
\Cref{ch:geometry-pressure} separates branchwise normalization from one
global label factor, transports the fine observation experiment and its
scalar normalization, and proves pressure and specific-VFE limits for
retained labels; there, erasing a label can destroy Gibbs regularity, and a
disappearing label can make the relative entropy to a limiting joint
infinite while the finite-ensemble VFE density stays finite.
\Cref{ch:thermodynamic-fisher-draft} proves the Fisher geometry of these
retained ensembles in volume limits.

The source family is classical. With hopping coupling \(h\) and plaquette
coupling \(j\), the identities \(e^{hz}=\cosh h(1+bz)\) and
\(e^{j\chi}=\cosh j(1+t\chi)\) for signs \(z,\chi\in\{-1,1\}\) show that
\cref{def:rg-spatial-source} is the \(\mathbb Z_2\) lattice gauge theory of
\citet{Wegner1971} coupled to a \(\mathbb Z_2\) matter (Higgs) field, as in
\citet{FradkinShenker1979}, written in the gauge-invariant link variables
\(z_e\) of unitary gauge. \citet{OsterwalderSeiler1978} proved existence and
analyticity of the infinite-volume limit of strongly coupled lattice gauge
theories, which is the small-\(t\) regime here, and treated the lattice
Higgs mechanism; \citet{FradkinShenker1979} used those results to show that
the Higgs and confinement regions are analytically connected for a matter
field in the fundamental representation. Dobrushin uniqueness and
comparison, Kolmogorov extension, Kozlov's Gibbs representation, and
Israel's high-temperature renormalization maps are cited where they are
used.

Relative to these antecedents, the statements proved here for this family
and its straight-path channel are the five results named in the first
paragraph, the path semigroup and reference square of
\cref{prop:rg-spatial-path-square}, and the finite-amplitude approximation
of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-finite-amplitude}. The block-size uniformity of the
domain \(12t<1\) in \cref{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification} is the
principal candidate for a new result: the
high-temperature renormalization results summarized by
\citet[Sec.~6.1.1]{VanEnterFernandezSokal1993} concern decimation,
Kadanoff, and averaging maps of Ising-spin interactions, not this
gauge--matter path channel. The literature check behind this paragraph is
limited to the works cited in this chapter, so priority for these
statements is not claimed. The limiting law and its region of attraction
are not claimed to identify a continuum theory, and no statement about
agents is made while the typing of the sites is open. The open items are
\cref{open:rg-lattice-typing,open:rg-spatial-generality}.

\section{The typing question and the notation of this part}
\label{sec:rg-lattice-typing}

\openproblemheading{Typing of the lattice sites}{open:rg-lattice-typing}
The lattice family of this part is a law on the sign configurations of the
edge set \(E=\mathbb Z^2\times\{1,2\}\), together with its matter signs, and
nothing in its construction declares what a site \(x\in\mathbb Z^2\) is in
the bundle-geometric model of the main manuscript. Two readings are
available. In the first, the sites are agents at \(c_*\), the structure
group is \(G=\mathbb Z_2\), the matter sign \(\phi_x\) is the frame
coordinate \(U_x\) of \cref{prop:geo-edge-twist-invariance}, the raw link
\(\psi_e\) is an independent link \(\Theta_e\) of
\cref{def:geo-graph-links} on the edge copy \(e\), the dressed sign \(z_e\)
is the per-edge twist of \cref{eq:geo-edge-twist}, which here is invariant
under every vertex and reference change because \(\mathbb Z_2\) is
abelian, and a plaquette character is
the link holonomy of \cref{eq:geo-link-holonomy} around an agent-network
four-cycle; the
straight-path channel then keeps the agents at the sites \(Bx\) and composes
links along paths. In the second, the sites are points of a discretized
base, \(\psi_e\) is the parallel transport of a \(\mathbb Z_2\) connection
along an edge curve, a plaquette character is a discrete curvature, and the
channel \(C_B\) is a scale map over a base map from the fine to the coarse
lattice, which is deferred base material of the kind treated in
\cref{ch:base-geometry,ch:neighborhood-parents}. Every
statement of this part holds under either reading, since none uses the
typing, and the unique raw lift of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-source-law} makes
the matter signs independent and uniform, so the law depends on them only
through the dressed signs. What is unproved is either identification. The
first requires a countably infinite agent set, whereas the interaction
multigraph of \cref{def:geo-graph-links} is finite, and it requires belief
and model channels, a recognition law, and an ELBO, none of which the
family carries. The second requires a declared base manifold, edge curves,
and a connection whose transports are the raw links. The question is
settled by declaring one reading and proving it: for the first, by
realizing the lattice law as the link marginal of a normalized joint with
belief and model channels on a countable agent set at \(c_*\) that satisfies
the countable-index projective and DLR hypotheses of
\cref{ch:agent-network-rg}, which would make this family an instance of the
infinite networks of \cref{ch:renormalization}; for the second, by
constructing the base discretization and a connection whose holonomies are
the plaquette characters and showing that \(C_B\) is a scale map over a
declared base map.

\cref{ch:thermodynamic-fisher-draft}, and the proof appendices

\section{The lattice law and its geometric channels}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-source}

\definitionheading{Dressed square-lattice source}{def:rg-spatial-source}
Let \(E=\mathbb Z^2\times\{1,2\}\), with \(e=(x,\iota)\) joining \(x\) to
\(x+e_\iota\), and retain the four distinct edge copies of every unit
plaquette. The matter sign is \(\phi_x\in\{-1,1\}\), the raw link is
\(\psi_e\in\{-1,1\}\), and the dressed sign is
\(z_e=\phi_x\psi_e\phi_{x+e_\iota}\). For a finite free edge set
\(\Lambda\) and fixed exterior dressed signs \(\eta\), define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 r_\Lambda^\eta(z)&=\prod_{e\in\Lambda}(1+bz_e)
 \prod_{p:p\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}(1+t\chi_p(z\eta)),\\
 Z_\Lambda^\eta&=2^{-|\Lambda|}\sum_zr_\Lambda^\eta(z),\qquad
 \varsigma_\Lambda^\eta(z)=\frac{2^{-|\Lambda|}r_\Lambda^\eta(z)}{Z_\Lambda^\eta},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-source-law}
\end{equation}
where \(\chi_S(z)=\prod_{e\in S}z_e\) is the character of a finite edge
set and \(z\eta\) agrees with \(z\) on \(\Lambda\) and with \(\eta\)
outside. All touching plaquettes, including crossing plaquettes, occur, and
the reference is normalized counting. The finite algebra allows
\(0\le b,t<1\); the theorems below impose smaller domains explicitly.
Periodic versions retain all sectors and require side at least three, with
distinct edge and plaquette copies.

\theoremheading{An infinite-volume source}{thm:rg-spatial-source-law}
For \(0<b<1\) and \(0<t<1/6\), the specification
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-source-law} has a unique dressed Gibbs law
\(\mu_{b,t}\), without a translation-invariance premise, and this law is
translation invariant. Every finite cylinder is the boundary-independent
limit of expanding boxes and of locally embedded full tori. If
\(\operatorname{dist}_\square(e,\Lambda^c)\) is co-plaquette graph distance, then, for
\(A\subset\Lambda\),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}((\varsigma_\Lambda^\eta)_A,(\varsigma_\Lambda^{\eta'})_A)
 \le\min\left\{1,\sum_{e\in A}(6t)^{\operatorname{dist}_\square(e,\Lambda^c)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-boundary-comparison}
\end{equation}
Independent uniform matter signs and \(\psi_e=\phi_xz_e\phi_{x+e_\iota}\)
give the unique Gibbs lift for the raw gauge--matter factors.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The proof in \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-measure} constructs a countably
additive law, as in the Kolmogorov extension theorem for a countable
product of finite spaces \citep{Kallenberg2021}, and establishes its DLR
equations separately from uniqueness. Uniqueness is Dobrushin's theorem for
an influence matrix with row sums at most \(6t<1\)
\citep{Dobrushin1968}\citep[Sec.~8.1]{Georgii1988}, and
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-boundary-comparison} is a boundary comparison of the
kind derived from that condition \citep[Ch.~8]{Georgii1988}.

\definitionheading{Straight-path blocking}{def:rg-spatial-path-channel}
For an integer \(B\ge1\), the deterministic channel \(C_B\) retains
\(\phi'_x=\phi_{Bx}\) and multiplies the raw links along
\(\mathcal P_B(x,\iota)=\{(Bx+ke_\iota,\iota):0\le k<B\}\). Its dressed
output is
\begin{equation}
 z'_{(x,\iota)}=\prod_{e\in\mathcal P_B(x,\iota)}z_e,
 \qquad \mu_B=(C_B)_*\mu_{b,t}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-path-channel}
\end{equation}
Every output lattice is identified with \(\mathbb Z^2\), and its cell
alphabet is \(\{-1,1\}^2\); this identification makes fixed-law
statements meaningful. On a torus of side \(L\), require \(B\mid L\) and
\(L/B\ge3\).

\propositionheading{Geometry and reference square of the path channel}{prop:rg-spatial-path-square}
Distinct retained paths have disjoint edge supports, although their
endpoints may coincide. The channel is gauge equivariant, pushes normalized
counting to normalized counting, and satisfies \(C_aC_B=C_{aB}\), including
\(C_2C_3=C_3C_2=C_6\). For any positive finite density \(f\), its coarse
density is \(\E_{\nu}[f\mid C_B]\), where \(\nu\) is the fine counting
reference. A coarse plaquette holonomy equals the product of all fine
plaquette holonomies in its square, and a retained winding Wilson loop
retains the corresponding fine winding loop. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Along a path every internal matter sign occurs twice and cancels. Distinct
axes distinguish edge copies, and along each grid line consecutive intervals
of length \(B\) partition its edges. Each prescribed parity has exactly half
the assignments on its path, so disjoint parities and the retained uniform
frames have the asserted reference. Concatenating intervals gives the
indices \(Bk+r\), once each from zero to \(aB-1\), which proves
composition. Interior edges occur twice in the plaquette product and
cancel. Conditional summation over counting fibers proves the density
formula and its composition without any change of reference.
\hfill\(\square\)

\propositionheading{Large blocks converge as full cylinder laws}{prop:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}
Put
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\tanh(h+2j)=\frac{b(1+t^2)+2t}{1+t^2+2bt}<1.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-rho}
\end{equation}
For any fixed \(0\le b,t<1\), any Gibbs law of the specification
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-source-law}, and any \(M\) distinct retained
\(B\)-paths, the law \(\mu_{B,M}\) of their parities satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(\mu_{B,M},\nu_M)
 \le\min\left\{1,\frac12\sum_{s=1}^M \binom Ms
              \rho^{\lceil sB/7\rceil}\right\},
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}
\end{equation}
where \(\nu_M\) is the fair product law. The same bound is uniform over
finite volumes and dressed boundaries when all counted path edges are free.
In the source uniqueness domain, fixed blocking commutes with the
thermodynamic cylinder limit. Both iterated limits and the joint box or
torus limit \(B_n\to\infty\), \(L_n/B_n\to\infty\), under the stated
embedding and divisibility conditions, yield the fair product law on each
fixed coarse cylinder. The raw limit has independent uniform matter and
links, and negative plaquette holonomy has probability \(1/2\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The single-edge conditional field has absolute value at most \(h+2j\). A
greedy independent subset of an \(sB\)-edge support has at least
\(\lceil sB/7\rceil\) members, because co-plaquette degree is at most six.
Conditional on its complement the selected signs are independent, and each
conditional mean has magnitude at most \(\rho\). This bounds every nonempty
Walsh moment, and Fourier inversion with the triangle inequality gives
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-cylinder-attraction}. The exponent is not sharp: no
plaquette contains two edges of one straight path, so for \(s=1\) the whole
path is independent and its parity mean is at most \(\rho^B\). A fixed
channel pulls a cylinder back to finitely many fine signs, so
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-boundary-comparison} gives the fixed-block passage. The
volume-uniform moment bound gives the increasing-block and joint passages
directly; it does not amplify a fixed-support boundary estimate to growing
support. Independent uniform frames preserve the total-variation bound,
and a nonempty plaquette character is fair at the product limit.
\hfill\(\square\)

\section{Every-exterior coarse laws and all generated interactions}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-generated}

\theoremheading{A common high-temperature domain at every depth}{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification}
Fix \(0<b<1\), \(0<t<1/12\), and choose \(\tau>0\) with
\(\mathrm q=12te^\tau<1\). Put \(\mathrm c_1=16\mathrm q/(1-\mathrm q)\) and
\(p_{\min}=(1-\rho)^2/4\). For every integer \(B\ge1\), \(\mu_B\) has a
unique positive, translation-covariant, continuous specification
\(\varsigma^{(B)}_A\) defined at every coarse exterior, and \(\mu_B\) is the
unique Gibbs law of that specification. For exterior patterns \(y,y'\)
agreeing in the radius-\(R\) neighborhood of a finite cell set \(A\),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}\bigl(\varsigma_A^{(B)}(\cdot\mid y),
                       \varsigma_A^{(B)}(\cdot\mid y')\bigr)
 \le\min\{1,\mathrm c_1|A|B^2(R+1)e^{-\tau(R+1)}\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-coarse-modulus}
\end{equation}
Each one-cell atom lies between \((1-\rho^B)^2/4\) and
\((1+\rho^B)^2/4\), hence is at least \(p_{\min}\). The same modulus
compares a whole-block finite conditional calculation against its matching
infinite kernel. These statements include infinite exterior prescriptions
of probability zero, constructed by normalized finite limits.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The proof in \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-constrained} uses fine Hamming
transport and column incidence rather than a block total-variation row
estimate, whose constant grows in proportion to \(Bt\). This is the source
of the block-size uniformity of the domain \(12t<1\).

\definitionheading{Canonical vacuum interaction}{def:rg-spatial-vacuum-potential}
Fix the reference cell state \(*=(1,1)\) and a translation-invariant
lexicographic order on cells. At the cell \(x\), let \(\eta^{x,r}\) use
\(\sigma_y\) for past cells \(y<x\) within distance \(r\), and \(*\)
elsewhere. Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf e_{x,r}^{(B)}(\sigma)=-\log
 \frac{\varsigma_x^{(B)}(\sigma_x\mid\eta^{x,r})}
      {\varsigma_x^{(B)}(*\mid\eta^{x,r})},\qquad
 A_{x,r}=\{x\}\cup\{y<x:|y-x|_\infty\le r\}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-odds}
\end{equation}
Set \(\mathscr U_{B,\{x\}}=\mathsf e_{x,0}^{(B)}\),
\(\mathscr U_{B,A_{x,r}}=\mathsf e_{x,r}^{(B)}-\mathsf e_{x,r-1}^{(B)}\) for
\(r\ge1\), and all other terms of \(\mathscr U_B\) to zero. This convention fixes
an interaction representative; it does not set the finite partition
function to one.

\theoremheading{Complete interaction and norm attraction}{thm:rg-spatial-generated-interaction}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-constrained-specification},
define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \mathrm c_2&=\frac4{1-\rho^2},\qquad \mathrm c_3=\frac{2\mathrm c_1}{p_{\min}},\qquad
 \lambda_v=\tau/8,\qquad z_v=e^{-\tau/4},\\
 \mathcal V_B&=\mathrm c_2\rho^B+\sqrt{2\mathrm c_2\mathrm c_3}B\rho^{B/2}
 \frac{z_v(9+14z_v+z_v^2)}{(1-z_v)^4}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-bound}
\end{equation}
Then the canonical interaction \(\mathscr U_B\) generates exactly
\(\varsigma^{(B)}\), and
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathscr U_B\|_{\lambda_v}
 :=\sum_{A\ni0}e^{\lambda_v\operatorname{diam}A}
                   \|\mathscr U_{B,A}\|_\infty
 \le\mathcal V_B\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{A\ni0}\operatorname{diam}(A)\|\mathscr U_{B,A}\|_\infty
 \le\mathcal V_B/\lambda_v.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-norm}
\end{equation}
No generated shell is removed. Canonical reconstruction after \(C_a\)
defines an integer-block action \(\mathscr U_B\mapsto\mathscr U_{aB}\) on this orbit,
which respects composition and fixes the zero interaction.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The proof and the explicit spatial tail are in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-potential}. The existence of some absolutely
summable interaction for a quasilocal, uniformly nonnull specification is
the Gibbs representation theorem, proved by \citet{Kozlov1974}; for a
finite single-site space nonnullity suffices, as observed by
\citet{Sullivan1973} and by Kozlov
\citep[Sec.~2.3.3, Thm.~2.12]{VanEnterFernandezSokal1993}. The canonical
interaction is one explicit
translation-invariant representative, with the displayed exponential bound.
Its norm is a cell-interaction norm; the character norm introduced next is
a different topology (\cref{prop:rg-spatial-norm-obstruction}), and entry
into its ball requires the separate argument of
\cref{thm:rg-spatial-basin-entrance}.

\section{An RG map on an open interaction ball}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-open-ball}

\definitionheading{Consolidated character coordinates}{def:rg-spatial-character-space}
Let \(J=(J_S)\) be a translation-invariant real coefficient family over
nonempty finite edge supports, with exponential density convention
\(\exp(\sum_SJ_S\chi_S)\). Its finite conditional energies retain all terms
touching the free set. For \(\kappa,\lambda>0\), use the Banach norm
\begin{equation}
 \|J\|_{\kappa,\lambda}
 =\sup_e\sum_{S\ni e}|S|e^{\kappa|S|+\lambda\operatorname{diam}S}|J_S|.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm}
\end{equation}
Diameters use fine-edge basepoints and Chebyshev distance. The empty
character is a separate scalar; it is absent from these coordinates, while
every finite law retains its own normalizer.

\theoremheading{Same-norm differentiability, remainder, and composition}{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}
For \(a\in\{2,3,6\}\), put
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_a&=\kappa(1-1/a),\qquad
 \mathcal K_a=\frac{8ae^{\lambda(1-1/a)}}{\delta_a},\\
 \mathfrak c_a&=a^{-1}e^{-\kappa(a-1)},\qquad M_a=11\mathcal K_a/a,\\
 \varepsilon&=\min_a\left\{\frac1{48},\frac1{2\mathcal K_a},
                              \frac{1-\mathfrak c_a}{2M_a}\right\}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-local-radius}
\end{equation}
On the open \(\varepsilon\)-ball, the path push of the unique Gibbs law has
exactly one scalar-free representative \(\mathscr R_aJ\) in the same ball,
and its specification is continuous at every exterior. The map
\(\mathscr R_a\) is Fr\'echet \(C^1\), and, writing
\(L_a=D\mathscr R_a(0)\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 (L_aJ)_T&=J_{\operatorname{lift}_a(T)},\qquad \|L_a\|\le\mathfrak c_a<1,\\
 \|\mathscr R_aJ-L_aJ\|&\le M_a\|J\|^2,\qquad
 \|D\mathscr R_a(J)-L_a\|\le2M_a\|J\|.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-local-estimates}
\end{equation}
On the ball \(\|\mathscr R_aJ\|\le\frac{1+\mathfrak c_a}2\|J\|\), so zero
is the only fixed point of \(\mathscr R_a\) there, and
\(\mathscr R_2\mathscr R_3=\mathscr R_3\mathscr R_2=\mathscr R_6\). There is
no area multiplier in the coefficient map. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The coefficient construction and the law passage are proved in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-operator}; the cumulant step is the bounded
dependency-graph inequality of \citet[Sec.~9.4.2, Eq.~(47)]{feray2016modphi}.
The classical precedent is Israel's construction of decimation and Kadanoff
maps at high temperature on a Banach space of absolutely summable
interactions \citep{Israel1981}\citep[Sec.~6.1.1]{VanEnterFernandezSokal1993}.
Since \(\|L_a\|<1\), the linearization at zero contracts every direction;
interacting fixed points and scaling exponents lie outside this construction
(\cref{open:rg-spatial-generality}), and the asymptotic apparatus of this
supplement is in \cref{ch:renormalization}.

\propositionheading{The two interaction norms are not interchangeable}{prop:rg-spatial-norm-obstruction}
Arbitrarily small vacuum-vanishing interactions in the diameter norm can
have infinite norm \cref{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm}. Thus
\cref{thm:rg-spatial-generated-interaction} alone does not imply entry into
the ball of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

For interaction spaces weighted by support cardinality,
\citet[Sec.~2.6.7]{VanEnterFernandezSokal1993} survey the corresponding
dichotomy: an exponential weight gives analyticity of the pressure near
zero interaction, whereas a weight not bounded below by an exponential
admits complex zeros of finite-volume partition functions arbitrarily close
to every real interaction. The factor \(e^{\kappa|S|}\) in
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm} is of the exponential kind.

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
On an \(R\times R\) cell square put a translated term
\(-a_R(z_{x_R,1}-1)\prod_{x\ne x_R}z_{x,1}\), anchored at its maximal cell,
with \(a_R=\eta_0e^{-2\lambda R}/R^4\), \(\eta_0>0\), and \(R\ge2\). Its
diameter-norm sum is at most \(2\eta_0\sum_{R\ge2}e^{-\lambda(R+1)}/R^2\),
which is arbitrarily small with \(\eta_0\). Its full-square character has
coefficient \(a_R\), distinct from every removed-corner character, so it
cannot cancel. Its contribution at a horizontal edge to
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-character-norm} is
\(\eta_0e^{\kappa R^2-\lambda R-\lambda}\), which does not tend to zero,
while the ordinary anchored absolute coefficient sums still converge.
\hfill\(\square\)

\theoremheading{Complete finite-amplitude approximation}{thm:rg-spatial-finite-amplitude}
Let \(J\) lie in the ball of \cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator} and have
finitely many nonzero translation orbits. For every order \(\bar n\), the
Taylor polynomial \(\widehat J_{\bar n}\) of \(\mathscr R_aJ\), the sum of
its terms of orders one through \(\bar n\), has finitely many nonzero
orbits and contains every support generated through order \(\bar n\); its
coefficients are obtained by finitely many exact rational operations and
are rational when those of \(J\) are. With
\(\omega=\mathcal K_a\|J\|<1\), its error satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathscr R_aJ-\widehat J_{\bar n}\|
 \le\frac{\|J\|}{a}
       \frac{\omega^{\bar n}[(\bar n+1)-\bar n\omega]}{(1-\omega)^2},
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-taylor-tail}
\end{equation}
which tends to zero as \(\bar n\to\infty\). For a finite word
\(a_1,a_2,\ldots\), choose one radius \(r<\varepsilon\); then
\(\operatorname{Lip}_a(r)=\mathfrak c_a+2M_ar<1\) is a Lipschitz constant
of \(\mathscr R_a\) on the \(r\)-ball, and if every approximate input
remains in that ball, the errors obey
\(e_{k+1}\le\operatorname{Lip}_{a_k}(r)e_k+e'_k\), where \(e'_k\) is the
Taylor error of step \(k\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

The proof in \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-finite-amplitude} counts all
translations, repeated source occurrences, and omitted orders.

\theoremheading{Constructive entry of the spatial source into any prescribed norm}{thm:rg-spatial-basin-entrance}
Fix a compact box \(0<b_-\le b\le b_+<1\), \(0<t_-\le t\le t_+<1/12\) and
\(\kappa,\lambda>0\), all rational. There is an integer \(B_0\), determined
by the explicit gates of \cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-basin} and found by a
finite rational search, such that for every integer \(B\ge B_0\) the law
\(\mu_B\) has a unique translation-invariant scalar-free character
representative \(J^{(B)}\) in the open ball of
\cref{thm:rg-spatial-local-operator}, with a norm bound that decays
exponentially in \(B\) uniformly on the box. For such \(B\),
\(\mathscr R_aJ^{(B)}=J^{(aB)}\). For rational source parameters, a finite
rational library approximates \(J^{(B)}\) to any prescribed positive error
in the norm \(\|\cdot\|_{\kappa,\lambda}\). If an input has error \(\delta\)
and a finite-word Taylor approximation has error \(e\), with both exact
trajectories in one ball of radius \(r<\varepsilon\), the total error after
the word is at most \(e+\delta\prod_k\operatorname{Lip}_{a_k}(r)\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The construction and its constants are given in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-basin}. The approximation concerns the
interaction of the path-pushed law; scalar-free coefficients determine
neither a discarded partition scalar nor an ELBO.

\section{Regular statistical geometry at fixed depths}
\label{sec:rg-spatial-fisher}

\theoremheading{Fisher density and regular finite hierarchies}{thm:rg-spatial-fisher-density}
In the natural parameters \((h,j)\), fix a compact rectangle in \(h>0\),
\(0<j<\operatorname{atanh}(1/12)\), and an integer \(B\). The coarse finite
marginals and every constrained local row are \(C^3\) functions. The
canonical interaction is \(C^3\) in one exponential diameter norm, and its
derivatives through order three tend to zero as \(B\) increases, uniformly
on each such compact rectangle. Let \(I_{B,\Lambda_L}\) be the Fisher
matrix of the \(L\)-square marginal, and
\(I_{\mathrm{cell},B}(h,j\mid\mathrm{past})\) the predictive row Fisher
matrix with its complete past fixed and its future integrated. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_B(h,j)=\E_{\mu_B}I_{\mathrm{cell},B}(h,j\mid\mathrm{past})
 =\lim_{L\to\infty}L^{-2}I_{B,\Lambda_L}(h,j)
 \quad\text{in }C^2\text{ on compact parameter sets}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density}
\end{equation}
For each fixed depth, and for every prescribed finite list of depths, there
is a nonempty open region with positive \(j\) on which these densities are
positive-definite metrics. Their Levi--Civita connections are \(C^1\),
their curvatures are continuous, and the connections and curvatures of the
area-normalized finite-marginal metrics converge to them on compact regular
subcharts. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The response, moving-expectation, and positive-floor arguments are in
\cref{sec:rg-spatial-proof-fisher}. The predictive row in
\cref{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density} integrates its future, whereas the
canonical interaction row of \cref{eq:rg-spatial-vacuum-odds} fixes its
future to \(*\); substituting the latter for the former would change the
Fisher-density construction.

\propositionheading{Area-qualified information contraction}{prop:rg-spatial-fisher-contraction}
For integer blocking by \(a\),
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{aB}\preceq a^2\mathcal I_B,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal I_{aB}}{(aB)^2}\preceq\frac{\mathcal I_B}{B^2}.
 \label{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-contraction}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
By \cref{prop:rg-spatial-path-square}, \(\mu_{aB}=(C_a)_*\mu_B\), and a
coarse \(L\)-square is the image under a parameter-independent
deterministic map of the fine \((aL+1)\)-square marginal, which contains
every path it uses. The finite marginals are positive \(C^3\) families, hence
differentiable in quadratic mean, so by \cref{thm:cg-fisher-contraction}
the Fisher matrix of the image is at most that of the fine marginal, the
defect being the expected conditional covariance of the fine score. Divide by \(L^2\). The
fixed-depth marginal limit in \cref{eq:rg-spatial-fisher-density}, together
with \((aL+1)^2/L^2\to a^2\), gives the factor \(a^2\), and dividing by the
fine-cell area \((aB)^2\) gives the second inequality. \hfill\(\square\)

\openproblemheading{Limits beyond the admitted high-temperature construction}{open:rg-spatial-generality}
The constructions of this part hold on the high-temperature domain
\(0<t<1/12\), at fixed blocking depth, with Fisher floors on finitely many
depths and every blocking word fixed before volume limits. They do not
supply an interacting critical fixed point, blocking-independent critical
exponents, a continuum gauge-field measure, or a continuous-scale generator;
the finite-depth Fisher floors may vanish as depth increases, so neither a
regular Fisher inverse nor a curvature limit uniform in unbounded depth is
available; unrestricted simultaneous volume and depth limits of the full
labeled experiment of \cref{ch:thermodynamic-fisher-draft} are not
controlled; and a Fisher-curvature action computed from the same unknown
law that it reweights is not shown to have a solution that exists, is
unique, is normalizable, or is regular. Settling these requires, in the
same order, new parameter domains with the corresponding tightness,
interaction, and scaling estimates; positive Fisher floors uniform in
depth; joint volume and depth estimates for the labeled experiment; and an
existence and regularity theorem for the self-consistent normalized law,
whose curvature term needs its own normalized generative model and scalar
normalizer.
