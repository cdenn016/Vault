### L09-3: The stated reason for reporting no uncertainty is incorrect, and the b = -1 question depends on weighting and leverage
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:61 (abstract), 98, 1306-1315, 2180, 2189.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The manuscript says "Raw per-seed records are unavailable, so no resampling interval or hypothesis test is reported" (abstract) and "Because the per-seed values are unavailable, the analysis performs no bootstrap, nested test, significance test" (2180). A nested test of b = -1 needs only the eleven means. Output of `L09_scaling_recon.py`:
- Three-parameter unweighted fit: a = 1805.5529, b = -1.048919, c = 61.16989, R^2 = 0.99981995, an exact match to lines 1310-1313. The asymptotic standard errors are SE(a) = 60.8, SE(b) = 0.0158, and SE(c) = 0.80, with corr(b, c) = -0.935.
- Nested fit with b = -1: a = 1629.1 and c = 58.74. RSS rises from 3.498 to 7.752, giving F(1,8) = 9.727 and p = 0.0143. The value requested by the panel is p(F(1,8) >= 9.73) = 0.01424. The degrees of freedom are right: 11 means minus 3 parameters leaves 8 residual df, and the restriction removes 1 parameter. This reproduces the historical F(1,8) = 9.73, which was therefore an aggregate-level lack-of-fit test, not a per-seed test.
- The rejection is not robust. The SE-weighted fit (SE = SD/sqrt(n)) gives b = -1.020 +/- 0.020 with chi^2/dof = 1.62, so b = -1 lies within one standard error. Leave-one-K-out values of b range over [-1.0552, -0.9619]. Dropping K_q = 10 alone moves b to -0.962 and c to 58.4. A parametric seed-level bootstrap from the stored SDs gives a 95% interval of [-1.100, -0.997].
- Why the historical F-test rejected b = -1 while the bootstrap interval contained it: the F-test estimates the noise from the lack-of-fit residual variance of the means, s = sqrt(RSS/8) = 0.66 PPL, and treats it as the same at every K_q. It therefore reads the mid-K_q residuals (+0.74 at K_q = 40 and -1.06 at K_q = 20, each 2 to 3 standard errors) as evidence against b = -1. A seed-level bootstrap instead propagates the per-K_q seed scatter, which is heteroscedastic (SD 3.74 at K_q = 10 against 0.46 at K_q = 50), and K_q = 10 has the largest leverage on b. Different resampling units and different implied weights give different answers. With n = 3 per K_q, each SD is known only to within a factor of about two, so no choice of weighting is secure.

The sentence "The point estimate is close to an inverse-$K_q$ dependence over this grid" (1315) is thus neither supported nor refuted: b lies 3.1 unweighted SE from -1 but only 1.0 weighted SE from -1. Printing b to seven significant figures (-1.048919) and R^2 to eight is false precision against a statistical uncertainty of order 0.02.
**Fix:** Replace the "no test is possible" rationale with the real one and report the fit with an uncertainty: "Treating the eleven means as the data, the unweighted fit gives $b=-1.05\pm0.02$ (asymptotic standard error); weighting by the stored standard errors gives $b=-1.02\pm0.02$, and omitting the $K_q=10$ point gives $b=-0.96$. Whether $b$ differs from $-1$ therefore depends on the weighting and on the smallest dimension, and we make no claim either way." Round a, c, and R^2 to three significant figures in the abstract, or drop the coefficients from the abstract entirely (see the physics-audience notes).
**Falsifies:** Recovered per-seed data showing a seed-resampled interval for b that excludes -1 under every reasonable weighting and with K_q = 10 omitted.
**Status:** E, descriptive. The current wording understates what the table supports and misstates why. "Close to inverse-K_q" is S at best.

### L09-4: The schedule confound is disclosed only in Scope, contradicts the Methods description of the table, and overstates its effect on c
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:126, against 1306, 1315, 1319 (caption), 2178, and 61.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** From the table, line 126 derives "30,000 optimizer steps at 4,096 tokens per step for $K_q \le 80$, against 60,000 steps at 2,048 tokens per step for $K_q \ge 90$, with $K_q = 30$ anomalous at 22,500 steps and 5,461.33 tokens per step", and concludes "The reported trend consequently cannot be attributed to capacity". The arithmetic holds: all three products equal 122,880,000 (script output). But Methods line 2178 says the table does not preserve "batch schedules", and line 1306 says the table "cannot support ... reconstruction of the training procedure". The Results section, the figure caption, and the abstract describe the sweep only as "iso-token" and never mention the regime change. A reader of Section 1304-1324 alone does not learn that the three largest dimensions ran a different optimizer schedule. Nor does that reader learn that K_q = 30, the point with the second-largest SD (2.35), ran yet another schedule. Separately, the claim that the K_q >= 90 points "are precisely the points that fix the asymptote $c$" is not borne out. Fitting K_q <= 80 alone gives c = 62.5 +/- 1.1 and b = -1.069 +/- 0.019, against c = 61.2 +/- 0.8 on all points. That is a shift of about 1.3 PPL, roughly one standard error. The leave-one-out table shows that K_q = 10, not the large-K_q rows, is the most influential point for both b and c.
**Fix:** Move the schedule disclosure into Section `sec:scaling_validation`, and add one clause to the caption and the abstract ("iso-token but not iso-step: dimensions $K_q\ge90$ used twice the steps at half the batch"). Reconcile 2178 with 126: the table evidently records per-row compute, from which the steps and tokens per step follow. Replace "precisely the points that fix the asymptote" with the measured sensitivity: "refitting on $K_q\le80$ alone moves $c$ from 61.2 to 62.5 and $b$ from $-1.05$ to $-1.07$."
**Falsifies:** Per-run configurations showing identical schedules across K_q, or a demonstration that the step and batch values at 126 came from a field other than the one 2178 says is absent.
**Status:** E. Line 126 is a correct confound disclosure with one overstated sub-claim. Its omission from Results is a defect for a reader of that section alone.

### L09-6: The prospective multi-scale protocol is not decision-grade and does not say which formation rule it tests
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1732-1750 (`sec:participatory`), 2162-2172 (`sec:methods_metagent`), 1741 and 2168 (`eq:prospective_population_identity`).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The protocol fixes "at least three seeds" and refutes the claim "if the qualitative organization does not reproduce across the declared seeds". It names no primary statistic, no persistence window length, no minimum cluster size, no effect size, and no decision rule for "reproduce". The text requires these to be predeclared but does not declare them, so this is a template for a preregistration, not a preregistration. With three seeds and a qualitative criterion, a single aberrant seed decides the outcome. The population identity $N_{\mathrm{total}}(t)=\sum_s N_s(t)$ is a bookkeeping definition: its failure refutes the accounting of the software, not the tower hypothesis. Yet it is listed among the scientific refutation criteria at 1748 and 2172. The protocol and the identity are written out twice (1736-1748 and 2164-2172). The protocol does not state which rule a future run would validate. At 2166-2169 the text cites "Eq.~\eqref{eq:prospective_population_identity} below" immediately before that display, and the display ends in a comma followed by a capitalized sentence.
**Fix:** Keep one copy, in Methods, and state it as a numbered list of predeclared quantities with values: the statistic, the window, the threshold, the seed count with a justification, and a pass rule such as "all seeds" or "at least k of n". Move the accounting identity to a software-validity precondition rather than a refutation criterion. Add one sentence naming the formation rule tested and pointing to the marked-parent construction of the Theory as its rigorous successor. For a physics journal, consider moving the whole protocol to the MAgent companion or to supplementary material, leaving one paragraph plus fence F2.
**Falsifies:** Predeclared numeric values located elsewhere in the manuscript or in a cited preregistration.
**Status:** C (prospective, no result). The text does not inflate it, but the refutation criteria are not operational.

## Physics-audience and standalone notes

The manuscript itself says that the language-model experiments "test the architecture and not the participatory reading" (line 102). The thesis of the paper is a formal realization of the participatory "it from bit" of Wheeler, so for a physics referee the WikiText-103 sweep carries no evidential weight for that thesis. The abstract spends four sentences on fit coefficients (a to four decimals, b to six, R^2 to five), which will read as belonging to a machine-learning venue.

I recommend three cuts. First, reduce the sweep sentences of the abstract to one clause: "the general architecture trains as a working language model; its descriptive behavior is reported in the companion". Second, reduce Section `sec:scaling_validation` to two or three sentences in the Discussion, or to a short appendix carrying the eleven-row table. Third, cite the ML companion that owns these runs (the GL(K) attention manuscript in `Research/manuscripts`, or the vfe3 paper) for the full sweep, the schedule confound, and the statistics of L09-3.

If the section is kept, a physicist will need perplexity defined (L09-5) and will expect a fitted exponent reported with an uncertainty and no more than two significant figures. That reader will also ask why a power law with offset is the model. Nothing in the manuscript predicts the functional form or the exponent, so the fit is a phenomenological summary and should be called one.

"Iso-token", "optimizer step", "batch", "layer normalization", "output projection", "MLP", and "activation" are undefined for this audience. If the section is condensed, all of them can go. The keyword "Transformer architectures" (line 66) signals the wrong venue for the stated goal of the paper.

The prospective protocol (1732-1750 and 2162-2172) is also unusual for a physics journal: it is a two-page statement, written twice, of an experiment not yet run. The standalone reader would be better served by one paragraph naming the predicted observable (persistent multi-child clusters under an active, untied slow channel) and its refutation, with the operational details in supplementary material.
