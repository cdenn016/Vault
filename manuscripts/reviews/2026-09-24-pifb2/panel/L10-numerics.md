## Findings

### L10-1: The existence hypothesis of the frozen-attention covariance fixed point is gauge dependent; for the transports of the manuscript it is always satisfiable, and with holonomy there is a real collapse obstruction the text does not state
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2757-2770, proposition "Fixed point of the frozen-attention covariance map" and its proof sketch (`eq:sigma_fixed_point_map`).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The proposition assumes lambda_max(C_i) < 2 with C_i = sum_j beta_ij (Omega_ij Omega_ij^T)^{-1}, and the proof calls this "a quantitative conditioning bound on the links" for GL+ transports. The map T is gauge covariant (Sigma_i -> g_i Sigma_i g_i^T, Omega_ij -> g_i Omega_ij g_j^{-1}, Sigma_p,i and Lambda_o,i transformed accordingly), but C_i is not: it picks up g_j^{-1} g_j^{-T}. For the vertex-local transports of `eq:transport_def`, Omega_ij = U_i U_j^{-1} with U_i = exp(phi_i), the gauge g_i = U_i^{-1} sets every link to the identity and C_i = I. `L10_appD_E.py` part (1): with |phi_i|_F = 1.5 in GL+(3), lambda_max(C_i) = (0.851, 25.0, 2.69) in the given gauge, so the proposition does not apply as written; the iteration from 2 Sigma_p nonetheless converges (fixed-point residual 8.0e-15, Thompson contraction ratio 0.50 per step), after the gauge change lambda_max(C_i) = (1, 1, 1), and the two fixed points agree under congruence to 6.1e-16. With nontrivial loop holonomy (independent links, allowed at one base point) the obstruction is genuine and the text is silent on it. Part (2), K = 1, N = 2, beta = 1, prior precision 1, links w = Omega_12, v = Omega_21, holonomy h = wv: the composite map T_1 o T_2 is concave increasing with slope 4h^2 at zero, so for h <= 1/2 the only nonnegative fixed point is Sigma = 0. Measured: h = 0.36 gives Sigma = 3.0e-15 after 100 steps and underflow to 0 by 2000; h = 0.45 gives 3.9e-93 after 2000; h = 0.5 decays algebraically (6.7e-4 at 2000); the flat case w = 0.1, v = 10 (h = 1, lambda_max(C_1) = 100) converges to (2.94e-2, 1.49). In every gauge lambda_max(C_1) lambda_max(C_2) = h^{-2}, so the stated hypothesis correctly excludes h < 1/2 but also excludes flat configurations it should admit.
**Fix:** Replace the hypothesis by "assume there exist frames g_i in which the transformed links satisfy lambda_max(sum_j beta_ij (g_i Omega_ij g_j^{-1} g_j^{-T} Omega_ij^T g_i^T)^{-1}) < 2", and add after the proof: "For links of the vertex-local form Omega_ij = exp(phi_i)exp(-phi_j), or more generally graph-flat links, the frames g_i = exp(-phi_i) make every link the identity, so the fixed point exists, is unique, and is reached geometrically for every frame configuration. With loop holonomy the covariance can collapse: for two agents in GL+(1) with holonomy h, no SPD fixed point exists when h <= 1/2." Delete "for general GL+ transports it is a quantitative conditioning bound on the links".
**Falsifies:** A flat (cocycle) configuration for which the iteration fails to converge to an SPD fixed point, or a K = 1 two-agent example with h < 1/2 whose iteration converges to a positive limit.
**Status:** The proposition is P as a sufficient condition (the Thompson-contraction step with lambda = max m_i/(a_i + m_i) checks by weighted AM-GM, and the numerics agree). The "conditioning bound on the links" reading is S and misdirects; the holonomy obstruction is an omitted D-level statement.

### L10-2: The displayed frame update is the normal-equations form of a square solve; it squares the condition number, costs O(K^6) per agent, and contradicts the complexity paragraph
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2497-2498 (complete update equations), 2506 (Computational Complexity), with `eq:gauge_natural_gradient_def` and `eq:pullback_metric` at 976-990 and the telescoping sentence at 1004.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The update forms zeta = G_pull^{-1} grad_phi F with G_pull = J^T J, where J = Dexp_phi is a K^2 x K^2 matrix, then xi = dexp^L(zeta). Line 1004 already proves xi = U^{-1} grad_U F exactly (L03-6 verified the identity; I confirm it to 4.1e-15, and the Psi_R form of `eq:pullback_metric` equals J^T J to 1.0e-14 in `L10_pullback_cond.py`). As a numerical recipe the displayed route is the classical normal-equations detour: cond(G_pull) = cond(J)^2. Measured medians over 30 draws per scale in gl(3): |phi|_F = 1, 3, 5 give cond(J) = 2.7, 15.5, 106 and cond(G_pull) = 7.2, 242, 1.1e4. In fp32 against a float64 reference the relative error of xi is 1.4e-7, 6.1e-7, 9.0e-6 (median; max 2.8e-4 at |phi|_F = 5) for the G_pull route and 4.5e-8, 6.3e-8, 1.7e-7 (max 3.0e-6) for xi = U^{-1} grad_U F. On the dexp-singular locus (see L10-3) G_pull becomes singular (cond 3.0e17 at a skew frame of angle pi) while cond(U) = 1.000, so the direct form stays defined where the displayed one fails. Cost: forming J takes K^2 Frechet derivatives at O(K^3) each and the solve is O(K^6); wall time per agent is 1.66 s at K = 16 (256 x 256 system) against 1.1e-5 s for the direct solve, with agreement 3.7e-15. Line 2506 says "the natural-gradient projections and exponential-map retractions each cost O(K^3) per agent", which holds for the direct form, not for the displayed one.
**Fix:** Replace the two lines at 2497-2498 by "xi_i^t = (U_i^t)^{-1} grad_{U_i} F_red, equivalently dexp^L applied to the pullback-preconditioned chart vector (Eq. 1004)", and add to 2506: "Assembling and inverting the K_q^2 x K_q^2 pullback metric explicitly would cost O(K_q^6) per agent and square its condition number; the direct form avoids both."
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the implementation or the intended pure path never forms G_pull explicitly (then only the display and 2506 wording change), or a regime in which G_pull is better conditioned than U.
**Status:** The identity is P; the complexity statement is incorrect for the displayed recipe.

### L10-3: In the gl(K) chart the dexp-singular locus for a skew frame sits at rotation angle pi, not at the "familiar 2 pi rotation locus"
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:999 (sentence after `prop:dexp_regularity`), used by the frame update at 2497-2498; the frames are gl(K) coordinates throughout (2375, 2639: skew 7 x 7 initialization evolved with gl(7) increments).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The proposition (singular iff spec(ad_phi) meets 2 pi i Z minus 0) and the guard "clip ||ad_phi|| below 2 pi" are correct. The following gloss, "in the standard so(3) rotation-vector normalization this reduces to the familiar 2 pi rotation locus", holds only when ad_phi acts on so(3). On gl(3), for skew phi = theta G_z the eigenvalues of phi are 0, +i theta, -i theta, so ad_phi has eigenvalues +-2 i theta, and dexp is singular already at theta = pi. `L10_pullback_cond.py` and its inline probe: in the gl(3) chart sigma_min(J) = 1.5e-16 and cond(J) = 6.7e15 at theta = pi, cond(J) = 3.1e4 at theta = pi -+ 1e-4; in the so(3)-restricted chart sigma_min stays 0.90 at theta = pi and first vanishes near 2 pi (2.3e-5 at 2 pi - 1e-4). The guard ||ad_phi||_2 < 2 pi therefore stops a skew frame at theta < pi in the chart the manuscript uses. A reader who takes the 2 pi gloss at face value will believe rotations up to 2 pi are regular.
**Fix:** Replace the gloss with: "On the full gl(K_q) chart a skew frame of rotation angle theta has ad-eigenvalues +-2 i theta, so the singular locus is theta = pi; the familiar 2 pi locus applies only to the chart restricted to so(3)."
**Falsifies:** A computation showing J = Dexp_phi on gl(3) is regular at phi = pi G_z.
**Status:** The proposition is P; the gloss is a false specialization.

### L10-4: The numerical-stability paragraph names the wrong controlling quantity, misattributes fp32 loss of positive-definiteness, and misstates the Cholesky error bound
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2510 ("Positive-Definiteness" paragraph); also 2613 (KL formula used in fp32 practice).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** (a) Omitted conditioning source. The paragraph controls only kappa(Sigma_i). Every pair term inverts A = Omega_ij Sigma_j Omega_ij^T with kappa(A) <= kappa(Omega)^2 kappa(Sigma_j), and kappa(Omega) grows exponentially in the frame norms. `L10_transport_cond.py` (K = 5, 200 draws per scale, kappa(Sigma) about 10): for |phi|_F = 1, 3, 4, 5 per frame the median kappa(Omega) is 3.7, 60, 202, 1.0e3, the median kappa(A) is 40, 6.6e3, 8.0e4, 1.7e6, and the fp32 relative error of the transported KL (Cholesky route, float64 reference) is 2.0e-7, 2.1e-5, 2.2e-4, 6.6e-3 at the median and 7.3e-7, 2.7e-4, 3.7e-3, 0.15 at the 90th percentile. The 1 percent crossover at the 90th percentile lies between |phi|_F = 4 and 5, with well-conditioned Sigma throughout. (b) Storage, not the eigendecomposition. The paragraph says "the eigendecomposition underlying the retraction can lose positive-definiteness". `L10_retraction_fp32.py` (K = 8, 200 random rotations per kappa) shows that the fraction of fp32-rounded matrices that are already indefinite (eigenvalues of the stored matrix computed in float64) equals the fraction for which fp32 `ssyevd` returns lambda_min <= 0, at every kappa: 0 up to 3e7, 0.020 at 1e8, 0.335 at 1e9, 0.95 at 1e12. A float64 eigendecomposition would not help; only the eigenvalue floor (or storing a factor) does. The onset lies between 3e7 and 1e8, consistent with the stated order 1/u_fp32 = 1.68e7. Diagonal Sigma never fails at any kappa tested. (c) Cholesky. "Whose backward error grows with kappa(Sigma_i)" is wrong: the computed factor satisfies A + dA = R^T R with ||dA||_2 <= c_n u ||A||_2 (Higham, "What Is a Cholesky Factorization?", 2020 blog summarizing Higham 2002 Ch. 10), independent of kappa. Measured fp32 backward error 3.6e-8 to 4.5e-8 median for kappa = 10 to 1e6. What depends on conditioning is completion and forward error, and completion depends on the diagonally scaled matrix: fp32 `spotrf` succeeds on diag(1, 1e-12) (kappa = 1e12) and on D C D with kappa(Sigma) = 1.1e16 and kappa(C) = 3.5, with forward error 3.7e-12, while rotated spectra fail at rates 0.11 (1e8) and 0.49 (1e9).
**Fix:** Rewrite 2510 as: "In exact arithmetic the retraction stays in the cone. In single precision the stored matrices themselves can be indefinite once the relevant condition number exceeds about u^{-1} = 1.68e7, and this applies to the transported covariances Omega_ij Sigma_j Omega_ij^T as well as to Sigma_i; since kappa(Omega_ij Sigma_j Omega_ij^T) <= kappa(Omega_ij)^2 kappa(Sigma_j) and kappa(Omega_ij) grows exponentially with the frame norms, a frame-norm bound is needed alongside the eigenvalue floor. Cholesky factorization is backward stable independent of conditioning; it runs to completion when the diagonally scaled matrix has condition number safely below u^{-1} [Higham 2002, Ch. 10]." Report the frame-norm cap the implementation uses, if any.
**Falsifies:** A precision budget in the implementation that keeps kappa(Omega_ij) small (a frame-norm cap with a stated value), or fp32 runs where the transported KL stays accurate at |phi|_F >= 5.
**Status:** The fp32 roundoff constants are P; the causal claims of the paragraph are partly false; the transport conditioning is an omitted D-level statement.

### L10-5: The Cholesky-Euclidean default and the affine-invariant retraction are stationarity equivalent but not stability equivalent; the stable step of the default shrinks with lambda_min(Sigma*)
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2655-2660 ("This approach proved essential for numerical stability"; "share stationary points ... but not trajectories"), 2502 (timescale ratios), 2855 (Euclidean gradient flow in Appendix D).
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** `L10_step_stability.py` linearizes each update at the frozen-attention fixed point Sigma* = 2 M^{-1} (K = 3, rotated spectra) and bisects for the largest step with spectral radius below one. For kappa(Sigma*) = 1, 1e1, 1e2, 1e3, 1e4 (lambda_min = 1 to 1e-4): the affine-invariant retraction gives eta_max = 1.0000 at every kappa (analytically, the whitened update is multiplication by 1 - 2 eta); Euclidean descent on Sigma gives 2.0, 2.0e-2, 2.0e-4, 2.0e-6, 2.0e-8 (= 2 lambda_min^2); Euclidean descent on the Cholesky factor gives 0.50, 6.9e-2, 8.7e-3, 9.6e-4, 9.9e-5 (tending to lambda_min). "Essential for numerical stability" is an empirical claim with no data shown, and in step-size terms the ordering is the reverse of what the sentence suggests. Under the default the covariance relaxation rate is conditioning dependent, so a fixed ratio eta_mu : eta_s : eta_phi does not fix the physical timescale ratio of the adiabatic hierarchy. The chain rule itself is right (see Verified).
**Fix:** Add to 2660: "The two schemes also differ in discrete stability: at a fixed point the retraction is stable for eta < 1 independent of conditioning, whereas a Euclidean step on L_i requires eta below about lambda_min(Sigma_i*). Positive-definiteness, not step-size stability, is what the Cholesky parametrization guarantees." Qualify "proved essential" as an implementation observation or support it with data.
**Falsifies:** A default configuration with a conditioning-adaptive step on L_i, or runs showing the retraction path unstable where the Cholesky path is stable.
**Status:** E claim without data; the stationarity equivalence is P (for softplus; see L02-9 for absolute value).

### L10-6: Two first-order statements about the frame chart are misstated
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2512 ("given to first order in xi = log g by the additive shift phi_i -> phi_i + xi, with Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff corrections of order (1/2)[phi_i, xi]"); 1004 ("the additive chart truncation ... is exact in the abelian sector and near-identity to first order, while noncommuting finite-frame updates require the dexp^L conversion").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** (a) log(e^phi e^{t xi}) = phi + t (ad_phi/(1 - e^{-ad_phi}))(xi) + O(t^2) = phi + t (xi + (1/2)[phi, xi] + (1/12)[phi,[phi, xi]] + ...) + O(t^2). The (1/2)[phi, xi] correction is itself first order in xi, so the additive shift is the first-order action only when phi is also small. `L10_bch_shift.py` with |xi| = 1: the deviation of the exact first-order shift from xi is 3.5e-3, 3.2e-2, 6.6e-2, 0.39 at |phi|_F = 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1, and it matches the series through the double commutator to 4.7e-10, 1.7e-8, 5.3e-6, 8.6e-4. (b) The chart step exp(phi - eta v) and the group step U exp(-eta dexp^L_phi(v)) agree to O(eta^2) at every phi, by definition of dexp. `L10_pullback_cond.py` at |phi|_F = 3: |difference|/eta^2 = 2.14, 2.03, 2.02 for eta = 1e-1, 1e-2, 1e-3. The conversion is needed to insert a chart vector into the group retraction, not to make the chart step first-order correct away from the identity.
**Fix:** At 2512 write "to first order in both phi_i and xi"; or give the exact first-order action phi_i -> phi_i + (ad_phi/(1 - e^{-ad_phi}))(xi). At 1004 write "The additive chart step agrees with the group retraction to first order in eta at every phi; the dexp^L conversion is required only when a chart vector is inserted into the group exponential."
**Falsifies:** Finite differences showing the first-order-in-xi shift equals xi at |phi| = O(1).
**Status:** D statements with a wrong order qualifier.
