## Findings (ranked)

### G1-1: The cited radius is Kendall's bound for a different objective; for the global argmin in eq:meta_agent_frame_barycenter the uniqueness claim fails below the stated π/2
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1573 (eq:meta_agent_frame_barycenter, "exists and is unique on convex geodesic balls below the curvature-dependent radius bound of Kendall, Afsari (π/2 under the unit-curvature normalization)"); 3762 ("locally unique ... π/2 ..., which also lies below the injectivity radius"); thm:rg_covariance hypothesis at 3807.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Afsari's paper separates three statements, and they govern different objectives. (i) Karcher's local bound, p. 658, eq. (1.3): "ρ < ½ min{inj M, π/(2√Δ)}". (ii) Kendall, as reported by Afsari p. 658: "if ρ < min{inj M, π/(2√Δ)}, then there exists a unique solipsistic Karcher mean". That is uniqueness for the distance *restricted to the ball* (Groisser's "solipsistic" mean). The same page adds that with the global distance, "Kendall's result yields the bound in (1.2)". (iii) Afsari, p. 657, eq. (1.2), "ρ < r_cx ≜ ½ min{inj M, π/√Δ}", and Theorem 2.1, eq. (2.1), p. 659, with conclusion on p. 660: the Lᵖ center "is a unique point and lies inside B" (1 < p ≤ ∞; ρ_{Δ,p} = r_cx for p ≥ 2). This is the global Fréchet minimizer over all of M. Eq. meta_agent_frame_barycenter is an argmin over all of G with the global d_G, so (iii) is the statement that applies. SO(3) under the bi-invariant metric is RP³ of constant curvature. Normalized to Δ = 1, its injectivity radius is π/2, so r_cx = ½ min{π/2, π} = π/4 (π/√2 in d_G, rotation angle π/2, consistent with L06-10). The printed π/2 is Kendall's solipsistic number min{inj, π/(2√Δ)} = min{π/2, π/2}, and it is not a bound for the global objective. Counterexample (`G1_radius_detector.py` (1)): frames U_j = R_z(2πj/3), j = 0,1,2, lie in the ball about I of rotation-angle radius 2π/3, i.e., unit-curvature radius π/3 < π/2. This ball satisfies Kendall's bound and is weakly convex (Afsari p. 658). The global objective has minimum value 5.84865446 = 16π²/27, attained at three distinct points (60 random starts found exactly 3; the objective on the v-orbit of one minimizer is 5.8486544599 three times). The "below the injectivity radius" clause at 3762 is also false at unit normalization, since π/2 equals inj(RP³). At 3762, "locally unique" undersells what Afsari proves, because the conclusion that applies below r_cx is global uniqueness. That global uniqueness is also what makes the proof of thm:rg_covariance at 3811 immediate. Attribution: Afsari p. 656 credits the general-manifold construction to Grove and Karcher [10] (Math. Z. 1973), with Cartan earlier on Hadamard manifolds. "The construction is Karcher's" should therefore read Grove-Karcher, with Karcher 1977 supplying the estimates.
**Fix:** Replace the radius clause at 1573 and 3762 with: "exists and is unique, as the global minimizer over G, whenever the weighted frames lie in a geodesic ball of radius ρ < r_cx = ½ min{inj G, π/√Δ} (Afsari 2011, Thm. 2.1), with Δ an upper bound on the sectional curvature; for SO(K_q) with d_G² = ‖log(U⁻¹V)‖_F² this is ρ < π/√2, a rotation angle of π/2 (Kendall 1990 obtains the larger radius min{inj, π/(2√Δ)} only for the mean defined with the distance restricted to the ball)." Delete "which also lies below the injectivity radius" and change "locally unique" to "unique". Cite Grove-Karcher 1973 for the construction.
**Falsifies:** A reading of Kendall's Theorem 7.3 under which the global-distance Fréchet minimizer is unique on balls of radius min{inj, π/(2√Δ)}. The three-minimizer SO(3) configuration would refute that reading, so the retraction condition is that the configuration fails to be three global minimizers (a lower value found elsewhere on SO(3)).
**Status:** The uniqueness statement is P once the hypothesis is corrected to Afsari's. As printed, with the π/2 hypothesis, it is false. The prose "the sharp radius bound is Kendall's and Afsari's" merges two bounds for two objectives.

### G1-2: The Definition of a meta-agent omits the frame-coherence hypothesis that the frame barycenter needs, and the detector cannot supply it
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1580-1585 (Definition, conditions (1)-(3)); 1611-1613 (Γ = P·C_q·C_s); contrast 3758 ("local frame coherence on a convex normal ball of G") and 3807 ("G is compact with locally unique Karcher means").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Every factor of Γ is built from transported divergences, which are gauge invariant, so Γ is blind to how far apart the frames U_i are. With q_j = (U_j)_# q₀ and U_j = R_z(2πj/3), the maximum transported KL is 2.2e-16 and C_q = 1.000000, so Γ = 1 with P = C_s = 1 (`G1_radius_detector.py` (1)). The cluster passes every condition of the Definition, yet eq:meta_agent_frame_barycenter has three global minimizers (G1-1), so the Definition's "constructed via the gauge-covariant averaging (meta_agent_barycenter)-(meta_agent_frame_barycenter)" is set-valued there. The observable content is unaffected, as 1575 predicts: across the three minimizers, the frame-free parent pair (m, S) = (U_I⁻¹μ_I, U_I⁻¹Σ_I U_I⁻ᵀ) varies by 1.8e-15. The same tuple is the one used in the no-equivariant-map argument at 1575.
**Fix:** Add a fourth condition to the Definition: "(4) the constituent frames at c lie in a geodesic ball of G of radius below r_cx (Afsari 2011, Thm. 2.1), so that (meta_agent_frame_barycenter) is single-valued; this condition is not detectable by Γ, which is gauge invariant." Alternatively, following 1575, state that U_I is a declared gauge convention, drop the uniqueness requirement, and say that the parent is defined by (m, S).
**Falsifies:** A proof that Γ > Γ_min forces the frames into an r_cx-ball. The Γ = 1 cyclic configuration contradicts this.
**Status:** The Definition is definitional. The implied claim that the admitted cluster determines a unique frame barycenter is unsupported (S presented as D).

### G1-3: On GL⁺(K_q) the open problem is narrower than stated, the log "distance" is not a metric, and the canonical bi-invariant mean is missing from the candidate list
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1573 (two candidates; "the noncompact GL⁺(K_q) generalization ... under either metric remains an open problem"); 2150 (implementation register); 1655 ("first-order Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff approximation of the group-valued barycenter").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Four executed checks (`G1_frame_mean_glplus.py`, `G1_radius_detector.py`).
(a) Transplanting d(U,V) = ‖log(U⁻¹V)‖_F from the compact case to GL⁺ does not give a metric. Of 10023 random det > 0 2×2 matrices, 2037 have no real principal logarithm, so the Fréchet objective is undefined on open sets. The triangle inequality fails in 4672 of 18825 finite random triples, including a violation with both legs below 1. With X = [[-0.0454, 0.9354],[0.0896, -0.1755]] and Y = [[-0.3929, 0.4683],[-0.4576, 0.5365]] (‖X‖ = 0.957, ‖Y‖ = 0.933), d(I, e^X e^Y) − d(I, e^X) − d(e^X, e^X e^Y) = +0.414. This was recomputed with an eigendecomposition log independent of `logm`, which agrees to 5.7e-16.
(b) The function is also not the Riemannian distance of the left-invariant Frobenius metric. Geodesics of that metric are one-parameter subgroups only for normal generators. A discrete-path upper bound on d_Riem(I, e^X) gives 1.609 < ‖X‖_F = 2.000 for X = [[0,2],[0,0]], 2.146 < 3.082 for [[0.5,3],[0,−0.5]], and equality 1.414 for the normal generator J.
(c) Convexity of t ↦ d(e^{tZ}, V)² along one-parameter subgroups is lost between ‖log V‖_F = 1.5 (minimum second derivative +0.132) and 2.0 (−0.110; flat value 2). So Karcher/Afsari theory does not apply to this d on GL⁺.
(d) Under any left-invariant Riemannian metric, GL⁺(K) is complete and homogeneous, so inj is a positive constant and the sectional curvature is bounded above. Afsari Thm. 2.1 therefore applies verbatim. For the Frobenius left-invariant metric on GL⁺(2), the Levi-Civita curvature (formula validated on so(3): K = 0.125000) gives maximum sectional curvature Δ = 0.500000 (sampled range [−3.47, 0.498]). The SO(2) loop is a closed geodesic of length 2√2π, so r_cx = ½ min{inj, π√2} is positive and at most π/√2. Existence and uniqueness on such balls is thus P, not open. The resulting mean is left-equivariant (residual ≤ 4e-8) but not right-equivariant (residual 1.8e-2 to 2.2e-1 for the d-Fréchet minimizer). It therefore breaks the global right-translation compatibility asserted at 3803 once G is noncompact.
(e) The object that keeps both actions is the exponential barycenter Σ_i w_i Log(m⁻¹x_i) = 0 of the canonical Cartan-Schouten connection (Pennec-Arsigny, Def. 3; Thm. 11 gives "left-, right- and inverse-invariant"; Thm. 12 gives local existence and uniqueness "if the data points belong to a sufficiently small normal convex neighborhood", with linear convergence of the fixed-point iteration). Measured on GL⁺(2), the iteration M ← M exp(Σ w_i log(M⁻¹U_i)) converges in 5-11 steps for spreads 0.1-1.5, with left and right equivariance residuals ≤ 1.0e-15. The implementation's additive average exp(Σ w_i φ_i) is exactly the first iterate from M = I, at distance 3.7e-5 (spread 0.1) to 2.1e-2 (spread 1.0) from the fixed point. That fixed point is not a Karcher or Fréchet mean of d: the gradient of Σ w_i d² there is 1.6e-3 to 4.1e-1, and its distance to the d-Fréchet minimizer is 7.8e-4 to 1.5e-1. None of this contradicts 1573's point that no bi-invariant *Fréchet objective* exists. It does show that the "obstruction" is to a variance characterization, not to a bi-invariant mean.
**Fix:** At 1573, after "the obstruction ... stands", insert: "The obstruction is to a bi-invariant variance, not to a bi-invariant mean: the exponential barycenter Σ_i w_i log(U_I⁻¹U_i) = 0 of the canonical Cartan-Schouten connection is left-, right- and inversion-equivariant and exists uniquely for sufficiently concentrated frames (Pennec and Arsigny 2013, Thms. 11-12); it is not the minimizer of any Riemannian variance. Under a left-invariant Riemannian metric instead, the Riemannian center of mass exists and is unique below Afsari's radius (Afsari 2011, Thm. 2.1), but is only left-equivariant." At 1655 and 2150, describe the additive form as "the first fixed-point iterate, from the identity, of the exponential barycenter", and restrict the open problem to the choice of convention, which 1575 already argues is a gauge choice. Do not use ‖log(U⁻¹V)‖_F as a distance on GL⁺.
**Falsifies:** A proof that ‖log(U⁻¹V)‖_F satisfies the triangle inequality on a neighborhood of I in GL⁺(2) containing the displayed legs (the explicit X, Y above would contradict it), or a demonstration that the exponential barycenter is a Fréchet mean for some left-invariant Riemannian metric (Pennec-Arsigny §1 state it is not in general).
**Status:** The paper's "open problem" statement is too broad. Existence and uniqueness under a left-invariant metric is P (Afsari), and the bi-invariant local mean is P (Pennec-Arsigny). The additive form's standing is D once relabeled.

### G1-4: The detector pass band does not imply the hypothesis of lem:weight_contraction; inside the pass band the weight map can be bistable and the parent can be captured by one member
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1633 ("convergence in the high-coherence regime is guaranteed by the following contraction property"); 1620 (the regime identified with Γ > Γ_min); lem:weight_contraction 1636-1641; 1644 ("warns on non-convergence").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The lemma needs the *maximum* pairwise transported KL to be at most ε < ε₀. The detector bounds the *unweighted mean* KL, ε̄ < τ log 2 = 0.03466 at τ = 0.05. One far outlier among many coherent members keeps the mean small. `G1_weight_contraction.py` (3): n = 610, one member at distance D = 4.60 (identity covariances), ε̄ = 0.03463 < 0.03466, and a maximum pairwise KL of 10.58, which is 305 times the band value. The weight map has two stable fixed points: μ̄ = 0 with local slope 0.000, and μ̄ = 4.479 with slope 0.542, where the outlier carries total weight 0.974 of 610 members. At n = 1000 and D = 5 (Γ = 0.607, well inside the band), the captured fixed point exists as well (`G1_dispersion_drop.py` (iii)). For ε₀ itself, the lemma leaves C(λ_min, λ_max) unspecified. The exact family "two members at ±a, identity covariances" reduces T to m ↦ a·tanh(a·m) with ε = 2a². Uniqueness is lost by a pitchfork at ε = 2: fixed points 0.0000 for ε = 1.9 and ±0.3799 for ε = 2.1. So any valid constant must give ε₀ ≤ 2 in these units. In the examples above, the uniform-weight start reaches the benign fixed point. I did not read the implementation's initialization, so whether the captured branch is reachable in practice is not checked. A non-convergence warning cannot detect bistability, because the iteration converges.
**Fix:** At 1633 replace "whose convergence in the high-coherence regime is guaranteed" by "whose convergence is guaranteed when the maximum pairwise transported divergence satisfies ε < ε₀ (Lemma below); the detector's pass band bounds only the mean, which implies max_{i≠j} KL ≤ |I|² τ log(1/Γ_min) and not ε < ε₀, so for large clusters a separate maximum-divergence check is required." State ε₀ ≤ 2 (identity-covariance units) as a necessary bound on any admissible constant.
**Falsifies:** A proof that Γ > Γ_min implies a max-pairwise bound independent of |I|. The n = 610 instance would refute it.
**Status:** The lemma is D (sketch, see G1-5). The sentence linking the lemma to the detector regime is inflated ("guaranteed" attached to an implication that does not hold).

### G1-5: lem:weight_contraction is true with a better rate, but its proof uses the wrong coordinates and leaves the norm, ball, and support condition implicit
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1637-1641.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Re-derivation. With normalized weights w̄_i and g_i = ∇_q̄ KL(ζ_i ‖ q̄), the derivative is dT[h] = Σ_i w̄_i (⟨g_i − ḡ, h⟩)(ζ_i − ζ̄), a weighted covariance between the constituent moments ζ_i = (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i) and the gradient differences. The proof bounds the gradients uniformly and so obtains O(√ε). But the gradient differences are themselves O(√ε): the mean part is Σ̄⁻¹(μ̃_j − μ̃_i) independent of q̄, and the covariance part is O(√ε(1 + R)) on a ball of radius R. So Lip(T) = O(ε). Measured (`G1_weight_contraction.py` (1)): on the convex hull, which T maps into after one step, Lip/ε = 0.47, 0.52, 0.53, 0.45, 0.41, 0.44, 0.73 for ε = 1e-4 ... 1 (K = 2, n = 4, spectrum [0.5, 2]), and 0.37-0.56 for K = 3. On a fixed ball of radius 1, Lip is larger (3.0 at ε = 1, so not a contraction there). The pitchfork family gives Lip = ε/2 exactly. The proof writes T as "the normalized-weight convex combination of the constituent expectation parameters η̃_i", but the implementation map drops the dispersion term (eq:meta_agent_sigma_impl). It is a convex combination of (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i), not of η̃_i = (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i + μ̃_iμ̃_iᵀ); the latter would reinstate the dispersion. The weights also vanish if every χ_i(x) = 0, so Σ_i χ_i > 0 must be assumed. No counterexample to the lemma as stated was found. With C unspecified it is only falsifiable through the necessary condition ε₀ ≤ 2.
**Fix:** State: "In the coordinates (μ, Σ) with the Frobenius norm, T maps the bounded-spectrum set into the convex hull H of {(μ̃_i, Σ̃_i)}, and on H, Lip(T) ≤ C(λ_min, λ_max)·ε; for ε < ε₀ = 1/C (necessarily ε₀ ≤ 2 in identity-covariance units) the fixed point is unique and the iteration converges geometrically from any start." Replace "expectation parameters η̃_i" by "moments (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i)" and add "Σ_i χ_i > 0".
**Falsifies:** A configuration in the hull with Lip(T)/ε growing without bound as ε → 0.
**Status:** D (proof sketch, true), with rate understated and a coordinate slip.

### G1-6: The dispersion-drop bound is correct, but the pass-band corollary uses the wrong mean; a valid replacement inequality is available
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1620-1625 (eq:dispersion_drop_bound; "inside the detector pass band the right side is at most 0.035 max_j λ_max(Σ̃_j)").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** The bound itself holds: over 180,000 random instances (n ∈ {2,3,5}, K ∈ {1,2,3}, arbitrary normalized weights), the minimum relative slack (rhs − lhs)/rhs is +2.9e-6 (`G1_dispersion_drop.py` (i)). It is tight for collinear equal-covariance configurations. The corollary replaces ε̄_w by the unweighted ε̄ that the pass band controls (L05's caveat). Numerical test with the implementation's self-consistent weights from the uniform start (K = 2, 40,000 samples per n): with χ = 1, the maximum of ε̄_w/ε̄ over admitted samples is 1.000 for n = 3-6, and the spread never exceeds 0.95 of the stated cap. With χ free in [0,1], ε̄_w/ε̄ reaches 1.41, but the presence factor P lowers the admissible ε̄, and the cap was not exceeded (maximum 0.86). The cap does fail at the captured fixed point of G1-4 (n = 1000, D = 5): the dropped spread is 0.102, three times the cap of 0.0347. So I found no violating instance on the benign branch, and a violating instance on the second branch.
**Fix:** Replace the corollary by the always-valid "ε̄_w ≤ |I|²(max_i w_i)² ε̄, so inside the pass band the right side is at most |I|²(max_i w_i)² τ log(1/Γ_min) max_j λ_max(Σ̃_j), which reduces to 0.035 max_j λ_max(Σ̃_j) for uniform weights at the implementation defaults."
**Falsifies:** A proof that the self-consistent coherence weights always satisfy ε̄_w ≤ ε̄. The n = 1000 captured fixed point refutes this.
**Status:** The bound is P (re-derived, numerically verified). The pass-band corollary is D only for uniform weights and is stated too broadly.
