### L11-1: The Lorentzian tensor and the tensor that carries time, sectors, and gravity are different objects

The clock (P1, 1830, 1840) is Fisher arc length on the belief fiber, which is Riemannian. The sectors (2031-2033) are eigen-directions of G_i(c) = sigma_i^* g_B, which the text calls positive semidefinite (1896). The temporal-direction ansatz `eq:lor_belief_metric` places a minus sign on g_B(q-dot, q-dot) in the fiber tangent space T_q B. Perceived space (P3) and gravity (P8, 2073-2075) use the structural Fisher pullback G^(s), described at 1877 as the "score-only" default, with the horizontal term "not the training metric". The sentence at 1994, "a purely real frame … reaches the same indefinite form without them (Proposition 4d)", is false: the proposition produces an indefinite form on T_c C built from the connection, not a sign on g_B on T_q B. For the combined tensor of `eq:induced_metric_full`, signature is not fixed by the horizontal block. `L11_gl2c_signature.py` section 8: with the real four-generator frame and a single Gaussian-mean Fisher term mu = kappa psi_tau, the combined tensor is diag(-2 + kappa^2/sigma^2, 2, 2, 2), which is Riemannian whenever kappa^2/sigma^2 > 2. No single tensor in the manuscript is shown to be both Lorentzian and the carrier of time, perceived space, or curvature.
**Fix:** Name one candidate spacetime tensor and use it throughout Part II. (induced_metric_full) depends on the relative size of the two blocks. We do not identify a single tensor that is both Lorentzian and the carrier of these readings." Delete "reaches the same indefinite form" at 1994 and state that `eq:lor_belief_metric` has no construction behind it.

### L11-2: In Regime I the frame-twist form is pure gauge, not merely gauge-noninvariant
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1975 ("an observable feature of the within-an-agent's-gauge-fixing pullback"), 1906 (`post:regime1`), 2023 (`prop:regulator_obstruction`), 1898.
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** The text concedes gauge non-invariance (1975, 2023) but still calls the result observable. The problem is stronger than non-invariance. Under the manuscript's own local transformation U_i -> U_i g(c) (2023), every transport Omega_ij = U_i U_j^{-1} is unchanged, so every KL coupling and attention weight is unchanged. Choosing g = U_i^{-1} gives A^(i) = 0 and therefore tr(A A) = 0 for that agent. `L11_gl2c_signature.py` section 9 (K = 3, random frames along a base curve): tr(A A) = 1.9002 before the transformation, 2.6932 under a generic local g, and 1.95e-22 under g = U_i^{-1}. A physicist reads "observable" as "invariant under the redundancy", so a quantity that the redundancy can set to zero carries no physical information. The same script shows that the relative-frame form tr((Omega_ij^{-1} d Omega_ij)^2) is gauge invariant (1.785464 before and after, difference 1.1e-10 from finite differencing) and equals tr((A_i - A_j)^2). It is indefinite on gl(K,R) for the same reason as tr(A A).
**Fix:** Replace "an observable feature of the within-an-agent's-gauge-fixing pullback" with "a gauge-fixed quantity: in Regime I the connection is pure gauge and a local transformation that preserves all transports sets tr(A_mu A_nu) to zero for any one agent, so the form has no gauge-invariant content." If the author wants to keep a signature route, the natural invariant candidate is the relational form tr((A_i - A_j)_mu (A_i - A_j)_nu) on agent pairs, which fits the participatory reading. It requires base derivatives of frames, so it counts as deferred work (Theory Supplement B) and should not be developed in PIFB2.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the right action U_i -> U_i g(c) is not a symmetry of the PIFB2 functional (for example, a term depending on A^(i) itself rather than on Omega_ij).
**Status:** The regulator discussion is D. "Observable" is inflation.

### L11-3: The construction presupposes the continuum and a background metric, which Wheeler's program rejects
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1870-1872, 1892, 2033 (flat background chart metric g_0; sectors defined relative to a fixed chart), 2042 ("the base manifold C carries no intrinsic metric").
**Severity:** high (for goal 1, standalone realization of Wheeler)
**Evidence:** In the source, Wheeler's "four no's" include "No continuum" and "No space, no time", and the third begins "No continuum in mathematics and therefore no continuum in physics" (Wheeler 1990, in Zurek (ed.), *Complexity, Entropy and the Physics of Information*, pp. 314-315). PIFB2 assumes a smooth base manifold C with a fixed dimension, charts, and smooth sections (1870). It also declares a flat background chart metric g_0 to define the sectors (2033), and nine lines later says C "carries no intrinsic metric, distance, or temporal direction" (2042). So the pullback construction derives at most a metric tensor. Topology, dimension, differentiable structure, and a reference metric are inputs. A foundations referee will see "it from bit" realized as "metric from bit on a presupposed it", and will also flag the direct contradiction between 2033 and 2042.
**Fix:** Say so in the pullback section: "The construction takes the base manifold C, including its dimension and smooth structure, as given, and derives only metric data from beliefs. It therefore realizes Wheeler's thesis for the metric alone, not his rejection of a presupposed continuum; see Wheeler 1990, 'four no's'." At 2042 replace "carries no intrinsic metric" with "carries no metric that the construction treats as physical; the sector definition uses a declared computational background g_0 (Section 6.3)". A route toward "no continuum" exists in the literature: causal structure fixes topology, differential structure, and conformal metric (Malament 1977; Hawking, King and McCarthy 1976), and causal-set theory reconstructs geometry from order plus counting (Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin, PRL 59, 521, 1987). Cite it as the direction that would address the gap, without constructing it.
**Falsifies:** A passage in PIFB2 that derives the dimension or smooth structure of C from agent data.
**Status:** S. "Precise sense" and "physics-like structure is recovered" (1892) inflate it.

### L11-4: The GL(2,C) example's seven postulates are not independent; the three that do the work insert the signature by hand
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1906-1929 (Postulates), 1933 (`prop:conditional_signature`), 1942-1960 (worked example), 1962 (`prop:rank_obstruction`), 1968-1975 (`prop:4d_signature` and remarks).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** `L11_gl2c_signature.py`:
(a) Without separability, with frame jet d psi_tau = (p1, p2) and d psi_x = (p3, p4), Re G - 2(d psi_x d psi_x^T - d psi_tau d psi_tau^T) = 0 identically, and det Re G = -4 J^2 with J the Jacobian. So the signature is (-,+) wherever d psi_tau and d psi_x are independent. `post:separable` only makes the (tau, x) chart diagonal. Its stated role at 1916, that without it "cross-derivative terms render Re(G_tau tau) sign-indefinite and dissolve the split", conflates a chart component with the signature. A non-separable example gives Re G_tau tau = +6 with eigenvalues {-0.081, 7.901}: still Lorentzian.
(b) `post:regime1` is implied by `post:collapse`. For U = exp((i s1 + s2) T), A = d phi and F = 0 identically (section 4).
(c) `post:noncompact` says a compact generator "would forbid the sign flip" (1910). That is false. For T = J skew (tr J^2 = -2), Re G = diag(2a^2, -2b^2), which is again signature (1,1) with the timelike role moved to x. Only tr T^2 = 0 (nilpotent T) kills the form (G = 0).
(d) The indefiniteness comes from `post:pairing`, `post:imaginary`, and `post:realpart` taken together: Re((i a + b)^2) = b^2 - a^2, the hyperbolic quadratic form Re(z^2) on C = R^2. Choosing which base direction receives the factor i is choosing which direction is timelike. The result is therefore circular in the precise sense that the conclusion's signature is the signature of the postulated form. The manuscript already concedes (1960) that the gauge apparatus contributes only a normalization. The new points are (a)-(c).
(e) `prop:rank_obstruction` lists the eigenvalues as {-(d psi_tau)^2, sum (d psi_a)^2, 0, 0}. With tr T^2 = 2, as in the text, they are {-2a^2, 2|b|^2, 0, 0} (section 6). This is a normalization slip.
(f) `prop:4d_signature` and the remark at 1975 say K_q >= 6 is needed for traceless spatial generators and K_q >= 5 if tracelessness is relaxed. Neither minimum is sharp. S = J (+) 0 together with generators a I_2 (+) diag(c, d, e), 2a + c + d + e = 0, gives three traceless, commuting, trace-orthogonal symmetric generators at K = 5 (section 7, Gram matrix diag(-2, 2, 2, 2), all commutators zero). Without tracelessness K = 4 suffices (I_2 (+) 0, 0 (+) diag(sqrt2, 0), 0 (+) diag(0, sqrt2)). The proof's "Disjoint supports make the four generators commute" is also inexact. S has disjoint support from the T_a, but the T_a commute because they are diagonal, not because of disjointness.
**Fix:** Cut the two-dimensional complex route to one paragraph, or move it to an appendix. Proposition 4d dominates it, since the real route needs three fewer postulates and has no rank-changing projection. Reduce the postulate list for the example to the three that act (pairing, imaginary coefficient, real-part projection) plus tr T^2 != 0. Say that separability is a chart choice and flatness is automatic. Correct 1910 to "a compact generator exchanges which direction is timelike; only tr T^2 = 0 removes the form". Fix the factor 2 in `prop:rank_obstruction`. Replace "K_q >= 6 … K_q >= 5" with "K_q >= 5 (traceless) or K_q >= 4 (general)".
**Falsifies:** A non-separable frame for which Re G has signature (2,0) or (0,2) while d psi_tau and d psi_x are independent (excluded by det Re G = -4 J^2).
**Status:** D. The postulate count overstates the independent content, and the three misstated roles are D-level errors.

### L11-5: The Appendix B claim of vanishing covariance back-action is false in the framework's own geometry
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2575-2587 (`eq:rock_back_mu` 2575, `eq:rock_back_sigma` 2584, and "Both mean and covariance back-action scale as epsilon and vanish in the high-precision limit"); also 2079 ("the rock appears certainly there") and 2533.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** `L11_rock_backaction.py` reproduces the displayed formulas exactly: grad_mu F, mu-dot, the coupling covariance gradient beta/2 (Lambda_h - Lambda_R), and Sigma-dot^back = eta beta (eps - eps^2/v_r) = O(eps) in absolute units. The absolute size is not the right measure for a covariance of size eps. The relative rate is Sigma-dot^back / Sigma -> eta beta as eps -> 0, so the Fisher speed eta beta / sqrt2 is O(1) and independent of eps. The stationary rock variance with the coupling is v* = eps v_r (1 + beta) / (beta eps + v_r), so v*/eps -> 1 + beta. The rock's precision drops by the factor 1/(1 + beta) at every eps. The Fisher-Rao distance between the uncoupled and coupled rock is log(1 + beta)/sqrt2 (0.067, 0.287, 0.490 for beta = 0.1, 0.5, 1), which does not vanish as eps -> 0. Only the mean back-action is suppressed: shift beta eps (mu_r - mu_p)/(beta eps + v_r), Fisher length O(sqrt eps). The cause is that each KL term contains -H[q_R], so coupling to any broad agent reweights the rock's entropy term.
**Fix:** Replace the covariance paragraph's conclusion with: "The mean back-action is O(epsilon) in absolute terms and O(epsilon^{1/2}) in the rock's Fisher metric. The covariance back-action is O(epsilon) in absolute terms but O(1) relative to Sigma_R: at stationarity the rock's precision is Lambda_R = (Lambda_p + beta Lambda_h)/(1 + beta), reduced by the factor 1/(1 + beta) for any epsilon. Precision dominance therefore suppresses belief-mean back-action but not precision back-action." Weaken "the rock appears certainly there" (2079) to match.
**Falsifies:** A normalization of the coupling (for example, beta entering as a tempered likelihood rather than a full KL) under which the entropy term of q_R is not reweighted. That would be a change to the functional, not to Appendix B.
**Status:** D is claimed ("direct consequences", 2538). It is correct for the mean and wrong for the covariance in any scale-invariant measure.

### L11-6: The causal route writes down the Lorentzian form rather than deriving it, and omits the standard theorems and analog literature
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1978-1981 (`sec:causal_cone_route`), P5 in the register at 1814-1825, table row "c" in `tab:physical_correspondences` (2050-2065).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The route assumes a shared update parameter tau, a positive-definite h_ab, and a maximal speed c_I, and then writes g = -c_I^2 d tau^2 + h. The non-trivial physical assumption is left unstated: the region of possible influence must be the h-ball h(dx, dx) <= c_I^2 d tau^2, that is, isotropic with respect to the same h. A direction-dependent speed bound gives a Finsler cone, not a quadratic one. "Lorentzian … by Sylvester's law" (1980) misuses the theorem. The signature is read off a diagonal form; Sylvester's law concerns invariance under congruence. The shared tau is a preferred foliation, and the first-order dynamics are not boost invariant, so the construction is the analog-gravity situation (a Lorentzian cone for signals in a medium with a preferred frame). The text honestly notes the parabolic tension. The standard results are not cited: Malament (J. Math. Phys. 18, 1399, 1977) and Hawking, King and McCarthy (J. Math. Phys. 17, 174, 1976) show that for distinguishing spacetimes the causal structure fixes topology, differential structure, and conformal metric. Both presuppose a Lorentzian manifold, so they cannot supply the signature and would not rescue the route from circularity. They are the right citations for "the cone fixes g only up to a conformal factor", which as written is only the pointwise algebraic fact. Neither is in references.bib (grep: 0 hits); neither is analog gravity (Barceló, Liberati and Visser, Living Rev. Relativ.) nor causal sets.
**Fix:** Add after the null-cone sentence: "The quadratic cone requires the influence region to be an h-ball; an anisotropic speed bound gives a Finsler cone. The shared parameter tau is a preferred foliation, so Lorentz boosts are symmetries of the cone but not of the first-order dynamics, as in analog gravity [Barceló, Liberati, Visser]. Conversely, for a spacetime already known to be Lorentzian and distinguishing, the causal order fixes topology, smooth structure and conformal metric [Hawking, King, McCarthy 1976; Malament 1977]; these results do not produce a signature from a non-Lorentzian order." Replace "by Sylvester's law" with "by inspection".
**Falsifies:** An argument in PIFB2 that derives h-isotropy of the influence region from the variational dynamics.
**Status:** S presented as a "parallel existence statement" (1981). It is an assumption restated, and the text's disclaimer "neither deriving the signature" is accurate.

### L11-10: The regulator-obstruction proposition misattributes the obstruction for compact groups
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2023 (`prop:regulator_obstruction`), labeled a "Level-2 mathematical fact" at 2022.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** "Compactness of G does not rescue this average, since g^{-1}dg ranges over the Lie algebra, a vector space carrying no normalizable uniform measure." This is the wrong reason. The integration variable is the map g, not g^{-1}dg, which g determines. For any finite set of agents or base points (the framework's actual setting, and the Theory's single-point stance), the local gauge group is a finite product of copies of G. For compact G its Haar product measure is normalizable, which is the lattice-gauge-theory regulator. The genuine obstructions are the continuum limit (infinitely many copies) and, for the framework's actual structure group GL(K) (and SO(1,3)), infinite Haar volume already for a single copy. The text states the latter correctly.
**Fix:** Replace the compactness sentence with: "For compact G a lattice or finite-agent regularization gives a normalizable product Haar measure, so the obstruction there is the continuum limit; for the non-compact GL(K_q) and SO(1,3) the Haar volume is infinite even for a single copy."
**Falsifies:** An argument that the product Haar measure on G^N for compact G and finite N is not normalizable (it is, with total mass 1).
**Status:** D claimed. The conclusion stands for GL(K), but the compact-case reasoning is wrong.
