### G3-1: The Karcher-mean uniqueness radius "pi/2 under the unit-curvature normalization, which also lies below the injectivity radius" is wrong for SO(K_q), K_q >= 3
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1573 (under `eq:meta_agent_frame_barycenter`) and 3762 (App. I, same statement with "which also lies below the injectivity radius").
**Severity:** medium (it is the stated hypothesis for existence and uniqueness of the meta-agent frame; the barycenter is defined as a global argmin over G).
**Evidence:** Afsari's bound, as restated by Afsari, Tron and Vidal (arXiv:1201.0925, Sec. 2.1, eq. 2.1), is r_cx = (1/2) min{inj M, pi/sqrt(Delta)}, with Delta an upper bound on sectional curvature; data in an open ball of radius below r_cx have a unique global L^p center of mass (their Theorem 2.1, citing Afsari 2011). The Afsari 2011 abstract adds that under these conditions the global and local centers coincide, and that the paper reproves "a uniqueness result by W. S. Kendall". `G3_karcher_radius.py` gives, for the metric d(U,V) = ||log(U^{-1}V)||_F: SO(3) has K_max = 0.125000 and inj = pi sqrt 2 = 4.442883; SO(4) and SO(5) have K_max = 0.250000 and the same inj. In rotation-angle units the Afsari radius is pi/2 = 1.570796 for all three groups. After normalizing the metric to unit maximal curvature, r_cx = pi/4 = 0.785398 for SO(3) (with inj = pi/2 = 1.570796) and r_cx = 1.110721 = pi/(2 sqrt 2) for SO(4) and SO(5). No case gives pi/2. For SO(3) the claim "pi/2 ... below the injectivity radius" fails as well, since pi/2 equals inj. A direct witness: two rotations at angles +alpha and -alpha about one axis have Frechet values F(I) = 9.869604 and F(R_pi) = 9.869604 at alpha = pi/2 (a tie, so the mean is not unique). At alpha = pi/2 + 0.05 the global minimizer jumps to the antipode R_pi (F = 9.251286 against 10.507923). In unit-curvature units these data lie at distance pi/4 + 0.025 from I, well inside the claimed pi/2 ball. The value pi/2 is the classical hemisphere bound for the unit sphere S^n, where inj = pi. It does not transfer to SO(K_q), whose injectivity radius is halved by the identification of antipodes.
**Fix:** At 1573: "exists and is unique when the constituent frames lie in an open geodesic ball of radius below r_cx = (1/2) min{inj G, pi/sqrt(Delta)} [Afsari2011]; for SO(K_q) with this metric that is a rotation-angle radius below pi/2, that is, a Frobenius-log radius below pi/sqrt 2 [Afsari2011, Kendall1990]." At 3762, replace the parenthesis and the injectivity clause with the same statement. If "the sharp radius bound is Kendall's and Afsari's" is kept, say which is which: Kendall's regular-ball theorem is a uniqueness statement for the barycenter within the ball, while Afsari's radius gives the global center, which is what the argmin in `eq:meta_agent_frame_barycenter` requires.
**Falsifies:** A normalization of the SO(K_q) metric with maximal curvature 1 and injectivity radius at least pi. None exists, because inj times sqrt(K_max) is scale-invariant and equals pi/2 for SO(3) and pi/sqrt 2 for SO(K_q >= 4) (script output).
**Status:** P once corrected (by citation to Afsari). As written, the constant and the injectivity clause are false. This closes the "for the geometry lens" residual left open in L14-1, L15-5 and L16.

### G3-2: The Wilson and Creutz pin-cites at 385 point to the wrong equation and the wrong chapter
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:385 (`\cite[Eq.~12]{WilsonConfinement1974}`, `\cite[ch.~5 Eq.~5.1]{Creutz1983}`), supporting `eq:omega_gauge_law` (383).
**Severity:** low (the law is correct and correctly attributed to Wilson; only the locators are wrong).
**Evidence:** Wilson, PRD 10, 2445 (1974), full text read. The paper numbers equations by section, (3.1), (3.2), and so on, so there is no "Eq. 12". Eq. (3.12) is the full lattice action. The abelian gauge transformation is Eq. (3.4), A_nmu -> A_nmu - (chi_{n+mu} - chi_n)/a. The non-Abelian link law is Eqs. (3.27)-(3.28), b_nmu -> chi_n b_nmu chi_{n+mu}^{-1} and U(b_nmu) -> U(chi_n) U(b_nmu) U^{-1}(chi_{n+mu}), on the page that introduces the non-Abelian generalization. That is exactly the form of `eq:omega_gauge_law`, Omega_ij -> g_i Omega_ij g_j^{-1}, with i the source site n and j the target n+mu. For Creutz, the Cambridge Core contents list Chapter 5 as "Fermions" (pp. 20-28 in the 2022 reissue), Chapter 6 as "Gauge fields" and Chapter 7 as "Lattice gauge theory". A fermion chapter's first equation is not the link-variable transformation law. I could not open the chapters, so the correct Creutz equation number is not verified.
**Fix:** `\cite[Eqs.~(3.27)--(3.28)]{WilsonConfinement1974}`. For Creutz, cite the chapter only, `\cite[Ch.~6]{Creutz1983}` (or Ch. 7), unless the author checks the equation number in the book. Also add the DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.10.2445 to the bib entry.
**Falsifies:** An edition of Wilson 1974 with continuous equation numbering, or a Creutz edition whose Chapter 5 treats gauge fields. The APS scan and the CUP contents page rule both out for the cited editions.
**Status:** P attribution with wrong locators.

### G3-3: Primary check of Higham Ch. 10 confirms L10-4(c) and tightens its proposed replacement text
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2510 (`\cite[Ch.~10]{Higham2002}`).
**Severity:** low (it refines an existing finding; see L10-4).
**Evidence:** Chapter 10 of Higham 2002 is "Cholesky Factorization" (SIAM chapter DOI 10.1137/1.9780898718027.ch10). Theorem 10.3 gives the backward error R^T R = A + dA with |dA| <= gamma_{n+1} |R^T||R|, which has no condition-number factor. Theorem 10.5 (Demmel) restates it in the scaled form. Theorem 10.7 (Demmel, p. 200) states that with A = DHD and D = diag(A)^{1/2}, Cholesky succeeds if lambda_min(H) > n gamma_{n+1}/(1 - gamma_{n+1}) and is certain to fail if lambda_min(H) <= -n gamma_{n+1}/(1 - gamma_{n+1}). The text notes that the success condition can be weakened to kappa_2(H) n gamma_{n+1}/(1 - gamma_{n+1}) < 1. It also quotes Wilkinson's older condition 20 n^{3/2} kappa_2(A) u <= 1. PIFB2's "backward error grows with kappa(Sigma_i)" is therefore contradicted by the cited chapter, as L10-4(c) found from a secondary source. L10's replacement sentence ("runs to completion when the diagonally scaled matrix has condition number safely below u^{-1}") is directionally right, but the cited sufficient condition is about a factor n(n+1) tighter than u^{-1}. For K_q = 10 in fp32 it gives kappa_2(H) below 1/(110 u), about 1.5e5, not 1.68e7.
**Fix:** In the L10-4 replacement, write: "Cholesky factorization is backward stable independent of conditioning [Higham2002, Thm. 10.3]; it is guaranteed to run to completion when kappa_2(H) n gamma_{n+1}/(1 - gamma_{n+1}) < 1, with H the unit-diagonal scaling of the matrix, roughly kappa_2(H) < 1/(n(n+1)u) [Higham2002, Thm. 10.7]."
**Falsifies:** A different edition in which Chapter 10 is not the Cholesky chapter, or in which Theorem 10.3 carries a kappa factor. The second edition read here has neither.
**Status:** P (numerical-analysis citation), misquoted at 2510.

### G3-4: The Yedidia-Freeman-Weiss and Wainwright-Jordan citations at 3490 do not state the non-monotonicity claim they are attached to
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3490 ("larger Kikuchi or region graphs form a family of more expressive approximations whose error need not improve monotonically~\cite{Wainwright2008,Yedidia2005}").
**Severity:** low.
**Evidence:** A full-text search of the Wainwright-Jordan preprint (301 pp.) for "monoton", "not necessarily", "more accurate" and "larger regions" near Kikuchi or region material found no such statement. Yedidia, Freeman and Weiss (IEEE Trans. IT 51, 2282, 2005; journal text read) say in Sec. VIII.C ("Discussion and Heuristics", p. 2300) that constructing region graphs that give accurate marginals is "still very much an open research problem". They also say that maxent-normal approximations "reliably give more accurate estimates" than Bethe, and that invalid region graphs give poor approximations. That supports "accuracy depends on the region choice and is not guaranteed". It does not support a statement about monotonicity in region size.
**Fix:** "...larger Kikuchi or region graphs form a family of more expressive approximations whose accuracy depends on the choice of regions and is not guaranteed to improve [Yedidia2005, Sec. VIII.C]." Add the primary source for the cluster-variation method named here and at 3516: Kikuchi, Phys. Rev. 81, 988 (1951), as L14-5 proposes (metadata verified below). For the tree-exactness sentence just before ("On a tree, minimizing this functional over the local marginal polytope returns the exact marginals"), cite Wainwright-Jordan Prop. 4.1 (M(T) = L(T)). That is where Prop. 4.1 belongs, rather than at 3228 and 3265 (L05-10(a)).
**Falsifies:** A passage in either source stating that error can grow as regions are enlarged. I found none in the texts searched.
**Status:** S claim cited as if P.

### G3-5: Field-theory attributions at 3554-3560: Faddeev-Popov is cited for background-field covariance, and the background-field method's origin is uncited
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3554 (`\cite{Abbott1982,FaddeevPopov1967}` after "retain covariance under background gauge transformations").
**Severity:** low.
**Evidence:** Abbott 1982 (Acta Phys. Pol. B 13, 33; INSPIRE record) is the standard introduction to the background-field method, so it is correctly cited. Faddeev-Popov 1967 (abstract): "we propose a simple method for calculation of the contribution from arbitrary diagrams" in gauge theories. This is the gauge-fixing determinant, which is the object of the preceding sentence ("includes one Faddeev--Popov factor"), not background covariance. Background-field covariance goes back to DeWitt (Phys. Rev. 162, 1195, 1967; Crossref verified), and its multi-loop form to Abbott (Nucl. Phys. B 185, 189, 1981; abstract: "the gauge-invariant effective action computed using this method"). Cornwall-Jackiw-Tomboulis at 3556 is attributed correctly: its abstract describes "an effective action and potential for composite operators", which is the 2PI construction.
**Fix:** Move `FaddeevPopov1967` to the "one Faddeev--Popov factor" sentence. Cite `DeWitt1967, Abbott1981, Abbott1982` for background covariance.
**Falsifies:** A background-field covariance argument in Faddeev-Popov 1967. The two-page letter contains none, per its abstract.
**Status:** P attributions, one misplaced.

### G3-6: The Lieb-Robinson citation proposed in L15-2 supports only an analogy; the classical lattice statement is elementary and bears on the "tension" sentence at 1980
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1980 ("first-order natural-gradient dynamics, whose naive continuum limit is parabolic with infinite signal speed"); L15-2 fix.
**Severity:** low.
**Evidence:** Lieb and Robinson (CMP 28, 251, 1972; abstract) prove a finite group velocity for finite-range quantum spin interactions under unitary time translation. PIFB2's dynamics are classical and dissipative, so LR is a precedent by analogy only. The classical counterpart is Marchioro, Pellegrinotti, Pulvirenti and Triolo, "Velocity of a perturbation in infinite lattice systems", J. Stat. Phys. 19, 499-510 (1978) (Crossref verified; content not read). For linear first-order dynamics on a graph of maximal degree Delta, the elementary bound |(e^{tL})_{xy}| <= sum over k >= d of (2 Delta t)^k/k! follows because (L^k)_{xy} vanishes for k < d(x,y). Responses outside d > 2e Delta t are therefore super-exponentially suppressed. `G3_lieb_robinson.py` confirms the bound on an 81-site chain (maximum ratio of actual response to bound 1.1e-2; response at d = 30, t = 1 is 5.3e-34). PIFB2's sentence is accurate as written ("naive continuum limit"). But on the finite agent graph at one base point, which is where PIFB2's dynamics actually run, an effective finite speed already exists. The "tension" belongs to the continuum limit only.
**Fix:** If L15-2's citation is adopted, write: "...in tension with first-order natural-gradient dynamics only in the continuum limit; on a bounded-degree agent graph, first-order dynamics already has an effective light cone of Lieb-Robinson type [LiebRobinson1972, Marchioro1978]." Developing a base-manifold causal structure from this is new base-transport work and is deferred (Theory Supplement B).
**Falsifies:** A classical first-order lattice dynamics with bounded couplings whose response at distance d grows faster than (2 Delta t)^d/d!. That is impossible for linear dynamics by the series bound above.
**Status:** D (elementary bound, checked). The PIFB2 sentence is not inflated.

### G3-7: RG attributions: "abelian RG group", Laplace-approximation pins to Cardy, and the missing primary source for Kadanoff's variational RG

**Severity:** low.
**Evidence:** (a) A physics referee will note that a coarse-graining RG is a semigroup (it is not invertible). Its commutativity comes from its being a one-parameter family of scale changes. Saying it is "replaced by the full structure group G" conflates the scale semigroup with the gauge structure group. The Wilson 1971 abstract describes the RG as differential equations in the scale variable, which is consistent with a one-parameter flow. (b) I could not open Cardy 1996 (INSPIRE: CUP 1996, 238 pp., Cambridge Lecture Notes in Physics 3). Whether it presents either the Radon-Nikodym form of the block-spin pushforward or a Laplace approximation "around the parent configuration" is not verified. A bare book citation for a single step is weak support at a physics venue. (c) The Mehta-Schwab abstract confirms the attribution ("the variational renormalization group, first introduced by Kadanoff"), but the primary sources are absent from references.bib: Kadanoff, PRL 34, 1005 (1975), and Kadanoff, Houghton and Yalabik, J. Stat. Phys. 14, 171 (1976), both Crossref verified. `kadanoff1966scaling` is in the bib and uncited (L14-5). Its DOI is 10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.2.263 (INSPIRE).
**Fix:** (a) "...with the one-parameter RG semigroup of scale changes supplemented by the structure group G". (b) Give chapter pins for Cardy after checking the book, or cite the Laplace/saddle-point step to a text that states it with an equation number. (c) At 1683: "Kadanoff's variational renormalization group [Kadanoff1975, KadanoffHoughtonYalabik1976]".
**Falsifies:** For (b), a Cardy chapter that states both steps. I could not check.

## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A physics referee checks equation-level pins in the lattice-gauge literature, and a wrong one (G3-2) signals that the source was not opened. Pins should follow each source's own numbering, which is sectional in Wilson 1974. Physics journals expect DOIs. Most of the existing physics entries lack them (L14-12), and the entries above supply them for the additions. Appendix G's mean-field material leans on machine-learning sources (Bishop, Wainwright-Jordan). For a statistical-physics readership, the primary cluster-variation source (Kikuchi 1951) and a statistical-physics reference for the mean-field and Bethe free energies should be named where the objects first appear. The one-loop and 2PI paragraphs (3554-3560) are in standard field-theory language and read well to a physicist once the Faddeev-Popov citation is moved. The Karcher radius (G3-1) is the kind of constant a mathematical-physics referee recomputes, and it is off by a factor of two in the stated normalization.
