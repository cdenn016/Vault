### L07-1: Under the stated kinetic-metric postulate every mode has unit frequency, so "omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff" is false, not definitional
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1330 (section intro), 1391 ("exhibits Newtonian-analogy scaling"), 1512 (Postulate, kinetic metric, `sec:velocity_quadratic`), 1762 (Discussion).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** The postulate (1512) makes the kinetic coefficient the same frozen-beta Hessian M that is the potential stiffness at consensus. With L = (1/2) xi_dot^T M xi_dot - (1/2) dxi^T M dxi, the Euler-Lagrange equation is M xi_ddot = -M xi, so xi_ddot = -xi. Every normal mode has omega = 1, whatever the precision. `L07_mass_vs_friction.py (d)`: det(K - w^2 M) = 0 with K = M has the single root w^2 = 1 for a generic symbolic 2x2 SPD M. The text says the harmonic relation "reduces to a per-direction identity", which is correct, and then says omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff is "a definitional consequence of the postulate" (1512, 1762). That is incompatible with omega = 1. The only reading that gives omega^2 of order 1/m is the one at 1330 ("modes whose stiffness is dominated by contributions independent of the agent's own prior precision"). That reading takes the inertia to be the bare prior precision alone while the stiffness keeps the coupling blocks: omega^2 = (lambda_p + k_c)/lambda_p, which is of order k_c/lambda_p only when k_c >> lambda_p (script (d)). The paper therefore carries two incompatible inertia assignments. The claim at 1391 that the metric "exhibits" Newtonian scaling in the isolated-agent limit is false under both readings: there M = Lambda_p = stiffness, so omega = 1.
**Fix:** Under the postulate as written, replace every "omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff" with: "Under the kinetic-metric postulate the stiffness and the inertia coincide, so every linear mode about consensus has the same frequency (unity in flow-time units). A precision-dependent frequency would require an inertia tensor that is not the stiffness tensor, which the construction does not supply." Delete "which justifies calling it a mass matrix" at 1391. If the bare-mass reading at 1330 is intended, state it as a second, different postulate: inertia equal to the prior precision, stiffness equal to the full Hessian. Then omega^2 = eig(Lambda_p^{-1} M) = 1 + eig(Lambda_p^{-1} K_coupling). That relation is testable, and it is not an identity.
**Falsifies:** A kinetic term in the paper whose coefficient differs from the potential Hessian at consensus, or an equation of motion other than the Euler-Lagrange equation of T - F from which the stated scaling follows.
**Status:** S (postulated analogy). The prose inflates it to a "consequence" and says the metric "exhibits" the scaling.

### L07-2: In the dynamics the paper actually runs, precision plays the role of friction, not inertia; "hard to move", "slower response" and "difficulty of accelerating" are false as dynamical statements, and the proposed omega test cannot work
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1509 ("hard to move under the dynamics"), 1515 ("higher precision implies larger coefficient implies slower response"), 1512 (operational test: "fitting omega from the autocorrelation of a natural-gradient trajectory"), 1758-1762 (overdamped analogy; "difficulty of accelerating").
**Severity:** high (for a physics venue)
**Evidence:** The flow the paper defines (961, 972-975) is first order: xi_dot = -eta G^{-1} grad F, with G the Fisher metric. Compare the paper's own display at 1758, gamma q_dot = -grad V. The Fisher metric G/eta occupies the friction slot gamma. No term in the dynamics carries the m slot. Three consequences were computed.

(a) Isolated agent at q = p, `L07_mass_vs_friction.py (a)`. Under the natural-gradient flow the linearized mean and covariance relaxation rates are exactly eta, for prior-precision scale s = 1, 10, 1000 (rates `[1. 1.]` and `[1. 1. 1.]` in every case). Under the Euclidean flow the rates are eta times the eigenvalues of Lambda_p (2.08, 0.92, rising to 2083, 917 at s = 1000), so a precise agent relaxes faster. Only the static compliance H^{-1} = Sigma_p (1.09 falling to 0.00109) and the mobility eta Sigma make a precise agent "hard to move". Both are overdamped response properties, not inertia.

(b) For any gradient flow with G SPD and H symmetric, G^{-1}H is similar to G^{-1/2} H G^{-1/2}. Its spectrum is therefore real (theorem), and there is no oscillation for an autocorrelation fit to measure. Numerically, max |Im eig| = 0 exactly over 2000 random GL(2) instances with asymmetric frozen beta, using the full receiver-plus-sender Hessian (script (b)).

(c) Complex eigenvalues appear only in the receiver-only truncation (script (c): directed 3-cycle with SO(2) holonomy, eigenvalues -1.769 +/- 0.743i, -1.409 +/- 0.951i, -0.122 +/- 0.208i; the full gradient on the same data gives real eigenvalues -0.144, -2.718, -3.438). That is the nonpotential field that 1392 says "has no established conservative-Hamiltonian reading". The proposed operational test measures an omega only where the mass reading has been disowned.

Status of 1755: the "kinetic term" (1/2) dmu^T Sigma^{-1} dmu is the Fisher line element ds^2/2, identical to the bit count of `eq:kl_arclength`. In the first-order flow it enters as the Rayleigh dissipation function R = (1/(2 eta)) xi_dot^T G xi_dot, with dF/dt = -2R. That is exactly the clock-dissipation identity `eq:clock_dissipation`.
**Fix:** Rewrite 1509 and 1515 as static statements: "a high-precision agent has large stiffness and small compliance, so a given force displaces it little; under the natural-gradient flow its linear relaxation rate is eta, independent of precision". Replace "difficulty of accelerating" (1762) with "difficulty of displacing". Replace the operational test at 1512: a first-order gradient flow has no omega. A dynamical test of an inertia postulate requires either a second-order equation of motion stated as a separate postulate (then write it, e.g. M xi_ddot + gamma G xi_dot = -grad F, and name its overdamped limit), or a measurement of static response. Consider adding one sentence that unifies Part I and Part II: "the only velocity-quadratic form carried by the actual dynamics is the Rayleigh dissipation (1/2 eta) xi_dot^T G xi_dot, whose integral is the information clock of Section `sec:time_info`." A physics referee will accept that reading. The mass reading, as it stands, contradicts the paper's own equations.
**Falsifies:** A second-order equation of motion used anywhere in the paper's computations or implementation; or a non-symmetric H in the potential (full-gradient) flow.
**Status:** The rate computations are D (exact here). The dynamical "hard to move" is inflated beyond what the paper's dynamics supports.

### L07-3: The adiabatic hierarchy states "normally attracting" but not Tikhonov's uniform hypotheses, cites the wrong theorems, and the hypothesis fails generically at attention switching, which is where reorganization happens
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:963-965 (`sec:adiabatic_hierarchy`), citations `Born1927` and `Carr1981`.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The paragraph states the right kind of condition ("normally attracting equilibrium at frozen slow variables"). Tikhonov's theorem needs more: a compact slow region K on which the frozen fast equilibrium is isolated and uniformly asymptotically stable, which for a metric gradient flow is d^2_ff F >= c_1 G_f on K; a slow trajectory that stays in K; and a fast initial state in the basin. It also needs the effective separation to be small, which is the ratio of the slowest fast rate to the fastest slow rate (eta times the spectra of G^{-1}H in each block), not the bare ratio of learning rates eta_s/eta_q. The Theory states exactly these conditions (`c08_two_timescales.tex:255-275`, `open:obs-slow-manifold`). The cited Carr (1981) is center-manifold theory for nonhyperbolic equilibria. The relevant results are Tikhonov (1952, Mat. Sb. 31(73), 575-586) and Fenichel (1979, J. Differential Equations 31, 53-98); the latter is in `references.bib` but used only in Appendix I (3934).

Failure witness (`L07_clock_and_fold.py (5)`): one agent with a slow prior mean m, anchor strength alpha = 0.2, and two unit-precision neighbors at x = +/-1 under softmax attention at tau = 0.05. The reduced (envelope) free energy is F_red(mu; m) = (alpha/2)(mu - m)^2 - tau log sum_j exp(-(mu - x_j)^2/(2 tau)), the paper's `-tau sum log Z` form. For m below the critical value there are three fast equilibria (two minima with F_red'' = 1.2 and a saddle). At m* = 4.222329 the left minimum and the saddle merge (F_red'' = 0.0035 and -0.0012), a saddle-node, and the fast state must jump. Normal hyperbolicity is lost exactly at an attention-switching event. The paper's differentiation-convention paragraph (1399) already identifies that regime as the one where "the frozen-beta blocks are unreliable". The Theory's `prop:obs-attention-reduced-hessian` (c06:357) shows the same failure after source rows are optimized.
**Fix:** Replace the analogy sentence with: "Under the Tikhonov hypotheses (a compact slow region on which d^2_ff F >= c_1 G_f uniformly, a slow orbit remaining in it, and a fast initial state in the basin), the fast variables track the conditional minimizer as epsilon -> 0 (Tikhonov 1952), and for smooth data a locally invariant slow manifold exists O(epsilon)-close to the critical manifold (Fenichel 1979). These hypotheses fail at attention-switching folds of the critical manifold, where the reduction must be replaced by jump or relaxation-oscillation analysis." Keep Born-Oppenheimer as a one-clause analogy at most; in BO the slaving is to instantaneous quantum eigenstates, which is a different mechanism. Cite Theory `open:obs-slow-manifold` and `prop:obs-attention-reduced-hessian`.
**Falsifies:** A proof that F_red has a uniformly nondegenerate conditional minimizer along every slow orbit the model visits; or evidence that the folds lie outside the visited region.
**Status:** C (conditional, correctly fenced as conjectural for GL+). The missing uniform hypotheses and the fold failure are not stated.

Remove "updated on the slow M-step" or confine it to the transformer PriorBank realization, where p_i is a trained parameter and not a shadow.
**Falsifies:** A derivation that the meta-belief update is slower than constituent updates by a factor that vanishes with epsilon, independent of f_coh.

### L07-6: Appendix D. The covariance problem is linear in precision; the exact gauge-invariant existence and global-stability criterion is rho(L) < 2; the stated condition is gauge dependent; the alignment-dominated limit generally does not exist
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2720-2738 (proposition "Fixed point of the frozen-attention covariance map" and proof), 2800-2866 (alignment-dominated regime, `eq:beta_weighted_precision`), 2842-2856 (gradient flow and local stability).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** In precision coordinates Lambda_i = Sigma_i^{-1}, `eq:sigma_fixed_point_beta` reads 2 Lambda = A + L(Lambda), where A_i = Sigma_{p,i}^{-1} + Lambda_{o,i} and L(Lambda)_i = sum_j beta_ij Omega_ij^{-T} Lambda_j Omega_ij^{-1}, a completely positive linear map. The natural-gradient flow dSigma/dt = -2 eta Sigma (dF_i/dSigma_i) Sigma becomes the linear system Lambda_dot = eta (A + L(Lambda) - 2 Lambda). Three exact statements follow. The first is derived here; the second and third use Krein-Rutman for the cone-preserving L and its adjoint on the self-dual PSD cone.

(i) An SPD fixed point exists if and only if rho(L) < 2, and it is then unique, Lambda* = sum_k (L/2)^k A/2. For necessity, pair with a nonzero PSD Perron eigenvector W of L*: (2 - rho) <W, Lambda> = <W, A> > 0.

(ii) If rho(L) < 2 the receiver-only natural-gradient flow converges globally and exponentially from any SPD initial condition, at rate 2 eta (1 - rho(L)/2) (script check 5: max Re eig = -0.76378 against the prediction -0.76378, residual 3e-15).

(iii) If rho(L) >= 2 the pairing <W, Lambda> grows without bound, so the precision diverges and the covariance collapses. Script check 7: holonomy diag(0.6^2, 1), rho(L) = 2.78; after flow time 30, Lambda_1 has entries of order 2.8e10 along e_1, and the Picard iterate gives Sigma_1 of order 1.2e-9 there.

The proposition's hypothesis, lambda_max(C_i) < 2 with C_i = sum_j beta_ij (Omega_ij Omega_ij^T)^{-1} = L(I)_i, is sufficient because rho(L) <= max_i lambda_max(L(I)_i). It is not gauge invariant. For a flat GL connection Omega_ij = U_i U_j^{-1}, the map L is conjugate to the row-stochastic beta, so rho(L) = 1 for every choice of frames. Check 6: U = diag(10, 1), I, [[1, 3], [0, 0.5]] gives max lambda_max(C_i) = 610 while rho(L) = 1.0000, and the fixed point exists (min eig Lambda* = 0.358) and is stable. For orthogonal transports rho(L) = 1 as well. The condition bites only for non-orthogonal holonomy, which is where the paper's GL emphasis lies.

The local-stability paragraph (2847-2851) establishes stability of each agent's flow with the others frozen. Joint stability of the simultaneous receiver-only flow does not follow from own-block positivity in general. Here it holds exactly under natural gradient, by (ii). The displayed flow at 2842 is the Euclidean one, which contradicts the caveat at 2736 that the flow "must be" the affine-invariant natural-gradient flow. For the Euclidean joint Jacobian -(I - L/2) D, a 4000-trial random search with rho(L) < 1.95 found no unstable instance (largest max Re eigenvalue -8e-8). That is an observation, not a theorem.

Check 8, holonomy diag(0.9, 1): rho(L) = 1.2346, a fixed point at A_c = 1 and none at A_c = 10, 100, 1000. Holonomy diag(1.2, 1): the precision in the contracted direction goes to zero like 1/A_c (0.766, 0.247, 0.032, 0.0033), so that covariance diverges. The boxed limit `eq:beta_weighted_precision` is the eigen-equation Lambda = L(Lambda). It has an SPD solution only when rho(L) = 1 with an interior Perron eigenvector, as for flat or orthogonal transport. The validity condition at 2864 does not capture this.
**Fix:** Replace the proposition with the exact statement: "In precision coordinates the frozen-attention receiver-only stationarity condition is linear, 2 Lambda = A + L(Lambda). A unique SPD solution exists if and only if rho(L) < 2, and the receiver-only natural-gradient flow then converges to it globally at rate 2 eta_Sigma (1 - rho(L)/2). For flat or orthogonal transport rho(L) = 1; rho(L) differs from 1 only through non-orthogonal holonomy. If rho(L) >= 2 the precisions diverge." **Falsifies:** An SPD fixed point of `eq:sigma_fixed_point_beta` for some instance with rho(L) >= 2 (excluded by the Perron pairing argument), or a flat-connection instance with rho(L) != 1.

### L07-7: "Time emerges; no external time is presupposed" is stronger than the construction; for autonomous flows the clock stops at a finite reading, and for driven flows the dissipation identity fails

(b) Finite total time. Add: "for an autonomous flow converging to an equilibrium the total clock reading is finite (Theory `prop:hist-analytic-finite-duration` for analytic F), so sustained clock advance requires sustained driving." At 1986 replace "singles out a preferred direction" with "singles out a line; its orientation is inherited from the flow parameter". The 1840 proper-time wording was raised in `pifb-geometry-review.md` (line 161); not re-raised here.
**Falsifies:** An autonomous convergent trajectory of the paper's flow with infinite Fisher length (possible only for non-analytic F, per the Theory counterexample), or an orientation for tau defined without t or F.

### L07-10: Frame-sector well-posedness and subgroup scope
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1002 (telescoping to xi = U^{-1} grad_U F), 1000 ("keeps U_i in G exactly at every step").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Telescoping holds for G = GL+(K), which is open in gl(K). For a proper subgroup (e.g. SO(3)), D exp[v] is the orthogonal projection of grad_U F onto T_U G, not grad_U F itself. ## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A physics referee will read "mass", "inertia", "kinetic term" and "omega^2 = k/m" literally and will immediately note that every equation of motion in the paper is first order. The cleanest repair is to call the Hessian a stiffness throughout, call the Fisher metric the friction (inverse mobility) tensor of an overdamped flow, and move the inertia postulate into a clearly marked speculative subsection with its own equation of motion. As written, the postulate yields omega = 1 for every mode (L07-1), which removes the one physical content the mass analogy claimed.

The timescale section uses machine-learning vocabulary that a physics reader will not parse: "learning rate", "M-step", "PriorBank", "per-token statistics", "autograd", "companion transformer codebase", and "value aggregation sum alpha_ij V_j" in Appendix D (2885). In the physics version, replace "learning rate" by "block rate" or "mobility", move PriorBank and code remarks to a methods footnote, and state the flow time's units. The Born-Oppenheimer analogy is fine as one clause. The standard dynamical-systems citations are Tikhonov and Fenichel, and a statistical-physics referee will also expect the overdamped adiabatic-elimination literature.

The information clock section is closest to Wheeler's thesis. Its physics reading is stronger if it says plainly that the clock is a reparameterization-invariant length of an oriented history, that orientation comes from dissipation, and that a sustained clock needs sustained driving (L07-7). That is a more defensible and more interesting statement for a physics audience than "time emerges".

Appendix D can be shortened by half. The linear precision-coordinate formulation (L07-6) replaces the Thompson-metric machinery with a two-line argument and a sharper, gauge-invariant result that a physicist can check by hand.
