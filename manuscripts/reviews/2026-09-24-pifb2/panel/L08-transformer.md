## Findings

### L08-1: Multi-head attention is not a block-diagonal restriction of the posited functional; per-head rows and tau = kappa sqrt(d_head) are extra, undeclared structure
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1266-1272 (`eq:multihead_gauge_group`), 1020, 1231 ("per-head identification $K_q \to d_{\mathrm{head}}$"), 418 (per-head holonomy), against `eq:pointwise_free_energy` (669-673) and `eq:free_energy_functional_final`.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The canonical functional carries one normalized row $\beta_{ij}$ per agent, with energy $\mathrm{KL}(q_i\|\Omega_{ij}q_j)$ over the full $K_q$ fiber and $\tau=\kappa\sqrt{K_q}$ (673). Restricting $\Omega$ and $\Sigma$ to $\mathrm{GL}(d_{\mathrm{head}})^H$ makes the energy additive over blocks, $E_{ij}=\sum_h E^{(h)}_{ij}$ (residual 1.8e-15), so the single row becomes $\mathrm{softmax}_j(-\sum_h E^{(h)}_{ij}/\kappa\sqrt{K_q})$: one attention pattern shared by all heads. Standard multi-head attention has $H$ independent rows. In `L08_heads_rope_causal.py` (1), with $H=3$ and $d_h=2$, the single row is (0.000, 0.035, 0.030, 0.000, 0.934); the three per-head rows differ from it by up to 0.589 and from each other by up to 0.381. Per-head rows require $H$ exclusive source labels (one entropy term and one multiplier per $(i,h)$), and only then does $\tau$ scale with $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{head}}}$. The Theory shows the gap is not cosmetic: with a shared receiver state the head-label posterior does not factor (`prop:obs-selective-head-coupling`, odds ratio $e^{a^2/3}\neq1$), and the label-level multi-head model is open (`open:obs-multihead-attention`). There is a second conflation. In Vaswani et al. each $W_Q^h\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_{\mathrm{head}}}$ reads the whole residual vector, so the head subspaces are overlapping projections, not a direct-sum coordinate split of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$. The block-diagonal group acts on the concatenated projected space, not on the embedding, so "consistent with the low-rank lift" (1266) does not follow.
**Fix:** Replace the identification by: "Multi-head attention requires, in addition to the block-diagonal restriction $\mathrm{GL}(d_{\mathrm{head}})^H$, one exclusive source label per receiving pair $(i,h)$, hence $H$ attention rows per agent, each with its own entropy term and temperature $\kappa\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{head}}}$. This per-head row structure is declared rather than derived; with a shared receiver state the head labels do not factor (Theory, `prop:obs-selective-head-coupling`), and the label-level model is open (Theory, `open:obs-multihead-attention`)." Drop "consistent with the low-rank lift", and state at 1231 that the replacement $K_q\to d_{\mathrm{head}}$ presupposes per-head rows.
**Falsifies:** A term in `eq:pointwise_free_energy` or `eq:free_energy_functional_final` that carries a head index on $\beta$, or a derivation that the block-diagonal restriction alone produces $H$ independent normalized rows.
**Status:** S presented as an identification ("identifies the multi-head gauge group"); inflated.

### L08-2: Hypothesis (N) as stated in Theorem `thm:transformer_recovery` does not remove the bias the proof actually produces
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1144 (hypothesis (N)), 1172-1188 (key-bias analysis), 1226 ("Under hypothesis (N) in its exact pre-projection form").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Under (R1)-(R3) the surviving key term is $-\|\mu_j\|^2/(2\sigma^2)$ on the raw means (1164-1170; recomputed, query-norm cancellation residual 1.1e-16). The theorem's (N) reads "$\|K_j\|$ is constant in $j$", with $K_j=\mu_j^\top W_K$ (1146). The two conditions differ whenever $W_K$ is not a scaled orthogonal matrix. In `L08_kl_expansion.py` (e), keys with $\|K_j\|=1$ for all $j$ (spread 2.2e-15) and $\|\mu_j\|$ spread 34.4 give $\max_j|\beta^{\mathrm{model}}_{ij}-\beta^{\mathrm{Vaswani}}_{ij}|=0.997$; with $\|\mu_j\|$ constant the difference is 1.1e-16. The proof concedes that post-projection normalization works "along the present route only when the Gaussian beliefs are taken over the projected key coordinates" (1183) and then invokes (N) "in its exact pre-projection form" (1226), which is not the form stated in the theorem.
**Fix:** Restate (N) as "$\|\mu_j\|$ is constant in $j$ (for example, exact pre-projection normalization without a learned gain), or the residual $-\|\mu_j\|^2/2\sigma^2$ is absorbed into a learned per-key bias." Alternatively, put the query and key maps inside the divergence, as the Theory's comparison maps $S^Q_{\mathrm{ref}},S^K_{\mathrm{ref}}$ do (`cor:obs-selective-softmax`, `eq:obs-selective-dot-product`). On that route the bias is the whitened projected key norm, the current wording of (N) becomes correct, and (R3) becomes a choice of comparison record instead of a replacement of the cross term.
**Falsifies:** A reading under which $K_j$ in (N) denotes the unprojected mean, or a hypothesis elsewhere in the theorem that forces $W_K$ to be scaled orthogonal.
**Status:** P claimed ("proving Theorem"), but the stated hypotheses do not entail the conclusion; the corrected statement is P.

### L08-3: The canonical scalar potential is not autoregressive under a causal prior; the reported language model runs the receiver-only field that the manuscript calls optional and unused
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:594 ("a deliberately asymmetric receiver-only field that is not used by the checked-in optimizer"), 733, 1441, 2430 (sender rows), 605-612 (`eq:causal_prior`), 1008 and 1302 (the section trains "the general gauge architecture itself" as "a working language model").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** With the causal prior, $\beta_{\ell i}=0$ for $i>\ell$, so the sender rows $\sum_\ell\beta_{\ell i}\partial E_{\ell i}/\partial\mu_i$ that the manuscript puts in the canonical gradient (2430: "Its receiver and sender terms together form $\nabla S_{\mathrm{surr}}$") come only from later tokens $\ell>i$. Descending the full scalar therefore moves $q_i$ toward $\Omega_{\ell i}^{-1}\mu_\ell$ with $\ell=i+1$, the embedding of the token that $q_i$ is asked to predict. In `L08_heads_rope_causal.py` (5), changing token 2 changes the full-scalar gradient at token 0 by 0.229, while the receiver-only gradient at token 0 does not depend on tokens 1 and 2. The companion code at HEAD `ce59523` detaches the key slot (`mu_k, sigma_k = mu.detach(), sigma.detach()`, `kernels.py` 585 and 780; default `gradient_mode = "filtering"`, `config.py` 382), which is the receiver-only field of 2430. The trained model of Section `sec:scaling_validation` is therefore an instance of the field that 594 calls unused and that 1441 says has "no established conservative-Hamiltonian reading". The statement at 594 holds for the multi-agent `FullVFE` path and fails for the transformer.
**Fix:** Add after `eq:causal_attention`: "Under a causal prior the sender rows of the scalar potential carry information from later to earlier positions, so an autoregressive model must use the receiver-only field of Appendix A (the key slot held fixed during each update). The language model of Section `sec:scaling_validation` does so; the symmetric-Hessian and stiffness statements of Section `sec:mass` apply to the full scalar of the multi-agent path, not to that trained model." Qualify 594 to match.
**Falsifies:** A shipped transformer configuration that differentiates the key slot and passes a no-future-leakage test, or a statement elsewhere in PIFB2 that the language model uses the receiver-only field.
**Status:** D (the gradient structure), with an implementation statement that is wrong for the reported experiment. Code note outside this manuscript: the registered alternative `gradient_mode="smoothing"` differentiates both slots, and a grep of `config.py` found no guard against pairing it with a causal prior (not exercised).

### L08-4: The causal mask does not make holonomy "purely formal"; frustration needs undirected cycles, which every causal mask on three or more tokens contains
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:418 ("closed loops in the data flow do not exist, and the holonomy ... is a property of the connection field ... rather than of the actual message-passing pattern"), 1780 ("acquires direct dynamical content for bidirectional/encoder attention rather than purely autoregressive (causal-DAG) attention").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** For tokens $1<2<3$ under a causal mask, row 3 attends to 1 and 2 and row 2 attends to 1. The directed graph is acyclic, but its underlying undirected graph is a triangle. With $q_2$ at its target $\Omega_{21}q_1$, row 3 faces the two targets $\Omega_{31}\mu_1$ and $\Omega_{32}\Omega_{21}\mu_1$, which coincide only when the holonomy $\Omega_{13}\Omega_{32}\Omega_{21}$ fixes $\mu_1$. In `L08_heads_rope_causal.py` (3), receiver-only minimization with unit covariances and $\beta=1/2$ gives minimal row-3 energy 0, 0.0306, 0.1149, 0.3540 for holonomy angle 0, 0.5, 1, 2, equal to $\|H-I\|_F^2/16$ in every case. The holonomy therefore changes the fixed-point energy, the attention row, and the belief of a causal model. This is the frustration mechanism of lattice gauge and spin-glass models on undirected plaquettes; no directed cycle is needed. The 2026-09-23 GL(K) panel's minimal-experiment remark ("a graph with cycles, which the causal graph lacks") makes the same claim, and this finding contradicts it too.
**Fix:** Replace the two sentences by: "The causal mask removes directed cycles but not undirected ones: every triple $i<j<k$ spans a plaquette, and a non-identity holonomy on it frustrates the consensus of the later token. Holonomy therefore has dynamical content in autoregressive attention once the connection is promoted to Regime II; bidirectional attention adds edges traversed in both directions, not the first nontrivial plaquettes." Keep the Regime II conditional.
**Falsifies:** A causal-mask energy in which the row-$k$ minimum is independent of $\Omega_{kj}\Omega_{ji}\Omega_{ik}$ at fixed $q_i,q_j$.
**Status:** S stated as structural fact; wrong as stated.

### L08-6: At a point in Regime I every frame is pure gauge, so "gauge-fixed", the untied route, and RoPE-as-gauge-frame need a gauge-invariant restatement for a physics referee
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1008 ("gauge-fixed"), 1098-1135 (untied route: "retains $\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$ non-trivially", "$M_{ij}$ ranges over all of $\mathrm{GL}(d_k)$", "genuinely untied"), 1274-1280 (RoPE), 1771.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The manuscript's own KL invariance gives $\mathrm{KL}(q_i\|\Omega_{ij}q_j)=\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal N(x_i,P_i)\|\mathcal N(x_j,P_j))$ with $x=U^{-1}\mu$ and $P=U^{-1}\Sigma U^{-\top}$ (residual 2.7e-10), and the untied objects are $H_j=P_j^{-1}$ and $k_j=P_j^{-1}x_j$ (residuals 2.1e-15, 1.0e-15). In gauge-invariant variables the transport is the identity, the query-key bilinear is the SPD precision $P_j^{-1}$, and the untiedness comes entirely from the key covariance. The $\mathrm{GL}(d_k)$ range of $M_{ij}$ (1126) is a property of writing the same bilinear against local-frame means, that is, of a gauge choice. (R2) is correctly called gauge fixing only because Regime I at a point is flat, so the per-agent gauge $h_i=UU_i^{-1}$ sets $\Omega_{ij}=I$. For RoPE the transport is a constant $\mathrm{U}(1)^{d_k/2}$ potential on a one-dimensional lattice, which has no plaquettes and is pure gauge. Its physical content is that the content vector (and, in the implementation, the token priors) are held position-independent in the local frame, so the frame field acts as a fixed background section, not a dynamical gauge field. The manuscript's treatment of explicit symmetry breaking (735-928) is the natural home for this reading.
**Fix:** Add one paragraph at the start of `sec:untied_qk`: "In Regime I at a single point the frames are pure gauge, and by the KL invariance theorem the attention depends only on the common-frame data $(x_i,P_i)$ with trivial transport. The frame dependence below is therefore a statement about which quantities the model holds fixed in local frames (content means, token priors), that is, about an explicit background section rather than a dynamical gauge field. In common-frame variables $Q_i=x_i$ and $K_j=P_j^{-1}x_j$." Recast "RoPE is the position-dependent gauge specialization" as "RoPE is a constant pure-gauge $\mathrm{U}(1)^{d_k/2}$ background on the token chain."
**Falsifies:** A gauge-invariant quantity at a single point in Regime I (a holonomy, an invariant of $(x_i,P_i)$) that depends on the $U_i$ beyond the common-frame beliefs.
**Status:** D algebra, S interpretation; "captures the rotary positional structure" and "genuinely untied" inflate a parameterization statement into structure.

### L08-7: RoPE paragraph: inconsistent sign convention and a too-narrow statement of when the relative-position property holds
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1131-1135 (untied-route rotary identification), 1274-1280 (RoPE paragraph).
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** The untied route gives $Q_i=U_i^\top\mu_i$, which matches Su et al.'s $R_{\Theta,m}W_qx_m$ (Eq. 16) only for $U_i=R(\theta_i)^{-1}$; the RoPE paragraph sets $U_i=R(\theta_i)$. With $U_i=R(\theta_i)$ the KL cross term is $\mu_i^\top R_{i-j}\mu_j$ (residual 2.2e-16), the transpose of the RoPE kernel $R_{j-i}$ (difference 0.335 on a random draw). Both are relative, so nothing breaks, but the two paragraphs use opposite identifications. Second, "the relative-position-only property is therefore specific to the abelian rotation sector" is too narrow for the gauge-KL score. With $U_m=\exp(mA)$ for any $A\in\mathfrak{gl}(d_k)$, including non-antisymmetric (noncompact) $A$, and a position-independent $C$, the full energy is relative: $E(3,1)-E(6,4)=-1.1\times10^{-12}$, while non-commuting frames give 3009. Orthogonality is forced only in the dot-product form, where $R_m^\top R_n=R_{n-m}$ at $m=n$ gives $R_m^\top R_m=I$. Linearity of $\phi_m$ in $m$ is also required and is not stated.
**Fix:** Write $U_i=R(\theta_i)^{-1}$ in both places (or note the frequency sign flip once), and replace the sentence by: "The relative-position property of the gauge score requires the position-to-frame map to be a one-parameter subgroup, $U_m=\exp(mA)$; in the transported-KL score $A$ may be any element of $\mathfrak{gl}(d_k)$, while the dot-product form additionally requires $A^\top=-A$."
**Falsifies:** An $A\in\mathfrak{gl}(d_k)$, not antisymmetric, for which the transported KL with fixed $C$ fails to depend only on $i-j$.
**Status:** D; minor imprecision.

### L08-8: The self pair carries the row-minimum energy $E_{ii}=0$; under the causal prior the diagonal carries the largest weight in every row
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:605-612 (`eq:causal_prior` includes $j=i$), 580-590 (`eq:softmax_attention_general`), 1083 ($\Omega_{ii}=I$).
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** $\Omega_{ii}=I$ gives $E_{ii}=\mathrm{KL}(q_i\|q_i)=0$, and $\mathrm{KL}\ge0$, so for any prior uniform on the support the self weight is the row maximum; `L08_heads_rope_causal.py` (4) finds this in 2000 of 2000 random rows. The physics is reasonable (keeping one's own belief costs nothing), but a reader comparing `eq:causal_attention` with GPT masking ("precisely the masked attention", 612) is not told that the unmodified gauge row concentrates on self as $\tau$ decreases.
**Fix:** After `eq:causal_attention` add: "Because $\Omega_{ii}=I$, $E_{ii}=0$ is the smallest energy in every row, so the self pair carries the largest weight whenever the prior is uniform on the support; the equality with masked dot-product attention refers to the support and normalization, and a prior that removes or down-weights $j=i$ is a separate modeling choice."
**Falsifies:** A row with a prior uniform on its support in which some $j\ne i$ has $E_{ij}<0$.
**Status:** D; omission.
