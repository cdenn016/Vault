**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Afsari's paper separates three statements, and they govern different objectives. (i) Karcher's local bound, p. 658, eq. (1.3): "ρ < ½ min{inj M, π/(2√Δ)}". (ii) Kendall, as reported by Afsari p. 658: "if ρ < min{inj M, π/(2√Δ)}, then there exists a unique solipsistic Karcher mean". (iii) Afsari, p. 657, eq. (1.2), "ρ < r_cx ≜ ½ min{inj M, π/√Δ}", and Theorem 2.1, eq. (2.1), p. 659, with conclusion on p. 660: the Lᵖ center "is a unique point and lies inside B" (1 < p ≤ ∞; ρ_{Δ,p} = r_cx for p ≥ 2). This is the global Fréchet minimizer over all of M. That global uniqueness is also what makes the proof of thm:rg_covariance at 3811 immediate. Attribution: Afsari p. 656 credits the general-manifold construction to Grove and Karcher [10] (Math. Z. 1973), with Cartan earlier on Hadamard manifolds. "The construction is Karcher's" should therefore read Grove-Karcher, with Karcher 1977 supplying the estimates.

None of this contradicts 1573's point that no bi-invariant *Fréchet objective* exists. It does show that the "obstruction" is to a variance characterization, not to a bi-invariant mean.

Under a left-invariant Riemannian metric instead, the Riemannian center of mass exists and is unique below Afsari's radius (Afsari 2011, Thm. 2.1), but is only left-equivariant." At 1655 and 2150, describe the additive form as "the first fixed-point iterate, from the identity, of the exponential barycenter", and restrict the open problem to the choice of convention, which 1575 already argues is a gauge choice. [G1-meta-agent-construction-recompute] ### G1-5: lem:weight_contraction is true with a better rate, but its proof uses the wrong coordinates and leaves the norm, ball, and support condition implicit
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1637-1641.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Re-derivation. With normalized weights w̄_i and g_i = ∇_q̄ KL(ζ_i ‖ q̄), the derivative is dT[h] = Σ_i w̄_i (⟨g_i − ḡ, h⟩)(ζ_i − ζ̄), a weighted covariance between the constituent moments ζ_i = (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i) and the gradient differences. The proof bounds the gradients uniformly and so obtains O(√ε). But the gradient differences are themselves O(√ε): the mean part is Σ̄⁻¹(μ̃_j − μ̃_i) independent of q̄, and the covariance part is O(√ε(1 + R)) on a ball of radius R. So Lip(T) = O(ε). Measured (`G1_weight_contraction.py` (1)): on the convex hull, which T maps into after one step, Lip/ε = 0.47, 0.52, 0.53, 0.45, 0.41, 0.44, 0.73 for ε = 1e-4 ... 1 (K = 2, n = 4, spectrum [0.5, 2]), and 0.37-0.56 for K = 3. On a fixed ball of radius 1, Lip is larger (3.0 at ε = 1, so not a contraction there). The pitchfork family gives Lip = ε/2 exactly. The proof writes T as "the normalized-weight convex combination of the constituent expectation parameters η̃_i", but the implementation map drops the dispersion term (eq:meta_agent_sigma_impl). It is a convex combination of (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i), not of η̃_i = (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i + μ̃_iμ̃_iᵀ); the latter would reinstate the dispersion. The weights also vanish if every χ_i(x) = 0, so Σ_i χ_i > 0 must be assumed. No counterexample to the lemma as stated was found. With C unspecified it is only falsifiable through the necessary condition ε₀ ≤ 2.
**Fix:** State: "In the coordinates (μ, Σ) with the Frobenius norm, T maps the bounded-spectrum set into the convex hull H of {(μ̃_i, Σ̃_i)}, and on H, Lip(T) ≤ C(λ_min, λ_max)·ε; for ε < ε₀ = 1/C (necessarily ε₀ ≤ 2 in identity-covariance units) the fixed point is unique and the iteration converges geometrically from any start." Replace "expectation parameters η̃_i" by "moments (μ̃_i, Σ̃_i)" and add "Σ_i χ_i > 0".
**Falsifies:** A configuration in the hull with Lip(T)/ε growing without bound as ε → 0.
**Status:** D (proof sketch, true), with rate understated and a coordinate slip.

## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A mathematical-physics referee will want the uniqueness hypotheses stated as a theorem with every constant fixed in the paper's own units, not as a parenthetical with a normalization the reader must undo. The frame mean needs one short proposition: "Let G be a compact Lie group with bi-invariant metric d_G² = ‖log(U⁻¹V)‖_F², sectional curvature bounded by Δ, and injectivity radius inj. If the frames {U_i} lie in a geodesic ball of radius ρ < ½ min{inj, π/√Δ}, the weighted Fréchet mean exists, is unique in G, lies in the ball, and is left- and right-equivariant; for SO(K) this radius is π/√2 in d_G, a rotation angle of π/2." The referee will also want local and global statements kept apart, and Karcher, Kendall, and Afsari cited for what each proved. The trap is that "Karcher mean" names both a local critical point and a global minimizer. Two further points will matter at a physics venue. First, a gauge theorist will ask at once whether U_I is observable. Section 1575 answers no, so the Definition should say so, rather than presenting the frame barycenter as the defining datum and later retracting it. Second, a physicist knows GL(n) as the frame group of a vector bundle and knows it has no invariant positive metric. They will not know that a bi-invariant *mean* still exists through the Cartan-Schouten connection, which is standard in the Lie-group statistics literature and short to state. The lemma, the detector constants (Γ_min = 0.5, τ = 0.05), and the implementation file path are machine-learning register. For a physics journal, the detector subsection (1606-1615) and the implementation paragraphs of 1620-1655 could move to an appendix or a methods note. The body would keep the variational barycenter, the licensing criterion, and one sentence that the implemented detector is a similarity filter.
