# Findings digest for C05

[L02-geometry] ### L02-3: The "mass matrix" is a coordinate Hessian, which is not a tensor away from critical points; the kinetic-metric postulate does not specify a connection
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1402 ("defines a configuration-space metric on belief space"), 1445-1449, 1521-1526 (postulate, `eq:full_kinetic`).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The second variation is taken in the flat affine chart of `Sym(K)` (line 1447). Such a Hessian is chart-independent only at critical points of `F`. The postulate evaluates it "at consensus" (`mu_i = tilde mu_k`, `Sigma_i = Omega Sigma_k Omega^T`), which is not a critical point whenever the prior shadow or sensory terms are off target. In `L02_hessian_blocks.py`, for `f(Sigma) = KL(N(0,Sigma)||N(0,Sigma_p)) + ½ tr(Lambda_o Sigma)`, the second variation along the same tangent vectors agrees in the `Sigma` and `Lambda = Sigma^{-1}` charts at the critical point (relative difference 6.1e-7, finite-difference level) and differs at a non-critical point (0.3297 versus 0.4922, relative 0.49). The flat-chart Hessian and the Riemannian Hessian of the Fisher/AIRM metric that the paper uses for natural gradients differ by a Christoffel-times-gradient term, and the implementation parameterizes by Cholesky factors, which is a third chart. The Hessian is also not a metric off consensus: the manuscript itself records a negative-semidefinite `-tau^{-1} Cov` correction (line 1413) and a rank-one negative prior term (line 1510).
**Fix:** Either impose the postulate only at critical points of `F` (full stationarity, not only consensus) or define `M` as the covariant Hessian of a declared connection, naturally the Fisher Levi-Civita connection. Replace "defines a configuration-space metric" with "defines a symmetric bilinear form, positive definite at the reciprocal-consensus critical point".
**Falsifies:** A statement in the text that "consensus" includes stationarity of the prior and sensory terms, or an explicit connection choice elsewhere.
**Status:** The block formulas are P (verified below). The metric reading is a postulate (F4) and is correctly fenced, but its chart dependence is unstated.

[L02-geometry] ### L02-4: The gauge-sector kinetic metric `G_phi = G_pull` is flat and not invariant under the declared gauge redundancy; "gauge-group curvature" is metric-dependent
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1526, `eq:pullback_metric` (984-995), 429 (gauge-group curvature).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Line 1526 takes `G_phi` from `eq:pullback_metric`, the pullback of the flat ambient Frobenius metric through `exp`. It is not left-trivialized, although line 1526 calls it "left-trivialized". Since `exp` is a local diffeomorphism where `dexp^L` is regular, `(gl(K), G_pull)` is locally isometric to flat `R^{K²}`, so its Riemann tensor vanishes (a derivation: a pullback of a flat metric by a local diffeomorphism is flat). The kinetic term `½ G_pull(phi_dot, phi_dot) = ½ ||U_dot||_F^2` is not invariant under the manuscript's global redundancy `U -> U g` (line 341). In `L02_retraction.py` (10), `||U_dot g||^2 / ||U_dot||^2 = 5.795` for a random `g` in GL(3), and exactly 1 for orthogonal `g`. It is not left invariant either. This is expected, since GL⁺(K) has no bi-invariant Riemannian metric (Milnor 1976, Lemma 7.5), but line 1526 does not record it. Line 429 treats "the structure group G" as "a curved manifold" and says group curvature "determines how gauge transformations compose". Composition is governed by noncommutativity, which is metric-free. The Riemannian curvature of GL(K) depends on the chosen metric: zero for `G_pull`, and mixed sign for left-invariant metrics (Milnor 1976). The SO(3) ≅ RP³ remark holds for the bi-invariant metric and does not transfer to the canonical GL(K).
**Fix:** At line 1526 state that `G_pull` is flat and breaks the right-translation redundancy for non-orthogonal `g`, so `eq:full_kinetic` is frame-dependent in its gauge sector. At line 429 separate noncommutativity from curvature and name the metric whenever a group curvature is claimed.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that `½||U_dot||_F^2` is invariant under `U -> U g` for non-orthogonal `g`. The script shows it is not.
**Status:** The line-429 curvature paragraph is S presented as structure. The `G_pull` construction itself is P (verified below).

**Status:** D. Internal inconsistency, not status inflation.

[L07-dynamics] ### L07-1: Under the stated kinetic-metric postulate every mode has unit frequency, so "omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff" is false, not definitional
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1330 (section intro), 1391 ("exhibits Newtonian-analogy scaling"), 1512 (Postulate, kinetic metric, `sec:velocity_quadratic`), 1762 (Discussion).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** The postulate (1512) makes the kinetic coefficient the same frozen-beta Hessian M that is the potential stiffness at consensus. With L = (1/2) xi_dot^T M xi_dot - (1/2) dxi^T M dxi, the Euler-Lagrange equation is M xi_ddot = -M xi, so xi_ddot = -xi. Every normal mode has omega = 1, whatever the precision. `L07_mass_vs_friction.py (d)`: det(K - w^2 M) = 0 with K = M has the single root w^2 = 1 for a generic symbolic 2x2 SPD M. The text says the harmonic relation "reduces to a per-direction identity", which is correct, and then says omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff is "a definitional consequence of the postulate" (1512, 1762). That is incompatible with omega = 1. The only reading that gives omega^2 of order 1/m is the one at 1330 ("modes whose stiffness is dominated by contributions independent of the agent's own prior precision"). That reading takes the inertia to be the bare prior precision alone while the stiffness keeps the coupling blocks: omega^2 = (lambda_p + k_c)/lambda_p, which is of order k_c/lambda_p only when k_c >> lambda_p (script (d)). The paper therefore carries two incompatible inertia assignments. The claim at 1391 that the metric "exhibits" Newtonian scaling in the isolated-agent limit is false under both readings: there M = Lambda_p = stiffness, so omega = 1.
**Fix:** Under the postulate as written, replace every "omega^2 proportional to 1/m_eff" with: "Under the kinetic-metric postulate the stiffness and the inertia coincide, so every linear mode about consensus has the same frequency (unity in flow-time units). A precision-dependent frequency would require an inertia tensor that is not the stiffness tensor, which the construction does not supply." Delete "which justifies calling it a mass matrix" at 1391. If the bare-mass reading at 1330 is intended, state it as a second, different postulate: inertia equal to the prior precision, stiffness equal to the full Hessian. Then omega^2 = eig(Lambda_p^{-1} M) = 1 + eig(Lambda_p^{-1} K_coupling). That relation is testable, and it is not an identity.
**Falsifies:** A kinetic term in the paper whose coefficient differs from the potential Hessian at consensus, or an equation of motion other than the Euler-Lagrange equation of T - F from which the stated scaling follows.
**Status:** S (postulated analogy). The prose inflates it to a "consequence" and says the metric "exhibits" the scaling.

[L07-dynamics] ### L07-2: In the dynamics the paper actually runs, precision plays the role of friction, not inertia; "hard to move", "slower response" and "difficulty of accelerating" are false as dynamical statements, and the proposed omega test cannot work
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1509 ("hard to move under the dynamics"), 1515 ("higher precision implies larger coefficient implies slower response"), 1512 (operational test: "fitting omega from the autocorrelation of a natural-gradient trajectory"), 1758-1762 (overdamped analogy; "difficulty of accelerating").
**Severity:** high (for a physics venue)
**Evidence:** The flow the paper defines (961, 972-975) is first order: xi_dot = -eta G^{-1} grad F, with G the Fisher metric. Compare the paper's own display at 1758, gamma q_dot = -grad V. The Fisher metric G/eta occupies the friction slot gamma. No term in the dynamics carries the m slot. Three consequences were computed.

(a) Isolated agent at q = p, `L07_mass_vs_friction.py (a)`. Under the natural-gradient flow the linearized mean and covariance relaxation rates are exactly eta, for prior-precision scale s = 1, 10, 1000 (rates `[1. 1.]` and `[1. 1. 1.]` in every case). Under the Euclidean flow the rates are eta times the eigenvalues of Lambda_p (2.08, 0.92, rising to 2083, 917 at s = 1000), so a precise agent relaxes faster. Only the static compliance H^{-1} = Sigma_p (1.09 falling to 0.00109) and the mobility eta Sigma make a precise agent "hard to move". Both are overdamped response properties, not inertia.

(b) For any gradient flow with G SPD and H symmetric, G^{-1}H is similar to G^{-1/2} H G^{-1/2}. Its spectrum is therefore real (theorem), and there is no oscillation for an autocorrelation fit to measure. Numerically, max |Im eig| = 0 exactly over 2000 random GL(2) instances with asymmetric frozen beta, using the full receiver-plus-sender Hessian (script (b)).

(c) Complex eigenvalues appear only in the receiver-only truncation (script (c): directed 3-cycle with SO(2) holonomy, eigenvalues -1.769 +/- 0.743i, -1.409 +/- 0.951i, -0.122 +/- 0.208i; the full gradient on the same data gives real eigenvalues -0.144, -2.718, -3.438). That is the nonpotential field that 1392 says "has no established conservative-Hamiltonian reading". The proposed operational test measures an omega only where the mass reading has been disowned.

Status of 1755: the "kinetic term" (1/2) dmu^T Sigma^{-1} dmu is the Fisher line element ds^2/2, identical to the bit count of `eq:kl_arclength`. In the first-order flow it enters as the Rayleigh dissipation function R = (1/(2 eta)) xi_dot^T G xi_dot, with dF/dt = -2R. That is exactly the clock-dissipation identity `eq:clock_dissipation`.
**Fix:** Rewrite 1509 and 1515 as static statements: "a high-precision agent has large stiffness and small compliance, so a given force displaces it little; under the natural-gradient flow its linear relaxation rate is eta, independent of precision". Replace "difficulty of accelerating" (1762) with "difficulty of displacing". Replace the operational test at 1512: a first-order gradient flow has no omega. A dynamical test of an inertia postulate requires either a second-order equation of motion stated as a separate postulate (then write it, e.g. M xi_ddot + gamma G xi_dot = -grad F, and name its overdamped limit), or a measurement of static response. Consider adding one sentence that unifies Part I and Part II: "the only velocity-quadratic form carried by the actual dynamics is the Rayleigh dissipation (1/2 eta) xi_dot^T G xi_dot, whose integral is the information clock of Section `sec:time_info`." A physics referee will accept that reading. The mass reading, as it stands, contradicts the paper's own equations.
**Falsifies:** A second-order equation of motion used anywhere in the paper's computations or implementation; or a non-symmetric H in the potential (full-gradient) flow.
**Status:** The rate computations are D (exact here). The dynamical "hard to move" is inflated beyond what the paper's dynamics supports.

[L07-dynamics] ### L07-9: Notation and units in the dynamics sections
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:961 vs 1832 and 1985 (tau); 963 vs 1509 (epsilon); 1845 (Delta I = (eta/2)|Delta F|); 960 ("eta_q ~ O(1)").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** tau is reserved at 961 for the attention temperature. It is then the Fisher clock at 1832, a "coarse-grained shared update parameter" at 1985, and the curve parameter of q(tau) at 1998. A physics reader expects tau to be proper time, and 1840 invites that comparison. epsilon is both the timescale ratio (963) and the rock's precision scale (1509). The discrete bit count Delta I = (eta/2)|Delta F| holds with eta the dimensionless step size (unit step). In the continuous notation it is (eta Delta t/2)|Delta F|. `L07_clock_and_fold.py (3)`: KL(new||old) / ((h/2)|Delta F|) = 1.0638 at h = 0.1 and 1.0060 at h = 0.01, so correct to leading order. The rate eta has units of inverse flow time times inverse metric units, so "eta_q ~ O(1)" has no content until flow-time units are fixed.
**Fix:** Rename the clock to s or t_F, keep tau for temperature, and rename the rock precision scale (e.g. delta). Write Delta I = (h/2)|Delta F| with h the step. Replace "eta_q ~ O(1)" with a statement about the ratio of block rates.
**Falsifies:** Not applicable (notation).
**Status:** Notation only.

[L08-transformer] ### L08-3: The canonical scalar potential is not autoregressive under a causal prior; the reported language model runs the receiver-only field that the manuscript calls optional and unused
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:594 ("a deliberately asymmetric receiver-only field that is not used by the checked-in optimizer"), 733, 1441, 2430 (sender rows), 605-612 (`eq:causal_prior`), 1008 and 1302 (the section trains "the general gauge architecture itself" as "a working language model").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** With the causal prior, $\beta_{\ell i}=0$ for $i>\ell$, so the sender rows $\sum_\ell\beta_{\ell i}\partial E_{\ell i}/\partial\mu_i$ that the manuscript puts in the canonical gradient (2430: "Its receiver and sender terms together form $\nabla S_{\mathrm{surr}}$") come only from later tokens $\ell>i$. Descending the full scalar therefore moves $q_i$ toward $\Omega_{\ell i}^{-1}\mu_\ell$ with $\ell=i+1$, the embedding of the token that $q_i$ is asked to predict. In `L08_heads_rope_causal.py` (5), changing token 2 changes the full-scalar gradient at token 0 by 0.229, while the receiver-only gradient at token 0 does not depend on tokens 1 and 2. The companion code at HEAD `ce59523` detaches the key slot (`mu_k, sigma_k = mu.detach(), sigma.detach()`, `kernels.py` 585 and 780; default `gradient_mode = "filtering"`, `config.py` 382), which is the receiver-only field of 2430. The trained model of Section `sec:scaling_validation` is therefore an instance of the field that 594 calls unused and that 1441 says has "no established conservative-Hamiltonian reading". The statement at 594 holds for the multi-agent `FullVFE` path and fails for the transformer.
**Fix:** Add after `eq:causal_attention`: "Under a causal prior the sender rows of the scalar potential carry information from later to earlier positions, so an autoregressive model must use the receiver-only field of Appendix A (the key slot held fixed during each update). The language model of Section `sec:scaling_validation` does so; the symmetric-Hessian and stiffness statements of Section `sec:mass` apply to the full scalar of the multi-agent path, not to that trained model." Qualify 594 to match.
**Falsifies:** A shipped transformer configuration that differentiates the key slot and passes a no-future-leakage test, or a statement elsewhere in PIFB2 that the language model uses the receiver-only field.
**Status:** D (the gradient structure), with an implementation statement that is wrong for the reported experiment. Code note outside this manuscript: the registered alternative `gradient_mode="smoothing"` differentiates both slots, and a grep of `config.py` found no guard against pairing it with a causal prior (not exercised).
