[G2-appendix-theory-crosswalk] ### G2-2: The Theory has no replacement for the representation theorem; it declares the transported KL rather than deriving it, and what it can supply is a normalized-model statement about a different object

**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2956-3161 (`thm:representation_app` 3039, `eq:geometric_mean_target_app` 3048, `cor:closure_forces_kl` 3058, `lem:richness_app` 3120). Cited from 594, 2893, 2970, 3069 and 3160.

**Severity:** medium.

**Evidence:**
1. *The Theory declares the divergence.* The Theory defines the transported divergence KL(q_i‖(Θ_e)_#q_j) (c12 `def:full-graph-transported-divergences`) and the role divergence (c06 `eq:obs-selective-role-divergence` in `def:obs-selective-comparison`) by declaration. The softmax result (`cor:obs-selective-softmax`, `prop:full-graph-gibbs-rows`) holds for any fixed energies. c03 `prop:prob-law-point-energy` shows that such a law-point divergence is not the expectation of any sample energy. A grep of c*, g* and b* for f-divergence, argument-order and direction language finds no statement that selects KL or its direction for this object. The only KL direction the Theory derives is KL(Q‖Π) in the evidence bound (c05 `thm:elbo-exact-identity`). So the C1 program review's N6 entry, "Not needed once the source-label joint fixes the divergence", and L03-1's alternative fix ("anchor the KL direction on the Theory's source-label joint") overstate the Theory. The source-label joint fixes the row form. It does not fix the divergence.
2. *What the Theory can supply is about a different object.* In a normalized joint the exact block E-coordinate (c05 `prop:elbo-exact-e-coordinate` (iii); c12 `rem:full-graph-inference-loop`; Gaussian case g04 `thm:restrict-exact-block-optimum`, `eq:restrict-cavi-mean`) is log-linear in the factors with unit exponents (a product of experts). The agent's appearance in its neighbors' factors is included automatically, which answers the sender-row gap of L03-1 for that object. `G2_repr_crosswalk.py` (2) takes L03-1's priors p1=N(0,1) and p2=N(3,0.25) with a normalized pair factor e^{−(k1−k2)²/2}. The CAVI means equal the exact posterior mean (4.4e-16), the variances are 1/(A_i+w) (unit exponents), and the gradient of the joint negative ELBO at the fixed point is 2.5e-10 in all four coordinates. c06 `prop:obs-selective-receiver-update` gives the exact receiver update for the comparison-record model.
3. *The forward half of Theorem F is one exact identity.* KL(q‖p) + Σ_j β_j KL(q‖r_j) = (1+B)·KL(q‖g) − (1+B)·log Z_g, where g ∝ p^{1/(1+B)} Π r_j^{β_j/(1+B)}. `G2_repr_crosswalk.py` (1) confirms it by quadrature with random Gaussians and a non-optimal q; the maximum residual is 8.9e-16. The 1/(1+B) exponents are the reciprocal of the entropy weight (see G2-3).

**Fix:** Keep the theorem, scoped to the frozen-neighbor best response as L03-1 requires. Condense App. F to about 600 words: the identity above as a displayed lemma (it proves Step 2 and the dual-cost line at once), the scoped theorem statement, and one paragraph on opinion pooling (per L03-8). Move `lem:richness_app`, `cor:closure_forces_kl`, `rem:analyticity_role` and the proof of the converse to the supplement. Add after the statement: "The companion theory [DennisTheory] declares this transported divergence rather than deriving it. In a normalized joint the analogous coordinate update is a product of the prior and pair factors with unit exponents, and its stationarity includes each agent's appearance in its neighbors' factors [DennisTheory, Ch. 5, 'Local Gibbs bound and the exact block E-coordinate']. The present theorem characterizes the choice only for the frozen-neighbor best response." Do not claim that the Theory fixes the divergence.

**Falsifies:** A Theory statement that selects KL(q_i‖Θ_#q_j) among divergences, or that derives its argument order, for law-point energies.

**Status:** Theorem F is P for the receiver-only problem (L03-1). The phrase "pinned" is S beyond that scope. The C1 N6 claim about the Theory is unsupported.

[G2-appendix-theory-crosswalk] ### G2-3: The ½ in App. D and the 1/(1+B) in App. F share one origin: the consensus potential equilibrates each belief at temperature 1+B_i relative to Bayesian updating

**Location:** PIFB2.tex:2718-2795 (`eq:sigma_fixed_point_beta` 2746, `eq:sigma_fixed_point_map` 2755, the unlabeled proposition at 2759, the homogeneous limit 2770-2795), 2885-2890 (collapse to message passing), 2892-2945 (App. E), and 3048.

**Severity:** medium.

**Evidence:**
1. *Where the factor comes from.* The receiver functional KL(q‖p) + Σ_j β_ij KL(q‖Ω_ij q_j) carries the belief entropy −½log det Σ_i with weight 1+B_i. Minimizing E_q[U] − (1+B)H(q) gives q ∝ e^{−U/(1+B)}, a Gibbs state at temperature 1+B in units where Bayesian updating has temperature 1. `G2_appD_crosswalk.py` (1) finds the minimizer at Σ = (1+B)M^{-1} with M = Σ_p^{-1} + Λ_o + Σ_j β_ij(Ω_ijΣ_jΩ_ij^T)^{-1}. The residuals are 2.5e-8 at B=1 (which is `eq:sigma_fixed_point_beta`) and 7.6e-9 at B=2.5. The geometric-mean exponents of G2-2 (3) are the same effect.
2. *The Theory's normalized counterpart.* For a normalized Gaussian joint in the interaction family (g03 `def:gauss-interaction-family`), the mean-field covariance is Λ_ii^{-1} = (A_i + Σ_j W_ij)^{-1} (g04 `thm:restrict-exact-block-optimum`; g02 `prop:gauss-conditional-precision`). Script (2) confirms it by direct minimization of KL(Q‖P) over block-diagonal Gaussians with N=3 and K=2: covariance error 1.9e-8, mean error 4.7e-8.
3. *Homogeneous case.* PIFB2 finds Σ_∞ = Σ_0, "the equilibrium covariance reproduces the shared prior" (2795); script (3) reproduces this to 8.9e-16. A normalized two-agent joint with edge precision Σ_0^{-1} gives Σ_0/2 (4.4e-16), which is precision addition.

5. *App. E.* With a fixed coupling W, the expected quadratic form is exactly the common-covariance KL plus a trace term (g05 `prop:ig-fixed-covariance-kl` with g03 `prop:gauss-interaction-energy-kernel`); script (4) agrees by Monte Carlo to relative 1.7e-3 at n = 4e5. L07-6 and L10-1 (existence criterion ρ(L)<2, gauge dependence of the stated hypothesis) are not repeated here.

In a normalized Gaussian joint the entropy enters once, and the mean-field covariance is the inverse diagonal precision block, fixed in one step and independent of the neighbors' covariances [DennisTheoryG, 'Block optimum']."

[L03-infogeo] ### L03-1: The conditional representation theorem covers the receiver-only best response, but the body cites it for the canonical scalar functional
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3000-3036 (`eq:F_i_app`, `thm:representation_app`), cited at 528, 594, 678, 2894; the executable authority is stated at 733 (`eq:free_energy_functional_final`, one scalar total).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** The theorem's variational problem is agent $i$'s local problem $F_i=\min_{q_i}\{\mathrm{KL}(q_i\|p_i)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\mathcal D(q_i,q_j)\}$ with every neighbor $q_j$ held fixed (line 3003). In the canonical functional, which line 733 names as "the executable authority used throughout", $q_i$ also sits in the second slot of every sender row $\beta_{\ell i}\mathrm{KL}(q_\ell\|\Omega_{\ell i}q_i)$. The manuscript itself separates the full scalar from a "receiver-only field that is not used by the checked-in optimizer" (594, 733, 2430). Three executed checks in `L03_representation_gap.py`:
(1) SymPy gives the second-slot functional derivative of $\int p f(q/p)$ as $f(r)-rf'(r)$. For KL this is $1-r$, the same kind of $1/q_i$ term that line 2964 uses to rule out the reverse KL. If both slot derivatives are required to be affine in $\log r$, then $f''=a/r$ and $-rf''=c/r$, which forces $a=c=0$. So no nonzero $f$-divergence gives free-form exponential-family closure once an agent is also a sender.
(2) Take two agents in 1D with $\Omega=I$, $\beta_{12}=\beta_{21}=1$ ($B=1$), $p_1=\mathcal N(0,1)$, $p_2=\mathcal N(3,0.25)$. The geometric-pool fixed point is $q_1=\mathcal N(2,0.5)$, $q_2=\mathcal N(2.667,0.333)$. At that point the receiver-only gradient is exactly 0, but the gradient of the joint scalar is $(-1.333,-0.278,2.000,-0.917)$, norm 2.59. The joint Gaussian minimizer sits elsewhere: $q_1=\mathcal N(1.877,0.835)$, $q_2=\mathcal N(2.531,0.598)$, with $F=3.139$ against 3.536 at the pool.
(3) At that joint minimizer the free-form stationarity residual $2\log q_1-\log p_1-\log q_2-q_2/q_1$ varies from 0.92 to 1.43 over $x\in[-2,4]$. It is not constant, so no free-form Gaussian stationary point exists.
So the claim at 678, that "the geometric-mean Boltzmann belief ... follow[s] exactly" from the canonical functional, is false as written. Line 594's "unique $f$-divergence consistent with the geometric-mean Boltzmann stationary form for the belief update" is true only for the frozen-neighbor best response. In the implemented Gaussian-parametric setting the family imposes closure, so the closure criterion cannot select the divergence of the executed functional.
**Fix:** Restate the scope wherever the theorem is invoked (528, 594, 678, 2894, 2959, 3152, 3158). Suggested replacement at 594: "Within that class, the forward KL is the unique $f$-divergence for which agent $i$'s best response, with neighbor beliefs and attention held fixed, is the geometric-mean Boltzmann form; in the joint scalar functional, where each belief is also a transported template in its neighbors' rows, no nonzero $f$-divergence yields free-form exponential-family closure, and the Gaussian implementation imposes closure through the family." Delete "the geometric-mean Boltzmann belief" from the list at 678, or qualify it the same way. Alternatively, anchor the KL direction on the Theory's source-label joint (see complementarity notes), which fixes the divergence without the closure argument.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the stationary condition of `eq:free_energy_functional_final` with respect to $q_i$ contains no sender-row terms, or that the geometric pool is a stationary point of the joint scalar. The two-agent witness would then show a zero joint gradient.
**Status:** P for the receiver-only problem (Step 2 and Lemma `lem:richness_app` verified). As cited in the body, D is inflated to "pinned" and "follow exactly" for an object the theorem does not cover.

[L03-infogeo] ### L03-4: Interpretation paragraph misstates what is special about KL among f-divergences
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3152 ("whose Hessian induces the Fisher-Rao metric and yields the mixed/exponential family-projection Pythagorean theorem. These global properties are not shared by generic $f$-divergences").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Every smooth $f$-divergence has second-order jet $\tfrac{f''(1)}{2}g_F$. That is the invariance content of Čencov's theorem and appears in the divergence treatment of Amari and Nagaoka 2000, Ch. 3. `L03_divergences.py` (c) checks this on the 1D Gaussian family: $D/\varepsilon^2$ against $\tfrac{f''(1)}2 g_F$ gives relative differences of $5.7\times10^{-4}$ (KL), $3.1\times10^{-4}$ (squared Hellinger, $f''(1)=1/2$) and $1.1\times10^{-3}$ (chi-squared, $f''(1)=2$) at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. Each is ten times smaller than at $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$, which is the expected $O(\varepsilon)$ third-order remainder. What is specific to KL is being both an $f$-divergence and a Bregman divergence on normalized distributions. Amari, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 55(11) (2009) 4925-4931, abstract: "this is the only such one in the space of probability distributions". The companion property is the dually flat ($\pm1$) structure with its Pythagorean relation (Amari and Nagaoka 2000, Ch. 3). The abstract was read from the IEEE listing; the full PDF was not accessible.
**Fix:** "the forward KL is the Bregman divergence of the negative-entropy potential; among $f$-divergences on normalized distributions it is the only Bregman divergence (Amari 2009), and it is the canonical divergence of the dually flat $e/m$ structure, whence the Pythagorean relation. The Fisher metric, by contrast, is the local Hessian of every smooth $f$-divergence, up to the factor $f''(1)$." Add the Amari 2009 reference.
**Falsifies:** A smooth $f$-divergence whose second-order jet on a regular family is not proportional to Fisher. The executed check found none for three generators.
**Status:** S-level interpretive sentence containing one false comparative.

## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A physics referee will read Section `sec:time_info` next to three bodies of literature that PIFB2 does not engage there. First, thermodynamic length. Salamon and Berry, Phys. Rev. Lett. 51 (1983) 1127, and Crooks, "Measuring thermodynamic length", Phys. Rev. The clock-dissipation identity `eq:clock_dissipation` is the gradient-flow counterpart of that relation, and a statistical-physics referee will ask for the comparison. Second, Wootters, Phys. Rev. D 23 (1981) 357, is the physics source for Fisher arc length as a count of distinguishable states. It supports the unit discussion at 1831 better than Cover and Thomas does. Third, Caticha's entropic dynamics. It is cited at 132 and 2084 but not in the time section, even though Caticha, J. Phys. A 44 (2011) 225303, introduces an "entropic time" as a bookkeeping device for change within an information-geometric dynamics. That is close to register entry P1.
