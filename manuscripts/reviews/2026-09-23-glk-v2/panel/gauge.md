## Findings

### F1. The frames are a local dressing field: the covariance is automatic and constrains no learned map
**Location:** Main 69–71, 340–347, 357–359, 402, 450–455, 650–660, 683, 913–924, 1169, 1189; title and abstract (“local endpoint covariance law”).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** (i) *Recomputation.* Under the passive action the reference moments are invariant (sympy, exact, generic 2×2 symbols). The routing, message, and link laws hold numerically to 8.5e-14, 2.6e-12, and 6.3e-12 for nonorthogonal `g_i` with condition numbers up to 73.6 (checks 1a–1d). Choosing `g_i = U_i^{-1}` sets every frame to `I` (4.9e-16) and reduces the operator to the frame-free map `xi_i + sum_{j,h} beta_ij^h O_h T_h xi_j` (3.6e-15, checks 2a–2c). The reference identification dresses to the identity, `U_i^{-1} Omega_ij U_j = I` (2.0e-15, check 2d). In the dressing-field method, a group-valued field `u` with a prescribed gauge law is used to form invariant composite fields such as `u^{-1} phi` (François 2019, §2.1). Here `U_i -> g_i U_i` and `xi_i = U_i^{-1} mu_i` are exactly that. The value of `U_i` at token `i` depends only on `(x_i, i)`, so it is a *local* dressing field. François (2019, §2.2, arXiv v4 text) states criterion (C1): “A local dressing field in a gauge theory signals that its gauge symmetry is artificial.” The same section calls the dressing-field method “a reciprocal to the Stueckelberg trick: the latter aims at implementing an artificial gauge freedom, the former seeks to erase it to reveal the gauge-invariant content.” The frame-free operator is the gauge-fixed theory, and the frames are the compensating fields.

(ii) *No constraint.* Checks 1a–1c used unconstrained random `S_Q, S_K, T, O`, so covariance holds for arbitrary interfaces. By contrast, rechoosing the declared sections alone (`sigma_i -> sigma_i k_i` with the associated point fixed, i.e. `U_i -> U_i k_i`) changes routing by up to 0.965 (check 3a). The operator therefore depends on the chosen frames: the learned frames fix a global trivialization. Weiler et al. (2021, §2.1.1) write: “A frame field on the manifold consists of a unique frame at every point of the manifold. Gauge transformations are in this case trivial, which is described by the trivial group G={e}.” The same section places conventional Euclidean CNNs in this `{e}` class. The steerability constraint quantifies over `G`, so for `G={e}` it is vacuous. In Cohen et al. (2019, §2.4, Eq. 5) the kernel must satisfy `K(g^{-1}v) = rho_out(g^{-1}) K(v) rho_in(g)` for all `g` because “the gauge was chosen arbitrarily”. In the Gauge Equivariant Transformer the value map must satisfy `W_V(theta^{-1}u) = rho_out(theta^{-1}) W_V(u) rho_in(theta)` (He et al. 2021, §4.3, Eq. 15). Its query, key, and head-mixing matrices `W_Q, W_K, W_M` must satisfy the order-zero intertwiner condition Eq. (17a) (§4.4; text extracted from the NeurIPS PDF). Main 660 notes that the reference maps need not be intertwiners, but it never states the consequence.

(iii) *What the constraint would impose here.* Suppose the frames were gauge data rather than learned dressing. A shared interface acting on transported local coordinates would then have to be a `G`-intertwiner. For `G = prod_b GL(d_b)`, `End_G(V) = ⊕_b R·I_{d_b}`. Computed commutant dimensions: 2 for two blocks, 1 for `GL(K)`; `dim Hom_G(V, V_1) = 1`, `dim Hom_G(V_1, V_2) = 0` (checks 7a–7d). Every `O_h T_h` would then be block-scalar, and every content link would collapse to `L_ij = sum_b c_b Omega_ij^(b)`. Full-row-rank comparison maps would be `⊕ c_b pr_b` over a subset of blocks, so routing would reduce to frame-only Gaussian KL on those blocks. That is the complete-state alignment of Main 723–725, taken blockwise. The rank loss, block mixing, and composition defect that Sections 2.3–2.4 study exist only because the frames are learned dressing fields.

(iv) *Code.* The featured configuration has `block_mlp_mode=coordinate`, `norm_type_block=layernorm`, `decode_mode=linear`, `e_phi_lr=0.0`, `transport_mode=flat`, and `skip_belief_sigma_update=True` (`config.json`). The implementation registers this MLP as `gauge_class="coordinate_nonintertwiner"`, `intertwiner_compatible=False` (`vfe3\model\block_mlp.py:434–443`, echoed in the saved `executable_build.block_mlp` record).

**Fix:** Insert after the proposition (after Main 924):

```latex

The frames enter the attention operator only through the dressed variables $\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i$ and $C_i=U_i^{-1}\Sigma_iU_i^{-\top}$. Choosing $g_i=U_i^{-1}$ removes them, and the operator becomes Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected_transformer_residual} with every $U_i=I$. In the terminology of the dressing-field method, $U_i$ is a local dressing field, and the local $\mathrm{GL}(K)$ covariance of the attention operator is artificial in the sense of \citet[Sec.~2.2]{Francois2019Artificial}. Equivalently, the learned frames fix a global trivialization of the token bundle, an $\{e\}$-structure in the sense of \citet[Sec.~2.1.1]{weiler2021coordinate}, and the covariance places no constraint on $S_Q$, $S_K$, $T$, or $O$. ### F2. “A lattice-gauge structure at the level of link fields” describes composite bilocals with no gauge degrees of freedom
```
**Location:** Main 1189 (Conclusion), 71 (Introduction), 335, 594–615.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** (i) *The links are composites.* `L_ij^(h) = R_{i,h} S_{j,h}` with `R_{i,h} = U_i O_h` and `S_{j,h} = T_h U_j^{-1}` both site-local (Main 444–446). Main 611–613 already writes the link as an adjoint-valued site field times the pure-gauge transport, `L_ij = F_{i,h} Omega_ij`. Wetterich 2014, the paper’s precedent for general matrix links, separates two cases. In linear lattice gauge theory the links `L(x; mu)` are unconstrained matrices that are the integration variables of `Z = ∫DL exp(-S_L[L])` with a link potential and plaquette action (§II, Eqs. 2–4). Separately, in gauge theories “based on scalars or fermions instead of link variables”, “the associated composite link variables are scalar or fermion bilinears which do not obey constraints” (§I). The attention links belong to the second class. (ii) *Their invariant content.* In unitary gauge the routed link is `A_ij = sum_h beta_ij^h O_h T_h`. The trace of any closed walk is `W_gamma = sum_{h_1..h_r} (prod_k beta^{h_k}_{i_k i_{k+1}}) Tr(O_{h_1}T_{h_1} ... O_{h_r}T_{h_r})`: a polynomial in the gauge-invariant routing weights with loop-independent coefficients. This holds to 3.2e-13 (frame independence, relative) and 6.7e-15 (word polynomial, relative) over every closed walk of length 2–4 on 4 sites with 2 heads (checks 5a–5b), and exactly in sympy for a 2-head triangle. For one head, `W = (prod beta) Tr((O T)^r)` (3.3e-15, check 5c). (iii) *Support.* On the causal support (`j <= i`, self edge included) an exhaustive count finds zero closed walks through two or more distinct positions (N = 6, lengths 2–5; check 5d). So the only loop traces of the reported model are `Tr(A_ii^r)`. (iv) `Omega` is exact, a coboundary of the dressing field, hence pure gauge (check 2d). The endpoint law `C_ij -> g_i C_ij g_j^{-1}` is the transformation law of any bilocal `phi_i chi_j^T` built from a site vector field `phi` and a site covector field `chi`. It does not identify independent gauge degrees of freedom. Main 335 calls “the communication graph and its matrix links” additional data on the fibers; only the support graph is additional, since the links are determined by `(U, S_Q, S_K, T, O, beta)`.
**Fix:** Replace the last sentence of Main 1189 with:

```latex
These links are gauge-covariant composites of site data, of the bilinear kind distinguished by \citet[Sec.~I]{Wetterich2014LinearLattice}: in reference coordinates each routed link is $A_{ij}=\sum_h\beta_{ij}^{(h)}O_hT_h$, and each loop trace is a polynomial in the routing weights with coefficients $\operatorname{Tr}(O_{h_1}T_{h_1}\cdots O_{h_r}T_{h_r})$. The reference transport is pure gauge, and the causal support contains no closed walk through distinct positions. An independent edge connection with nontrivial holonomy, together with an action for it, is required before the construction carries lattice-gauge degrees of freedom.
```

At Main 71 replace “The endpoint law of the content links also connects this formulation to lattice gauge geometry.” with “The content links obey the endpoint law of lattice link variables, although they are composites of site data rather than independent gauge fields.” At Main 335 replace “The communication graph and its matrix links are additional data on the associated vector fibers.” with “The communication graph is additional data; the matrix links on it are determined by the frames, the shared interfaces, and the routing weights.”
**Falsifies:** An edge variable of the reported model not determined by site data; a closed-walk invariant not expressible as the stated routing-weight polynomial; or a directed cycle through distinct positions in the causal support.
**Status:** Main 1189 is S (suggested), written in the register of D (“identifies”), so it is inflated. Main 594–607 (loop-trace invariance and the causal no-cycle caveat) is D and correct.
**Novelty vs prior review:** New. The prior review recorded “Matrix-loop trace is invariant: derived identity” and “Causal attention supplies physical loop holonomy: not claimed”, but did not flag the Conclusion sentence or the composite nature of the links.

### F3. Main text and treatise type the principal section differently; the declared bundle gauge freedom is not the proved symmetry
**Location:** Main 296, 298–307, 315–333, 340–347; `gauge_vfe_rg_geometry.tex` 157–181, 464–496, 550–562, 591–593, 659–679.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Both documents use the same right-principal quotient: Main eq. `associated_quotient`, `(n·g, b) ~ (n, rho(g) b)`, and treatise eq. `geo-quotient-convention`, `(u·g, beta) ~ (u, g·beta)`. The Main restatement “(n,b) and (n·g, rho(g^{-1})b) represent the same associated point” is correct by substituting `b -> rho(g^{-1}) b`. The two documents differ in *which* section belongs to the agent.

In Main, the agent-selected section `sigma_i` defines `U_i(v) = [sigma_i, v]` and hence the *reference* coordinates `xi_i = U_i^{-1} mu_i` (Main 307, 325–331). The “local coordinates” in which `mu_i`, `Sigma_i`, and `U_i` are matrices (Main 320) are never introduced as a section, and Main 317 types `mu` as an element of the abstract fiber `E_i`. Yet the passive action writes `g_i mu_i` and `g_i U_i` with `g_i ∈ GL(K)` (Main 343–344), which presupposes such a chart. A rechoice of the declared section, `sigma_i -> sigma_i k_i`, acts on the frame by right multiplication and is not a symmetry: it changes routing by up to 0.965 (check 3a). Only its common form `sigma_i -> sigma_i a`, compensated by `S -> S a`, `T -> T a`, `O -> a^{-1} O`, is a redundancy (routing 8.9e-16, message 3.9e-14; checks 3c–3d). Hence Main 340 must describe the passive action as acting “instead” of the right action, while Main 296 says “The principal bundle carries gauge freedom.”

The treatise instead types the agent’s section as the *local* frame `u_i`, together with one reference section `sigma_0` (eq. `geo-frame-field`: `u_i = sigma_0·U_i^{-1}`). Reference coordinates are then `z_i = U_i^{-1} mu_i`, with `mu_i` the `u_i`-coordinates (text after eq. `geo-flat-regime`). In this typing the right action `u_i -> u_i a_i` gives exactly `(mu_i, U_i) -> (a_i^{-1} mu_i, a_i^{-1} U_i)`: checks 4a–4c reproduce this with an explicit background basis to ≤ 9.0e-14. That is the Main passive action with `g_i = a_i^{-1}`, so “passive” and “right action” coincide. `Omega_ij = U_i U_j^{-1}` is then the same-point frame comparison `T_ij` of eq. `geo-frame-comparison`, which the treatise describes as “same-point changes of coordinates. They involve no curve and no connection” (02_geometry:494). The global reference change `U_i -> U_i a` is a change of `sigma_0`. The treatise calls independent per-vertex rechoices “passive vertex-coordinate choices” (02_geometry:591–593) and reserves “principal gauge group” for the automorphisms of the bundle over the identity of the base (02_geometry:177–181), whereas Main labels the per-site action a gauge action (equation label `gauge_action_gaussians`; the title). The matrix formulas agree in both typings, so no equation changes; what changes is the meaning of gauge, passive, and reference.
**Fix:** Adopt the treatise typing in Main 298–347. Replace Main 298 and 307 with:

```latex
An agent over a contextual region $\mathcal U_i$ selects a local principal section $u_i:\mathcal U_i\to\mathcal P$, and its belief and prior sections have coordinates $(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ and $(\mu_{p,i},\Sigma_{p,i})$ in that section. Communication additionally uses one reference trivialization $\sigma_0$ of $\mathcal P$ on its working domain. The frame field is the relative element $U_i$ defined by $u_i=\sigma_0\cdot U_i^{-1}$, so that $\xi_i=U_i^{-1}\mu_i$ are the $\sigma_0$-coordinates of the same vector.
```

Replace the first two sentences of Main 340 with:

```latex
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:associated_quotient}, a rechoice $u_i\mapsto u_i\cdot g_i^{-1}$ of the section of agent $i$, with $\sigma_0$ held fixed, changes the coordinates as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge_action_gaussians}. These rechoices are independent passive coordinate changes at the token sites. A change of $\sigma_0$ is the common reference-basis change $U_i\mapsto U_ia$ of Section~\ref{sec:role_measurements}.
```

Rename the equation label and the surrounding prose from “gauge action” to “passive site action”.
**Falsifies:** An explicit second (local) section already declared in Main 2.1; or a reading of Main 2.1 under which rechoices of `sigma_i` leave routing invariant (check 3a contradicts this when the interfaces are shared).
**Status:** A definitional inconsistency, not a P or D claim; the proposition is unaffected. The current typing overstates the role of the bundle gauge freedom, which the construction breaks per site.
**Novelty vs prior review:** Disagrees with the prior gauge investigator (`investigators\gauge.md`, “Associated-bundle typing”), which judged the two conventions to be “different typed maps/conventions, not a sign or ordering error.” They agree at the matrix level, but they assign the bundle gauge freedom to different objects, and only the treatise assignment makes “passive” and the right action coincide.

### F4. Unstated internal redundancies of the head spaces and the reference frame; the reported conditioning diagnostics depend on the basis

### F5. Covariant constructions for the nonlinear block exist and are implemented; choosing one fixes which frame is physical
**Location:** Main 359, 924, 939, 1171.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Main 939 says the nonlinear maps “require a separate equivariance construction if the complete predictor is to have that symmetry.” Two such constructions are elementary and are registered in the reference implementation. I did not read its diagonal branch. I did not trace an invariant decoder path. ### F6. Diagonal storage is a gauge slice within frame blocks; the restriction it imposes is on cross-block covariance
**Location:** Main 1095, 1134, 1171; prior R6.

## What the gauge structure buys, and what would make the gauge field physical

In the reported model the gauge structure buys a consistent type discipline and one exact global redundancy. It buys no constraint, no prediction, and no invariant beyond the reference-coordinate quantities. *Constraints:* none on `S_Q, S_K, T, O` (F1). The only structural restrictions are the block-diagonal frame group, which is a parameter-count choice, and the sharing of interfaces across tokens, which is ordinary weight sharing. *Predictions:* none that distinguish the model from its unitary-gauge rewrite, which reproduces its logits to 3.1e-15 (check 15a). *Invariants:* `xi_i`, `C_i`, the routing weights, and loop traces that reduce to routing-weight polynomials; on the causal support these are only `Tr(A_ii^r)` (F2).

For the gauge field to be physical, two things must change. First, the edge data must not be a coboundary. Keep the vertex frames and add an edge factor, `Theta_ij = U_i V_ij U_j^{-1}` with `V_ij ∈ GL^+(K)`. On the causal DAG the natural loop observable is the triangle holonomy with the inverse reverse edge, `hol_ijk = Theta_ij Theta_jk Theta_ik^{-1}` for `k < j < i`. It transforms by conjugation at `i`, so its spectrum is gauge-invariant. With a learned per-offset table `V_ij = V(i - j)`, the spectrum deviates from 1 by up to 14.6 in a random example and is gauge-invariant to 5.7e-14. A one-parameter table `V(delta) = e^{delta J}` (the RoPE case) is flat to 2.9e-16 (checks 11a–11c). For a scalar table `V(delta) = e^{c_delta} I` the triangle holonomy is `e^{c_a + c_b - c_{a+b}}`, which is flat exactly when `c` is linear in the offset, the ALiBi form. The content links of Main eq. `main_processing_transport` then become `F_{i,h} Theta_ij`, whose unitary-gauge form `sum_h beta_ij^h O_h T_h V(i - j)` carries holonomy that no site field can remove. A matrix-valued relative-position table is thus the minimal non-flat extension. Content-dependent generators `V_ij = exp(sum_a f_a(xi_i, C_i, xi_j, C_j) G_a)` are also covariant and generically curved, but they remain composites of site data. The reference implementation had such a path (`transport_mode=regime_ii_covariant`). It was removed on 2026-09-20 in `V3_Transformer` commit `4cf3780` (“Remove the non-flat connection regimes (transport_mode is flat-only) (#286)”). Both the cleanup copy and `V3_Transformer` HEAD `ce59523` register only `flat` (`vfe3\geometry\transport.py:536`), so the vault page `[[Non-flat connection and the photon analogy]]`, which still describes that path as wired, is stale. Second, a curvature cost (a plaquette- or triangle-type action) or a likelihood that depends on holonomy is required for the connection to be selected by anything other than the prediction loss.

The alternative route makes the gauge principle constrain rather than decorate. Treat the frames as geometric data, for example derived from a declared token map `iota(i) = c_i` with a connection on `P` (F7), so that the interfaces must be intertwiners. Schur’s lemma then fixes the communication law to blockwise frame-only divergences and scalar multiples of the transport (F1 iii), and the nonlinearity to invariant gates, which the reference implementation already provides (`gauge_gate`, F5). The learned-interface model of this paper is the `{e}`-reduction of that constrained theory.

*Connection to the treatise.* The RG chapters retain the based holonomy representation `H_I^x: pi_1(Gamma_I, r_I) -> G` and dressed boundary edges `V_e^x = tau^x_{I<-i} Theta_e^x (tau^x_{J<-j})^{-1}` (`07b_agent_network_rg.tex:1845–1870`). They also condition parent laws on holonomy (`06_general_coarsegraining.tex:542–601`, theorem `thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal`). For the flat vertex-factorized transport of the reported model, the trivializing criterion (`02_geometry.tex`, `prop:geo-trivializing-criterion`, 610–629) gives `H_I = e`. These RG data are therefore trivial, and the holonomy-fixed parent family is unconstrained. The matrix content links are not group-valued and do not enter the treatise’s holonomy data at all. The attention model sits at the trivial-holonomy point of the treatise’s RG. Main 637 correctly states that multiplying reciprocal conditionals does not specify a joint. The gauge-theoretic reason is that the flat transport satisfies `Omega_ji = Omega_ij^{-1}`, so both residuals of a reciprocal Gaussian pair vanish on the `K`-dimensional subspace `k_i = Omega_ij k_j`, and the assembled precision is singular. The same observation is recorded in the vault page `[[GL(K) gauge-equivariant attention]]`, in the section on normalization and the ordered mask.

*For the neuron-free program.* The gauge language is the right typing for agents with private frames, but it selects architecture only where it constrains. The principled next step is the constrained regime above: frames as data, intertwiner interfaces, invariant nonlinear gates, and a non-flat connection with an action. In that regime the free relational parameters are geometric (connection, priors, temperatures). The present learned-interface model is then recovered as a stated `{e}`-reduction rather than presented as a gauge theory.
