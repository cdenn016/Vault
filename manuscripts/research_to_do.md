## Goal and the standard it has to meet

The goal is a single theoretical framework that covers physical systems (nucleons, cells, organs, the Earth system) and abstract informational systems (languages, cultures, markets, nations). A unification earns the name only if it commits to something the two kinds of system share that can be measured and could fail. Redescribing both in one vocabulary ("everything is information", "everything minimizes free energy") predicts nothing, and the Markov-blanket version of that move has a published critique that any referee will cite (Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst and Baltieri 2022, "The Emperor's New Markov Blankets", Behav. Brain Sci.). The program below therefore has a theoretical spine (workstream 1), an empirical spine (workstream 2), and three supporting lines whose results either feed the spines or test them.

## Workstream 1 (theoretical spine): physical and abstract coupling as two regimes of one channel class

The proposal is that physical and abstract collectives differ in how their members' states are coupled, not in what the members are made of. Physical coupling runs through a shared medium whose code is fixed by symmetry and conservation (a gauge field, a current, a wave equation). Abstract coupling runs through a learned code (a language, a price system, a statute) that sender and receiver must share by convention, and translation between codes can be lossy or non-invertible. The gauge-theoretic program already represents both: private frames are a gauge freedom, the transport Ω_ij is an identification between agents, and the multiplex of the Collective_Agents manuscript (`asm:int-a9`, Section 10) gives each modality its own content, code, range, message weight, leak and memory. The theorem to aim for is a limit statement: when code learning converges to an invertible, equivariant identification, abstract coupling behaves as a physical gauge connection, and when the code stays non-invertible a mistranslation residual remains that has no physical counterpart. Physical and abstract would then be two regimes of one channel class separated by a parameter (code invertibility, or a code-learning rate against a coupling rate), and the claim fails if no such parameter exists.

Status on 2026-11-20: the claim as posed has failed by this falsifier (`Desktop\coupling-regimes-2026-11-19\results.md`). No single parameter separates the regimes. The rank of the code, refined to the transmissibility ϑ, decides whether the communication energy has gauge form, the residual mistranslation decides which connection appears, and all four combinations occur. Reading the physical against abstract distinction as the mistranslation axis is a new conjecture, introduced after that result, and it still has to be tested. The limit theorem holds only for a communication energy that compares the receiver's belief with its reconstruction of the message (KL(q_i ∥ q̂_ij), or the same with the arguments swapped); for the divergence of the sender's belief, KL(q_j ∥ q̂_ij), the manuscript's measure of meaning lost, it fails. On 2026-11-20 the owner adopted the receiver order KL(q_i ∥ q̂_ij) as the program's communication energy, so the limit of the third item below holds in the form of Theorem 14 of the derivation.

Status on 2026-11-22: the limit of the third item is a pathwise statement (code models converging in operator norm, on compact sets of beliefs, as the check of the coupling-regimes derivation records it), and it should stay one. In Collective_Agents the plug-in lift O = PĤᵀ(ĤPĤᵀ)⁻¹ under a Gaussian error of the code model has an understanding error with a finite second moment only for K ≥ r_c + 2, and a square code, the rank at which the exact gauge form holds, has an infinite first moment at every error level σ_H > 0 (`thm:lrn-understanding-moment`(a), a theorem of the manuscript). A risk or mean-square version of the limit therefore does not exist for the plug-in lift in the invertible regime; it needs a regularized gain, a truncated moment, or convergence in probability. Whether the expected value of the adopted energy KL(q_i ∥ q̂_ij) is finite under Gaussian code error is a separate question, which the moment theorem does not answer and which has not been checked. ## Workstream 2 (empirical spine): the Markov length as the common observable

The scales table showed that no source at any scale measures the Markov length ξ_M, the decay length of the conditional mutual information between a site and everything beyond a shell, given the shell (`def:col-markov-length`). Correlation lengths and interaction ranges were found only patchily, and by `prop:col-markov-vs-correlation` they do not constrain blanket thickness. Conditional mutual information can be estimated from data in both physical and abstract systems, so ξ_M can be the shared observable. The test was first posed as whether ξ_M relative to system size, and relative to the crossing time, shows a common scaling across a physical and a social system; as registered on 2026-11-19 it is whether the Gaussian projection ξ_M^G, compared through the noise response S, the ratio R = ξ_M^G/(1 + ξ) and the drift D, shows a common scaling, with the size and crossing-time ratios dropped (see the status note below). A common scaling was first described here as a unifying empirical law; under the registered criteria it would be one severe test passed by one pair of systems, and a clear mismatch would falsify the strong form of the program; both outcomes are worth reporting. The quantity originates with Sang and Hsieh for quantum states and is defined for agents in the Collective_Agents manuscript. Its classical counterpart is not unclaimed: the conditional mutual information of classical Gibbs states under local noise was studied by Zhang and Gopalakrishnan (2025, arXiv:2502.13210), who prove exponential decay at high temperature for any local transition matrices, and by others since. What is new here is its application to agents and to measured data.

Status on 2026-11-20: the premise of this paragraph has not held as written. For the abstract data only ξ_M^G is estimable at the widths needed (the k-nearest-neighbor estimator collapses once the shell holds more than a few variables), so the registered criteria (`Desktop\markov-length-measurement-2026-11-19\working\pre-commitment-criteria.md`, Sections 0 and 2.7) compare ξ_M^G and not ξ_M. They exclude ratios to system size and to crossing or persistence times, because ξ_M is intensive whenever it is finite and the manuscript has no result relating it to a time, and they call a common verdict one severe test passed by one pair of systems, not a law (Section 6). By Proposition 22 of the coupling-regimes derivation, exact-code and mistranslated networks can have the same world-conditional covariance at every lag, so a comparison of Markov lengths cannot test workstream 1's distinction. The observables proposed there for workstream 1 are the static-level response Γ of the world-conditional mean (a one-sided test) and the rank of the lag-one coupling blocks of the world-conditional regression, each under stated conditions; neither has been run on data.

## Workstream 3: timescale separation as the condition for a collective to exist

In five rows of the scales table chosen as examples (eukaryotic cell, bacterium, human, town, Earth system) the crossing time is four to eleven orders of magnitude below the persistence time, and the table names three rows where the ratio falls below ten: prehistoric culture, the microbial collective and the forest landscape. Recomputed from the table's own entries (audit of 2026-11-20), neither fact holds for the table as a whole. When every quantified entry of the last column of Table 2 is admitted as a slow time, the lower end of the ratio falls below ten in nine rows: the atomic nucleus (unbound states), the small molecule (rotation), the molecular liquid (hydrogen-bond exchange), the protein (folding), the condensate, the microbial collective, the heart (the beat), prehistoric culture and the forest landscape. The ratio lies above eleven orders throughout for the nucleon and the nation state. Which entry counts as the persistence time (a lifetime, a turnover time or a cycle) was not fixed before the ratios were taken, and the set of rows below ten depends on that choice: the heart is below ten against its beat and far above against its lifetime. The hypothesis is that a blanket of blankets persists only when the internal mixing time is well below the turnover time of its contents, and that below a threshold the population fragments, as large prehistoric language areas fragmented into dialect continua. This is a reading of the table, not a sourced result, and it needs both a derivation and a test.

## Workstream 4: physical and social systems as different universality classes

Exponential decay dominates the physical rows of the scales table, while power laws dominate the social and economic rows (tie probability ∝ d⁻², trade ∝ d⁻⁰·⁹, Zipf city sizes, truncated power-law mobility). Only part of that list is a measured law of coupling against distance within one system. Zipf's law is a distribution of city sizes, not a distance law. The mobility law of González, Hidalgo and Barabási (2008) is a power law with an exponential cutoff κ of 80 to 400 km for displacements (350 km for the radius of gyration), so it has a length. Trade ∝ d⁻⁰·⁹ is the mean distance elasticity of international trade (Disdier and Head 2008), a law between systems. That leaves the tie probability ∝ d⁻² of Lambiotte et al. (2008), for which no distance range is stated, as the one within-system coupling law without a length, which is a thin base for a contrast between universality classes. One hypothesis is that these are different fixed points of one renormalization picture rather than different physics. A tie probability ∝ d⁻² in two dimensions gives a number of contacts at distance r that scales as r·r⁻² = 1/r, so the total diverges logarithmically. In the convention P(r) ∝ r^−(d+σ) this is σ = 0, the boundary of non-additive (strong) long-range coupling (Bouchet, Gupta and Mukamel 2010, Physica A 389, 4389; Mukamel 2009, arXiv:0905.1457); the universality-class crossovers of long-range Ising and O(n) models sit at σ = d/2 (Fisher, Ma and Nickel 1972, Phys. Rev. Lett. 29, 917) and σ = 2 − η_SR (Sak 1973, Phys. Rev. B 8, 281). So σ = 0 is marginal for the existence of an extensive energy, not for a change of universality class. The analogy also reads a link probability as a coupling constant J(r), an assumption that has to be checked. The arithmetic is mine, not a published claim, and nothing should rest on it before those checks.

- [ ] Check the reading against the long-range O(n) and Ising results (decay exponent d + σ; σ = 0 is the additivity boundary, and the universality-class crossovers are at σ = d/2 and σ = 2 − η_SR), including whether a link probability can stand for a coupling J(r), and against the network-embedding literature on gravity laws.
- [ ] Place the result in the vault's renormalization-group material on belief dynamics and in the coarse-graining section of Collective_Agents (`sec:ren`).
- [ ] Identify which social distance laws are measured well enough (range, resolution, platform bias) to support a universality-class claim.

## Workstream 5: a categorical formulation

Markov categories (Fritz 2020, Adv. Math.) and open systems with interfaces (lenses, categorical cybernetics) treat physical and informational processes as morphisms of one kind, so coarse-graining becomes a functor and the claim "the same law at every scale" becomes a precise statement about that functor. This line does not produce results by itself, but it makes the unification claim checkable and gives workstream 1 a clean language.

## Cautions

Landauer's principle links information and physics only after a temperature and Boltzmann's constant are given (vault page "Information is Physical"); it does not license deriving physical quantities from information alone. Blanket assignments for abstract systems are interpretive until a conditional independence is tested. The world-conditional construction of Collective_Agents (`def:col-world-conditional`) was described here as already separating a blanket produced by a shared driver from one produced by coupling, which is the distinction critics will probe. Corrected on 2026-11-22: the construction removes the common mode of the declared exogenous world and nothing more. It conditions on the world path and on broadcast noise shared by several receivers, and it keeps an agent's sensing noise as a private input even when the agent broadcasts its sensation. A source outside a candidate collective that every member hears is therefore a shared driver that survives the conditioning. In the audience instance of the manuscript (`obs:agy-audience`) four members who attend one outside source form a collective at contrast 5.38 while the member-restricted order parameter Ω_int(A | ζ_S) vanishes up to events of negligible probability, and with the source's sensing noise added to the conditioning set the members' covariance is diagonal and the contrast is 1. These are computed values at sampled parameter points, rebuilt by one reviewer on 2026-11-22 and not adjudicated. When agents act on the conditioned variable the construction errs in both directions: conditioning on an agent-driven world path creates dependence between agents that never interact and hides interaction that runs through the world (`ex:agy-collider`, `ex:agy-masking`). The statistic that separates a common outside source from coupling among members is the member-restricted order parameter Ω_int(C | ζ_{V∖C}) of `def:agy-order-parameter`, which conditions on the shared exogenous drive and on the private inputs of the non-members. `def:agy-meta-agent` reports it beside the grade and does not require it, and a collective whose contrast its members' interaction produces is the manuscript's open problem `open:agy-meta-agent-exists`. For measured data the statistic needs the shared drive to be recorded, and an estimator without that record is open (`open:agy-unrecorded-drive`). Ecological synchrony over ~1000 km is largely a shared weather driver (the Moran effect), the clearest example in the table of a correlation that conditioning on the world would remove; that example stands under the correction, because the weather is exogenous to the populations and would be the declared world.
