\section{Augmented Hierarchical Model, the Rigid-Link Predictive Density, and Mean-Field Fixed Points}
\label{app:augmented_joint}

The transported-copy specialization of the main text reads the cross-scale shadow $p_i^{(\ell)} = \Omega_{i,I}[q_I^{(\ell+1)}]$ of Section~\ref{sec:cross_scale_shadows} as the one-episode-lagged predictive prior of an episode-indexed joint. When a parent forms at episode $n$, the prior of its children at episode $n+1$ is the filtered parent law pushed through a declared transition, and for a transported-copy transition this is the shadow formula exactly. This appendix records what a same-time hierarchical joint does and does not supply. We define an augmented hierarchical generative model with Gaussian link kernels on the tree of agent latents, prove that it is a proper joint, state its mean-field fixed-point equations, and compute the variational predictive density whose rigid-link limit reproduces the shadow formula. That density is not generally an evidence-consistent downward message: evaluated against the full parent posterior it can reuse a constituent's own evidence, and the functional built from it is not an evidence bound (Remark~\ref{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}). The general statements behind this are a typing prohibition, under which no generative kernel reads a recognition law, and the fact that an energy appended to a joint without the corresponding normalizer is not an evidence bound. Downward kernels and the conditional cross-scale divergence give the evidence-consistent form of top-down influence.

\subsection{The Augmented Joint}

Let the hierarchy be a finite tree with one root, depth $\ell_{\max}$ and finite breadth, with $I_\ell$ the set of agents at level $\ell$ and $\pi(i)$ the parent of agent $i$. Each agent $i \in I_\ell$ carries a latent variable $k_i^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{K_q}$. The construction is made at a single base point, so it is a finite-dimensional probability law on the product space $\mathbb{K} := \prod_{\ell, i \in I_\ell} \mathbb{R}^{K_q}$. The root prior is proper. In this appendix the records are conditionally independent leaf records, $p(o\mid k^{(0)})=\prod_{i\in I_0}\ell_i(o_i\mid k_i^{(0)})$, with each record kernel normalized. This leaf-local assumption is what keeps the factor graph a tree and licenses the cavity-message formulas; the general record hypergraph of Section~\ref{sec:free_energy} need not have this property.

\begin{definition}[Augmented hierarchical joint]
\label{def:augmented_joint}
For each parent-child edge $(i, \pi(i))$ with $i \in I_\ell$, define the Gaussian link kernel
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}\big(k_i^{(\ell)} \mid k_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\big)
=
(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-K_q/2} \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}
\big\|k_i^{(\ell)} - \Omega_{i,\pi(i)} k_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\big\|^2\right],
\label{eq:gibbs_factor}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_{i,\pi(i)} = \exp(\phi_i^{(\ell)})\exp(-\phi_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}) \in G$ is the inter-level transport of Section~\ref{sec:transport_hierarchy} and $\sigma^2 > 0$ is a link noise variance. The kernel is a normalized transition density, a propagator from parent to child, and not a Boltzmann weight. Its isotropic covariance is specified in this chart. Under a general receiver change $g_i$, the noise covariance becomes $\sigma^2g_ig_i^\top$; isotropy is not preserved by every general linear frame change. The top-level prior $p_{\mathrm{top}}(k^{(\ell_{\max})})$ is a declared root, fixed as a boundary condition (Section~\ref{sec:ouroboros}). The augmented joint over all agent latents is
\begin{equation}
p_{\sigma}\big(\{k_i^{(\ell)}\}, o\big)
=
p(o \mid \{k_i^{(0)}\}) \prod_{\ell = 0}^{\ell_{\max}-1} \prod_{i \in I_\ell} \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}\big(k_i^{(\ell)} \mid k_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\big) p_{\mathrm{top}}\big(k^{(\ell_{\max})}\big).
\label{eq:augmented_joint}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Well-definedness]
\label{lem:aug_joint_welldefined}
Assume the observation likelihood $p(o \mid \cdot)$ is bounded above by a finite constant (unity in the discrete-observation case, the normalization constant in the Gaussian case). Then for every finite $\sigma^2 > 0$ and every finite hierarchical tree, the augmented joint~\eqref{eq:augmented_joint} is integrable over $\mathbb{K}$ for every fixed $o$, with normalization equal to the evidence $p(o)$; the prior portion, obtained by omitting the likelihood factor, is a proper probability density on $\mathbb{K}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each kernel $\mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}$ is a Gaussian density on $k_i^{(\ell)}$ with mean $\Omega_{i,\pi(i)} k_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}$ and covariance $\sigma^2 I$, and it integrates to unity for every value of $k_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}$. The factor graph of~\eqref{eq:augmented_joint} is the hierarchical tree itself. For tree-structured products of proper conditional densities, the integral over the leaves factorizes through the tree~\cite[\S3.2]{Lauritzen1996} and leaves the top-level prior $p_{\mathrm{top}}$, which is normalized, so the prior portion is a proper probability density on $\mathbb{K}$. The likelihood enters as a multiplicative factor at the leaves and is bounded above by hypothesis, so it does not affect integrability, and integrating it against the prior portion yields $p(o)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mean-Field Fixed-Point Equations}

At a record with finite positive evidence, mean-field inference factorizes recognition across agent latents. The displayed density variations assume finite terms and normalizable coordinate exponentials; they assert no update when a coordinate normalizer fails to exist.

\begin{lemma}[Mean-field fixed-point equations]
\label{lem:mf_fixedpoint}
The variational free energy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_{V,\sigma}[q]
=
\sum_{\ell, i} \mathbb{E}_{q_i^{(\ell)}}\big[\log q_i^{(\ell)}\big]
- \sum_{\ell < \ell_{\max}, i} \mathbb{E}_{q_i^{(\ell)} q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}}\big[\log \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}\big]
- \mathbb{E}_{q^{(\ell_{\max})}}\big[\log p_{\mathrm{top}}\big]
- \mathbb{E}_{q^{(0)}}\big[\log p(o \mid k^{(0)})\big]
\label{eq:mf_free_energy}
\end{equation}
is stationary under the mean-field factorization $q(\{k_i^{(\ell)}\}) = \prod_{\ell, i} q_i^{(\ell)}(k_i^{(\ell)})$ when each factor satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\log q_i^{(\ell)}(k_i)
={}&
\text{const}
+ \mathbb{E}_{q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}}\big[\log \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}(k_i \mid k_{\pi(i)})\big]
+ \sum_{j : \pi(j) = i} \mathbb{E}_{q_j^{(\ell-1)}}\big[\log \mathcal{K}_{j,i}^{(\ell-1)}(k_j \mid k_i)\big]\\
&+ \delta_{\ell,0} \mathbb{E}_{q^{(0)}_{\setminus i}}\big[\log p(o \mid k^{(0)})\big]
+ \delta_{\ell,\ell_{\max}} \log p_{\mathrm{top}}(k_i),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mf_fixedpoint}
\end{equation}
where $q^{(0)}_{\setminus i}$ is the product of the other leaf factors. The first expectation is the top-down message from the parent of agent $i$ (absent at $\ell = \ell_{\max}$), the sum collects the bottom-up messages from its children (absent at $\ell = 0$), and the data likelihood enters only at the leaves.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:mf_fixedpoint} is the standard coordinate-ascent mean-field update~\cite[\S5.3]{Wainwright2008}, \cite[\S10.1]{Bishop2006}. It follows by varying~\eqref{eq:mf_free_energy} with respect to $q_i^{(\ell)}$ at fixed values of the other factors, imposing $\int q_i^{(\ell)} = 1$ by a Lagrange multiplier, and collecting the terms that depend on $k_i^{(\ell)}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Rigid-Link Variational Predictive Density}

\begin{lemma}[Rigid-link variational predictive density]
\label{lem:shadow_mf_optimum}
Let the variational posterior of the parent be Gaussian, $q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)} = \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}, \Sigma_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)})$. The variational predictive density of agent $i$, the marginal of the link kernel against the parent's variational posterior, is
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i^{(\ell),\sigma}(k_i) := \int \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}(k_i \mid k_{\pi(i)}) q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}(k_{\pi(i)}) dk_{\pi(i)}
= \mathcal{N}\big(k_i; \Omega_{i,\pi(i)} \mu_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}, \Omega_{i,\pi(i)} \Sigma_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)} \Omega_{i,\pi(i)}^\top + \sigma^2 I\big),
\label{eq:shadow_soft}
\end{equation}
and as $\sigma^2 \to 0$ it converges to the pushforward $(\Omega_{i,\pi(i)})_* q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}$, which is the shadow formula of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross_scale_shadow}. The lemma computes a density. That density is generally different from the mean-field factor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mf_fixedpoint} and from an evidence-consistent downward message of the joint~\eqref{eq:augmented_joint} (Remark~\ref{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $k_{\pi(i)}$, the kernel~\eqref{eq:gibbs_factor} is Gaussian with mean $\Omega_{i,\pi(i)} k_{\pi(i)}$, linear in the parent, and covariance $\sigma^2 I$. The linear-Gaussian marginalization rule~\cite[\S2.3.3]{Bishop2006} gives a Gaussian marginal with mean $\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\mu_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}$ and covariance $\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\Sigma_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}^\top + \sigma^2 I$. As $\sigma^2 \to 0$ the kernel converges weakly to $\delta(k_i - \Omega_{i,\pi(i)} k_{\pi(i)})$, and the marginal converges to
\begin{equation}
\int \delta\big(k_i - \Omega_{i,\pi(i)} k_{\pi(i)}\big) q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}(k_{\pi(i)}) dk_{\pi(i)} = \big(\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\big)_* q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{General selective hierarchical channels.}
The normalization and coordinate-variation arguments extend to $k_i\mid k_{\pi(i)}\sim\mathcal N(L_{i,\pi(i)}k_{\pi(i)},N_i)$ with $N_i\succ0$, including rectangular or rank-deficient $L_{i,\pi(i)}$ and different latent dimensions. Their predictive law is $\mathcal N(L\mu,L\Sigma L^\top+N_i)$. With $N_i=\epsilon N_{i,0}$ and $\epsilon\downarrow0$, it converges weakly to $L_\#q_{\pi(i)}$; this limit can be singular and is then a measure, not an ambient density. The isolated child mean-field coordinate is instead $\mathcal N(L\mu,N_i)$, because the parent covariance enters its expected log kernel only as a constant. No inverse link is used by these conclusions. The noiseless invertible $\Omega$ case is retained below only to identify the earlier transported-copy formula.

\begin{remark}[Predictive marginals and cavity messages]
\label{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}
Four facts fix the status of Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow_mf_optimum}. First, the strict mean-field factor is a different density. Substituting the kernel into the top-down term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mf_fixedpoint} gives
\[
\mathbb{E}_{q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}}\big[\log \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}\big] = \text{const} - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\Big[\big\|k_i - \Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\mu_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\big\|^2 + \operatorname{tr}\big(\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\Sigma_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}^\top\big)\Big],
\]
so in isolation from children and data the mean-field factor is $\mathcal{N}(\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}\mu_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}, \sigma^2 I)$: the parent covariance enters only the constant, and in the rigid limit the factor collapses to a point mass. Second, the predictive density~\eqref{eq:shadow_soft} equals the prior marginal of $k_i$ under $p_\sigma$ when $q_{\pi(i)}^{(\ell+1)}$ is the prior marginal of the parent. A parent posterior, by contrast, has absorbed the evidence of every leaf below the parent, including the leaves below agent $i$.

Third, evaluated against the full parent posterior, the shadow reuses each constituent's own evidence; the evidence-consistent downward message uses the parent posterior with that constituent's contribution removed. On a tree, belief propagation is exact~\cite[\S8.4]{Bishop2006}: the exact posterior of $k_i$ is proportional to the bottom-up evidence of agent $i$ times the cavity predictive density $\int \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}^{(\ell)}(k_i \mid k_{\pi(i)}) q_{\pi(i)}^{\setminus i}(k_{\pi(i)}) dk_{\pi(i)}$, where $q_{\pi(i)}^{\setminus i}$ is the parent posterior computed without the message from agent $i$. Multiplying the full-posterior predictive density by agent $i$'s likelihood counts that likelihood twice. An exact scalar example uses $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, $K\mid Z\sim\mathcal N(Z,1)$, and observed $o=1$ under $O\mid K\sim\mathcal N(K,1)$. The exact child posterior is $\mathcal N(2/3,2/3)$. The full parent posterior is $\mathcal N(1/3,2/3)$; predicting through the child kernel gives $\mathcal N(1/3,5/3)$, and treating this as a new prior before multiplying the same likelihood gives $\mathcal N(3/4,5/8)$. The difference is exact and exhibits the repeated evidence.

Fourth, the functional built from the predictive density is not an evidence bound. Replacing the mean-field cross term $\mathbb{E}_{q_i q_{\pi(i)}}[-\log \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}]$ by the predictive cross-entropy $\mathbb{E}_{q_i}[-\log \hat{p}_i]$ can only lower the objective, since $-\log$ is convex and Jensen's inequality gives $\mathbb{E}_{q_i}[-\log \int \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)} q_{\pi(i)}] \le \mathbb{E}_{q_i q_{\pi(i)}}[-\log \mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}]$. The resulting same-time shadow functional $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\mathrm{top}}\|p_{\mathrm{top}}) + \sum_i [D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|\hat{p}_i) - \mathbb{E}_{q_i}\log p(o_i \mid k_i)]$ can therefore fall below the surprisal $-\log p_\sigma(o)$, which no variational free energy of $p_\sigma$ can do. A direct witness needs no numerical minimization. Take one standard-normal parent, two children with link variance $1/2$, independent record noise variance $1/2$, and records $(0,0)$. The record covariance is $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so the true surprisal is $\log(2\pi)+\tfrac12\log3$. Choose $q_{\mathrm{top}}=\mathcal N(0,1/2)$ and both child laws $\mathcal N(0,1/3)$. Each predictive child variance is one. Substitution into the shadow functional gives $\log(2\pi)+\log3-\tfrac12\log2-\tfrac14$, whose difference from the surprisal is $\tfrac12\log(3/2)-\tfrac14<0$, since $\log(3/2)<1/2$. Thus this functional is not an evidence bound of the stated joint. In the rigid limit the child-parent term becomes $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|(\Omega_{i,\pi(i)})_* q_{\pi(i)})$ with both factors varying. Its mixed third variation~\eqref{eq:consensus_third_variation} is nonzero, so Theorem~\ref{thm:state_level_elbo_nogo} applies to it exactly as to the peer terms, and the same-time shadow edge has no fixed state-level evidence-bound representation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Evidence-Consistent Readings of Top-Down Influence}

Lemmas~\ref{lem:aug_joint_welldefined}--\ref{lem:shadow_mf_optimum} do not make the same-time shadow a derived prior. Four readings of top-down influence are consistent with one normalized joint. The first is the conditional coupling $\mathbb{E}_{q_{\pi(i)}} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|\mathcal{K}_{i,\pi(i)}(\cdot \mid k_{\pi(i)}))$, the mean-field cross term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mf_free_energy}; it is the conditional cross-scale divergence, and its rigid limit diverges as $1/\sigma^2$ unless the factors degenerate. The second is the cavity predictive density of Remark~\ref{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}, the exact downward message on a tree. The third is the random-effects, or empirical-Bayes, reading: the parent is a point hyperparameter $\theta = (m, S)$, the constituents are conditionally independent draws $k_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\Omega_{i,I} m, \Omega_{i,I} S \Omega_{i,I}^\top)$, and the shadow is then an honest prior. Fitting $\theta$ is type-II maximum likelihood, the barycenter of Eq.~\eqref{eq:meta_agent_barycenter} with its dispersion term is the exact update of $\theta$ at fixed constituent factors~\cite{Neal1998}, and the parent carries no belief of its own; $\Sigma_I$ is then a population covariance and not a posterior uncertainty, so this is a different model from Definition~\ref{def:augmented_joint}. The fourth is the one-episode-lagged reading of the main text, stated at the opening of this appendix, with the transported-copy transition $k_i^{(n+1)} = \Omega_{i,I} k_I^{(n)}$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross_scale_shadow} is its formula with the variational parent standing in for the filtered law. The first three readings are same-time; the fourth carries the participatory reading of Section~\ref{sec:cross_scale_shadows}.

In hierarchical generative models of cortical inference~\cite{friston2008hierarchical, parr2022active} the level-$\ell$ prior is the conditional $p(x^{(\ell)} \mid x^{(\ell+1)})$ of the generative model; the link kernel~\eqref{eq:gibbs_factor} plays that role here, with a gauge-equivariant mean and an isotropic variance. It is also a special case of the top-down Gaussian conditional of hierarchical variational autoencoders~\cite{Sonderby2016}, whose conditional variances are learned per dimension. Their bound evaluates this conditional at parent samples, which is the conditional coupling above and not the predictive density of Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow_mf_optimum}, and the rigid limit $\sigma^2 \to 0$ is singular for that variational problem.

The transported-copy specialization is the zero-noise limit. The finite-$\sigma^2$ density~\eqref{eq:shadow_soft} adds $\sigma^2 I$ to the transported parent covariance, giving a nonsingular predictive law. Choosing that noisy channel changes the generative model; it is distinct from changing the variational update within a fixed model.

The lemmas concern the cross-scale terms alone. Reinstating the within-scale alignment terms gives a fixed-point system in which each level-$\ell$ posterior balances the transported parental pull against the within-scale alignment energy. Because the softmax weights $\beta_{ij}$ are row-stochastic, a ``small $\beta_{ij}$'' is not a small parameter. The dimensionless coupling strengths are the raw alignment weights $\tilde\beta_{ij}, \tilde\gamma_{ij}$ of Section~\ref{sec:raw_alignment_weights}, and the within-scale terms vanish from the functional in the limit $\tilde\beta_{ij}, \tilde\gamma_{ij} \to 0$. Perturbative control of the within-scale corrections is open (Section~\ref{app:mf_fixedpoint_status}).

\subsection{Status of the Mean-Field Fixed-Point Question}
\label{app:mf_fixedpoint_status}

At zero within-scale coupling and under the Gaussian hypotheses below, the mean-field optimum on the augmented joint is exactly computable and unique, although the true posterior generally remains correlated (Section~\ref{app:cross_scale_gaussian_exception}). Transported-copy prediction between episodes, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross_scale_shadow}, is a different operation from these same-time mean-field coordinate equations. Its dynamical guarantees require a separate analysis. The same-time substitution of a predictive marginal also differs from the coordinate equations (Remark~\ref{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}). The within-scale question has three separate answers: a general obstruction for a fixed joint on the original state variables, a self-consistent auxiliary model that becomes fixed only after an equilibrium is selected, and an exact Gibbs lift whose random variable is an entire belief configuration (Theorem~\ref{thm:belief_configuration_gibbs_lift}, Appendix~\ref{sec:meta_agent_rg}).

\subsubsection{Fixed Representation on the State Variables: General Obstruction}

Let $(\mathsf{X}_i,\nu_i)$ be the state space of agent $i$, and let $\mathsf{T}_{ij}$ denote a fixed mass-preserving positive linear map on densities induced by a normalized communication channel, including $(\Omega_{ij})_\#$ as a special case. A family $\mathcal{Q}_i$ of strictly positive densities is called mixture-open here when every zero-mass tangent $h_i$ under consideration with $h_i/q_i\in L^\infty(q_i)$ generates $q_i(t)=q_i+t h_i\in\mathcal{Q}_i$ for all sufficiently small $|t|$. All differentiated integrals below are assumed finite.

\begin{theorem}[State-level mean-field ELBO obstruction]
\label{thm:state_level_elbo_nogo}
Let the factors $q_i\in\mathcal{Q}_i$ vary independently, let at least one nonself coefficient $\beta_{ij}>0$, and consider
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{cons}}(q)
=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rest}}(q)
+\sum_{a\ne b}\beta_{ab}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_a\|\mathsf{T}_{ab}q_b),
\label{eq:population_consensus_nogo_functional}
\end{equation}
where $D^3_{q_iq_jq_j}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rest}}=0$ for $i\ne j$. Assume that for one active edge $(i,j)$ there is an admissible tangent $h_j$ such that
\begin{equation}
g_{ij}:=\left(\frac{\mathsf{T}_{ij}h_j}{\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j}\right)^2
\label{eq:nogo_nondegenerate_tangent}
\end{equation}
is bounded and nonconstant $q_i$-almost everywhere. Then there is no density $p_\theta$ on $\prod_i\mathsf{X}_i$, independent of $q$, and no constant $C_0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{cons}}(q)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\bigotimes_iq_i\middle\|p_\theta\right)+C_0,
\label{eq:state_level_elbo_representation_impossible}
\end{equation}
throughout the open product family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $Q_q=\bigotimes_iq_i$. The standard fixed-model mean-field decomposition~\cite{Wainwright2008} gives
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_q\|p_\theta)
=\sum_i\int_{\mathsf{X}_i}q_i\log q_i d\nu_i
-\int_{\prod_i\mathsf{X}_i}\left(\prod_iq_i\right)\log p_\theta d\nu_{1:N}.
\label{eq:fixed_joint_mean_field_structure}
\end{equation}
The entropy is separable, while the fixed-model energy is affine in each factor. Therefore
\begin{equation}
D^3_{q_iq_jq_j}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_q\|p_\theta)[a_i,h_j,h_j]=0,
\qquad i\ne j.
\label{eq:fixed_joint_third_variation_zero}
\end{equation}
For the active consensus edge, take $q_i(s)=q_i+s a_i$ and $q_j(t)=q_j+t h_j$. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
\left.
\frac{\partial^3}{\partial s\partial t^2}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i(s)\|\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j(t))
\right|_{s=t=0}
=\int_{\mathsf{X}_i}a_i g_{ij}d\nu_i.
\label{eq:consensus_third_variation}
\end{equation}
Choose $a_i=q_i(g_{ij}-\mathbb{E}_{q_i}[g_{ij}])$, which has zero mass and satisfies $a_i/q_i\in L^\infty(q_i)$ because $g_{ij}$ is bounded; it is therefore admissible for a sufficiently small mixture path. Equation~\eqref{eq:consensus_third_variation} becomes $\operatorname{Var}_{q_i}(g_{ij})>0$, contradicting Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed_joint_third_variation_zero}. The terms collected in $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rest}}$ cannot cancel this mixed variation by hypothesis. Every other ordered-edge term also has zero $q_i,q_j,q_j$ variation: an unrelated edge omits one varied factor, while the reverse edge is affine in $q_j$ outside its entropy and that entropy's second variation is independent of $q_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the obstruction]
\label{rem:state_level_elbo_nogo_scope}
The theorem applies directly to the entropy-retaining functional by holding its independently varied attention rows fixed. The self terms, likelihood terms with fixed observation models, and categorical attention entropies lie in $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rest}}$. The same argument applies to the child-parent edge of a same-time shadow (Remark~\ref{rem:shadow_marginal_vs_mf}). The result concerns an ordinary mean-field factorization on the original agent-state variables. It does not exclude frozen source templates, restricted quadratic families, compatible auxiliary variables, a model selected after a fixed point, or a probability law over belief configurations. If the attention optimum $\beta^*(q)$ has already been substituted, its response derivatives define a different reduced functional and require a separate representation test.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Exact Cross-Scale Gaussian Exception}
\label{app:cross_scale_gaussian_exception}

At zero raw within-scale coupling ($\tilde\beta_{ij}=\tilde\gamma_{ij}=0$, so the alignment sectors are absent), the augmented joint of Definition~\ref{def:augmented_joint} is a closed variational object. Assume the top-level prior $p_{\mathrm{top}}$ is a nondegenerate Gaussian with full-rank precision $\Lambda_{\mathrm{top}}$, and take the leaf observation term absent or Gaussian; this is needed both for the negative log joint to be a quadratic form in the stacked latents and for the positive-definiteness below. The precision matrix of that quadratic form is block-sparse along the hierarchical tree, with nonzero blocks only on the diagonal and on parent-child edges. The diagonal block is $\sigma^{-2}\sum_{j:\pi(j)=i}\Omega_{j,i}^\top\Omega_{j,i}$ plus $\sigma^{-2}I$ when $i$ has a parent, plus $\Lambda_{\mathrm{top}}$ at the root and any local Gaussian likelihood precision; the off-diagonal block on each parent-child edge is $-\sigma^{-2}\Omega_{i,\pi(i)}$ together with its transpose. The link terms alone are only positive semidefinite, with kernel the consistent-propagation subspace on which $k_i = \Omega_{i,\pi(i)}k_{\pi(i)}$ along every edge. The full-rank prior precision at the top removes this kernel, so for finite $\sigma^2 > 0$ the stacked precision is symmetric positive definite, the joint is a proper Gaussian, and the coordinate-ascent mean-field updates of Lemma~\ref{lem:mf_fixedpoint} are Gauss-Seidel sweeps on a symmetric positive-definite system, which converge to the unique global optimum. In this regime the evidence-bound interpretation is exact and the mean-field fixed point exists and is unique. The factorized optimum is not generally the exact correlated posterior; its covariance blocks are inverses of the diagonal precision blocks, rather than the diagonal blocks of the full inverse.

\subsubsection{Self-Consistent Equilibrium Representation}

\begin{theorem}[Existence of a self-consistent source model]
\label{thm:self_consistent_source_equilibrium}
Let the latent alphabet $\mathsf{X}$ and the agent set be finite. Let $p_i(k)>0$, let $\pi_{ij}>0$ with $\sum_j\pi_{ij}=1$, and let $a_i>0$, $\lambda>0$, and $\tau>0$. Assume every stochastic transport has a uniform floor: there is $\epsilon>0$ such that $(\mathsf{T}_{ij}q)(k)\ge\epsilon$ for every probability vector $q$, state $k$, and active edge. Define
\begin{equation}
E_{ij}(q):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i\|\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j),
\qquad
\beta_{ij}(q):=
\frac{\pi_{ij}\exp[-E_{ij}(q)/\tau]}
{\sum_m\pi_{im}\exp[-E_{im}(q)/\tau]},
\label{eq:self_consistent_attention}
\end{equation}
and $\bar{a}_i:=a_i+\lambda$. The geometric-pooling response map
\begin{equation}
B_i(q)(k):=
\frac{
p_i(k)^{a_i/\bar{a}_i}
\prod_j[\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j(k)]^{\lambda\beta_{ij}(q)/\bar{a}_i}
}{
\sum_{u\in\mathsf{X}}
p_i(u)^{a_i/\bar{a}_i}
\prod_j[\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j(u)]^{\lambda\beta_{ij}(q)/\bar{a}_i}
}
\label{eq:self_consistent_response_map}
\end{equation}
has at least one receiver-response equilibrium $q^\star=B(q^\star)$. Write $\beta^\star:=\beta(q^\star)$.

At any such fixed point, define normalized row distributions
\begin{equation}
P_i^\star(k,j):=\pi_{ij}[\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j^\star](k),
\qquad
Q_i^\star(k,j):=\beta_{ij}^\star q_i^\star(k).
\label{eq:equilibrium_source_joint}
\end{equation}
They satisfy the exact identity
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_i^\star\|P_i^\star)
=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^\star D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_i^\star\|\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j^\star)
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\beta_i^\star\|\pi_i).
\label{eq:equilibrium_row_kl_identity}
\end{equation}
The identity is algebraic: it holds with $(q^\star,\beta^\star)$ replaced by any $(q,\beta)$ in which each $q_i$ and each row $\beta_i$ is a probability vector, and in particular at the equilibrium.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The uniform floor makes every energy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:self_consistent_attention} continuous on the closed product simplex. Hence $\beta(q)$ and $B(q)$ are continuous, and $B$ maps that compact convex simplex into itself. Brouwer's theorem gives a receiver-response equilibrium. For fixed source templates and fixed $\beta_i$, the Euler equation for
\begin{equation}
a_iD_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|p_i)
+\lambda\sum_j\beta_{ij}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j),
\label{eq:frozen_row_geometric_pool_objective}
\end{equation}
under $\sum_k r(k)=1$ gives $r=B_i(q)$, so the fixed point is self-consistent with the frozen-row optimum. Expanding $\log[Q_i/P_i]=\log(\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij})+\log[q_i(k)/\mathsf{T}_{ij}q_j(k)]$ and summing against $Q_i$ over $(k,j)$, using $\sum_kq_i(k)=\sum_j\beta_{ij}=1$, proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:equilibrium_row_kl_identity} for every such $(q,\beta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the equilibrium construction proves]
\label{rem:equilibrium_model_scope}
The distributions $P_i^\star$ are fixed and normalized only after $q^\star$ has been selected. Their product is a proper auxiliary model over independent row variables, but it duplicates the transported source templates. It is not automatically a joint over shared latent variables $(k_1,\ldots,k_N)$. Such a joint additionally requires compatible conditionals, an acyclic factorization, or an undirected normalized potential. Equation~\eqref{eq:equilibrium_row_kl_identity} matches the ordinary row KL at unit attention temperature; arbitrary $\tau$ gives the tempered loss-based update of Eq.~\eqref{eq:self_consistent_attention}, a generalized-Bayesian construction and not one unscaled KL~\cite{BissiriHolmesWalker2016}. Because the response holds incoming source roles fixed, its equilibrium is not thereby a stationary point of the global smoothing scalar. The theorem proves existence, not uniqueness or convergence of a particular update schedule.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Scalar Objectives and Update Fields}

The within-scale construction requires a distinction between three objects. First, the canonical entropy-retaining row objective is $\mathcal{E}_{i,\mathrm{full}}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}E_{ij}+\tau\sum_j\beta_{ij}\log(\beta_{ij}/\pi_{ij})$. At its simplex optimum it is exactly $-\tau\log Z_i$, and the envelope theorem gives $\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}_{i,\mathrm{red}}=\sum_j\beta_{ij}^*\mathrm{d}E_{ij}$. Summing over rows therefore gives one scalar potential with a symmetric Hessian. Second, the entropy-suppressed quantity $S=\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}^*(x)E_{ij}(x)$ is a different scalar. Its response terms change its gradient but do not change the symmetry of its Hessian. Third, the deliberately asymmetric field
\begin{equation}
V_i^{\mathrm{rec}}(x):=\partial_{x_i}\left(\sum_j\beta_{ij}^*(x)E_{ij}(x)\right),
\qquad
\text{with all }\partial_{x_i}\left(\sum_m\beta_{\ell m}^*E_{\ell m}\right),\ \ell\ne i,\ \text{discarded},
\label{eq:receiver_only_field}
\end{equation}
is not the gradient of either scalar and is generically nonintegrable because cross-row mixed derivatives need not match. This nonpotential field is an optional update approximation, not a property of the softmax, the noncompact group, or differentiation of a scalar. The consensus functional remains a composite scalar potential and not a fixed state-level generative-model evidence bound; that distinction does not require a nonsymmetric Hessian.

Existence for the full Gaussian system is more delicate than the compact finite-state result of Theorem~\ref{thm:self_consistent_source_equilibrium}. A restricted stationary point exists under a symmetry hypothesis: the mean-only consensus state $\mu_i \equiv 0$ is stationary for every prior strength $\alpha>0$, temperature $\tau>0$, and finite $N$ when the prior means vanish and observation forcing is absent, because every pairwise alignment mean-gradient $\Lambda_{t,ij}(\mu_i-\Omega_{ij}\mu_j)$ and the prior pull vanish there. This does not prove a fixed point for the covariance and frame coordinates. For live state-dependent softmax weights on $\mathrm{GL}^+(K_q)$, local stability, global uniqueness, and global convergence remain open, and noncompact frame directions prevent a direct compactness argument unless one imposes a gauge slice, a bounded invariant container, or a coercive frame prior. A Banach guarantee holds in a restricted setting. Freeze covariances and attention weights, restrict transports to the orthogonal group, and take isotropic couplings $\Lambda_{t,ij}=\lambda_{ij}I$ with an arbitrary symmetric positive-definite $\Lambda_p$. The mean sub-map $\mu_i\leftarrow(\alpha\Lambda_p+b_iI)^{-1}(\alpha\Lambda_p\mu_{p,i}+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\lambda_{ij}\Omega_{ij}\mu_j)$, with $b_i=\sum_j\beta_{ij}\lambda_{ij}$, then has row norm $\sum_j\|(\alpha\Lambda_p+b_iI)^{-1}\beta_{ij}\lambda_{ij}\Omega_{ij}\|_2=b_i/[\alpha\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda_p)+b_i]<1$, so it is a contraction in the norm $\max_i\|\mu_i\|_2$. This row-norm proof uses isotropy. For anisotropic positive matrices, dividing by the largest eigenvalue of their sum is not a valid upper bound. For example, take $\alpha\Lambda_p=I/10$, $B=\operatorname{diag}(1,9/10)$, and $C=\operatorname{diag}(99,1/10)$. Then
\[
\|(\alpha\Lambda_p+B+C)^{-1}B\|_2=9/11,
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(B)/\lambda_{\max}(B+C)=1/100.
\]
Even commuting anisotropic matrices invalidate that proposed estimate. A valid general bound uses $\lambda_{\min}(\alpha\Lambda_p+B+C)$ in the denominator. Failure of a sufficient contraction estimate is not proof of divergent dynamics. A contraction, monotonicity, or Lyapunov argument for any enlarged class must be supplied separately.

The remaining obligations are a quantitative comparison between a normalized cross-scale model and an explicitly supplied consensus perturbation, compatibility when cyclic latent conditionals are asserted, and existence and stability for the chosen fully coupled selective dynamics. Setting one consensus channel weight to zero does not by itself reduce the other channels to this Gaussian hierarchical model.
