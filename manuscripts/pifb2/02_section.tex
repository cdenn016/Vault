\section{Assumptions, Results and Open Obligations}\label{sec:epistemic_status}

The framework has a small set of primitive choices: a continuous base and local domains; a gauge action on a statistical sample space; normalized priors, record kernels and interaction channels; a recognition family; and, when dynamics are discussed, a metric and mobility or a stochastic generator. Coarse-graining additionally requires a retained channel. An episode-history law for latent links is an additional normalized model with a declared increment penalty. Its Euclidean stationarity does not supply a real-time kinetic action, Lorentz soldering, thermodynamic interpretation or physical ontology.

Most mathematical results are conditional identities or constructions. An evidence bound follows from a normalized joint, source attention follows from an explicit label model, and coarse actions follow from integration. None of these identities selects the joint, the source alphabet or the retention by itself. The distinction lets the theory use exact results without claiming that the variational principle derives every modeling ingredient.

The technical appendices sometimes use the short status codes P for a proved conditional claim, D for an explicit derivation, S for a structural correspondence, C for a conjecture or open obligation, and E for empirical evidence. These codes classify statements rather than certify a whole section. No empirical realization of the physical program is reported here.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:scope_limitations}
Formation and inheritance are analyzed at one fixed base point. The smooth base remains the ambient setting; conditional base geometry appears only when a derivative or soldering is explicitly supplied. Gaussian families, fixed links, compact gauge reductions, prescribed partitions and imposed rate separation are named special cases. They are not assumptions of every result.

A normalizer matters whenever parameters are varied. A posterior from one inference problem cannot be silently reused as a likelihood in another. A small marginal divergence need not control derivatives, force errors or path laws. A successful first parent does not establish the next formation episode. These are the principal sources of overextension the revised construction avoids.

\subsection{Access and agent-level realization}\label{sec:access_obligations}
An agent's frame, belief and model are its own, and no other agent reads them except through a declared channel or record. Section~\ref{sec:access_convention} states what an agent holds and gives the collective law $Q$, the structural collection $X$, the free energy $\mathcal F_V$ and the consensus potential $\mathcal E$ their standing as population-level objects. Their stationarity conditions and flows are results about the population law. Whether an agent can carry out its part of such a flow from what it holds is a further question, and it is answered here on the receiver side only. A receiver can evaluate its response to a received law (Section~\ref{sec:selective_stiffness} and Appendix~\ref{sec:rock_derivations}), and in the full-state idealization of Remark~\ref{rem:full_state_idealization} the received law is the sender's whole law by hypothesis. The Gaussian consensus calculations of Section~\ref{sec:mass} and of the Gaussian appendices are the specialization of the received-law form to that idealization, evaluated at the level of the population.

Five constructions have no agent-level realization in this monograph, and none is claimed. In the full-state idealization each sender-row term of the mean and covariance gradients of the consensus potential is the attention weight $\beta_{ki}$ of an agent $k$ attending to the source $i$, multiplied by a function of the source's own state and of $k$'s law in the source's coordinates (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sender_row_reduced}). The weight belongs to the attention row of agent $k$, the source holds $k$'s law only when $k$ also sends to it, and no return channel carrying the weight is declared. The frame update of Appendix~\ref{app:gradients} contains sender-row terms with the same weights $\beta_{ki}$, and the frames of the attending agents cancel from them. The latent-link recognition law of Section~\ref{sec:latent_lie_links} and its learning rule read the moments of both endpoints, their cross covariance and a chart built from both frames, and no endpoint is assigned that law. The acceptance test for parameter learning in Section~\ref{sec:adiabatic_hierarchy} reads the value of $\mathcal F_V$. The cross-covariance terms of the correlated link cost of Eq.~\eqref{eq:correlated_link_cost} read $\operatorname{Cov}(k_i,k_j)$, which neither endpoint holds. An agent-level version would require further constructions, among them a declared return message carrying the weights, a link law owned by one endpoint and learned from what that endpoint receives, an acceptance rule that reads one agent's block, and a declared means by which a cross covariance reaches an endpoint. Whether rules built in this way would reproduce the population-level results stated here is open. Until such constructions are supplied, the statements describe the population and do not specify a protocol for its members.

\subsection{Location of the longer proofs}\label{sec:companion_theory}
Results are stated with their hypotheses where they are used, and the longer proofs are collected in the appendices. The fixed-temperature pairing-selection theorem is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:selection_proof}. The latent-link propositions, the measure-theoretic conditions and the coarse link laws are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:latent_link_proofs}, and the remaining supporting results are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:supporting_proofs}. The text does not treat its prototypes as the only possible agents, nor an unresolved prototype as a general impossibility theorem.
