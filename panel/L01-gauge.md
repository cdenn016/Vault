## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A physics referee will read "pure gauge" as "no physics " and Section 2 currently invites that reading without answering it. The honest answer exists and is short (L01-1): in Regime I the frames are a redundancy removable by $q_i\mapsto(U_i^{-1})_\#q_i$  and all frame-dependent content is relative to fields held fixed. Stating this once  with the dressing-field and "artificial versus substantial gauge symmetry" literature as precedent  would make the gauge vocabulary defensible and would also sharpen the Role A / Role B device at 194-198  which is new terminology a physicist has to learn and which the QRF citations only motivate.

The subsections on field strength (360-368)  Yang-Mills curvature  "four curvatures" (427-429)  and the north-pole holonomy illustration are Regime II or base-geometry material that the implemented rung never uses. For a physics journal they should shrink to a paragraph that states Regime I is flat and Regime II is the lattice-gauge extension on the agent graph  with the Wilson action and its $\mathrm{GL}^+$ caveat. "Gauge-group curvature" (429) is not a standard fourth curvature: for $\mathrm{GL}^+(K)$ it depends on a non-canonical left-invariant metric (no bi-invariant one exists)  and only the compact case has a canonical value. "Yang-Mills" should not appear outside Regime II; the paper already concedes it asserts no continuum Yang-Mills theory (418).

Symbol and convention clashes: $\beta$ is both the attention weight and the Wilson coupling (eq:wilson_action); $\mathcal N$ is the total space while $\mathcal N(\mu \Sigma)$ is a Gaussian; the paper mixes the right-action convention for the associated bundle (225) with left-action link conventions (380) without flagging the corresponding forms of the connection law (L01-3). "Čech cocycle" on patches indexed by agents  which coincide in the zero-dimensional limit  is really a groupoid of frame comparisons; the Theory's "frame comparisons at one base point" (prop:geo-frame-comparisons) is the cleaner language. "Zero-dimensional gauge theory" (512) will be read as a matrix model; here the active gauge group at a point is one copy of $G$  a global symmetry.

At 2112 the list "Higgs mass generation  QCD confinement  and the chirality of the weak interaction" as empirical consequences of gauge invariance proper will draw an objection: gauge invariance is a redundancy of description (a local gauge symmetry cannot be spontaneously broken)  confinement is a dynamical property of a particular gauge theory  and chirality is an input of the matter representation  not a consequence of gauge invariance. The sentence should be softened to "the structure of the gauge group and matter content has specific empirical consequences."

Wheeler's clue. The paper does not use "the boundary of a boundary is zero." Wheeler lists it as the first of five clues and calls it "the unifying theme of Maxwell electrodynamics  Einstein geometrodynamics" (Wheeler 1990  §19.4; full text fetched; cites Kheyfets and Wheeler  Int. J. Theor. Phys. 25 (1986) 573). The framework has it in two places already  unstated. Lemma thm:vanishing_holonomy is the statement that the coboundary of a coboundary is trivial ($\Omega=\delta U$ implies $\delta\Omega=I$)  the lowest nonabelian instance of $\delta^2=0$. In Regime II  once orientation reversal is imposed (L01-4)  the product of based face holonomies over the boundary of any 3-simplex of the agent complex is the identity  the lattice Bianchi identity (verified item 19). A short paragraph making these two identifications would tie the gauge structure to Wheeler directly. It should not claim more: the Kheyfets-Wheeler route to conservation laws needs a source coupled to the connection  which PIFB2 does not have.

What is condensable for a physics venue in my scope: the long multi-head/thin-SVD passage (1195-1212) and the GLU-gating paragraph (1282-1288) are machine-learning material; the executable-contract table (438-456) and code field names throughout Section 2 belong in a supplement or a methods appendix.
