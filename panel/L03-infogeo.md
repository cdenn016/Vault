### L03-1: The conditional representation theorem covers the receiver-only best response, but the body cites it for the canonical scalar functional
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3000-3036 (`eq:F_i_app`, `thm:representation_app`), cited at 528, 594, 678, 2894; the executable authority is stated at 733 (`eq:free_energy_functional_final`, one scalar total).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** The theorem's variational problem is agent $i$'s local problem $F_i=\min_{q_i}\{\mathrm{KL}(q_i\|p_i)+\sum_j\beta_{ij}\mathcal D(q_i,q_j)\}$ with every neighbor $q_j$ held fixed (line 3003). In the canonical functional, which line 733 names as "the executable authority used throughout", $q_i$ also sits in the second slot of every sender row $\beta_{\ell i}\mathrm{KL}(q_\ell\|\Omega_{\ell i}q_i)$. The manuscript itself separates the full scalar from a "receiver-only field that is not used by the checked-in optimizer" (594, 733, 2430). Three executed checks in `L03_representation_gap.py`:
(1) SymPy gives the second-slot functional derivative of $\int p f(q/p)$ as $f(r)-rf'(r)$. For KL this is $1-r$, the same kind of $1/q_i$ term that line 2964 uses to rule out the reverse KL. If both slot derivatives are required to be affine in $\log r$, then $f''=a/r$ and $-rf''=c/r$, which forces $a=c=0$. So no nonzero $f$-divergence gives free-form exponential-family closure once an agent is also a sender.
(2) Take two agents in 1D with $\Omega=I$, $\beta_{12}=\beta_{21}=1$ ($B=1$), $p_1=\mathcal N(0,1)$, $p_2=\mathcal N(3,0.25)$. The geometric-pool fixed point is $q_1=\mathcal N(2,0.5)$, $q_2=\mathcal N(2.667,0.333)$. At that point the receiver-only gradient is exactly 0, but the gradient of the joint scalar is $(-1.333,-0.278,2.000,-0.917)$, norm 2.59. The joint Gaussian minimizer sits elsewhere: $q_1=\mathcal N(1.877,0.835)$, $q_2=\mathcal N(2.531,0.598)$, with $F=3.139$ against 3.536 at the pool.
(3) At that joint minimizer the free-form stationarity residual $2\log q_1-\log p_1-\log q_2-q_2/q_1$ varies from 0.92 to 1.43 over $x\in[-2,4]$. It is not constant, so no free-form Gaussian stationary point exists.
So the claim at 678, that "the geometric-mean Boltzmann belief ... follow[s] exactly" from the canonical functional, is false as written. Line 594's "unique $f$-divergence consistent with the geometric-mean Boltzmann stationary form for the belief update" is true only for the frozen-neighbor best response. In the implemented Gaussian-parametric setting the family imposes closure, so the closure criterion cannot select the divergence of the executed functional.
**Fix:** Restate the scope wherever the theorem is invoked (528, 594, 678, 2894, 2959, 3152, 3158). Suggested replacement at 594: "Within that class, the forward KL is the unique $f$-divergence for which agent $i$'s best response, with neighbor beliefs and attention held fixed, is the geometric-mean Boltzmann form; in the joint scalar functional, where each belief is also a transported template in its neighbors' rows, no nonzero $f$-divergence yields free-form exponential-family closure, and the Gaussian implementation imposes closure through the family." Delete "the geometric-mean Boltzmann belief" from the list at 678, or qualify it the same way. Alternatively, anchor the KL direction on the Theory's source-label joint (see complementarity notes), which fixes the divergence without the closure argument.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the stationary condition of `eq:free_energy_functional_final` with respect to $q_i$ contains no sender-row terms, or that the geometric pool is a stationary point of the joint scalar. The two-agent witness would then show a zero joint gradient.
**Status:** P for the receiver-only problem (Step 2 and Lemma `lem:richness_app` verified). As cited in the body, D is inflated to "pinned" and "follow exactly" for an object the theorem does not cover.

### L03-4: Interpretation paragraph misstates what is special about KL among f-divergences
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3152 ("whose Hessian induces the Fisher-Rao metric and yields the mixed/exponential family-projection Pythagorean theorem. These global properties are not shared by generic $f$-divergences").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Every smooth $f$-divergence has second-order jet $\tfrac{f''(1)}{2}g_F$. That is the invariance content of Čencov's theorem and appears in the divergence treatment of Amari and Nagaoka 2000, Ch. 3. `L03_divergences.py` (c) checks this on the 1D Gaussian family: $D/\varepsilon^2$ against $\tfrac{f''(1)}2 g_F$ gives relative differences of $5.7\times10^{-4}$ (KL), $3.1\times10^{-4}$ (squared Hellinger, $f''(1)=1/2$) and $1.1\times10^{-3}$ (chi-squared, $f''(1)=2$) at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. Each is ten times smaller than at $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$, which is the expected $O(\varepsilon)$ third-order remainder. What is specific to KL is being both an $f$-divergence and a Bregman divergence on normalized distributions. Amari, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 55(11) (2009) 4925-4931, abstract: "this is the only such one in the space of probability distributions". The companion property is the dually flat ($\pm1$) structure with its Pythagorean relation (Amari and Nagaoka 2000, Ch. 3). The abstract was read from the IEEE listing; the full PDF was not accessible.
**Fix:** "the forward KL is the Bregman divergence of the negative-entropy potential; among $f$-divergences on normalized distributions it is the only Bregman divergence (Amari 2009), and it is the canonical divergence of the dually flat $e/m$ structure, whence the Pythagorean relation. The Fisher metric, by contrast, is the local Hessian of every smooth $f$-divergence, up to the factor $f''(1)$." Add the Amari 2009 reference.
**Falsifies:** A smooth $f$-divergence whose second-order jet on a regular family is not proportional to Fisher. The executed check found none for three generators.
**Status:** S-level interpretive sentence containing one false comparative.

### L03-7: The distinguishability unit and the "arc length" of a finite mean step

## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A physics referee will read Section `sec:time_info` next to three bodies of literature that PIFB2 does not engage there. First, thermodynamic length. Salamon and Berry, Phys. Rev. Lett. 51 (1983) 1127, and Crooks, "Measuring thermodynamic length", Phys. Rev. Lett. 99 (2007) 100602, define a Fisher-metric length on equilibrium states that bounds the dissipation of finite-time protocols. The clock-dissipation identity `eq:clock_dissipation` is the gradient-flow counterpart of that relation, and a statistical-physics referee will ask for the comparison. Second, Wootters, Phys. Rev. D 23 (1981) 357, is the physics source for Fisher arc length as a count of distinguishable states. It supports the unit discussion at 1831 better than Cover and Thomas does. Third, Caticha's entropic dynamics. It is cited at 132 and 2084 but not in the time section, even though Caticha, J. Phys. A 44 (2011) 225303, introduces an "entropic time" as a bookkeeping device for change within an information-geometric dynamics. That is close to register entry P1. The list of contrasted proposals at 1835 (Page-Wootters, thermal time, entropic spacetime) should add entropic time and say how P1 differs: P1 uses the agent's own belief trajectory and a Riemannian length, with no duration postulate fixed by a diffusion scale. The positioning against Frieden at 2084 is adequate, and I found no Fisher-information claim in my scope that relies on extreme physical information. The Crooks, Wootters and Caticha 2011 references were located by web search (publisher listings); Salamon and Berry 1983 is cited from the standard record and was not re-fetched.

The "proper time ... runs backward" wording at 1840 was already flagged on September 13. The correct contrast is that proper time is the Lorentzian length of a timelike curve and is maximized, not minimized, by geodesics.

The phrase "this granular bit reading is what realizes Wheeler's 'it from bit' at the level of temporal flow" (1831) attaches "realizes" to S-level content.
