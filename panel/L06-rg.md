### L06-1: The construction is an exact block-variable coarse-graining plus a truncation, not a renormalization group in the physicist's sense, yet the paper's organizing claim calls it an RG spine
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:81 ("one rung of a renormalization-group spine"); section titles at 1679 and 3569; 1804 ("follows a single renormalization-group spine"); 3561 ("the correct Wilsonian object"); 3721-3723 (Wilsonian relevance paragraph); 694 ("The same $\lambda_h$ appears at every scale in the renormalization-group free energy"); 3937 (finite-size scaling).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** A statistical physicist will look for four things: a coarse-graining step, a rescaling that maps the coarse theory back to the original space of couplings, a flow on that coupling space, and fixed points with linearized relevant and irrelevant directions. PIFB2 has only the first. (i) No rescaling is defined anywhere in 3569-3941; the parent state $Y_I$ lives on the same fiber as a child, but the number of agents drops. (ii) No coupling space is identified across scales. $\mathcal R_s$ maps a state space with $|\mathcal I_s|$ agents to one with $N_{s+1}<|\mathcal I_s|$, so the "projected RG map" $R_{\mathfrak M}(\theta_s)$ at 3814 maps $\Theta_s$ to a different space $\Theta_{s+1}$, and the fixed-point equation $\theta^*=R(\theta^*)$ and the multipliers of $DR_{\theta^*}$ invoked at 3723 have no meaning. The paper concedes this for the exact map at 3789 (`thm:rg_semigroup`: "not an RG semigroup acting on a coupling space"), but 3723 still classifies relevant, irrelevant, and marginal directions as if one existed. (iii) The system is finite. With blocking ratio at least $b>1$ the tower ends after at most $\log_b N$ steps, so no iterated limit exists in which a fixed point could appear. The Theory proves exactly this (`c11_network_coarsegraining.tex`, `prop:rg-interaction-finite-network-obstruction`: six binary agents blocked into three give interaction spaces of dimension 63 and 7, and no comparison isomorphism exists). (iv) Partitions are chosen adaptively by a detector (3791), so the map is state-dependent and not a homogeneous flow. (v) The "belief couplings" $\beta^{(s)}$ whose renormalization is written at 1683 are attention weights, which are state variables (softmax of the current edge energies, 3701) and not coupling constants. The coupling constants proper ($\tau$, $\pi$, $\lambda_h$, $\lambda_0$, $\rho$) are declared unrenormalized by fiat (694). (vi) At the operating point, $T_{\mathrm{cfg}}\to0$, which is the only point at which the paper says the Gibbs ensemble touches the implemented dynamics (3574), Eq. `eq:rg_exact_free_energy` reduces to constrained minimization over the fiber. All fluctuation-induced renormalization (the log-determinant, anharmonic corrections) is $O(T_{\mathrm{cfg}})$ and absent from what is implemented. At $T_{\mathrm{cfg}}=0$ what remains is a Landau-type mean-field coarse-graining. The paper's own status paragraph (1681) is accurate ("RG-inspired rather than a Wilsonian RG analysis"). The organizing sentence at 81 and the conclusion at 1804 contradict it in the physics sense. In physics terms, what is actually constructed is a Kadanoff-type block transformation of a posited Gibbs ensemble, without rescaling, followed by projection onto an ansatz.
**Fix:** Replace "renormalization-group spine" at 81 and 1804 with "hierarchical coarse-graining spine" and add one sentence: "The coarse-graining is an exact block-variable pushforward of a posited configuration ensemble followed by a truncation; no rescaling, coupling-space flow, or fixed point is constructed, and on a finite network none can be (the tower has at most $\log_b N$ levels)." Retitle Section 1679 "Coarse-Graining III: Exact Coarse-Graining, Closure, and Its Relation to the Renormalization Group" and Appendix I "Exact Coarse-Graining for Meta-Agent Formation". Delete the Wilsonian-relevance half of the "Retention rules" paragraph (3723), or state that it presupposes a declared comparison map between coupling spaces, which the construction lacks. At 694, write "is held fixed across scales by assumption; the exact pushforward would in general renormalize it". At 3561, add "up to the rescaling step, which is not defined here". Cite Theory `c01_program_scope.tex` (the scope paragraph at 179 declares that no semigroup with rescaling, fixed points, or beta functions is constructed) and `prop:rg-interaction-finite-network-obstruction` as the rigorous statement of why.
**Falsifies:** A definition in PIFB2 of a rescaling $\zeta_s$ plus a comparison isomorphism $\Theta_{s+1}\cong\Theta_s$ under which $R_{\mathfrak M}$ becomes a self-map with a computed fixed point.
**Status:** The exact pushforward is P. "RG spine" as the organizing claim is S and is inflated to a structural result by its placement in the introduction and conclusion.

### L06-2: Assumption (A6) is either unsatisfiable for the manuscript's own coarse-graining map or satisfiable by every map, so `thm:rg_exact_closure` has no content beyond a Gaussian integral; the quadratic-plus-log-determinant class does not close under iteration
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3733-3737 (Assumption (A6)); 3818-3846 (`thm:rg_exact_closure` and proof); 3848 (remark); 1683 and 3939 (body and summary citations of the theorem).
**Severity:** high
**Evidence:** (a) *Literal reading: unsatisfiable.* (A6) requires the full fiber $\mathcal R_s^{-1}(Y)$ to be a complete affine space $\mathbb R^r$ carrying Lebesgue measure. The map $\mathcal R_s$ retains the parent covariance $\Sigma_I=\sum_i w_i[\widetilde\Sigma_i+(\widetilde\mu_i-\mu_I)(\widetilde\mu_i-\mu_I)^\top]$ (3769), and the paper insists the dispersion term be retained. Fixing $\Sigma_I$ then forces $\sum_i w_i\xi_i\xi_i^\top\prec\Sigma_I$ on the mean deviations and bounds the child covariances as well, so every fiber is bounded. The remark at 3848 concedes this only for the compact frame factor. `L06_closure_fiber.py` (3), with $K_q=1$, $|I|=2$, $w=1/2$ and $\Sigma_I=1$, finds sup $|\xi|=0.9977\le1$ over $2\times10^5$ fiber samples. (b) *Chart reading: tautological.* If "a disintegration chart identifies the full fiber with $\mathbb R^r$" allows any diffeomorphism, then every fiber diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^r$ with a smooth positive density is mapped exactly onto a Gaussian by the monotone Knothe-Rosenblatt chart. (A6) then holds with $A_{\xi\xi}=I$ and $Q(Y)=\mathcal F^{\mathrm{exact}}_{s+1}(Y)+\tfrac{rT}{2}\log(2\pi T)$ (quadratic only if $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{exact}}_{s+1}$ is; otherwise let $A(Y)$ absorb the remainder). The displayed formula becomes a relabeling of $-T\log(\text{fiber mass})$. `L06_closure_fiber.py` (4) constructs this chart for an arbitrary density on $(-1,1)$ and confirms the mass identity. (c) *Idle hypotheses.* The proof uses only (A6). It says (A1)-(A5) and (A7) "enter upstream", but none appears in the derivation. (d) *Non-closure.* The theorem's title says "closure", but $V(Y)=\tfrac T2\log\det A_{\xi\xi}(Y)$ is not quadratic whenever $A$ depends on $Y$. SymPy gives $\partial_y^4\tfrac T2\log(a_0+a_1y^2)\ne0$. The next-scale fiber density is therefore non-Gaussian, (A6) fails at $s+1$, and the class is not preserved. In `L06_closure_fiber.py` (2), the next-scale free energy differs from its best quadratic fit by up to $0.145$ ($T=0.5$, $a_0=1$, $a_1=3$). Only the constant-block case iterates, and there the log-determinant is constant and the step is ordinary Gaussian decimation (a Schur complement). (e) The case the theorem is actually good for exists: a mean-only linear coarse-graining $Y=\mu_I=\sum_i w_i\widetilde\mu_i$ at fixed covariances, frames, and non-envelope $\beta$. There the five-term mean sector is exactly quadratic with constant blocks, the fiber is affine, and $A_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in `eq:rg_app_schur_complement` is exact.
**Fix:** Restate the theorem as a lemma: "For the mean-only linear coarse-graining at fixed covariances, frames, and couplings, the exact pushforward of the mean-sector Gibbs density is Gaussian with precision $A_{\mathrm{eff}}=A_{YY}-A_{Y\xi}A_{\xi\xi}^{-1}A_{\xi Y}$." Drop the $Y$-dependent log-determinant clause, or keep it explicitly as a one-step identity with the sentence "the class is not closed under iteration: a $Y$-dependent log-determinant generates non-quadratic terms at the next scale." Remove (A1)-(A5) and (A7) from the hypotheses. In (A6), say "affine in the native mean coordinates", and state that the map retaining $\Sigma_I$ does not satisfy it. Update 1683 and the summary at 3939 accordingly. The rigorous general statement is the Theory's law-level exact step, and closure generically requires hyperedges (`c11`, `prop:rg-hyperedge-generation`); cite it.
**Falsifies:** A coarse-graining map in PIFB2's state space that retains $\Sigma_I$ and has affine, unbounded fibers in native coordinates. Or a precise restriction on admissible charts that makes (A6) nontrivial and still satisfiable by the stated $\mathcal R_s$.
**Status:** As stated, P but vacuous, or P but tautological. The title and 3939 inflate it to a closure result.

### L06-3: The holonomy residual $\mathcal H_{IJ}$ and hypothesis (A7) are identically trivial in the appendix's own state space
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3739 ($\Omega_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}$); 3866-3880 (`\Omega_{IJ}^R`, $\mathcal H_{IJ}$, "one of the principal closure-error terms"); 3737 (A7); 3890 ($C_2\sum\mathcal H_{IJ}$ in `thm:rg_residual`).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** With pure-gauge transport, $\Theta^{IJ}_{ij}=\Omega_{Ii}\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{jJ}=U_IU_i^{-1}U_iU_j^{-1}U_jU_J^{-1}=U_IU_J^{-1}$ for every $i\in I$, $j\in J$. `L06_karcher_semigroup.py` (c) finds a maximum spread of $4.5\times10^{-16}$ over random SO(3) frames. A nonzero $\mathcal H_{IJ}$ requires independent link variables (Regime II, or the Theory's independent dynamic links), and the appendix's $X_s=\{q_i,p_i,U_i,\chi_i\}$ does not contain them. Base-point variation of $U_i(c)$ does not rescue it, since all edges in $\Theta$ are evaluated at the same $c$.
**Fix:** Either add independent links $\Omega_{ij}$ to $X_s$ (Regime II) and say $\mathcal H_{IJ}$ measures their non-flatness, or state "$\mathcal H_{IJ}\equiv0$ in Regime I; it becomes a closure error only with independent links", and drop (A7) as a separate hypothesis in Regime I.
**Falsifies:** A reading of the appendix in which $\Omega_{Ii}$ or $\Omega_{jJ}$ is not built from the same frames $U_i$, $U_J$ used in $\Omega_{ij}$.
**Status:** D (as a residual definition). The claim "principal closure-error term" is false in Regime I.

### L06-4: Functoriality is proved, but the content-bearing semigroup law fails for $\mathcal R_s$ in the frame sector
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3789-3803 (`thm:rg_semigroup`); 3805-3806.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The theorem $(\mathcal R_{s+1})_*(\mathcal R_s)_*=(\mathcal R_{s+1}\circ\mathcal R_s)_*$ is correct for any measurable maps, and the paper labels it composability. On a chain of resolutions, though, the composition law imposes no condition at all (Theory `c09`, `prop:rg-scale-functoriality`(i)). The physicist's semigroup statement is that blocking twice equals blocking once with the merged partition, $\mathcal R_{b'}\circ\mathcal R_b=\mathcal R_{bb'}$ (`prop:rg-scale-functoriality`(ii)). For PIFB2's map this fails through the Karcher frame. `L06_karcher_semigroup.py` (b): for six SO(3) frames within 0.5 rad, the two-stage Karcher mean (pairs of triples, then the two means) differs from the one-stage mean with product weights by a geodesic distance of $7.3\times10^{-3}$. The belief sector does compose. Given a common final frame, two-stage M-projection equals one-stage M-projection with product weights (mixture of mixtures): $|\Delta\mu|=2.3\times10^{-16}$, $|\Delta\Sigma|=2.2\times10^{-15}$. Frames enter the belief transport, so the full map inherits the frame failure. Adaptive partitions break homogeneity in addition, as the paper notes at 3806.
**Fix:** Add after `thm:rg_semigroup`: "On a chain of scales this composability imposes no condition. The stronger semigroup property, that two successive blockings equal one blocking with the merged partition, holds for the M-projection belief moments given a common final frame, but fails for the Karcher frame mean on a curved group, so $\mathcal R_s$ is not a semigroup even for frozen partitions."
**Falsifies:** A Karcher-composition identity on SO(3) for non-commuting data (the script's counterexample would then be a numerical artifact; tolerance there is $10^{-15}$ on the fixed-point step).
**Status:** P for the theorem as stated. It is correctly fenced; the gap is the missing negative statement a physics reader will ask for.

### L06-5: The belief-mean-sector paragraph is correct, but its positive result is a Galerkin (aggregation) coarse operator, not a renormalization, and its probabilistic meaning depends on the level
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:1685 (in the long paragraph beginning "That open question now has a partial answer").
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Recomputed in `L06_sign_tying.py`. (a) Off-diagonal block determinant signs are $(-1)^{K_q}$ for $K_q=1,2,3$, with GL$^+$ frames and pure-gauge transport, provided the edge weights are positive *definite*. With merely positive semidefinite weights, as the text says, the determinant can vanish and the sign condition is false as stated. (b) Marginalizing within-cluster deviations at fixed uniform barycenters on a six-node scalar chain with clusters {0,1},{2,3},{4,5} gives a positive coarse (1,3) precision entry in 2000/2000 random draws. For unit weights with diagonal 0.1 the coarse precision has off-diagonals $-0.808,-0.808,+0.112$. The triangle sign product is $+$, violating the necessary condition, which confirms the text. In physics language this is the generation of a longer-range coupling with the wrong (antiferromagnetic) sign, the standard way a decimation leaves a nearest-neighbor ferromagnetic ansatz. (c) Tying $\mu_i=\Omega_{iI}\mu_I$ gives $S^\top\Lambda S$ equal to the cut-edge-sum Laplacian to $1.8\times10^{-15}$ ($K_q=2$, six agents, seven edges, random GL$^+$ frames). The positive operation is exactly the Galerkin coarse form of aggregation algebraic multigrid. It integrates out nothing and carries no fluctuation correction, so it is not an RG step. Its probabilistic status also differs by level. At the state level it restricts the recognition family (bound-costing, as the text says). At the Gibbs-configuration level of Appendix I, identification is precomposition, which is conditioning on a null event and depends on the chosen reference (Theory `c09`, `prop:cg-hard-identification`(iii)). The paragraph states the two results side by side without saying they live at different probabilistic levels.
**Fix:** Write "positive definite" for the edge weights. Replace "Identifying constituents instead of integrating them out preserves the family exactly" with "Identifying constituents (the Galerkin aggregation coarse operator $S^\top\Lambda S$ of algebraic multigrid) preserves the family exactly. This is a restriction of the recognition family, not a coarse-graining of fluctuations, and it carries no renormalization content." Add: "The marginalization failure is the generation of a next-nearest coupling of the wrong sign." Cite Theory Supplement G `g09_operator_coarsegraining.tex`, `eq:grg-aggregation` and `prop:grg-aggregation-semigroup`, where the same cut-edge sum appears with a declared rescaling.
**Falsifies:** A chain example with positive diagonal in which fixed-barycenter marginalization keeps all coarse off-diagonals nonpositive for every weight choice (the script found none).
**Status:** D, recomputed. The "positive" framing mildly inflates what is a variational restriction.

### L06-6: The Laplace step does generate a coupling shift, and the paper never states it: the one-loop term renormalizes the parent's entropy weight in proportion to cluster size
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3719 (`eq:rg_laplace`); 3701 ($H_I^\perp\approx L_I\otimes F$); 694 (fixed coefficients at every scale).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** Restricted to the barycentric constraint $\sum_iw_i\xi_i=0$, $\log\det(L_I\otimes F)|_{\mathrm{constraint}}=K\log\det'L_I+(n-1)\log\det F+K\log[(\mathbf 1\cdot w)^2/(n|w|^2)]$, with $n=|I|$. `L06_oneloop_logdet.py` gives residuals $\le3.6\times10^{-15}$ over three random trials ($n=4$, $K=3$). In the mean sector $F(q_I)=\Sigma_I^{-1}$ at coherence, so $\tfrac{T}{2}\log\det'H_I^\perp=\mathrm{const}-\tfrac{T_{\mathrm{cfg}}}{2}(|I|-1)\log\det\Sigma_I$. This is a shift of the coefficient of $\log\det\Sigma_I$, the entropy-like term of the parent's KL, by an amount proportional to $|I|-1$. It is the one concrete coupling renormalization the construction supports, and it contradicts "the same coefficients at every scale" at $O(T_{\mathrm{cfg}})$. Two notational points follow. For non-uniform $w$, $\det'$ (kernel $\mathbf 1$) is not the constrained determinant, which has kernel direction $w$; the difference is the constant $c(w)$ above. In `thm:rg_exact_closure`, $A_{\xi\xi}\succ0$ makes $\det'=\det$.
**Fix:** After `eq:rg_laplace` add: "In the mean sector the fluctuation determinant equals, up to a $Y$-independent constant, $-\tfrac{T_{\mathrm{cfg}}}{2}(|I|-1)\log\det\Sigma_I$: integrating out internal modes shifts the parent's entropy weight in proportion to cluster size, an induced renormalization absent at $T_{\mathrm{cfg}}=0$." Replace $\det'$ by the determinant on the constraint subspace.
**Falsifies:** A parent functional in which $\log\det\Sigma_I$ has no coefficient to shift (it has one through the KL self-terms), or an $F(q_I)$ independent of $\Sigma_I$.
**Status:** D (derived here under the coherent-cluster approximation the paper uses).

If the physics sense is intended, define an entropy-production rate for a Langevin version of the dynamics. ### L06-10: The covariance theorem is stated more narrowly than its proof needs and interpreted more broadly than it supports; three cited keys in scope are missing from the bibliography
**Location:** PIFB2.tex:3808-3812 (`thm:rg_covariance`); 1577 ("the correct coarse-graining target is the content of the renormalization-group covariance theorem"); 1683 (restriction to compact SO); 3762 (radius bound); citations at 1573, 1685, 3762.

(d) `Kendall1990`, `Afsari2011`, and `DorflerBullo2013` are cited but absent from `references.bib` (the file has `DorflerBullo2013Kron`), so they will render as undefined citations. ## Physics-audience and standalone notes

A referee at a statistical-physics or mathematical-physics venue will reject the word "renormalization" at 81 and 1804 before reading further, because nothing is rescaled and nothing flows. The status paragraph at 1681 is the right register and should become the only place RG vocabulary is used for the construction. The "scale" $s$ is a hierarchy depth, not a length. The paper should say so where $s$ is introduced (1705), and nowhere pair it with correlation lengths or scaling dimensions. The term "mass" for the stiffness $m_I$ (3713, "Internal disagreement modes are massive") is acceptable in field-theory usage but should be tied once to "inverse susceptibility of the internal modes".

The finite-size-scaling paragraph (3935-3937) writes $M_N=N^{-\beta/\nu}\Phi((g-g_c)N^{1/\nu})$ in the number of agents $N$. With $N\sim L^d$ the conventional exponents appear as $\beta/(d\nu)$ and $1/(d\nu)$. On a graph without a dimension, the exponents in $N$ must be declared as their own quantities, as in complete-graph (Curie-Weiss) scaling. The "susceptibility" defined as $N$ times the variance of the mean cluster size $S_N$ is unusual. In percolation the susceptibility is the mean cluster size itself. The paragraph is correctly fenced as future work but should adopt the standard definitions.

The Appendix I "Two precedents supply the rigorous backbone this construction approximates" (1683) overstates things: the construction approximates neither Bény-Osborne nor Mehta-Schwab. "Related prior work" is accurate.

For a standalone physics paper, Appendix I can shrink to about a third of its length. Keep the block map, the exact pushforward (two lines), the explicit negative closure result (the sign witness of L06-5), the mean-only Gaussian lemma (L06-2 fix), the Laplace step with its induced entropy shift (L06-6), and the closure-residual definitions `eq:rg_closure`. Move or delete the $C_1$ numerology, the Hermite thresholds, the working-form conductance paragraphs, and the Fenichel metric discussion, or replace them by pointers to the Theory. The effective-action subsection (3505-3567) is standard QFT material written correctly; a physics reader can follow it. The sentence that the direct-quotient and Faddeev-Popov prescriptions "must not be multiplied together" is right and useful.
